Spell Check

A free spelling checker. Paste your words, press Check, and it marks the words it does not know and offers fixes. No sign-in, no payment, free forever. Your words never leave the page.

How it works

  1. Write or paste any English text in the box.
  2. Press Check spelling. Every word is looked up in the archive's 96,088 headwords, plus their regular forms — plurals, past tense, -ing words, and the rest. Words the list does not know are marked in red.
  3. Pick a fix. Under each marked word you get the closest real words. Press one and it replaces the word in your text. Mix-ups like their / there get a plain-words note about which one you meant.

What it does not do: it does not guess at names, brand-new words, or words from other languages. A word it marks may still be right — a name, a place, or a new word. You decide.

Common mix-ups

  • their / there / they're — Their shows belonging. There shows a place. They're is short for "they are".
  • your / you're — Your shows belonging. You're is short for "you are".
  • its / it's — Its shows belonging. It's is short for "it is" or "it has".
  • to / too / two — To points somewhere. Too means "also" or "too much". Two is the number 2.
  • affect / effect — Affect is usually the action, to change something. Effect is usually the result.
  • then / than — Then is about time. Than is for comparing.
  • lose / loose — Lose is to misplace or fail to win. Loose means not tight.
  • principle / principal — Principle is a rule you live by. Principal is the main person, or the main amount of money.
  • compliment / complement — Compliment is kind words. Complement is something that completes.
  • stationary / stationery — Stationary means standing still. Stationery means paper and pens.
  • weather / whether — Weather is rain and sun. Whether means "if".
  • accept / except — Accept means to take or agree. Except means "but not".
