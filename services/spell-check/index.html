How it works

Write or paste. Put any English text in the box above — a sentence, a letter, a whole page.
Press Check spelling. Every word is looked up in the archive's 96,088 headwords, plus their regular forms — plurals, past tense, -ing words, and the rest. Words the list does not know are marked in red.
Pick a fix. Under each marked word you get the closest real words. Press one and it replaces the word in your text. Mix-ups like their / there get a plain-words note about which one you meant.

What it does not do: it does not guess at names, brand-new words, or words from other languages. A word it marks may still be right — a name, a place, or a new word. You decide.

Common mix-ups

Twelve pairs that trip up nearly everyone. Each one has a full record in the Spell Check Service Database, with the rule, the examples, and a way to remember it.

their / there / they're

Their shows belonging. There shows a place. They're is short for "they are".

Read the full record →

your / you're

Your shows belonging. You're is short for "you are". If you can swap in "you are", use you're.

Read the full record →

its / it's

Its shows belonging. It's is short for "it is" or "it has". No apostrophe ever means belonging here.

Read the full record →

to / too / two

To points somewhere. Too means "also" or "too much". Two is the number 2.

Read the full record →

affect / effect

Affect is usually the action — to change something. Effect is usually the result — the change itself.

Read the full record →

then / than

Then is about time — first this, then that. Than is for comparing — bigger than, smaller than.

Read the full record →

lose / loose

Lose is to misplace or fail to win. Loose means not tight. Lose has one o, like "lost".

Read the full record →

principle / principal

Principle is a rule you live by. Principal is the main person, or the main amount of money.

compliment / complement

Compliment is kind words. Complement is something that completes — the other half.

stationary / stationery

Stationary means standing still. Stationery means paper and pens — think "e" for envelope.

weather / whether

Weather is rain and sun. Whether means "if" — as in "whether or not".

accept / except

Accept means to take or agree. Except means "but not" — everyone except Sam.
