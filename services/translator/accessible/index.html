Translator

A free phrasebook translator. Type a phrase in English, pick Spanish, French, German, or Italian, and read the translation from a book of checked phrases — the same phrases held in the Translator Service Database. No sign-in, no payment, free forever. Your words never leave the page.

Open the working translator (needs JavaScript)

How it works

  1. Type a phrase. Write English words in the box — a greeting, a question, a few plain words.
  2. Pick a language. Choose Spanish, French, German, or Italian, then press Translate.
  3. Read the answer. The tool looks each phrase up in its book and shows the translation. Words the book does not hold are marked plainly as not in this phrasebook — it never guesses.

What it covers: the phrases in its book — greetings, thanks, numbers, days, food, asking the way, at the shop, at the doctor, family, colours, weather, time, and emergency words — across four languages. What it does not do: it does not translate a sentence it has never seen, a joke, or a long speech. What it cannot translate, it says so in plain words.

The phrasebook in words

The book holds 80 phrases and words. A few to show how it reads:

  • hello — Spanish: hola; French: bonjour; German: Hallo; Italian: ciao.
  • good morning — Spanish: buenos dias; French: bonjour; German: Guten Morgen; Italian: buongiorno.
  • thank you — Spanish: gracias; French: merci; German: danke; Italian: grazie.
  • please — Spanish: por favor; French: s'il vous plait; German: bitte; Italian: per favore.
  • where is the station — Spanish: donde esta la estacion; French: ou est la gare; German: wo ist der Bahnhof; Italian: dov'e la stazione.
  • water — Spanish: agua; French: eau; German: Wasser; Italian: acqua.
  • one, two, three — Spanish: uno, dos, tres; French: un, deux, trois; German: eins, zwei, drei; Italian: uno, due, tre.
  • help — Spanish: ayuda; French: au secours; German: Hilfe; Italian: aiuto.

The full book, all 80 phrases and words in all four languages, is downloadable as phrasebook.json.

What it does

  • What a translator does — saying the same thing in another language.
  • Greetings in four languages — hello, good morning, and good evening.
  • Please, thank you and sorry — three words that carry a traveller further than any other three.
  • Yes, no and maybe — with these three, a traveller can answer nearly any simple question.
  • Numbers one to ten — needed for what things cost, for times, and for addresses.
  • Days of the week — Monday to Sunday in four languages.
  • Food and drink words — water, bread, coffee, cheese, and wine.
  • Asking the way — where is the station, left, right, and straight ahead.
  • At the shop — asking what things cost, and asking for the change.
  • At the doctor: plain words — I need a doctor, and it hurts here.
  • Hello and goodbye — goodbye, good night, see you tomorrow, and welcome.
  • Family words — mother, father, friend, and child.
