How it works

  1. Find an element by its place in the table, or search by name, symbol, or atomic number on the main page.
  2. Read its square: atomic number, symbol, name, and atomic mass.
  3. Read its category, group, and period below the table, with a plain-words note about what it does.
  4. Filter by category — alkali metals, halogens, noble gases, lanthanides, actinides, and the rest — to see one family at a time.

What it cannot do: it cannot show what an element looks like in a laboratory, it cannot predict a reaction, and the masses shown for elements made only in laboratories are the whole numbers of their longest-lived forms, such as 294 for oganesson, not measured averages.
