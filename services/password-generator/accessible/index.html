How it works

  1. Pick a length from 8 to 64 and tick the sets of letters to use: lower-case, upper-case, digits, and marks.
  2. Press Make a password. The letters are drawn from the browser's secure random source.
  3. Read the strength meter: it shows the length, the bits of guessing work, and a rating from weak to very strong.
  4. Copy the password into your keeper. Give every place its own password.
  5. Or press Make a passphrase for four words picked at random from a list of 128 ordinary words, or Make a 6-digit PIN for a device that locks after a few wrong tries.

What it cannot do: it cannot remember your passwords for you, it cannot check whether a password has leaked elsewhere, and a password it makes is only strong if you keep it secret and use it in one place only. The letters are drawn with crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's secure random source — never from a fixed list and never from a simple formula. A passphrase uses a word list of 128 ordinary words, loaded from the files below.

The rules

The rules the tool follows, in plain words:

  • Length matters most. 12 letters is the minimum; 16 is the standard; 20 is for a master password.
  • Four sets. Lower-case letters, upper-case letters, digits, and marks such as ! and #. Each letter drawn from all four sets carries more guessing work.
  • Random means unpredictable. Letters come from crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's secure random source, never from a fixed list.
  • Strength in bits. A 16-letter password from all four sets carries about 105 bits of guessing work and rates very strong; an 8-letter password of lower-case letters alone carries about 38 bits and rates weak.
  • Passphrases. Four words picked at random from a list of 128 ordinary words carry about 28 bits each, 112 bits in all — strong, and plain enough to remember.
  • PINs. A 6-digit PIN carries about 20 bits. It is safe only where the device locks after a few wrong tries.
  • One password per place. Never reuse a password. A leak in one place must not open the rest.
