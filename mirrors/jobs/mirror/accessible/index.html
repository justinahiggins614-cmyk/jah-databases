Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Jobs is a mirror of the jobs kind of website: a website whose job is to list openings for work, with the employer and the place, so a person looking for work can find an opening that fits and learn how to apply. The jobs kind of website grew from printed notices of work on boards and in papers, then from lists of openings by trade and place, and then from searchable lists with notes on each opening. This mirror follows that same shape: titles, employers, places, types, and notes, with a search over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample openings made for this mirror, about made-up trades, employers, and places. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample openings, not as real jobs, employers, or vacancies. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and an opening J with a title, an employer, a place, a type, and notes, the opening matches when:

match(Q, J) is true when every word of Q appears in J's title, employer, place, type, or notes

Every matching opening scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "full time" against the sample set. Six openings carry the words "full" and "time" in their type: Baker, Teacher, Driver, Cook, Carpenter, and Nurse. All six match, all score 2, and Baker lists first because B comes before C, D, N, and T. The query "harbour" matches two openings: Baker and Painter, and again the first in alphabetical order lists first.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how work is matched to people in plain words. An opening is described by its title (the trade), its employer (who offers the work), its place (where the work happens), and its type (full time or part time). A person looking for work holds the same four facts in mind, and a match is when the words they ask for appear in the opening's description.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned openings that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching openings in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule - every query word must appear - favours precision: it returns fewer openings, but every opening it returns carries every word you asked for. The openings, employers, and places are samples; they are not real jobs or vacancies.
