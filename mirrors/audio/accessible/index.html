Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Audio is a mirror of the audio kind of website: a website whose job is to let a listener hear spoken audio and shows, grouped by genre, so a listener can pick a show and listen to it. The audio kind of website began as spoken recordings on discs and tapes, then as lists of shows with lengths, and then as searchable show lists on a screen. This mirror follows that same shape: shows with a title, a genre, a length in minutes, and a short description, with a search over the shows.

What this mirror holds is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample shows made for this mirror, with made-up titles and descriptions, about mornings, gardens, stories, tunes, weather, books, and everyday things. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample shows, not as real shows, real audio files, or real-world events. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a show S with a title, a genre, and a description, the show matches when:

match(Q, S) is true when every word of Q appears in S's title, genre, or description

Every matching show scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are listed in the order the shows were written for this mirror, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "talk" against the sample set. Five shows carry the word "talk" as their genre: "Morning Notes", "The Quiet Garden Hour", "Weather in Plain Words", "The Night Desk", and "How Things Work". All five match, all score 1, and the first listed is "Morning Notes". The query "stories" matches three shows: "Stories by the Window", "The Bread Basket Hour", and "Letters from the Library".

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how spoken audio works: a voice makes sound waves in the air, a recording keeps the pattern of those waves, and playback turns the pattern back into sound a listener can hear. A show list adds a second idea: each show carries a title, a genre, and a length, so a listener can pick by subject and by how long they have. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of shows, each carrying a title, a genre, a length in minutes, and a short description, with a filter that keeps only the shows carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned shows that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching shows in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer shows, but every show it returns contains every word you asked for. The shows here are samples made for this mirror, with no real audio files behind them.
