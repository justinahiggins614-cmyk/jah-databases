Section 1

Description and history

Signature Wellness is a mirror of the health kind of website: a website whose job is to give health information, describe common topics, and point to services, so a reader can learn in plain words and know where to turn next. The health kind of website began as printed health guides, then as lists of topics by area, and then as searchable topic lists. This mirror follows that same shape: topic titles, an area for each, and a search over the topics.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample topics made for this mirror, with short plain notes. They are labeled plainly as Signature samples and as general information only. This mirror gives no diagnosis, makes no cure claim, and gives no specific dosing. For real symptoms, see a clinician. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's search rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a topic T with a title, an area, and a text, the topic matches when:

match(Q, T) is true when every word of Q appears in T's title, area, or text

Every matching topic scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query. The area count is the plain count of topics carrying one area name.

Worked example: the query "rest" against the sample set. Two topics carry the word "rest": "Rest breaks" (area Rest) and "Sleep: why rest matters" (area Rest). Both match, both score 1, and "Rest breaks" lists first by title. The Rest area holds 2 topics in total.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how health information is organized and found: by area, so a reader can read one area at a time. A reader who wants rest reads the rest topics; a reader who wants movement reads the movement topics. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of topics, each carrying a title, an area, and a short plain text, with a filter that keeps only the topics carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned topics that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching topics in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer topics, but every topic it returns contains every word you asked for. The notes are general information only; they describe common habits in plain words and never claim a cure, never give a dose, and never name a condition in any one reader.
