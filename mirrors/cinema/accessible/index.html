Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Cinema is a mirror of the cinema kind of website: a website whose job is to show films and shows, grouped by genre, so a viewer can pick something to watch and find more of the same kind. The cinema kind of website grew from printed listings of films by genre, then from shelves of titles, and then from searchable lists with notes on each title. This mirror follows that same shape: titles, genres, years, and notes, with a search over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample films made for this mirror, about made-up stories and places. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample films, not as real-world films or shows. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a film F with a title, a genre, and notes, the film matches when:

match(Q, F) is true when every word of Q appears in F's title, genre, or notes

Every matching film scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "comedy" against the sample set. Two films carry the word "comedy" in their genre: "The Clockwork Garden" and "The Laughing Baker". Both match, both score 1, and "The Clockwork Garden" lists first because C comes before L. The query "garden" matches one film: "The Clockwork Garden".

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how films are grouped so they can be found: by genre. A genre is a shared shape - a comedy aims to make people laugh, a mystery hides an answer until the end, an adventure follows a journey. Grouping by genre lets a viewer who liked one film find others with the same shape, without watching every title first.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned films that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching films in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule - every query word must appear - favours precision: it returns fewer films, but every film it returns carries every word you asked for. The years are the years of the sample films; they are not years of real-world releases.
