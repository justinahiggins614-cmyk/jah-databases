Section 1

Description and history

Signature Money is a mirror of the money kind of website: a website whose job is to show accounts, prices, markets, and budgets, so a reader can see where money sits and what a plan costs. The money kind of website began as printed price lists and account books, then as tables of figures, and then as searchable lists of accounts and budgets. This mirror follows that same shape: named accounts, a type for each, a balance in model coins, and a search over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample accounts, baskets, and budgets made for this mirror, with model figures only. They are labeled plainly as Signature samples, not as real market data, real prices, or real rates. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's search rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and an account A with a name, a type, and a note, the account matches when:

match(Q, A) is true when every word of Q appears in A's name, type, or note

Every matching account scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered by balance, largest first; ties are broken alphabetically by name, so the order is the same every time for the same query. The total balance is the plain sum of the ten sample balances.

Worked example: the query "savings" against the sample set. Four accounts carry the word "savings": "Harbour Savings Jar" (1,200 model coins), "Holiday Savings Jar" (800), "Rainy Day Fund" (600, type Savings), and "Tool Savings Jar" (500). All four match, all score 1, and the largest balance lists first. The total of all ten sample balances is 1,200 + 350 + 800 + 450 + 275 + 120 + 90 + 600 + 500 + 400 = 4,785 model coins.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how money figures are organized and compared: by account, by type, and by size. A reader who wants one type of account reads that type's rows; a reader who wants the biggest balance reads by size. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of accounts, each carrying a name, a type, a balance in model coins, and a short note, with a filter that keeps only the accounts carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned accounts that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching accounts in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer accounts, but every account it returns contains every word you asked for. All figures are model figures in model coins; none is a real price, a real rate, or a real market value.
