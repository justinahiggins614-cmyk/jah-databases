Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's game rule is fixed by a seed. With seed 7, the secret number is:

secret = (seed × 6) mod 100 = (7 × 6) mod 100 = 42

Each guess is judged by comparison: a guess below the secret is answered "higher", a guess above it is answered "lower", and a guess equal to it is correct. The score depends only on how many tries the correct guess took:

score = 100 − 10 × (tries − 1), down to a minimum of 0

Worked example: a player guesses 30 (higher), then 50 (lower), then 42 (correct). That is 3 tries, so score = 100 − 10 × (3 − 1) = 100 − 20 = 80. A correct guess on the first try scores 100. A correct guess on the 12th try scores 100 − 110 = −10, which the minimum lifts to 0.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is the science of play and feedback. A game is a loop: the player acts, the game answers, and the player acts again with new information. The guessing game is the smallest honest form of that loop — each answer narrows the range of numbers the secret can be, so a careful player finds it in a few tries by halving the range each time.

Games also train timing and attention: the eye watches, the hand answers, and the brain keeps score. A fixed seed matters here: because the secret comes from a stated rule and not from a hidden draw, any two players face the same game, their scores can be compared fairly, and the demo can be checked by hand. Nothing on this page is left to chance.
