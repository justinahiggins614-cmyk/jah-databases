Section 1

Description and history

Signature Library is a mirror of the online bookstore kind of website: a website where visitors find books by subject, read about each one, and read or buy the ones they want. The kind grew out of the printed bookshop and the public library: first came shelves a visitor could walk, then printed lists of books by subject, then network catalogs where a search word returns every matching book with its page count and price. This mirror follows that same shape — a catalog, a search by subject, and a book page — rebuilt with Signature branding and the network's own data.

What this mirror lists is different from the kind it mirrors: it lists only its own sample catalog of Signature-made books, in Section 4. It keeps no reading history, asks for no account, and sells nothing at a price — every book here is free forever. The mirror is part of the mirror program: one Signature mirror for each kind of website, each one searching or playing only the network's own data.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's page-count rule is a plain sum. For a set of matching books with page counts P1, P2, … Pn, the total pages are:

T = P1 + P2 + … + Pn

The average book length is T ÷ n. The mirror matches a subject word against the subjects on its sample books: a book joins the results when any word of the query appears in its subjects or in its title.

Worked example: the subject word "stories" matches four sample books, with page counts 214, 186, 164, and 267. T = 214 + 186 + 164 + 267 = 831 pages. The average book length is 831 ÷ 4 = 207.75 pages.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is the science of reading and of print. A book is ink on paper: letters shaped so the eye can follow them line by line, bound in order so the story or the lesson runs from the first page to the last. Reading turns those marks into meaning — the eye takes in a few words at a glance, the brain holds them, and the sense of the sentence builds as the line goes on.

A catalog is an index: for every book it records a title, a subject, and a page count, so a search word can find its matches without opening every book. The mirror's demo is that index in its smallest honest form: it searches the subjects on its own sample books and adds up the page counts by the rule in Section 2. Nothing here is read from any outside shelf.

Section 5

Spec / patent + 3D-print version

Spec. The Signature spec for this mirror is a draft specification for a book mirror over a fixed, network-owned catalog: the book record format (title, subjects, page count, short description), the subject-matching rule and the page-count sum from Section 2, and the no-tracking, no-account, no-server design. The spec is a draft — ready to file, not filed, not a granted patent. It is part of the mirror program's spec pack.

3D-print version. A bookstore mirror is software, and a book's printed form is a bound stack of pages made by a printer and a binder, not by a 3D printer. This mirror carries no invented 3D-print version of a book, and says so plainly.
