Section 1

Description and history

Signature Words is a mirror of the dictionary and language kind of website: a website whose job is to give quick word help — a plain-word definition, a part of speech, and spelling help — so a reader can check one word fast. This mirror is distinct from the network site The Signature Dictionary: that site is the full dictionary, while this mirror is a different job, quick word help over a small sample word list. The dictionary kind of website began as lists of words with short meanings beside them, then as books ordered by word, then as searchable word lists. This mirror follows that same shape: words in word order, each with a part of speech and a short definition.

What this mirror defines is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten common words with the mirror's own plain-word definitions, made for this mirror. They are labeled plainly as the mirror's own sample dictionary, not copied from any real dictionary and not presented as real dictionary entries. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's lookup rule is a deterministic function. For a word W typed by the visitor, the mirror cleans W to lower-case letters only, then looks for an exact match in its word list:

lookup(W) returns the definition of W when W is in the list, and nothing when it is not

The search rule is the same shape as the other mirrors. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and an entry E with a word, a part of speech, and a definition, the entry matches when every word of Q appears in E's word, part of speech, or definition. Results are ordered alphabetically by word, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "bread" as a lookup. The cleaned word is "bread", it is in the list, and the mirror returns "a food made from flour, water, and heat, baked in an oven." The query "water" as a search matches two entries: "bread" and "rain", because both definitions carry the word "water", and "bread" lists first in word order.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how words carry meaning: a word is a sound or a set of letters that people agree to use for one thing, and a definition is a short set of plain words that says what that thing is. Spelling is the agreed order of the letters in a word, and the part of speech says how the word is used — as a thing, an action, or a describing word. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of words, each carrying a word, a part of speech, and a short plain-word definition, with a lookup that finds one word and a search that finds every entry carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a search is. Precision is the share of returned entries that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching entries in the set that the search actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer entries, but every entry it returns contains every word you asked for. The definitions are the mirror's own plain-word definitions; they are not copied from any real dictionary.
