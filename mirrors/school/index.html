Section 1

Description and history

Signature School is a mirror of the education kind of website: a website whose job is to offer courses and lessons by subject, so a learner can pick a subject and work through its lessons in order. The education kind of website began as printed lesson books, then as lists of courses by subject, and then as searchable course lists with lesson counts. This mirror follows that same shape: course titles, a subject for each, a lesson count, a level, and a search over the courses.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample courses made for this mirror, with plain titles and lesson counts. They are labeled plainly as Signature samples, not as real-world courses or quoted lessons. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's search rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a course C with a title, a subject, and a level, the course matches when:

match(Q, C) is true when every word of Q appears in C's title, subject, or level

Every matching course scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query. The total lessons is the plain sum of the ten sample lesson counts.

Worked example: the query "maths" against the sample set. Three courses carry the word "maths": "Adding Small Numbers" (10 lessons), "Counting to One Hundred" (12 lessons), and "Shapes in the World" (7 lessons). All three match, all score 1, and "Adding Small Numbers" lists first by title. The total lessons across all ten sample courses is 87.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how learning is organized and found: by subject, so a learner can read one subject at a time, and by level, so a learner can start where they are. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of courses, each carrying a title, a subject, a lesson count, and a level, with a filter that keeps only the courses carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned courses that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching courses in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer courses, but every course it returns contains every word you asked for. The lesson counts are counts of sample lessons in the mirror's own set; they are not counts of any real course.
