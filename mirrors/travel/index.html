Section 1

Description and history

Signature Travel is a mirror of the travel kind of website: a website whose job is to help a person plan a trip - a place to go, a place to stay, and a way to get there. The travel kind of website grew from printed guides to towns and roads, then from lists of stays and routes, and then from searchable trip plans with a stay and a way of getting there on each one. This mirror follows that same shape: places, stays, transport, and notes, with a search over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample trips made for this mirror, about made-up places and journeys. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample trips, not as real trips, places, prices, or timetables. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a trip T with a title, a place, a stay, transport, and notes, the trip matches when:

match(Q, T) is true when every word of Q appears in T's title, place, stay, transport, or notes

Every matching trip scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "train" against the sample set. Four trips carry the word "train" in their transport: "Coast Walk", "Market Tour", "Old Town Stay", and "Lake Retreat". All four match, all score 1, and "Coast Walk" lists first because C comes before L, M, and O. The query "mountain" matches one trip: "Mountain Pass".

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how a trip is put together from three parts: a place to go, a place to stay, and a way to get there. A trip plan is a match between those three parts - the stay must sit near the place, and the way of getting there must reach both. Listing trips by transport, by stay, or by place is three views of the same matching problem.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned trips that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching trips in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule - every query word must appear - favours precision: it returns fewer trips, but every trip it returns carries every word you asked for. The places, stays, and journeys are samples; they are not real places, prices, or timetables.
