Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Weather is a mirror of the weather kind of website: a website where a visitor picks a place and reads the forecast — the conditions, the temperature, and what it will feel like. Weather websites began as printed tables of readings, then as maps with symbols for sun, cloud, rain, and snow, then as models that compute a forecast from measurements. This mirror follows the same shape — a place, a condition, and a temperature — rebuilt with Signature branding and the network's own data.

What this mirror forecasts is different from the kind it mirrors: it forecasts nothing real. Its table is a sample set of ten named places with sample conditions, made for this mirror. The demo shows how a forecast is computed from a table, and it says so plainly wherever the numbers appear. No sample condition here is a real current condition anywhere.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's demonstration model is a deterministic rule. For a place with sample temperature T and sample wind speed W, the feels-like temperature is:

feels-like = T − W ÷ 2

The temperature band is a second rule: 25 degrees or more is warm; 15 to 24 degrees is mild; 5 to 14 degrees is cool; below 5 degrees is cold.

Worked example: Silver Bay has a sample temperature of 18 degrees and a sample wind speed of 6. The feels-like temperature is 18 − 6 ÷ 2 = 18 − 3 = 15 degrees. At 18 degrees the band is mild. The same place always gives the same demonstration forecast, because the rule depends only on the fixed sample table.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is weather measurement: temperature, wind, and precipitation. A real forecast starts from readings — thermometers, wind gauges, and rain gauges — feeds them into a model of the air, and reads out what the air is likely to do next. This mirror shows the shape of that work in miniature: a fixed table of readings and a rule that turns readings into a feels-like temperature and a band.

That is where the honesty matters. A demonstration model over a sample table is not a real forecast: it has no live readings, no live model, and no claim about the sky anywhere. The mirror labels it as a demonstration model in the hint, in this section, and in Section 6, so no reader mistakes a sample condition for a real current condition.
