Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Encyclopedia is a mirror of the encyclopedia kind of website: a website whose job is to explain subjects in articles, each article linked to related articles, so a reader can learn one subject and follow the links to the next. The encyclopedia kind of website began as printed books of articles arranged by subject, then as sets of volumes, and then as websites where every article links to others. This mirror follows that same shape: a search box, articles in plain words, and links between them.

What this mirror holds is the network's own material. Its sample set is twelve articles made for this mirror, summarizing network subjects in general terms. The full record set lives in the network's databases: 63,047 databases holding 12,349,330 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's lookup rule is a deterministic scoring function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and an article A with a title and keywords, the score is:

score(Q, A) = 3 × (number of query words appearing in A's title) + 1 × (number of query words appearing in A's keywords)

An article appears in the results only if every query word appears in its title or its keywords. Results are ordered by score, highest first; ties are broken alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "stars" against the article "Stars" (keywords: stars light sky night). The word "stars" appears in the title (3 points) and in the keywords (1 point), so score = 3 × 1 + 1 × 1 = 4. The query "rivers" against "Bridges" (keywords: bridges roads rivers crossing) scores 1 × 1 = 1, because "rivers" is in the keywords only. The query "water" matches three articles at score 1 each, so they order alphabetically: Rain, Rivers, Ships.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how knowledge is organized: an encyclopedia groups what is known into articles, one per subject, and links articles that share ideas. A reader who opens one article can follow the links to nearby subjects. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of articles, each carrying a title, keywords, and a short summary, with a search that scores titles above keywords.

Two measures describe how good such a search is. Precision is the share of returned articles that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching articles in the set that the search actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear in the title or keywords — favours precision: it returns fewer articles, but every article it returns contains every word you asked for.
