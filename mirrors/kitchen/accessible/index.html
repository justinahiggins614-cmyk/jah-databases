Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Kitchen is a mirror of the recipes kind of website: a website whose job is to help a reader find and follow recipes, so a cook can pick a dish by its main food and read its steps in order. The recipes kind of website began as printed cookbooks, then as lists of dishes by main food, and then as searchable recipe lists with times. This mirror follows that same shape: dish names, a main food for each, a time in minutes, and a search over the recipes.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample recipes made for this mirror, with short plain steps. They are labeled plainly as Signature samples, not as real-world dishes or quoted recipes. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's search rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a recipe R with a name, a main food, and steps, the recipe matches when:

match(Q, R) is true when every word of Q appears in R's name, main food, or steps

Every matching recipe scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered by time, shortest first; ties are broken alphabetically by name, so the order is the same every time for the same query. The total time is the plain sum of the ten sample times.

Worked example: the query "soup" against the sample set. Three recipes carry the word "soup": "Carrot Soup" (25 minutes), "Pea Soup" (25 minutes), and "Tomato Soup" (30 minutes). All three match, all score 1, and the two 25-minute recipes list first, Carrot Soup before Pea Soup by name. The total time across all ten sample recipes is 330 minutes.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how cooking is organized and found: by main food and by time. A reader who wants one food reads that food's recipes; a reader in a hurry reads the shortest recipes first. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of recipes, each carrying a name, a main food, a time in minutes, and short steps, with a filter that keeps only the recipes carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned recipes that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching recipes in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer recipes, but every recipe it returns contains every word you asked for. The steps are short plain directions for the sample dish; they use no exact measures beyond the food list, and they make no claim about any real dish.
