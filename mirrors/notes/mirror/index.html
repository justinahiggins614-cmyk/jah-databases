Section 1

Description and history

Signature Notes is a mirror of the notes kind of website: a website whose job is to let a visitor write and keep notes, each note carrying a title, a tag, and a short body, so a note can be found again by its words. The notes kind of website began as paper notebooks, then as lists of short written notes that could be searched by word. This mirror follows that same shape: titles, tags, bodies, and a search over the notes.

What this mirror keeps is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample notes made for this mirror, about the network's own subjects. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample notes, not as real notes from real people. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a note N with a title, a tag, and a body, the note matches when:

match(Q, N) is true when every word of Q appears in N's title, tag, or body

Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "science" against the sample set. Three notes carry the word "science" in their tag: "How rain forms", "Why clocks keep time", and "Star spotting notes". All three match, and "How rain forms" lists first because H comes first in title order. The query "gardening" matches two notes: "Garden bed plan" and "Seed sorting list", and again the earlier title lists first.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how writing is stored and found: by word and by tag. A tag groups notes that belong together; a word in a note's title or body marks what the note is about. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of notes, each carrying a title, a tag, and a short body, with a filter that keeps only the notes carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned notes that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching notes in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer notes, but every note it returns contains every word you asked for. The bodies are the bodies of the sample notes; they are not notes from real people.
