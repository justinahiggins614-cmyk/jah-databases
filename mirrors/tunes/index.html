Section 1

Description and history

Signature Tunes is a mirror of the music streaming kind of website: a website that plays songs and albums on demand, over the network, instead of asking the visitor to own a copy of each song. The kind grew out of recorded music and network radio: first songs were sold one by one as files, then services kept the songs on their own machines and sent the sound to the listener while it played, so a listener could hear any song in the catalog without storing it. This mirror follows that same shape — a catalog, a search by mood, and a playlist — rebuilt with Signature branding and the network's own data.

What this mirror plays is different from the kind it mirrors: it plays only its own sample catalog of Signature-made tracks, listed in Section 4. It keeps no listening history, it asks for no account, and it sells nothing, because there is nothing here to sell. The mirror is part of the mirror program: one Signature mirror for each kind of website, each one searching or playing only the network's own data.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's playlist rule is a plain sum. For a playlist of tracks with lengths L1, L2, … Ln in seconds, the total play time is:

T = L1 + L2 + … + Ln

The average track length is T ÷ n. The mirror matches a mood word against the tags on its sample tracks: a track joins the playlist when any word of the query appears in its tags or in its title.

Worked example: the mood word "calm" matches three sample tracks, with lengths 184, 201, and 176 seconds. T = 184 + 201 + 176 = 561 seconds. 561 ÷ 60 = 9 with a remainder of 21, so the total play time is 9 minutes 21 seconds. The average track length is 561 ÷ 3 = 187 seconds, which is 3 minutes 7 seconds.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is the science of sound and hearing. Sound is vibration moving through the air: a plucked string or a struck drum shakes the air, the shaking reaches the ear as a wave, and the ear turns the wave into what we hear as a note. Pitch is how fast the wave repeats; loudness is how tall the wave is; tempo is how fast the notes follow one another.

A recording stores the height of the wave, measured many thousands of times per second, so the wave can be rebuilt later by a speaker. Streaming sends those measurements over the network while the song plays, a little ahead of the speaker, so the music keeps playing without waiting for the whole file. The mirror's demo works from the track lengths in its own sample catalog, added up by the rule in Section 2.
