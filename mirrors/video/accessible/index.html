Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Video is a mirror of the video kind of website: a website whose job is to hold videos, grouped by subject and by channel, so a viewer can find a video to watch and follow a channel for more. The video kind of website began as short clips on pages, then as channels that gather videos by maker, and then as searchable catalogs with lengths and subjects. This mirror follows that same shape: a search box, a catalog with channels and lengths, and subjects to browse.

What this mirror holds is the network's own material. Its sample set is twelve catalog entries made for this mirror, each a title, a channel, a subject, and a length in minutes and seconds. They are labeled plainly as the mirror's own sample catalog. The network itself holds 63,047 databases and 12,349,330 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's lookup rule is a deterministic scoring function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a video V with a title, a channel, and a subject, the score is:

score(Q, V) = 3 × (number of query words appearing in V's title) + 1 × (number of query words appearing in V's channel or subject)

A video appears in the results only if every query word appears in its title, channel, or subject. Results are ordered by score, highest first; ties are broken alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query. A length in seconds is shown as minutes and seconds: minutes = floor(seconds / 60), and the rest is the remaining seconds.

Worked example: the query "song" against "How a song is built" (channel: Song Room, subject: music). The word "song" appears in the title (3 points) and in the channel (1 point), so score = 3 × 1 + 1 × 1 = 4. Its length is 360 seconds, which is 360 / 60 = 6 minutes and 0 seconds, shown as 6:00. The query "science" matches seven videos at score 1 each, so they order alphabetically, starting with "How birds fly".

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how video is stored and found: a video is a sequence of still pictures shown fast enough to look like motion, with sound beside it, and a catalog finds a video by its title, its channel, and its subject. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample catalog of entries, each carrying a title, a channel, a subject, and a length, with a search that scores titles above channels and subjects.

Two measures describe how good such a search is. Precision is the share of returned videos that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching videos in the catalog that the search actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear in the title, channel, or subject — favours precision: it returns fewer videos, but every video it returns contains every word you asked for.
