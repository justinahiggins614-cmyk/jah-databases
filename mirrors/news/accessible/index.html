Section 1 — Description and history

Signature News is a mirror of the news kind of website: a website whose job is to publish current reports, grouped by subject and ordered by date, so a reader can see what is new and find reports on one subject. The news kind of website began as printed papers, then as lists of headlines with dates, and then as searchable archives. This mirror follows that same shape: headlines, subjects, dates, and a search over the reports.

What this mirror reports is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample reports made for this mirror, about the network's own mirrors and about general subjects. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample reports, not as real-world news. The network itself holds 63,047 databases and 12,349,330 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a report R with a headline, a subject, and a body, the report matches when:

match(Q, R) is true when every word of Q appears in R's headline, subject, or body

Every matching report scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered by date, newest first; ties are broken alphabetically by headline, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "science" against the sample set. Two reports carry the word "science" in their subject: "Science notes: how rain forms" (2026-11-04) and "Science notes: why clocks keep time" (2026-11-03). Both match, both score 1, and the newer one lists first. The query "bread" matches two reports: "Cookbook mirror adds bread and soup pages" (2026-11-08) and "Food notes: baking bread at home" (2026-11-02), and again the newer one lists first.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how reports are organized and found: by subject and by date. A reader who wants one subject reads that subject's reports; a reader who wants what is new reads by date. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of reports, each carrying a headline, a subject, a date, and a short body, with a filter that keeps only the reports carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned reports that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching reports in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer reports, but every report it returns contains every word you asked for. The dates are the dates of the sample reports; they are not dates of real-world events.
