Section 1

Description and history

Signature Photos is a mirror of the photos kind of website: a website whose job is to hold pictures, grouped by subject, so a viewer can browse one subject and share a picture with someone else. The photos kind of website grew from printed albums of pictures by subject, then from folders of images on a screen, and then from searchable collections with a title and notes on each picture. This mirror follows that same shape: titles, subjects, and notes, with a search over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample photos made for this mirror, about made-up scenes and places. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample photos, not as real-world photos. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a photo P with a title, a subject, and notes, the photo matches when:

match(Q, P) is true when every word of Q appears in P's title, subject, or notes

Every matching photo scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "garden" against the sample set. Two photos carry the word "garden" in their subject: "Morning in the Garden" and "The Garden Gate". Both match, both score 1, and "Morning in the Garden" lists first because M comes before T. The query "river" matches two photos: "River at Dusk" and "River Stones", and "River Stones" lists first in alphabetical order.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how a picture is found by words. A picture on its own carries no words, so each picture is given a title, a subject, and short notes that say what the picture shows. Those words are the picture's index: a search over the words finds the picture, and a browse by subject groups pictures that share a subject.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned photos that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching photos in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule - every query word must appear - favours precision: it returns fewer photos, but every photo it returns carries every word you asked for. The notes describe sample scenes; they are not records of real-world places.
