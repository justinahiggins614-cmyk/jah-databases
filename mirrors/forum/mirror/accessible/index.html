Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Forum is a mirror of the forum kind of website: a website whose job is to host public discussions, grouped by topic, where each discussion starts with a question or a thought and gathers replies, so a reader can follow one topic and join in. The forum kind of website began as public notice boards, then as lists of topics with threads of replies under each one. This mirror follows that same shape: topics, discussion titles, reply counts, and a search over the discussions.

What this mirror discusses is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample discussions made for this mirror, about the network's own subjects, with made-up starter names. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample discussions, not as real discussions by real people. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a discussion D with a title and a topic, the discussion matches when:

match(Q, D) is true when every word of Q appears in D's title or topic

Results are ordered by reply count, most replies first; ties are broken alphabetically by title, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "science" against the sample set. Three discussions carry the word "science" in their topic: "How clocks keep time" (17 replies), "Why does rain fall?" (13 replies), and "Stars to spot in spring" (6 replies). All three match, and the one with the most replies lists first. The query "garden" matches two discussions: "How do I start a garden bed?" (14 replies) and "Growing herbs in a garden bed" (12 replies), and again the one with more replies lists first.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how discussions are organized and found: by topic and by thread. A topic groups discussions that belong together; a thread keeps one discussion's replies in one place, in order, so a reader can follow what was said. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of discussions, each carrying a title, a topic, a reply count, and a starter name, with a filter that keeps only the discussions carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned discussions that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching discussions in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer discussions, but every discussion it returns contains every word you asked for. The reply counts are the counts of the sample discussions; they are not counts of real discussions.
