Section 1 — Description and history

Signature Sports is a mirror of the sports kind of website: a website whose job is to show scores, fixtures, tables, and reports, so a reader can see who played, what the score was, and where a team stands. The sports kind of website began as printed results lists, then as tables with points, and then as searchable fixture lists. This mirror follows that same shape: team names, scores, leagues, dates, and a search over the fixtures.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample fixtures made for this mirror, with made-up team names and sample scores. They are labeled plainly as Signature samples, not as real-world scores or results. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's search rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a fixture F with two team names and a league name, the fixture matches when:

match(Q, F) is true when every word of Q appears in F's team names or league name

Every matching fixture scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are ordered by date, newest first; ties are broken alphabetically by home team name, so the order is the same every time for the same query. The total goals is the plain sum of all home and away sample scores.

Worked example: the query "kites" against the sample set. Two fixtures carry the word "kites": "Red Kites 3 - 1 Blue Foxes" (2026-11-10) and "Yellow Bees 1 - 3 Red Kites" (2026-11-04). Both match, both score 1, and the newer one lists first. The total goals across all ten sample fixtures is 27.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how results are organized and found: by team, by league, and by date. A reader who wants one team reads that team's fixtures; a reader who wants one league reads that league's table; a reader who wants what is new reads by date. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of fixtures, each carrying two team names, two scores, a league name, and a date, with a filter that keeps only the fixtures carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned fixtures that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching fixtures in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer fixtures, but every fixture it returns contains every word you asked for. The dates are the dates of the sample fixtures; they are not dates of real-world matches.
