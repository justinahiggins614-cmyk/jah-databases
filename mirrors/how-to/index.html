Section 1

Description and history

Signature How-To is a mirror of the how-to kind of website: a website whose job is to show step-by-step guides for doing things, so a reader can follow the steps in order and finish the task. The how-to kind of website began as printed instruction sheets, then as lists of steps with pictures, and then as searchable guides on a screen. This mirror follows that same shape: guides with a title, a task, and steps in order, with a search over the guides.

What this mirror holds is the network's own material. Its sample set is ten Signature sample guides made for this mirror, for general everyday tasks only, with safe steps: folding, packing, watering a plant, listing, tidying, making tea, shelving books, writing a note, planning a morning, and cleaning a window. They are labeled plainly as Signature sample guides, not as real-world instructions from any other source. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's filter rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q of words q1 … qn and a guide G with a title, a task, and steps, the guide matches when:

match(Q, G) is true when every word of Q appears in G's title, task, or steps

Every matching guide scores the same: the number of query words, which is the same for all matches of one query. Results are listed in the order the guides were written for this mirror, so the order is the same every time for the same query.

Worked example: the query "water" against the sample set. Four guides carry the word "water": "Pack a bag for a day trip" (a bottle of water), "Water a house plant" (pour in a small amount of water), "Make a cup of tea" (boil water, warm the cup with hot water), and "Clean a window with a cloth" (dampen a cloth with plain water). All four match, all score 1, and the first listed is "Pack a bag for a day trip". The query "list" matches three guides: "Pack a bag for a day trip", "Make a shopping list", and "Plan a morning routine", whose steps begin "List the morning tasks".

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how instructions work: a task is broken into small steps, the steps are put in order, and each step is checked before the next one begins. A good guide names the task, lists the steps in the order they are done, and keeps each step short enough to do in one go. The mirror uses the simplest honest form of that idea: a fixed sample set of guides, each carrying a title, a task, and steps in order, with a filter that keeps only the guides carrying every word asked for.

Two measures describe how good such a filter is. Precision is the share of returned guides that actually match what was asked. Recall is the share of matching guides in the set that the filter actually returned. This mirror's rule — every query word must appear — favours precision: it returns fewer guides, but every guide it returns contains every word you asked for. The guides here cover general everyday tasks only, with safe steps.
