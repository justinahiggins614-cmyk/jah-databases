Section 1

Description and history

Signature Translate is a mirror of the translation kind of website: a website whose job is to carry words from one language into another, so a reader can understand words they do not know. The translation kind of website grew from printed word lists between two languages, then from books of phrases for travellers, and then from searchable dictionaries on a screen. This mirror follows that same shape: a word, a language, and the matching word, with a lookup over the set.

What this mirror shows is the network's own material. Its sample dictionary holds ten Signature sample words made for this mirror, each with a matching word in three sample languages. They are labeled plainly as a Signature sample dictionary, not as a full translator. The network itself holds 63,826 databases and 12,367,686 records across the 39 websites and the 2,700 per-artifact websites.

Section 2

Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The mirror's lookup rule is a deterministic function. For a query Q, the mirror splits the query into words; the last word must be one of the three sample languages (spanish, french, german), and the words before it are the word to translate. The rule is:

translate(Q) is the dictionary entry for the word in Q, in the language named by the last word of Q

A query with no language word, an unknown language, or a word outside the sample dictionary returns a blank, and the page says the word is not in the sample dictionary.

Worked example: the query "bread french" splits into the word "bread" and the language "french". The dictionary holds bread in french as "pain", so the mirror answers "pain". The query "thank you german" splits into the two-word phrase "thank you" and the language "german", and the mirror answers "danke". The query "hello italian" names a language outside the sample set, so it returns a blank.

Section 3

Science proof

The science behind this mirror is how translation works in plain words. A word carries a meaning, and each language has its own word for that meaning: the English word "bread" and the French word "pain" point at the same thing. A dictionary is a list of such pairings, word to word, language to language, built up one word at a time.

Two limits keep this mirror honest. First, a word list translates words, not whole pages with full grammar: word order, endings, and small connecting words differ between languages, so a list of single words cannot carry a whole sentence across. Second, many words carry more than one meaning, and a short sample dictionary holds only one pairing per word. The sample dictionary is a sample: it shows how the lookup works, and it never claims to hold every word.
