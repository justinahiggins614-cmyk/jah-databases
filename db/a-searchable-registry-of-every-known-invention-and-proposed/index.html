A Registry Entry for a Way to Lift Water Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

An invention registry website: a visitor searches by plain words for a kind of invention, such as a way to lift water or a way to keep food cool, and the registry shows illustrative sample entries of that kind, each with a plain description of what it does, what it needs, and what problem it answers. Every entry is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind, never a real invention and never a claim that the registry is complete. This record covers A Registry Entry for a Way to Lift Water.

A registry entry for a way to lift water is the first sample entry the registry can hold now: what it does, what it needs, and what problem it answers, each in plain sentences. The entry says: it lifts water from a low place to a high place; it needs a wheel, a rope, and buckets; it answers water that sits too low to use. The registry must keep every entry in this plain shape, because an entry without its problem is a device with no reason, and a device with no reason is a poor entry.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a registry entry for a way to lift water runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

The entry in many languages needs the same device, the same needs, and the same problem in each language, with the meaning carried across, not just the words.
