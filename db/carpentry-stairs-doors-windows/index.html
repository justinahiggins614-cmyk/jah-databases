Straight Stair Rank 1 · simple · stairs · intermediate

A straight stair runs in one flight from one floor to the next, and it is the simplest stair to lay out because every riser and tread is the same. The layout starts from the total rise, the vertical distance between finished floors, divided into equal risers no higher than the local code maximum, commonly 7-3/4 inches. Keep every riser within 3/8 inch of the others, because an uneven riser is what trips people. The stringers carry the stair, the treads sit on them, and a landing or floor at each end gives a safe place to step on and off.

Diagram shows a straight stair flight with 14 risers at 7.5 in and 10 in treads, with total rise dimensioned.

Specifications

Dimensions: Total rise 105 in; 14 risers at 7.5 in; tread run 10 in; stair width 36 in

Materials: stringer (spruce-pine-fir lumber, 1-1/2 in); tread (oak, 1 in); riser board (poplar, 3/4 in)

Cut list

stringer: 144 in long (cut) x 9-1/4 in x 1-1/2 in (x2); tread: 36 in x 10 in x 1 in (x14); riser: 36 in x 7-1/2 in x 3/4 in (x14)

Fasteners: 3 in construction screws; construction adhesive; 16d nails

Tolerances: Riser variation under 3/8 in; tread level within 1/16 in

Load notes: Residential stair loads; stringers sized per span tables.

Numbers check: Numbers check: stair: 14 risers at 7.50 in each, total run 130.0 in, stringer 167.1 in

How to make it

  1. Measure the total rise between finished floors at both stringer positions.
  2. Divide the total rise into equal risers at or under the maximum allowed.
  3. Lay out the stringers with a framing square, marking rise and run at each step.
  4. Cut the stringers, test-fit them, and fasten them plumb and level.
  5. Install risers, then treads with adhesive and screws, checking each tread level.

Tools required

framing square, circular saw, hand saw, level, measuring tape, drill/driver

Safety gear

safety glasses, hearing protection, dust mask, work gloves

Safety notes

Wear eye and hearing protection, keep hands clear of blades and cutters, unplug tools before changing blades or bits, and never reach across a running blade. Work at a steady feed rate and use push sticks and guards where the tool provides them.

L-Stair Basics Rank 2 · simple · stairs · intermediate

An L-stair turns 90 degrees at a landing, which lets a stair fit a corner and breaks a long climb into two shorter flights. The landing is the key: it must be at least as wide as the stair and deep enough to step onto comfortably, and both flights must meet it with the same riser height so the rhythm of the climb never changes. Lay out the lower flight, the landing height, and the upper flight as one calculation, not as two separate stairs, or the risers will disagree at the landing and trip the climber.

Materials: stringer (spruce-pine-fir lumber, 1-1/2 in); landing framing (spruce-pine-fir lumber, 1-1/2 in); tread (oak, 1 in)

Fasteners: 3 in construction screws; construction adhesive; joist hangers

Tolerances: Riser variation under 3/8 in across both flights; landing level within 1/16 in

Load notes: Residential stair loads; landing framed to carry both flights.

Numbers check: Numbers check: stair: 7 risers at 7.50 in each, total run 60.0 in, stringer 79.7 in

How to make it

  1. Measure the total rise and plan the landing height so both flights share one riser height.
  2. Frame the landing level, square, and at least as wide as the stair.
  3. Lay out and cut the lower flight stringers to meet the landing.
  4. Lay out and cut the upper flight stringers from the landing to the upper floor.
  5. Install treads on both flights, checking the riser rhythm across the landing.

Stair Handrail Rank 3 · simple · stairs · beginner

A stair handrail gives a continuous grip from the bottom of the stair to the top, and it is a safety device first and a trim piece second. The rail height is measured vertically from the nosing line, commonly 34 to 38 inches, and the rail must run continuously without breaks that force a hand to let go. Balusters, the vertical spindles under the rail, are spaced so that a 4 inch sphere cannot pass between them, which keeps small children from slipping through. Fasten the rail solidly into studs or blocking; a rail that wobbles is worse than no rail at all in a fall.

How to make it

Interior Door Hanging Rank 4 · simple · doors · intermediate

Hanging an interior door means fitting a pre-hung or slab door into its frame so it swings freely, latches cleanly, and shows even gaps, called reveals, of about 1/8 inch on the sides and top. Start with the frame: it must be plumb, level, and square, or no amount of planing the door will fix the swing. Hang the door on its hinges first, check the reveals, then mark and cut the latch and strike positions. A door that is planed to fit a crooked frame will bind again when the seasons change.

Fasteners: 3 in hinge screws; finish nails; shims

Tolerances: Reveals 1/8 in, even within 1/32 in; frame plumb within 1/16 in

Load notes: Interior use only; no weather or security loads.

Numbers check: Numbers check: rough opening 30.0 in + 2 in = 32.0 in wide, 80.0 in + 2.5 in = 82.5 in high

How to make it

  1. Check the frame for plumb, level, and square, shimming it true.
  2. Mark and mortise the hinge positions on the door edge and jamb.
  3. Screw the hinges to the door, then hang the door on the jamb.
  4. Check the reveals and plane the door edge only where it binds.
  5. Mark, drill, and fit the latch and strike plate, then test the swing.

Exterior Door Frame Rank 5 · simple · doors · advanced

An exterior door frame must do everything an interior frame does, plus keep out weather, so it is built with a sill, weatherstripping, and flashing that sheds water outward. The rough opening is framed about 2 inches wider and 2-1/2 inches taller than the door unit to leave room for shims and the sill, and the sill sits on a bed of sealant over flashing, never directly on bare framing. Set the hinge side plumb first, shim the frame true, and check the door against its weatherstripping before foaming or trimming, because a frame foamed out of square is very hard to correct.

Sliding Barn Door Rank 6 · compounded · doors · intermediate

A sliding barn door hangs from a wall-mounted rail and slides sideways instead of swinging, which saves floor space but needs a solid wall and a level rail. The rail must fasten into studs or a header board, because a door hanging from drywall anchors alone will pull out. The rail length is at least twice the door width so the door can slide fully clear of the opening, and floor guides keep the bottom from swinging. Hang the door, check it rolls freely and stays on the rail at both ends, and fit soft stops so it cannot roll off.

The sill slope is the detail that matters most, because a flat sill holds water against the wall and rots the frame. Build the frame on a flat bench, check both diagonals equal before the glue sets, and size the opening so the frame fits with a small, even shim gap all around.

Door casing is the trim that covers the gap between the door frame and the wall, and a clean casing job is mostly a miter job: two side casings and a head casing joined at 45 degree miters at the top corners. Cut the head casing first, then the sides, and keep the reveal, the small step of jamb showing beside the casing, even all around, usually about 1/4 inch. Basement Stair Rank 11 · complex · stairs · advanced

A basement stair must handle headroom, moisture, and a concrete landing, three things an ordinary interior stair often avoids. Headroom is the first check: the stair needs a clear height above every tread, and a basement stair that ducks under a low beam is a hazard every day. Concrete at the bottom needs a treated bottom step or a moisture break so the stringer ends do not wick water and rot, and the bottom of the stair must land on a solid, level pad. Lay the stair out from the finished basement floor upward, keeping the risers equal all the way to the top floor.

  3. Lay out the stringers from the finished basement floor upward.
  4. Cut, fit, and fasten the stringers, protecting the treated ends.
  5. Install risers and treads, checking riser rhythm to the top floor.

Safety gear

Safety notes

Weatherstripping at the meeting stiles matters as much as at the frame, because the middle joint is where exterior pairs leak first.

Door Threshold Rank 16 · complex · doors · beginner

Remove the old threshold without damaging the jambs, bed the new one in sealant, and fasten it into the framing below.
