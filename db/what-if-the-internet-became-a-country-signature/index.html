A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Passport for the Internet Country, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Country Stories, in the Signature edition.

History

Imagining a new country is an old habit: a story about a faraway island, a plan for a town that was never built, a flag drawn for a place that exists only in a book. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain guide for each part of the imagined country. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
