Writing a Short Guide for a Household Task Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A freelance marketplace website: a visitor picks a generic kind of work, such as writing a short guide or drawing a simple picture, and the site shows illustrative sample jobs of that kind, each with a plain description of the work, the time it takes, and the pay. The site names no real person, copies no real listing, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real job. This record covers Writing a Short Guide for a Household Task.

Writing a short guide for a household task gives a worker a plain job with a plain end: a guide of about five pages, written in plain words, that a household can follow. The listing must say what the guide covers, how long it should be, and what the pay is, so a worker can judge whether the job fits. Without those plain facts, a worker cannot tell a short guide from a long book, and a job taken blind is a poor way to earn. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, writing a short guide for a household task runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

Writing short guides is an old habit, from a note left for the next cook to a printed leaflet passed from hand to hand.

Proposed solution

Name the freelance job and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every freelance job the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one freelance job without signing in; Check the freelance job works the same way on a phone and on a computer.
