This database holds twenty-three full records about the multiplayer quiz arena, ordered from simple to compounded to complex, and a reader arriving without a map cannot tell which record to read first, which records belong together, or where the harder designs begin. The field guide index solves that by listing every record by number and by name, in order, with a one-line account of what each record covers and which rung of the ladder it sits on. Without the index, the simple records and the complex records blur together, and a reader who needs the basics may open a complex design first, while a reader who wants the hardest designs may never find them. The index is the map that keeps the ladder readable, and it is the record that turns twenty-three separate designs into one set a reader can walk through from start to finish.

History

Field guide indexes descend from the tables of contents of books and the catalogues of libraries, where a list of what a collection holds came first and the collection itself came second; this index is the same old habit, written for a database of designs.

Proposed solution

List every record by number, in ladder order; Give each record a one-line account of what it covers; Mark each record simple, compounded, or complex; Point simple readers to records 1 to 8, and advanced readers to records 17 to 23; Keep the index beside the records, never apart from them.

Dependencies

The twenty-three records themselves, listed in order; the ladder ranks simple, compounded, and complex; agreement that an index lists, and never replaces, the records it points to.
