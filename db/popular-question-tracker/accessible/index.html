  1. A First List of Five Sample Questions

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The popular question tracker is a board that shows which questions many people are asking, in plain words, with every entry labeled as an illustrative sample, never as a claim about real questions asked anywhere. A first list of five sample questions gives a new reader a small place to start: five short questions with plain answer sketches, each labeled as an invented example, so the reader learns the shape of a tracker without mistaking any sample for a real question. The simple board shows five sample questions at a time, each with a plain-word answer sketch, a first-asked note, and a count explained in words. A reader picks a question, reads its sketch, and moves on, with no sign-in and no payment, and every entry carries its illustrative-sample label so no reader mistakes an invented example for a real question. Worked example: in a website built this way, a first list of five sample questions runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Lists of questions people are asking are an old habit: a teacher's list of questions from the class, a newspaper's letters page, a noticeboard of the week's queries. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a board of questions with plain-word answer sketches and a clear label on every sample entry. Lists of questions people ask are an old habit of teachers, who kept the class questions in a notebook, and the record A First List of Five Sample Questions follows that habit in its own corner of the design.
