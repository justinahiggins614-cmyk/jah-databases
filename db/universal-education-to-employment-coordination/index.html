A School-Leaver's First Job Plan Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A coordination website: a visitor picks a kind of learner, such as a school-leaver or a career-changer, and the site shows the plan in plain words, the learning first, the practice next, the work after, with each step named and no step promised as a real job. Every plan is a generic study, and the site never claims to place a real person in real work. This record covers A School-Leaver's First Job Plan.

A school-leaver's first job plan, written in plain words so the learner can see the next step from a lesson to a job. The design for this record must state the idea in plain sentences, name what a visitor does first, and say what good looks like when it works, so a reader can judge the design without building anything. Without that plain statement, the idea stays vague, a visitor cannot tell what the design promises, and a promising idea is lost because nobody could explain it in words a newcomer can follow. A reader who can follow the design in words can also check it, and a design that can be checked is a design worth building.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a school-leaver's first job plan runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

A School-Leaver's First Job Plan grows out of an older habit: matching a school-leaver to a first job, and keeping the introduction in a plain letter, and the website form adds a plain list, a search, and a written record for it.

Proposed solution
