1. Description

A CSS editor writes style rules and shows the page they make, side by side: type the rules, press Apply, and see the page. The editor counts the rules, finds broken rules, and fixes them. Everything runs in this browser; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Counting the rules gives 6 rules. A font size of 16 pixels doubled is 32 pixels. Margins of 10, 20, 10 and 20 add to 60 pixels. A border of 2 pixels around a box of 100 pixels is 104 pixels wide. Heading levels run from 1 to 6: 6 levels.

3. Science proof

A CSS editor works because a page is rules in order, and each rule names its parts and their values: a font size of 16 pixels doubled is 32 pixels, and margins of 10, 20, 10 and 20 add to 60 pixels. A broken rule is one that is missing its closing brace, and the editor shows it. That is why the editor shows the rules and the preview side by side, so every change can be seen at once.
