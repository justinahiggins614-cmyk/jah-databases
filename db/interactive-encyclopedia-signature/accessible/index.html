  1. An Entry That Answers in Plain Words

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The interactive encyclopedia is a set of entries that answer in plain words, link to their neighbors, and show their sources, with a timeline, a map, and a question a reader can ask of every entry. An entry that answers in plain words must give the reader the answer in the first paragraph, without jargon and without a long introduction. The problem is that many entries begin with the history of the topic and only reach the answer at the end, so a reader in a hurry leaves before finding it. This record puts the answer first, the detail after, and the sources at the end, so every reader gets what they came for in the first few lines. The website form grows out of that habit, adding links between entries, timelines, maps, and a search box that finds any entry in seconds. Plain-word entries grew out of the old habit of reference books that began each entry with a short definition before the detail.
