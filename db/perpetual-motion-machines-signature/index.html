Signature OE3 Loop Design One JAH-PMMOS-000001

Signature design: this record describes an original design in the Signature edition. It is labeled Signature throughout and is never presented as an established public fact or as a working product. Its feasibility tier states plainly how far the design has been carried: buildable-now, research-stage, or speculative.

The Signature OE3 loop design one is an original speculative design for a closed energy loop: a store, a converter, a load, and a return path, drawn with an explicit accounting gate that measures energy in and out of every element on every cycle. The design's rule is that the gate's reading decides the loop's status, and no element may skip the gate. The design is labeled speculative because no loop of this kind has demonstrated net gain, and the record claims no gain.

The loop's value as a design is its accounting discipline rather than its mechanism: by forcing every element through one measured gate, it makes hidden inputs visible, which is exactly where past claims have failed. A loop that passes its gate with a balance is a conventional storage loop; a loop that appears to pass only by going around the gate is a failed test, and the design says to report it as such.

The demo simulates ten cycles through a loop with a stated efficiency below unity, printing stored energy as it decays. The simulation demonstrates the accounting rule: with honest losses, the loop runs down. It is not evidence for or against any future mechanism.

The Signature OE3 loop design two is an original speculative design that adds a second store to the loop, so energy can rest in one store while the other discharges, smoothing the load's supply. Two stores do help a real system in one way: they let the load run steadily while charging happens in bursts, which is useful for intermittent sources. The design distinguishes that genuine benefit, load smoothing, from the speculative benefit, net gain, and it claims only the first. That distinction is the design's honesty mechanism.

The Signature OE3 accounting gate is an original design for a measurement point that every energy element in a loop must pass through: the gate logs energy in, energy out, time, and element identity for each cycle, and it refuses to report a balance for any cycle with a missing log entry. The gate is the design's core invention, not because measurement is novel, but because making measurement unavoidable is what past claims avoided.

The gate's rules are deliberately boring: same units, same cycle boundaries, uncertainties stated, no term labeled negligible without a bound. Boring rules are the point, because exciting rules are how gaps hide. A gate built to those rules can be implemented with ordinary instruments, and the design includes a worked example showing the log format.

The demo implements the gate's balance check on a small cycle log, printing the balance and whether any entry is missing. It is a logging illustration, not a measurement.

The Signature OE3 friction audit is an original design for listing every friction source in a loop before any gain is discussed: bearings, gear meshes, seals, air drag, and electrical resistance, each with a measurement method and an uncertainty. The audit's rule is that an unlisted loss is treated as unknown, not as zero, and the loop's balance is reported as unknown until the audit is complete. That rule alone would have settled most historical claims.

The audit template gives each loss a row, a method, and a bound, and it requires the auditor to sign the completed audit before the loop runs. Signing matters because it makes the audit a commitment rather than a wish list, and a later missing loss can then be traced to a specific row rather than to vague optimism.
