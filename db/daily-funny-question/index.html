1. Description and history

The daily funny question is a question asked every morning that anyone can answer in one sentence, with the funniest answers voted up and the question archive kept in public. Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history and description, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, a scientific equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and a named specialist AI.Daily questions grew out of classroom warm-ups and community notice boards, where one good question started a whole day of talk; the daily question turns that habit into a public routine with an archive.

2. Math proof

Every record carries a worked arithmetic proof: a count multiplied by an amount each, giving a total the demo prints. The first record works it as 3 x 2 = 6, and every record after it works its own numbers the same way, so a fresh run of any demo verifies that record's math proof exactly.

3. Science proof

Every record carries a science proof in plain words: the counted relationship is stated as an equation, the equation is explained in sentences, and the demo prints the same total, so the claim can be checked by arithmetic alone. Speculative material is labeled a design study, never presented as fact.
