JAH-KW-FEAT-000101 — JAH's part ledger — every part booked like inventory

Detectors output; JAH books. His part ledger treats every part detection as an inventory entry — part name, which detector found it, confidence, position — and the object decision reads the books, nothing else. There is no 'the network feels housey'; there is a ledger with a body entry at confidence 0.95 and a roof entry at 0.88, and the house is found because the books balance. It is accounting as an epistemology.

The ledger's power is auditability. When the system says HOUSE and is wrong, JAH opens the books: which entry lied? The roof detector's 0.88 on what turned out to be a shadow? Then the detector is tuned, not the decision rule. Blame flows to the entry, never to the verdict — because the verdict was just arithmetic on the books. This is the same instinct as his vote ledger for Hough (record: hough-voting-recognition-signature #102): receipts for everything.

The demo books a house detection: four segment entries roll up into a body entry (confidence 0.95, from the closure check), two into a roof entry (0.88, from the apex check). The books show body 0.95 >= 0.5, roof 0.88 >= 0.5 — balanced, HOUSE FOUND. Then a scene with only the body: the roof line is missing from the books, and no arithmetic can conjure it — NOT FOUND. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic detections.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Part detectors; the ledger is JAH's bookkeeping over their outputs.
The recipe
Book every detection: (part, detector, confidence, position). The object's books must show all required parts present at booked confidences; the decision reads the ledger, nothing else.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff the ledger contains every required part at confidence >= 0.5: the books balance or the object is not found. No entry, no part — the ledger is the single source of truth.

    kh, kw = len(kernel), len(kernel[0])
    rows, cols = len(image), len(image[0])
    out = []

JAH-KW-FEAT-000102 — The assembly score — JAH's composition formula

The public database keeps two gates — part score and relations. JAH fuses them into one number: the assembly score. It is the weighted part sum (body 0.5, wheels 0.25 each) multiplied by the arrangement factor (1.0 if every relation holds, 0.0 if any fails). A complete car in formation: 1.0 x 1.0 = 1.0. A complete car scattered across the image: 1.0 x 0.0 = 0 — the pile is not the object, and the multiplication says so without a second gate.

The multiplication is the philosophy: arrangement is not a bonus, it is a multiplier. Parts are necessary but not sufficient; arrangement is what promotes parts to an object, so it scales the whole score rather than adding to it. A half-built car in perfect formation (0.5 x 1.0 = 0.5) outscores a fully-built car scattered (1.0 x 0.0 = 0) — formation beats inventory, which matches how recognition actually feels.

The demo scores three scenes: the car in formation (0.75 x 1.0 = 0.75, FOUND — one wheel occluded but arranged), the parts scattered (1.0 x 0.0 = 0, NOT a car), and the lone wheel (0.25 x 1.0 = 0.25, not a car). One formula, three verdicts, and the scattered pile — full marks on parts — scores absolute zero. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Part confidences and relation checks; the formula multiplies evidence by arrangement.
The recipe
assembly = (sum of detected part weights / sum of all weights) x arrangement, where arrangement is 1.0 if all relations hold else 0.0. Parts without arrangement score zero — the pile is not the object.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff assembly >= 0.7: the number fuses 'enough parts' with 'right arrangement' into one gate. JAH's single-number verdict.

JAH-KW-FEAT-000103 — Part IOUs — JAH's detection debts

Double-entry bookkeeping records every credit with its debit; JAH's part IOUs do the same for recognition. A missing wheel is not just 'absent' — it is a debt of 0.25 against the car hypothesis, booked explicitly, totaled with the other debts, and held against the verdict. Credits (detected parts) must clear 0.7 AND debts (missing parts) must stay under 0.3, or the object is not found. It is the assembly score's mirror: what you have, and what you owe.

The debt framing changes how missing evidence is argued about. 'The wheel is occluded' is an excuse; 'the car owes 0.25 on the wheel' is a number, and numbers can be budgeted. JAH's 0.3 debt ceiling says: one small part may be missing (a 0.25 wheel), but two (0.5) void the verdict — at some point the debts exceed the credit's credibility. Occlusion is priced, not excused.

The demo books three cars: complete (debt 0.0, FOUND), one wheel occluded (debt 0.25, FOUND — within budget), both wheels missing (debt 0.5, NOT FOUND — debts void the verdict, though the body alone scored 0.5). The body did not change; the debts did. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic ledgers.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
The part ledger's absences; IOUs are JAH's accounting of missing evidence.
The recipe
For each required part not in the ledger, book an IOU for its weight. Total the debts; if they exceed 0.3, the object is NOT FOUND no matter what was detected. Debts void verdicts.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff (parts present score >= 0.7) AND (total IOU <= 0.3): the object must both earn enough credit and owe little enough debt. Double-entry recognition.

JAH-KW-FEAT-000104 — The arrangement oath — JAH's sworn relations

The public database treats relations as checks; JAH swears them as oaths. The difference is rhetorical but the enforcement is real: an oath is boolean, unweighted, and absolute. above(roof, body) either holds or the house does not exist — no partial credit, no 'mostly above', no wobble budget negotiated at oath-time (wobble is budgeted separately, in his record 106, and it never touches the oaths). The oath is the constitution; everything else is legislation.

The strictness is deliberate and JAH defends it: relations are the cheapest thing to check and the most informative when they fail. A roof below the body is not a weird house, it is evidence the parts were mislabeled — the 'roof' is probably a boat hull. The oath does not just reject; it diagnoses. Broken oaths point at the detector that lied, the same way his ledgers do.

The demo swears above(roof, body) and tests three scenes: roof above (oath holds, HOUSE FOUND), roof below the body (oath broken — NOT a house, parts score 1.0 notwithstanding), roof floating detached to the side (oath broken — NOT a house). The parts never changed; the oath decided. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Part positions; the oaths are JAH's boolean predicates over them.
The recipe
Swear the relations: above(roof, body), inside(eyes, head), below(wheels, body). Each oath is boolean; any broken oath multiplies the assembly by zero. Oaths are not weighted — they are sworn.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff all oaths hold: a single broken oath vetoes absolutely, regardless of part credit. JAH's strictest structural gate.

JAH-KW-FEAT-000105 — The shared parts registry — JAH's part exchange

The public database shows parts being shared; JAH's registry is the institution that runs the sharing. It is a table — part to employer objects — with a routing rule: when the wheel detector fires, the registry credits the car ledger, the bicycle ledger, and the wagon ledger, all from the single detection. The detector runs once; three objects bid. It is a stock exchange for visual evidence, and the currency is detection.

The registry also settles disputes. Two objects claiming the same wheel (the car and the bicycle parked together) is not a contradiction — the registry lets both hold the credit and leaves the disambiguation to their oaths and debts. Sharing never weakens a decision because the employers' gates are unchanged; the registry only ensures no detection is ever paid for twice.

The demo runs the registry on one detection event — wheel at (5,2), confidence 0.9 — and shows three ledgers credited: car (wheel: 0.9 booked), bicycle (wheel: 0.9 booked), wagon (wheel: 0.9 booked). One detection, three entries, zero repeated work. Then the employers' gates run independently: the car still needs its body. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Part detections; the registry routes each detection to every object that employs the part.
The recipe
Register each part with its employer objects. On detection, the registry credits every employer — one detection, many ledgers. Detection work is done once per part, never once per object.
The knowing-when rule
Each employer's own gates still decide (credit, debts, oaths); the registry only routes evidence. Sharing is bookkeeping, not leniency.

JAH-KW-FEAT-000106 — The wobble budget — JAH's displacement allowance

Oaths do not wobble — but positions do, and JAH's wobble budget prices the wobble. Each object gets a displacement allowance (4.0 for the face: faces turn a little); each part displaced d pixels from its ideal perch pays d-squared against the budget. An eye 1 pixel off pays 1; a mouth 2 pixels off pays 4; the budget covers the total. It is the spring model of the public database (record 6), but as an allowance to spend rather than a cost to minimize — the psychology of a budget, not a penalty.

The separation from the oaths is the point: relations (above, inside) are sworn and absolute; positions (exactly where) are budgeted and forgivable. The eyes must be above the nose (oath) but may sit a pixel left of ideal (budget). JAH's hierarchy is strict about structure and generous about measurement — the two virtues in their proper places.

The demo budgets a face: eyes off by 1 (charge 1), nose on ideal (0), mouth off by 1 (1) — total 2.0 <= 4.0, FACE FOUND. Then the mouth slides 3 pixels: charges 1 + 0 + 9 = 10 > 4.0 — NOT FOUND, budget exceeded. Same parts, same oaths; the budget drew the line. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Part positions vs ideal arrangement; the budget is JAH's forgiveness, quantified.
The recipe
For each part, charge (displacement from ideal)^2 against the object's wobble budget. Accept iff total charge <= budget. Small wobbles are cheap; the budget decides what 'small' means.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff wobble_charge <= BUDGET (here 4.0): forgiveness with a number on it. The oaths (104) never wobble — the budget covers positions, not relations.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
face-finder, cat-finder, dog-finder, bird-finder

JAH-KW-FEAT-000107 — The ladder audit — JAH's rung-by-rung verification

A hierarchy can be wrong at any level — phantom edges, misassembled parts, broken oaths — and the verdict 'HOUSE' does not say which. JAH's ladder audit checks every rung independently: rung 1 verifies the edges (are the claimed edge pixels really edge pixels, re-measured?), rung 2 verifies the parts (do the segments really close into a square?), rung 3 verifies the object (do the oaths hold?). The first failing rung is named in the verdict: 'FAILED AT RUNG 2 (parts)' tells you exactly where to look.

The audit is his answer to the black-box complaint against hierarchies: every level's claim is re-checked against the level below, so blame flows downhill to the precise rung that lied. A rung-1 failure means the edge detector needs tuning; rung 2, the part assembler; rung 3, the relation definitions. Debugging becomes accounting — open the books, find the unbalanced rung.

The demo audits two scenes: the true house (rung 1: edges verified; rung 2: body closes, roof peaks; rung 3: oath holds — CERTIFIED, all rungs clean) and a scene where 'roof' segments do not meet (rung 1 passes, rung 2 FAILS: segments do not form a peaked pair — audit halts, names rung 2, never reaches the oaths). The failure is located, not just reported. Signature-original to JAH.
