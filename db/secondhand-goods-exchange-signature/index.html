Section 1: Description and history

The secondhand goods exchange helps a person pass a used thing to someone who needs it, in plain words, with what it is, its condition, and its price written out before anyone sets out. Every listing is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind of used thing, never a real listing from a real person. A Used Bicycle Passed to Someone Who Needs It Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A secondhand exchange website: a visitor picks a generic kind of used thing, such as a used bicycle, a used coat, or a used table, and the site shows illustrative sample listings of that kind, each with a plain description, the condition, and the price. The site names no real person, copies no real listing, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real offer. This record covers A Used Bicycle Passed to Someone Who Needs It.

A used bicycle passed to someone who needs it gives a person a plain way to pass on a bicycle that is still sound, with what it is, its condition, and its price written out before anyone sets out. The exchange must show the bicycle in plain words, so a person who reads the listing knows whether the wheels are sound and what it costs. Without those plain facts, a person cannot tell a sound bicycle from a broken one, and a listing without a condition is a poor way to pass on a bicycle.

Worked example, Signature design: the same exchange listing is rebuilt from the Signature pattern, so a used bicycle passed to someone who needs it runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
