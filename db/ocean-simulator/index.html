1. Description

An ocean simulator simulates ocean currents and waves with real physics: type the depth, press Simulate, and read the wave speed. A wave in 10 metres of water travels 9.9 metres per second. At 100 metres the pressure is 1004.5 kilopascals. A tsunami in 4000 metres of water travels 197.99 metres per second. Everything runs in this browser; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A wave in 10 metres of water travels 9.9 metres per second. At 100 metres the pressure is 1004.5 kilopascals. A tsunami in 4000 metres of water travels 197.99 metres per second.

3. Science proof

An ocean simulator simulates ocean currents and waves with real physics works because each value is plain arithmetic: A current of 0.5 metres per second carries water 1800 metres in one hour. Surface water 25 degrees and deep water 5 degrees: a drop of 20 degrees. A wind of 30 metres per second piles a surge of 1.5 metres. That is why the local demo shows the work and the result together, and the local demo runs 0 oceans on a server: it runs in the browser.
