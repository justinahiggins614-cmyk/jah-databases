Gate Valves

JAH-VALVES-000001 · ladder rank 1 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A gate valve controls flow with a wedge-shaped gate that slides up and down across the pipe. Fully open, it barely restricts flow, which makes gate valves good for main shutoffs. They are poor throttling valves — partial opening vibrates and erodes the gate. Gate valves need many turns of the handle to open or close. Use them where the valve stays fully open or fully closed.

Ball Valves

JAH-VALVES-000002 · ladder rank 2 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A ball valve uses a drilled sphere that rotates 90 degrees: aligned with the pipe is open, crosswise is closed. Quarter-turn operation makes them fast and easy to use. Full-port ball valves match the pipe's inside diameter, so flow is nearly unrestricted. They seal tightly and work for water, gas, and oil. Don't use standard ball valves for precise throttling — they wear quickly in mid positions.

Globe Valves

JAH-VALVES-000003 · ladder rank 3 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A globe valve throttles flow by pressing a disc down onto a seat, giving fine control. The S-shaped flow path causes more pressure drop than gate or ball valves. Globe valves are the right choice for faucets, hose bibs, and anywhere flow must be adjusted precisely. Flow direction matters — most globe valves have an arrow showing the correct direction. They need periodic packing adjustment to stop stem leaks.

Butterfly Valves

JAH-VALVES-000004 · ladder rank 4 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A butterfly valve uses a disc that rotates on a central shaft inside the pipe. They are compact, light, and cheap in large sizes — common in 6-inch pipe and up. Quarter-turn operation with a lever or gear operator. The disc always sits in the flow stream, so they cause some pressure drop even when open. Wafer-style bodies fit between pipe flanges without their own flanges.

Check Valves

JAH-VALVES-000005 · ladder rank 5 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A check valve lets flow go one way and blocks reverse flow automatically. Swing checks use a hinged flap; spring checks and lift checks use spring-loaded discs. They prevent backflow contamination and protect pumps from reverse spin. Install them with the flow arrow pointing downstream. Water hammer can slam check valves — slow-closing or silent checks fix the banging.

Needle Valves

JAH-VALVES-000006 · ladder rank 6 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A needle valve uses a long tapered needle in a seat for very fine flow adjustment. They control tiny flows precisely — lab equipment, instrumentation, and calibration. The fine threads give many turns from closed to open. Needle valves are not shutoff valves for large flows; they are for metering. Keep them clean — a speck of debris ruins the fine seat.

Pressure Relief Valves

JAH-VALVES-000007 · ladder rank 7 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A pressure relief valve opens automatically when pressure exceeds a set point, protecting equipment from rupture. Springs hold the valve shut; pressure overcomes the spring at the set pressure. Size relief valves for the worst-case flow, not the normal flow. Test them periodically by lifting the test lever. Never plug, cap, or bypass a relief valve — it is the last line of defense.

Solenoid Valves

JAH-VALVES-000008 · ladder rank 8 · signature-generated-completion

Signature-original. A solenoid valve opens and closes with an electromagnetic coil — electricity in, flow on or off. Direct-acting solenoids work at zero pressure; pilot-operated ones need line pressure to assist. Normally-closed valves open when energized; normally-open close when energized. They switch fast and are ideal for automation, irrigation, and appliances. Match the coil voltage to the control system — 12V, 24V, 120V, or 240V.
