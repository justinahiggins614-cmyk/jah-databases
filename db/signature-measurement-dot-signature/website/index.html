Section 1: Description and history

The Dot Way measures the three dots of Manon's signature: dot A (331 px), dot B (320 px) and dot C (246 px). It gives each dot's position, size, spacing and alignment, in pixels, in Signature units (S) and in atoms. Manon uploaded his signature image on 2026-11-23 and asked for it to be measured many ways, as accurately as the Signature system allows, down toward atom scale. The parent agent measured the file the same day, the coordinator verified the numbers twice, and this wave recomputed them again before building, with identical results. Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history and description, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, a scientific equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

Section 2: Math proof

897 px = 331 + 320 + 246 px. 897 / 37,277 = 2.41 percent of the ink. Spacings: A-B 616.0 px, A-C 81.0 px, B-C 696.2 px. Triangle perimeter 1,393.2 px, area 3,669.8 px2. The demo of every record prints the same kind of total as its math proof, so a fresh run of any demo verifies its proof.

Section 3: Science proof

Connected-component analysis separates ink into its separate pieces, so a dot is defined by the analysis itself: a small component that touches no other component. A centroid is the mean position of a component's pixels, and a bounding box is its smallest axis-aligned frame.
