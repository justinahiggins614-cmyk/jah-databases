History
Goods have always come back: a buyer finds a fault, a size does not fit, a parcel arrives broken, and the goods travel back to the seller. From the market stall to the mail-order house, the honest handling of a return has rested on the same plain practice — write down what came back, why it came back, and what was given in its place, so that buyer and seller agree about what happened. Managing returns and refunds means keeping that record true for every return, from the first report to the final answer. This database is built on that public practice of recording every return whole.
