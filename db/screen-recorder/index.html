1. Description

A screen recorder captures what is on the screen as a video file. Press Record, choose the screen, a window or a tab, and press Stop to save the clip. It records only after permission is given, and where the browser does not allow capture, the page says so plainly and records nothing. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A recording's length is the time between start and stop: 60 seconds captures 60 seconds. Frames are seconds times frames per second: at 30 frames a second, 10 seconds hold 300 frames. Three recordings of 30 seconds each total 90 seconds, and a library of 4 recordings totalling 120 seconds averages 30 seconds each.

3. Science proof

A screen recorder works because a screen is a picture that changes, and a video is many still pictures in a row: at 30 frames a second the eye sees motion. Capture needs permission because the screen can show anything, so the browser asks first and the recorder starts only after yes. That is why the page says plainly when capture is not allowed, instead of pretending.
