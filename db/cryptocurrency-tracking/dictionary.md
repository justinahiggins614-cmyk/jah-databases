# Dictionary entries — The Cryptocurrency Tracking Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## cryptocurrency

Money kept as numbers in a shared public ledger, moved by signed messages, with no single keeper holding the book, and counted by the ledger's own entries.

## blockchain

The shared public ledger itself, a chain of blocks each holding a set of movements, kept by many keepers at once, so that no single keeper can rewrite it in secret.

## block

One set of movements written into the ledger together, with its own number and its own time, so that the ledger can be read block by block in order.

## transaction

One movement of coins from one address to another, written into the ledger with its time and its amount, so that the movement can be found and counted.

## address

A named place in the ledger where coins are held, written as a string of characters, so that movements to and from it can be counted without naming any person.

## wallet

The holder's own set of keys that control an address, kept by the holder, so that only the holder can sign a movement from that address.

## exchange

A public place where coins are bought and sold, with prices counted and published, so that any reader can see what a coin last sold for and when.

## price

The amount last paid for one coin, counted at a stated time, so that the price is always a fact about a moment, never a fact about all time.

## market value

The price of one coin multiplied by the number of coins, counted at a stated time, so that the whole currency's market size can be stated as one number.

## volume

The number of coins that changed hands in a stated period, counted and published, so that the market's activity can be seen beside its prices.

## ledger entry

One line of the public ledger, naming a movement, its time, and its amount, kept whole, so that any movement can be found again in the ledger.

## tracking record

A written account of what the tracking counted for a stated period — the movements, the prices, and the totals — with every figure traced to its public source.
