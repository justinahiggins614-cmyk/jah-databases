Engineering view: Nacre, or mother-of-pearl, is a composite of thin aragonite (calcium carbonate) tablets, about 0.5 micrometres thick, glued by organic biopolymer layers about 20 to 30 nanometres thick. The brick-and-mortar structure gives nacre both iridescence and remarkable toughness for a ceramic-like material.

History and description: Pearls form in molluscs around an irritant, layer by layer, and natural pearls large enough for jewellery are already rare; a gate-sized natural pearl does not occur. Science proof: Science proof: nacre's fracture toughness (about 3 to 9 MPa square-root-metre in published measurements, versus about 1 for monolithic aragonite) comes from crack deflection at tablet interfaces and tablet sliding. Its iridescence comes from thin-film interference across the stacked tablets, a measurable optical effect.
