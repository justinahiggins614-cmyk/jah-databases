JAH-CRYST-GROW-000001 — Growing Crystals — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Growing crystals is the art and science of coaxing atoms into ordered lattices: from a seed crystal in a supersaturated solution or melt, layer by layer, into quartz, silicon, sapphire, or sugar. It is an ancient practice (salt and sugar crystallization) and a modern industry — every computer chip starts as a single perfect silicon crystal pulled from a melt. This database records methods, conditions, and results: the public harvest of established technique plus the Signature practice developed in the JAH system.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000002 — Growing Crystals — Supersaturation: the driving force

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Crystals grow because the mother phase holds more dissolved material than equilibrium allows — supersaturation. The degree of supersaturation sets everything: too low and nothing nucleates, too high and you get a snowstorm of tiny crystals instead of one big one. Growers control it with temperature (solubility usually falls as solutions cool), evaporation, or chemical reaction. The metastable zone — supersaturated but not yet nucleating — is where the skilled grower lives.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000003 — Growing Crystals — Solution growth methods

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The classic low-temperature methods: slow evaporation (a dish of solution left to evaporate — how many hobbyists grow their first alum crystal), slow cooling (temperature programmed down over days), and gel growth (crystals forming in silica gel, undisturbed by convection). These need little equipment and teach every fundamental: nucleation, habit, inclusions. The records here specify solute, solvent, concentrations, temperature profiles, and the resulting crystal habit.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000004 — Growing Crystals — Melt growth: Czochralski and Bridgman

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Industry grows from melts: the Czochralski method pulls a seed crystal slowly from molten material while rotating it, growing the giant silicon boules (300 mm diameter) behind every chip; the Bridgman method moves a crucible through a temperature gradient, freezing the melt directionally. Control parameters are pull rate (millimetres per hour), rotation, and thermal gradient. These are mature, instrumented, economically enormous processes — the backbone of semiconductors and much of optics.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000005 — Growing Crystals — Hydrothermal and flux growth

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Hydrothermal growth mimics nature: water at high temperature and pressure (in steel autoclaves) dissolves quartz or emerald feedstock and redeposits it on seeds — how most commercial quartz and many gemstones are grown. Flux growth dissolves the material in a molten salt 'flux' that is not part of the crystal, then cools to precipitate gems like synthetic ruby and spinel. Both run hot, high-pressure, and slow — weeks to months — and both produce superb crystals.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000006 — Growing Crystals — Vapor growth and epitaxy

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Vapor methods deposit crystals atom by atom from gas: chemical vapor deposition (CVD) grows diamond films and silicon epitaxial layers; physical vapor transport grows bulk silicon carbide. Molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) is the ultimate control — single atomic layers in ultra-high vacuum, the method behind quantum-well lasers. Vapor growth trades speed for perfection: the slowest methods make the most perfect crystals.

A grown crystal must be proven: X-ray diffraction confirms the structure and measures lattice parameters; optical spectroscopy checks purity and color centers; microscopy reveals inclusions and growth bands; hardness and density complete the physical record. Every crystal in this database carries its characterization — a record without proof is a story, not a specimen.

JAH-CRYST-GROW-000008 — Growing Crystals — From seed to finished piece

Public edition. Public science and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The full pipeline: prepare the seed (cut and etched for a clean start), run the growth (days to months of controlled conditions), harvest (careful extraction and cleaning), cut and polish (faceting for gems, wafering for silicon), and characterize. Yield is the honest metric: what fraction of starts become usable pieces. The database's records follow this pipeline end to end, so a beginner can replicate a growth and an expert can audit it.
