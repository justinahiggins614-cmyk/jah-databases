Persian Rugs

JAH-RUG-000001 · ladder rank 1 · public-harvest

Persian rugs are hand-knotted pile rugs made in Iran, prized for intricate designs and natural dyes. Famous weaving centers include Tabriz, Kashan, Heriz, and Qom, each with a recognizable style. Quality is measured partly by knot density: fine rugs can exceed 300 knots per square inch (KPSI). Designs feature medallions, floral all-over patterns, and geometric tribal motifs. A genuine hand-knotted Persian rug is woven on a loom, never glued, and its pattern shows clearly on the back.

Oriental Rugs

JAH-RUG-000002 · ladder rank 2 · public-harvest

Oriental rugs are hand-knotted rugs from Turkey, the Caucasus, Central Asia, India, and China as well as Persia. Turkish rugs often use bold geometric designs and the symmetrical Turkish (Ghiordes) knot. Caucasian rugs are known for tribal patterns and strong colors. Indian rugs frequently copy Persian designs at lower prices. Chinese rugs often feature simpler, open designs with more empty field space.

Area Rugs

JAH-RUG-000003 · ladder rank 3 · public-harvest

Area rugs are large rugs that cover part of a floor, typically under furniture groupings. Standard sizes include 5x8, 8x10, and 9x12 feet. A living-room rug should be large enough that at least the front legs of sofas and chairs rest on it. Under a dining table, the rug should extend at least 24 inches beyond the table on all sides so chairs stay on it. Use a rug pad underneath to prevent slipping and to add cushioning.

Runner Rugs

JAH-RUG-000004 · ladder rank 4 · public-harvest

Runner rugs are long, narrow rugs made for hallways, entryways, and alongside beds. Common widths are 2 to 3 feet, with lengths from 6 to 14 feet. In a hallway, leave a few inches of bare floor on each side so the runner looks centered. Low-pile runners are best for high-traffic areas because they lie flat and are easy to clean. Outdoor runners made of synthetic fibers handle porches and patios.

Shag Rugs

JAH-RUG-000005 · ladder rank 5 · public-harvest

Shag rugs have a deep, long pile that feels soft underfoot. Pile height on shags can reach 2 inches or more, far deeper than standard cut-pile rugs. They suit bedrooms and living rooms but are poor choices for dining rooms, where chair legs sink and food spills hide. Vacuum shags with suction only — a beater bar can damage the long fibers. Shake them outdoors periodically to release trapped dust.

Braided Rugs

JAH-RUG-000006 · ladder rank 6 · public-harvest

Braided rugs are made by braiding strips of fabric or yarn into long ropes, then coiling and stitching the ropes into ovals or rounds. They originated as a thrifty way to reuse worn clothing and feed sacks. Cotton, wool, and synthetic braided rugs are common; many are reversible. Because they are flat and flexible, braided rugs suit kitchens and casual rooms. Most braided rugs can be spot-cleaned, and many cotton ones are machine washable.

Natural Fiber Rugs — Jute and Sisal

JAH-RUG-000007 · ladder rank 7 · public-harvest

Jute rugs are woven from soft plant fibers; they are inexpensive, earthy-looking, and best in low-traffic, dry rooms. Sisal is a stiffer agave fiber that wears very well and suits hallways and offices. Seagrass rugs are smooth and naturally stain-resistant but cannot get very wet. Natural fiber rugs should not be steam-cleaned — excess water causes shrinkage and browning. Layering a smaller patterned rug over a large jute rug is a popular decorating technique.

Rug Care, Cleaning, and Placement

JAH-RUG-000008 · ladder rank 8 · public-harvest

Vacuum rugs weekly, and rotate them 180 degrees once or twice a year so wear and sunlight stay even. Blot spills immediately — never rub, which spreads the stain and damages pile. Test any cleaner on a hidden corner first. Professional cleaning every 12 to 18 months keeps wool rugs in good shape. Keep rugs out of direct sun where possible, or expect gradual fading; rug pads extend rug life and protect floors.
