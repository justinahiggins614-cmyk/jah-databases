1. Description and History

Old-photo colorization adds plausible color to black-and-white photographs: skin, sky, grass, and clothes colored in tones that fit the photo's era, with every guess marked as a guess. This database designs that colorization one record at a time, from one portrait to a whole album in consistent color, and every record is a design, never a real product. It works on the user's own photos only.

Colorizing black-and-white photos began by hand, with artists painting dyes onto prints, and later moved to software that learned which things are usually which color: skies are blue, grass is green, skin is warm. The software guesses, and honest colorization says so. The design in this database colors in plausible tones, respects the photo's era, and marks every guess as a guess.

Every record here is a design, never a real product. Public records are fact-checked, real, and legal-only; Signature records are the Signature original version of the same subject; speculative material is labeled design-study, never as fact. Photo tools work on the user's own photos only, never anyone else's private images.

Each record states its counted relationship as a formula and proves it by computation — the demo prints the total the formula predicts. Where a record needs something no published work has yet shown, its feasibility label says so plainly, with the missing piece named in its reason.
