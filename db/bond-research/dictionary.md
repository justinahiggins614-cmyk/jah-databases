# Dictionary entries — The Bond Research Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## bond

A written promise by a borrower to repay money lent, naming the amount, the rate, and the dates, so that the loan can be read, held, and counted by anyone who holds the paper.

## issuer

The government or company that borrows by selling bonds, named on the bond, so that the promise has a named maker who answers for it.

## face value

The amount written on a bond as the sum to be repaid at its end, counted once, so that the repayment is known from the paper itself.

## coupon

The regular interest payment a bond promises, counted by a stated rate over a stated time, and dated, so that each payment can be checked when it falls due.

## maturity

The date on which a bond's face value must be repaid, written on the bond, so that the life of the loan is known from its first day to its last.

## yield

The gain a bond gives its holder, counted from its price and its promised payments, so that bonds of different prices can be compared on the same measure.

## credit rating

A published judgement of how likely a borrower is to keep its promises, given by a rating body in its own words, and cited, never invented, by the research.

## bond market

The public place where bonds are bought and sold, with prices counted and published, so that any reader can see what a bond last sold for and when.

## interest rate

The stated price of borrowing money, counted over a stated time, so that the cost of a loan can be compared with the cost of another loan on the same terms.

## default

A borrower's failure to pay what a bond promises, when it promises it, recorded with the date, so that the failure is a fact in the record, not a rumour.

## duration

A measure of how much a bond's price moves when interest rates move, counted from the bond's own promised payments, so that the risk of the bond can be stated as one number.

## research note

A written account of what the research found about a bond, with every figure traced to its published source, kept so the study can be checked and repeated.
