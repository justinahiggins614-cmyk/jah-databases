Desert Climate Overview Rank 1 · simple

Signature original companion record (not a public record). The Signature companion files climate as the city's operating envelope: every later Signature record must state how it performs inside that envelope, not in a temperate ideal. Companion to the public record of the same title: Mecca has a hot desert climate, classified BWh in the Koppen-Geiger system, set in a valley among mountains in western Saudi Arabia about 70 kilometres from the Red Sea. Summers are long, very hot and almost rainless, winters are warm by most standards, and the city receives strong sunshine for most of the year, with published sunshine totals around 3,387 hours per year. The valley setting shapes local weather: heat builds in the valley, night cooling is limited compared with open desert, and rare rain can run quickly off rock and paved surfaces into low ground. Climate is the background condition for every other record in this pair, because water supply, heat management, air quality and crowd safety in Mecca are all designed around sustained heat and scarce rain, and the crowd-science pair returns to heat as a safety factor. This record states climate in general factual terms; monthly figures are given in the next record with their sources. Rainfall and Temperature (Published Data) Rank 2 · simple

Signature original companion record (not a public record). The Signature companion stores each climate dataset separately with its period, and computes ranges across datasets rather than overwriting one dataset with another. Companion to the public record of the same title: Published climate datasets disagree on Mecca's exact averages, so this record states the range and the disagreement. Climate-data.org gives an average annual temperature of 28.6 degrees Celsius with annual rainfall of 130 millimetres, while timeanddate's Makkah table gives a mean temperature near 89 degrees Fahrenheit (about 32 degrees Celsius) with annual precipitation of 3.02 inches (about 77 millimetres), and other published summaries give about 121 millimetres. The honest summary is therefore an average annual temperature in the high twenties to low thirties Celsius, summer daytime highs commonly around 40 to 43 degrees Celsius with July the warmest month, winter daytime highs around 29 degrees Celsius, and annual rainfall roughly 77 to 130 millimetres depending on dataset and period, concentrated in the cooler months from November to January, with June often near zero. No single dataset is silently treated as exact, and no daily forecast is claimed here. Water Supply History Rank 3 · simple

Signature original companion record (not a public record). The Signature companion separates traditional water history from modern engineering history into two fields, so reverence for a historic source is never read as a modern supply statistic. Companion to the public record of the same title: Mecca's water history is a history of carrying water to a valley city with no large local river, for a population that swells seasonally. In early periods water came from wells, springs and cisterns, and from the historic Ain Zubayda aqueduct system associated with the Abbasid period, which brought water from springs toward Mecca along channels. In the modern period the city came to depend on piped supply, storage reservoirs, and, increasingly, desalinated water piped from the Red Sea coast, with groundwater as a smaller part of supply. The old well at the centre of the city, known as Zamzam, remains a religiously significant water source in Islamic tradition; this record notes its traditional significance and its historic role, and treats modern municipal supply volumes as a separate engineering matter. Where a historic volume or date is not verified in the public record consulted, it is not stated. The modern desalination and distribution system is treated in the next record. Flood History and Management (Published Events) Rank 6 · simple

Signature original companion record (not a public record). The Signature companion files every flood record as storm, ground and defence, so a flood is explained by its causes rather than remembered only as a date. Companion to the public record of the same title: Mecca's valley setting makes flash flooding a real, published risk: rare heavy rain on rock and paved ground runs quickly into low streets and valleys, and the city has experienced damaging floods in its history, including widely reported floods in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Published flood-management responses include dams and retention structures in the valleys above the city, drainage channels and culverts through urban areas, early-warning and road-closure procedures during storms, and development controls on the most flood-exposed ground. Because rainfall totals are small on average, flood risk is easy to understate from annual averages alone; it is the intensity of a single storm, not the annual total, that produces a flood, and this record therefore treats storm intensity, not annual rainfall, as the flood variable. Specific historic flood casualty figures and damage totals are not stated here, because this pair reports management and risk in general terms and does not dwell on individual events. Current flood warnings must come from official channels at the time, not from this record.
