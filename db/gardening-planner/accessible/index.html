  1. Drawing Your Garden Beds

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The gardening planner is a plain website where a person draws their garden beds, picks common plants such as tomatoes, herbs, and flowers, and builds a care calendar with watering and feeding notes, for ordinary plant care only. A garden with no drawn beds is a garden that is planted by guess, because nobody knows where one bed ends and the next begins. Drawing your garden beds fixes the map: each bed named, each bed measured, each bed ready for planting. Without the drawing, seeds are sown in the wrong bed, plants crowd each other, and the harvest is smaller than the garden promised. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. Worked example: in a website built this way, drawing your garden beds runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: People have always planned gardens on paper: a sketch of the beds, a list of what goes where, a calendar with watering days marked. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a bed map, a care calendar, and a harvest note for each plant. Drawing garden beds is an old habit of gardeners, who have always sketched the beds before sowing a seed.
