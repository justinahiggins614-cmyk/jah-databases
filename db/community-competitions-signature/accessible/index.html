  1. A Contest With Rules Written Before It Starts

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    Community competitions are friendly contests run by a community for its own members: clear rules, a fair judge, a public scoreboard, and prizes that are badges and praise, never money. A contest whose rules are written after it starts is a contest nobody trusts, because the winner can always be accused of winning by rules made up to suit them. The rules should be written before the first entry arrives, naming what counts, how it is judged, and what happens in a tie. Without rules written first, every result is argued about, and the argument lasts longer than the contest. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. Writing the rules first is an old habit of every fair contest, from the village race to the baking contest at the fair.
