First stage dilates the cervix through contractions; second stage pushes the baby out; third delivers the placenta. Each stage has expected progress and warning signs. Understanding stages turns fear into teamwork.

def due_date(lmp_month, lmp_day, lmp_year):
    """Naegele's rule: LMP + 7 days - 3 months + 1 year."""
    d = lmp_day + 7; m = lmp_month - 3; y = lmp_year
    if m <= 0: m += 12
    else: y += 1
    if d > 30: d -= 30; m += 1
    return f"Estimated due date: {m}/{d}/{y}"
print(due_date(1, 10, 2026))

Fetal Heart Monitoring

Continuous CTG tracings show the baby's heart rate against contractions. Reassuring patterns mean labor continues; concerning decelerations trigger position changes, oxygen, or expedited delivery. The strip is the baby's voice in labor.

Epidural Anesthesia

An epidural catheter in the lower back bathes spinal nerves in anesthetic, erasing labor pain while the mother stays awake and pushes. Anesthesiologists place it in minutes. It is the most effective labor pain relief available.

Cesarean Section

C-sections deliver through abdominal and uterine incisions when vaginal birth is unsafe — breech, placenta previa, or fetal distress. Most use spinal anesthesia so mothers are awake. Recovery takes weeks longer than vaginal birth.

VBAC

Vaginal birth after cesarean is safe for many women with one prior low-transverse incision, with success rates around 70 percent. Continuous monitoring watches for the rare uterine rupture. VBAC avoids repeat surgery's compounding risks.

Induction of Labor

Induction starts labor with cervical ripening agents, membrane rupture, or oxytocin when continuing pregnancy risks mother or baby. The Bishop score predicts success. Inductions need patience — they can take days.

Shoulder Dystocia

When the baby's shoulder sticks behind the pubic bone, the team runs rehearsed maneuvers: McRoberts positioning, suprapubic pressure, rotational moves. Seconds matter and drills save lives. Most resolve without injury.

Postpartum Hemorrhage

Heavy bleeding after birth is the leading cause of maternal death worldwide. Management is protocolized: fundal massage, oxytocin, then escalating drugs, transfusion, and procedures. Quantified blood loss and drills keep teams ready.

Preeclampsia

High blood pressure plus organ effects after 20 weeks threatens mother and baby. Magnesium prevents seizures; delivery cures it. Warning signs — headache, vision changes, swelling — demand immediate evaluation.

Delayed Cord Clamping

Waiting 30 to 60 seconds before clamping transfers precious blood, iron, and stem cells to the newborn. It is now standard for vigorous babies. A minute's patience pays lifelong dividends.

Birth plans communicate preferences — movement, pain relief, who catches the baby — while staying flexible for safety. Providers review them on admission. The best plans hold wishes loosely and trust tightly.

Midwifery Care

Midwives specialize in normal pregnancy and birth, offering personalized, low-intervention care with excellent outcomes. They collaborate with obstetricians when risk rises. Many women choose midwives for the relationship as much as the care.

OB Triage

OB triage sorts labor from false labor, ruptured membranes from discharge, and emergencies from reassurance. Fetal monitoring and exams decide: admit, observe, or home. It is the ED of maternity.

Water Birth

Laboring and delivering in warm water eases pain and buoyancy aids movement. Strict criteria keep it safe: low-risk, term, clear water. Many mothers describe it as transformative.
