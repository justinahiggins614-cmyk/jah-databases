  1. A Grade From Weighted Scores

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The grade calculator turns the scores a learner earned and the weight of each piece of work into a plain estimate of a course grade, with every figure shown and every figure labeled an estimate, never an official grade. A grade from weighted scores is fair only when the weights are shown: a test worth half the course must count for half the grade, and homework worth a tenth must count for a tenth. Without weights, a learner who aces the homework and fails the final test can end up with the same grade as a learner who did the opposite, and neither grade means what it says. The calculator fixes this by multiplying each score by its weight, adding the results, and showing the working, so any learner can check the grade by hand.     History: Working out a grade from weighted scores is an old school habit: marks in a book, weights written beside each test, an average worked out by hand at the end of term. The calculator form of that habit grew out of printed grade tables, then spreadsheets, then web pages that show the working. Weighted scores grew out of school marks books, where a teacher wrote the weight of each test beside its mark before working out the term average.
