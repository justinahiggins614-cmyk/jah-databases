The The Worldwide One Word Story Database

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. The worldwide one word story is a story told by the whole world, one word at a time: each visitor adds a single word, and the story grows in the open for everyone to read. Every story is a clearly labeled design study of a shared tale, and the guide never claims a story is a published book.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex.

Full record index: https://justinahiggins614-cmyk.github.io/jah-databases-data-2/data/the-worldwide-one-word-story/index.jsonl.gz

  1. A Story That Begins With River

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The worldwide one word story is a story told by the whole world, one word at a time: each visitor adds a single word, and the story grows in the open for everyone to read. Every story is a clearly labeled design study of a shared tale, and the guide never claims a story is a published book. A Story That Begins With River is one one-word story in this database, and it needs a plain design a visitor can read in one sitting, with every step written out and every rule stated, so the idea can be judged on its own words before anything is built. This record asks for a design that a visitor can try in one sitting, with the steps written out, the cost named plainly, and the rules stated so everyone gets the same fair start. Without those plain words, the idea can only be guessed at, and a guessed-at idea helps nobody, because a visitor who cannot see the steps cannot follow them. The record therefore writes the whole idea out, so a stranger to the idea can read it once and know what it does. Worked example: in a website built this way, a story that begins with river runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Telling a story one piece at a time is an old habit: a tale passed around a fire, each teller adding the next part, the story growing in the open. The website form grows out of that habit, adding one word per visitor and a written story everyone can read. A Story That Begins With River grows out of an older habit: telling a story one word at a time around a fire, each person adding the next word, and the website form adds a plain list, a search, and a written record for it.

    Proposed solution: Name the one-word story and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every one-word story the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one one-word story without signing in; Check the one-word story works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a shared story line, a rule that each visitor adds one word, a plain record of every word, and a review step before publishing. Nothing else is needed to open the first one-word story.

    Verification path: A visitor opens one one-word story, follows the rules in plain sentences, and finishes it without help. The demo program prints the same total as the math proof, and a second visitor repeating the steps gets the same result.
