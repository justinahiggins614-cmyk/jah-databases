What Carpentry Is Rank 1 · simple · overview · beginner

This flagship record defines the craft, names its core skills, and points to the project pairs that teach each skill by building. Measuring Basics Rank 5 · simple · measuring · beginner

Source: public-harvest

Accurate measuring means reading a tape or rule to the sixteenth of an inch, marking with a sharp pencil or knife line, and checking the mark before cutting. The builder measures from the same end every time, marks the waste side of the line with an X, and cuts on the waste side so the kept piece stays true to size. A second measurement after cutting catches drift early, and the demo below converts the practice board between inches and millimetres so both systems stay honest.

Source: public-harvest

Layout turns numbers on a plan into lines on wood. A combination square marks right angles and checks edges, a marking gauge scores a line parallel to an edge for a joint or a rip, and measuring both diagonals of a frame proves it square when the two diagonals match. Lines are marked on the face that will show, and the builder works from a single reference edge so small errors do not stack across a project.

Wood Selection Basics Rank 7 · compounded · materials · intermediate

Source: public-harvest

Wood choice follows use. Soft pine is cheap and easy to cut, which suits practice and painted work, but it dents easily. Poplar is smooth and stable for painted furniture. Oak is hard and strong for table tops and chair frames, and its open grain shows through a clear finish. Cedar resists weather outdoors and is light, but it is soft and splits if screws are driven near an end without a pilot hole. Boards are sighted along their length for bow and twist before buying, and the straightest boards are saved for the longest pieces.
