Signature: What Is Signature Biofonics Rank 1 · simple

Signature biofonics is the Signature-made counterpart of biofonics: original, labelled designs for the sounds of living things, built as models and synthesis code rather than presented as field data. Where the public edition reports what researchers have recorded in real habitats, the Signature edition designs what a call could sound like, how a soundscape could be modelled, and how a listener could learn to hear structure in sound. Every Signature record in this edition is labelled Signature in its title and body, and every synthesised call is labelled SIMULATED, never a field recording. The three soundscape layers are kept distinct here exactly as in the public edition: biophony for living organisms, geophony for earth sounds, and anthrophony for human-made sounds. This record covers the biophony layer as a Signature design subject, and it cross-links the soundscape models that cover the other layers rather than blurring them together.

Science proof

Science proof: synthesis is a legitimate design method in bioacoustics when it is labelled as such. A synthesised call built from a stated frequency, duration, and amplitude envelope can be compared with a real call's spectrogram to test which features matter for recognition, and model soundscapes can test how masking, distance, and layer mixing change what a listener hears. The Signature models in this edition are presented as design studies with stated parameters, so any reader can reproduce them, vary them, and judge them, which is the standard that separates a model from a fabrication.

Math proof

Math proof: wavelength equals speed divided by frequency, so 343 / 440 = 0.7795 metres, about 0.78 metres. The demo prints 343 / 440 = 0.78 m, 343 / 880 = 0.39 m, and 343 / 220 = 1.56 m, showing that doubling frequency halves wavelength at the same speed.

Signature: Dawn Chorus Model Rank 2 · simple

This Signature model designs a dawn chorus from first principles. A Signature chorus is specified as a list of virtual singers, each with a start time relative to sunrise, a centre frequency, a song duration, and a repetition rate, and the model mixes them into a schedule that shows when each voice is active. The design rule is the acoustic niche rule borrowed from the public edition: virtual singers are placed in different frequency bands and time slots so their songs overlap as little as possible, and the model reports the overlap that remains. The output is a schedule and a set of numbers, not audio, and any sound rendered from it is labelled SIMULATED. The model is useful for teaching: a student can move a singer's band, watch overlap rise, and learn why real choruses organise themselves the way they do.

Science proof

Science proof: the model implements the acoustic niche hypothesis as a design rule: species in an undisturbed habitat tend to occupy distinct frequency bands and time slots. By encoding that rule as band and slot assignments, the model reproduces the qualitative pattern of a real dawn chorus, spread across bands with limited overlap, while remaining honest that it is a model: its singers are invented, its parameters are chosen, and its overlap figures describe the model, not a real woodland.

Math proof

Math proof: if 3 virtual singers each sing for 15 minutes inside a 60-minute window, total singing time is 3 x 15 = 45 minutes and the average number singing at once is 45 / 60 = 0.75. The demo prints total 45 minutes, average 0.75 simultaneous singers, and one singer's share of the window, 15 / 60 = 25.0 percent.

Signature: Layer Model of a Soundscape Rank 3 · simple

This Signature model formalises Bernie Krause's three layers as a design tool. A Signature soundscape is specified as three independent layer tracks, biophony, geophony, and anthrophony, each with a level in decibels and a list of frequency bands it occupies, and the model combines them into a total soundscape description with a per-band occupancy table. The discipline the model enforces is the suite's core rule: layers are stored separately, mixed only arithmetically, and reported separately, so a reader can always ask how much of a band is living sound, how much is earth sound, and how much is human sound. The model can answer design questions such as how much louder the anthrophony layer must grow before it dominates a band, and it labels every output as a Signature model, never as a measurement from a real place.

Science proof

Science proof: layer separation is standard soundscape-ecology practice: recordings are analysed by band and by source class, and indices such as NDSI are defined precisely by contrasting a biophony band with an anthrophony band. The Signature model follows that practice by keeping layer powers separate until the final, labelled combination step, so its arithmetic mirrors published analysis methods while its inputs remain invented design parameters.

Math proof

Math proof: combining layer powers uses decibel addition. For layers at 50, 55, and 60 dB, the combined level is 10 x log10(10^5 + 10^5.5 + 10^6) = 61.51 dB. The demo prints 61.51 dB for those three layers, and also prints the simple level sum, 165, to show why decibel levels must never be added directly.

The design includes a repertoire parameter, so a virtual bird can hold many song types and choose among them, and a learning parameter, so a virtual juvenile's early, noisy syllables converge toward a tutor template over successive practice rounds. Science proof

Signature: Frog Chorus Synthesizer Design Rank 5 · simple

This Signature design specifies a frog chorus synthesizer. Each virtual frog is defined by a call duration, a call period, a dominant frequency, and a position in a chorus circle, and the chorus is scheduled so that neighbours alternate rather than overlapping, following the spacing behaviour of real choruses. The design includes a temperature parameter, because real frog calling speeds up with warmth, and a chorus-size parameter, so a student can hear, in numbers, how total call rate grows with the number of singers. The output is a schedule and a set of call-rate figures, and any rendered sound is labelled SIMULATED. The design is offered as a teaching model for chorus structure and as a composition tool for wetland-like sound designs, never as a recording of a real pond.

Science proof

Science proof: the design encodes two documented features of real frog choruses: species-specific call durations and periods, and neighbour alternation that reduces masking. By making those features explicit parameters, the model can be tested against real chorus recordings: if a real chorus alternates more strictly than the model, the model's alternation parameter is adjusted, and the adjustment is recorded as a design change, not hidden.

Signature: Insect Call Synthesizer Design Rank 6 · simple

This Signature design specifies an insect call synthesizer with two mechanisms. The stridulation mode models a cricket-like call as a train of pulses whose pulse rate is set by a tooth-strike parameter, and the tymbal mode models a cicada-like call as rapid pulse bursts set by a buckling-rate parameter. Both modes include a temperature parameter linked to pulse rate through a Dolbear-style rule, so a student can raise the virtual temperature and watch the virtual chirp rate rise. The design states its frequency, pulse rate, and duration for every call, and any sound rendered from it is labelled SIMULATED. The design is a teaching and composition tool: it shows how tiny mechanical actions produce loud, patterned sound, and it never claims to be a recording of a real insect.

Science proof

Science proof: the two mechanisms mirror the documented real mechanisms: cricket stridulation by wing file and scraper, and cicada sound by tymbal buckling. The temperature link mirrors the published Dolbear relationship between chirp rate and air temperature. The design is therefore anatomically inspired and parameter-explicit, which is the standard for an honest synthesis model.

Math proof

Math proof: using the Dolbear-style rule in this design, temperature = 50 + (chirps per minute - 40) / 4. For 100 chirps per minute, temperature = 50 + (100 - 40) / 4 = 65.0 degrees Fahrenheit. The demo prints 65.0 for 100 chirps, 55.0 for 60 chirps, and 75.0 for 140 chirps.

Signature: Bat Pulse Model Rank 7 · compounded

This Signature model designs bat-like echolocation pulses as a schedule of emissions and echoes. A virtual bat emits pulses with a stated frequency sweep, duration, and interval, and the model computes the echo delay for a target at a stated distance, then shortens the interval as the virtual bat closes on its target, ending in a rapid feeding-buzz schedule. The design distinguishes open-air pulses, long and narrowband, from clutter pulses, short and broadband, and it reports range resolution as a number derived from pulse duration. No ultrasound is emitted and no real bat is recorded: the model is arithmetic and scheduling, and any rendered pulse train is labelled SIMULATED. Its purpose is teaching the geometry of echolocation and giving composers a precise pulse language.

Science proof

Science proof: the model encodes the real physics of echolocation: range from echo delay, range resolution improving with shorter, broader-band pulses, and pulse-rate increase during prey approach, all documented in bat research. By computing delay from distance with the speed of sound, the model reproduces the quantitative relationship that real bats exploit, while remaining a labelled model with invented parameters.

Math proof

Math proof: echo delay equals twice the distance divided by the speed of sound. For a target at 2.0 metres, delay = 2 x 2.0 / 343 = 0.01166 seconds, about 11.66 milliseconds. The demo prints 11.66 ms for 2.0 m, 5.83 ms for 1.0 m, and 23.32 ms for 4.0 m.

Signature: Acoustic Niche Planner Rank 10 · compounded

This Signature planner is a design tool for arranging virtual voices without masking. The user enters a list of virtual singers with preferred frequency bands and active time slots, and the planner reports every band-time cell where two or more singers overlap, scores the plan by total overlap, and suggests moves, shifting a singer's band or slot, that reduce the score. The planner is the practical form of the acoustic niche hypothesis: it turns a descriptive idea about real habitats into a constructive tool for composers, game designers, and teachers. Every singer is invented, every parameter is stated, and every output is labelled a Signature design. This record covers all three layers, because a plan is only complete when geophony and anthrophony bands are entered alongside biophony.

Science proof

Science proof: the planner implements the niche principle that overlapping signals in the same band and time mask one another, a physical fact of hearing, and the design response, separation by band or time, that real communities exhibit. The planner does not claim that real species plan their niches; it claims only that separation reduces masking, which is measurable in its own overlap score.

Math proof

Math proof: if 5 singers occupy 5 distinct bands, overlap is 0 cells. If 2 singers share one band at the same time, overlap is 1 cell, and the overlap share over 5 band-time cells is 1 / 5 = 20.0 percent. The demo prints distinct-band overlap 0, shared-band overlap 1 cell, overlap share 20.0 percent, and the score after moving one singer to a free band, 0 cells.

Signature: Soundscape Index Calculator Design Rank 11 · compounded

This Signature design specifies a calculator for soundscape indices that students can run on their own invented layer powers. The calculator takes biophony power and anthrophony power in stated bands, computes NDSI as (biophony minus anthrophony) divided by (biophony plus anthrophony), and computes a simplified complexity score from the variation of level across time steps. The design includes worked examples with invented inputs so that a student can verify the arithmetic by hand, and it warns, in its own documentation, that indices summarise but never replace listening. The calculator is a Signature design tool: its inputs are invented, its formulas follow published definitions, and its outputs are labelled as model calculations, never as measurements from a real habitat.

Science proof

Science proof: the NDSI formula used here is the published definition, with biophony power from a higher band and anthrophony power from a lower band, yielding values from -1 to +1. The complexity score follows the published ACI idea that rapid level variation within bands indicates animal vocal activity rather than steady noise. Using published formulas on invented inputs is legitimate design and teaching practice, provided the inputs are labelled as invented, which this design does.

Math proof

Math proof: NDSI = (biophony - anthrophony) / (biophony + anthrophony). For biophony 0.9 and anthrophony 0.1, NDSI = (0.9 - 0.1) / (0.9 + 0.1) = 0.80. The demo prints 0.80 for those powers, 0.00 for equal powers 0.5 and 0.5, and -0.80 for reversed powers 0.1 and 0.9.

Science proof

The design states its data volumes, its checking rule that every automated detection is verified against a call library before it is trusted, and its limits, including that silent species are missed and wind and rain can fool indices. The design covers all three layers, because a monitoring network hears them together and its reports must separate them. No network is claimed to exist: this is a design specification, labelled Signature, that a builder could follow.

Science proof

Science proof: the design follows published passive acoustic monitoring practice: autonomous recorders, long deployments, index-based screening, and verified classification. Stating data volumes, duty cycles, and verification rules as design requirements mirrors how real monitoring networks are specified, and the design's stated limits match the published limits of the method.

Math proof: if each virtual recorder captures 12 hours per day for 30 days, it collects 12 x 30 = 360 hours.
