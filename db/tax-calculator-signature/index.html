1. Description

A tax calculator estimates tax from an income and a rate. This page is an ESTIMATOR for education: every rate on it is a stated example rate, shown on the page, not a real tax rate, and it is not tax advice. Type an income, pick an example rate, and read the estimate. Everything runs in this browser.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Tax is income times rate: an income of 1000 at an example rate of 20 percent gives a tax of 200. Two rates in steps add: 10 percent on the first 500 and 20 percent on the next 500 is 50 plus 100, which is 150 in all. A deduction takes money off first: 1000 minus 200 leaves 800 taxed, so at 20 percent the tax is 160. A yearly tax of 2400 is 200 a month.

3. Science proof

Tax estimation works because the arithmetic is simple: a rate is a fraction of an amount, a deduction lowers the amount before the rate is applied, and steps tax each part at its own rate. The estimate is only as good as the rate, which is why every rate on this page is labelled as an example and the page never claims to be real tax advice.
