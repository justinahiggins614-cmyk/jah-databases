Sixteen full records on the sounds of living things and the soundscapes they inhabit, ordered simple to compounded to complex. The three soundscape layers are kept distinct throughout: biophony, the sounds of living organisms; geophony, the sounds of the earth, such as wind, water, and weather; and anthrophony, human-made sound. Every record states which layer or layers it covers, carries a science proof, a verified math proof, a runnable demo program, and a named source. All sound on this page is described in prose or given as deterministic synthesis code; synthesised calls are labelled SIMULATED and are never field recordings. Everything is free forever, with no payments and no upsells.

Signature: Soundscape Primer Rank 1 · simple

This Signature primer designs a soundscape from three separate layer tracks, following the public edition's rule that biophony, geophony, and anthrophony are stored separately and mixed only arithmetically. A Signature soundscape is specified by listing, for each layer, a level in decibels, a set of frequency bands, and a schedule of activity across the day, and the primer's model combines those inputs into a total level, a band-occupancy table, and an NDSI-style balance figure. The primer is a teaching design: a student enters layer values, watches the balance move, and learns why a soundscape dominated by one layer sounds the way it does. Every input is invented, every output is labelled a Signature model, and no sound file is shipped or claimed to be a recording of a real place.

Science proof

Science proof: the primer implements the published three-layer model and the published decibel-addition and NDSI arithmetic, applied to invented inputs. That combination, published formulas plus labelled invented inputs, is the standard form of a design model in soundscape teaching, and it is honest precisely because the formulas are public and the inputs are declared as designs.

Math proof

Math proof: for layers at 55, 50, and 60 dB, the combined level is 10 x log10(10^5.5 + 10^5 + 10^6) = 61.51 dB. The demo prints the three layer levels, the combined level 61.51 dB, and the anthrophony power share, 10^6 / (10^5.5 + 10^5 + 10^6) = 70.6 percent.

Science proof

Science proof: the model encodes the published physics of ocean sound: faster sound in water than in air, long low-frequency range, and the deep sound channel's trapping effect. Math proof

Math proof: sound in seawater travels at roughly 1500 metres per second, about 1500 / 343 = 4.37 times the speed in air.
