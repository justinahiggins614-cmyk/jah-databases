The Signature Measurement Ink Mass Database (Public edition) - accessible version

No JavaScript is needed on this page. It is the plain-text version of the same database.

Honesty: pixel measurements are exact from the file 1939_0_0or1.jpg (1536 by 1024 px, ink darker than 128, 37,277 ink px). The 150 mm render width is an ASSUMPTION, stated wherever it is used. Atom numbers are scaled equivalents carried down the Signature scale, never direct physical measurements of ink atoms. The smallest resolvable unit in the file is 1 pixel. Nothing is invented. Ink mass assumes a 1 micrometre ink film and a density of 1 g per cubic centimetre; the formula mass = area x thickness x density lets any thickness or density recompute the mass.

1. Description and history

The Ink Mass Way turns ink coverage into an estimated ink mass at a stated print size. The whole signature covers 530.83 square millimetres at the assumed 150 mm render width, and at an assumed ink film 1 micrometre thick with density 1 g per cubic centimetre that is 0.5308 mg of ink. Manon uploaded his signature image on 2026-11-23 and asked for it to be measured many ways, as accurately as the Signature system allows, down toward atom scale. The numbers were measured from the file, verified twice, and recomputed for this wave with identical results.

2. Math proof

530.83 mm2 = 37,277 px x (150/1,257)^2. 0.5308 mg = 530.83 mm2 x 0.001 mm x 1 mg/mm3. The demo of every record prints the same kind of total as its math proof, so a fresh run of any demo verifies its proof.

3. Science proof

Mass is volume times density, and volume is area times thickness: three multiplications turn a counted area into a mass, provided the thickness and density are stated rather than hidden. The area is counted pixels; the thickness and density are stated assumptions with sensitivity tables.
