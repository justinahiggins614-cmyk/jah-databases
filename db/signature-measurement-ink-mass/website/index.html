Section 2: Math proof

530.83 mm2 = 37,277 px x (150/1,257)^2. 0.5308 mg = 530.83 mm2 x 0.001 mm x 1 mg/mm3. The demo of every record prints the same kind of total as its math proof, so a fresh run of any demo verifies its proof.

Section 3: Science proof

Mass is volume times density, and volume is area times thickness: three multiplications turn a counted area into a mass, provided the thickness and density are stated rather than hidden. The area is counted pixels; the thickness and density are stated assumptions with sensitivity tables.

The Formula: Mass Equals Area Times Thickness Times Density

JAH-SMIM-000002 · simple · Feasibility: buildable-now

The Ink Mass Way turns ink coverage into an estimated ink mass at a stated print size. The whole signature covers 530.83 square millimetres at the assumed 150 mm render width, and at an assumed ink film 1 micrometre thick with density 1 g per cubic centimetre that is 0.5308 mg of ink. The whole Ink Mass Way rests on one formula: mass = area x thickness x density. Area comes from counting ink pixels and scaling by the square of the millimetres per pixel, 0.119332 mm at the assumed 150 mm render width. Thickness is assumed at 1 micrometre, which is 0.001 mm. Density is assumed at 1 g per cubic centimetre, which is 1 mg per cubic millimetre. Multiply the three and the units work out: square millimetres times millimetres times milligrams per cubic millimetre gives milligrams. Any reader who prefers a different thickness or density can recompute every mass in this database from the areas given, because the formula and both assumptions are printed on every record.
