Ranking Framework: Criteria and Scoring Rank 2 · simple · beginner

Blueprints in this suite are ranked by six criteria, in order. First, source-faithfulness: does the blueprint match the myth, text, or fiction description? Second, level-fit: does the blueprint fit cleanly into the step-by-step technology-progress path, never leaping levels? A blueprint that leaps levels ranks below one that fits its level, however impressive. Third, engineering feasibility. Fourth, buildability at the stated scale. Fifth, completeness within the same technology level: a more complete map, with districts, ecosystems, and caves and underground all present, ranks above a sparser one at the same level. Sixth, testability: acceptance tests are defined and runnable. A faithful but not-established blueprint ranks below a faithful and buildable one, and the record says so plainly. Scores run from zero to ten per criterion, and the total runs from zero to sixty.

Family Overview: Port and Ship Cities Rank 5 · simple · beginner

Family Overview: Moving Cities Rank 6 · compounded · intermediate

Traction cities roll on wheels or tracks and harvest other settlements for resources, airborne cities float on gas or lift, and walking cities stride on legs. Because a moving city must prove motion, balance, and resource harvesting before it can grow, its manifestation path is strict: a digital traction or motion model at Level 1, a small mobile module, rolling or airborne test unit, at Level 2, a full mobile district at Level 3, and a roaming city at Level 4, with motion and resource-harvest proofs at Level 2 gating Level 3. Three system records in the moving pair are labeled as systems, not cities: traction mechanics, lift and buoyancy, and docking, harvesting, and governance.

Blueprint Method: Pool, Rank, Improve, Re-rank Rank 9 · compounded · intermediate

A blueprint pair is built by a fixed method. First, a pool of candidates is gathered, more than sixteen, from the source description and its interpretations. Second, the pool is ranked by the six criteria in the ranking framework, with level-fit and completeness as full criteria, not afterthoughts. Third, the top candidates are improved: gaps in districts, ecosystems, or caves are filled, level leaps are corrected, and acceptance tests are written. Fourth, the improved candidates are re-ranked, and the best sixteen ship with their ranks and scores shown. A candidate that stays faithful but not-established ranks below a faithful and buildable one, and the record says so plainly. No candidate ships without districts, ecosystems, and caves and underground sections.

Honesty Labeling Guide Rank 10 · compounded · intermediate

Every record in this suite carries an honesty label, and the label is part of the record, not a footnote. MYTHOLOGICAL means the city lives in myth or legend, not verified history. THEORIZED or SPECULATIVE means the city is a proposal or theory, with its source and status stated. FICTION means the city comes from a named work or franchise and is never presented as fact. Real-planet settlements are labeled THEORIZED because they are proposed, not existing. Blueprint records carry a feasibility label: achievable, demonstrated, research-required, or not-established. Physically impossible features are labeled design or speculative, never claimed working. Where archaeology exists, verified facts and legend are stated separately. Copyrighted text, lyrics, dialogue, and artwork are never reproduced; summaries are original, and names are used nominatively for reference.

Comparison Method: City-versus-City Tool Rank 14 · complex · advanced

The comparison method is a tool, not a verdict. To compare two cities, the tool lines up the same fields side by side: honesty label, planet, technology level, districts, ecosystems, caves and underground, source, status, and feasibility. It then scores each city on the six ranking criteria and shows where one outranks the other and why. A myth city and a theorized city can be compared on completeness and level-fit, but their labels are never traded: a myth city does not become more factual by outscoring a theorized city, and a theorized city does not become myth by losing. The tool's output is a comparison table plus a plain-language note stating what the comparison does and does not prove. The demo for this record runs a comparison between two sample cities and prints the table.
