The research subject covered here is What Post-Quantum Computing Is. Post-quantum computing is the study of computers and codes after quantum computers arrive: a quantum computer is a computer that uses quantum physics, the physics of very small things, to calculate in ways ordinary computers cannot, and post-quantum cryptography is the family of codes designed to stay safe even against such a machine. The research subject covered here is Shor's Algorithm and the Quantum Threat. Shor's algorithm is a quantum method for factoring large numbers and for solving discrete logarithms, which are the two hard problems behind RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman) and elliptic-curve cryptography, the codes that protect most internet traffic today. A quantum computer large and reliable enough to run it would break the public-key codes now in use, so defenders must move to codes based on problems that stay hard even for quantum computers.
