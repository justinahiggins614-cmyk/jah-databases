Swimming Rank 1 · simple · swimming · beginner

Signature swimming is taught as comfort first and speed second. A new swimmer learns to float, to breathe out slowly underwater, and to glide, before any stroke is named, because a swimmer who trusts the water will learn every stroke faster. Our programme teaches freestyle first, then backstroke for breathing ease, then breaststroke for rest, and butterfly last, as a reward for strong legs and shoulders. Every session ends with a safety drill, such as floating fully clothed or swimming to the side without panic, so the sport always stays tied to the life skill underneath it.

Training

Sessions pair floating and breathing drills, one stroke studied in depth, and a closing safety drill.

Techniques

Signature order is freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, then butterfly, each built from glide, pull, and kick separately.

Safety

Every session ends with a safety drill, and open-water practice always uses a buddy and a bright cap.

History

The Signature school follows the sport's Olympic history while teaching swimming first as a life skill.

Diving Rank 2 · simple · diving · beginner

Signature diving is taught from the water up, not from the platform down. A student learns the rip entry from poolside first, then from one metre, and only then from three metres, because a clean entry is the whole point of the sport and a high board cannot fix a poor one. Our coaches teach the hurdle step and the arm swing as a single rhythm, and every dive is filmed or watched from the side so the student can see what the judge sees. Platform diving comes late, and only when entries from lower heights are consistently clean. The hall's praise goes to the diver whose entry makes the least sound.

Training

Sessions pair poolside entry drills, hurdle-step rhythm work, low-board dives, and video review from the side.

Techniques

Signature basics are the rip entry, the hurdle step, and the arm swing, taught as one rhythm.

Safety

Height rises only when entries are clean, coaches supervise all platform work, and divers never dive alone.

History

The Signature school follows the sport's path from gymnastics exhibitions to Olympic competition.

Lifts are built slowly, with spotters in the water, and breath-holding is trained gradually and never pushed to distress. The hall's praise goes to the team that looks like one swimmer, not eight.

Training

Open-Water Swimming Rank 4 · simple · swimming · beginner

Signature open-water swimming is taught as navigation before endurance. A student learns to sight every six strokes, to breathe on both sides, and to draft, in a lake with an escort boat, before any long distance is attempted. Our programme builds distance slowly, adding no more than a tenth to the longest swim each week, and cold water is introduced gradually with short swims and warm recovery. Feeding from a pole is practised in training, not first tried in a race, and every swim ends with a roll call on the shore. The hall's rule is that no swim is worth a swimmer: conditions, not pride, decide whether a swim goes ahead.

Training

Sessions pair sighting drills, drafting practice, gradual distance building, and cold-water acclimatisation.

Techniques

Signature basics are sighting every few strokes, bilateral breathing, and drafting behind another swimmer.

Safety

Every swim uses an escort boat, bright caps, and a shore roll call, and conditions decide whether a swim goes ahead.

History

The Signature school follows the channel-swimming tradition from Webb's 1875 crossing to the Olympic marathon swim.

Rowing Rank 5 · simple · paddling · beginner

Signature rowing is taught as one boat, one movement. A new rower learns the stroke on a machine first, then in a stable training boat, and only then in a racing shell, because balance learned early saves months later. Our coaches teach the legs-first drive with a simple phrase, legs, back, arms, and they film crews from the bank so rowers can see when the boat stops running level. Coxswains are trained as crew members, not passengers: they learn to steer, to call a race plan, and to keep a crew calm in the last 500 metres. The hall's praise goes to the crew whose blades enter and leave the water together.

Training

Sessions pair machine technique, stable-boat rowing, racing-shell work, and bank-side video review.

Techniques

Signature basics are the legs-back-arms drive, clean blade entry, and feathering on the recovery.

Safety

Life jackets are worn in training boats, crews learn capsize recovery, and coaches follow in a launch.

History

The Signature school follows the sport from Thames watermen's races to the Olympic regatta.

Kayak Slalom Rank 7 · compounded · paddling · intermediate

Signature kayak slalom is taught as reading before racing. A student walks the course on foot, names each eddy and current, and paddles it slowly before any timed run, because a gate missed at speed is a gate never learned. Our coaches teach the eddy turn first, then the ferry across current, then the upstream gate, in that order, since each skill rests on the one before it. Timed runs come only when a clean run is possible, and the hall's praise goes to the paddler who finishes clean, even slowly, rather than the paddler who finishes fast with penalties.

Training

Sessions pair bank scouting, slow course paddling, eddy-turn drills, and only later timed runs.

Techniques

Signature order is eddy turns, ferries, upstream gates, then full-course runs.

Safety

Helmets and buoyancy aids are mandatory, courses are scouted on foot, and rescue cover stands on the bank.

History

The Signature school follows the sport from Alpine river touring to Olympic slalom.

Capsize recovery is taught in the first week, so no student fears the water their boat sails on. The hall's praise goes to the crew that gains by reading a shift, not by shouting at rivals.

Training

Safety

Windsurfing Rank 9 · compounded · sailing · intermediate

A student stands on a large board in light wind, learns to uphaul the sail without rushing, and practises steering by small weight shifts, before any attempt at planing or foiling. Our instructors teach the fall first: how to fall clear of the board and climb back on, so that falling stops being frightening and starts being ordinary. Foiling comes only when a student can sail, tack, and recover confidently on a normal board. The hall's praise goes to the rider who sails smoothly in light wind, because speed without control is only a faster fall.

Training

Techniques

Safety

Etiquette is taught as safety, not politeness: the surfer nearest the peak has priority, and dropping in on another surfer is treated as endangering them. The hall's praise goes to the surfer who reads the ocean well and rides within it.

Training

Techniques

Safety

History

The buddy rule is taught as the sport itself: a Signature freediver never enters the water to dive without a trained buddy watching. The hall's praise goes to the diver whose dive looks calmest, not deepest.

Training

Safety

Rivers are chosen by the weakest paddler in the crew, not the strongest, and the hall's praise goes to the crew that runs clean and together, not the crew that runs fastest.

Training

Techniques

Signature basics are paddling on command, high-siding, holding on, and swimming feet-first if thrown out.

Safety

Helmets and life jackets are mandatory, rivers are scouted, and trips are graded to the weakest paddler.

History

The Signature school follows rafting from post-war river exploration to commercial and racing sport.
