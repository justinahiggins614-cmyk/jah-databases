1. Description

Voice calling lets two people talk by voice over a connection. The demo on this page is a LOCAL DEMO ONLY: it simulates a call with simulated voices and a call log, with no server and no real call. A real voice call needs a network, and this page honestly says so. Nothing is recorded and nothing is uploaded.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A call is counted in people and minutes: a call with 2 people has 2 voices, and three calls of 10 minutes each total 30 minutes. Of 5 calls, 1 missed and 4 answered. A call log of 3 calls lists 3 lengths: 5, 10 and 15 minutes. A call of 60 seconds is 1 minute.

3. Science proof

Voice calling works because a microphone turns a voice into a signal, and a network carries that signal to the other person, whose speaker turns it back into a voice. The local demo on this page does the first half only in simulation: it shows simulated voices and a call log, so the shape of a call can be tried. That is why the page labels itself a local demo and makes no real call.
