A robot-swarm website: a visitor picks one imagined job for the swarm, such as tidying a room or building a pebble bridge, and the site shows the job in plain words, how the tiny robots share the work, and what could go wrong. Every job is a what-if exploration, and the site never claims such a swarm exists. This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Billion Tiny Robots Tidy One Room, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Swarm Stories, in the Signature edition.

History

Imagining many tiny helpers is an old habit: a story of ants carrying a feast, a tale of bees building a hive, a dream of a thousand small hands. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain guide for each imagined job. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
