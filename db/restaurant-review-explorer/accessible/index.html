The Restaurant Review Explorer Database

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. The restaurant review explorer helps a reader make sense of what other diners have written, in plain words, with clearly labeled illustrative sample reviews that show how a review is read, never presenting any sample as a real review of a real place.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex.

  1. Reading Reviews of a Noodle Shop

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The restaurant review explorer helps a reader make sense of what other diners have written, in plain words, with clearly labeled illustrative sample reviews that show how a review is read, never presenting any sample as a real review of a real place. Reading reviews of a noodle shop takes a careful eye, because the broth, the noodles, and the wait each matter, and a review that praises one may hide a fault in another. The explorer must show how to read such reviews: look for the broth first, then the noodles, then the wait, and trust the reviewer who names all three. Without that guidance, a reader trusts the loudest review, and the loudest review is often the least careful one. Worked example: in a website built this way, reading reviews of a noodle shop runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
