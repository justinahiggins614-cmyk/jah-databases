2. Math proof

Matching is an inequality: a buy order at price p matches a sell order at price q when p is at least q. The spread is a subtraction: best ask minus best bid. With a best bid of 11 credits and a best ask of 13 credits, the spread is 13 minus 11, which is 2 credits, and the midpoint is 12 credits. A trade value is a multiplication: 10 Aurora Tokens at 12 credits each is 120 credits. A fee is a rate times a value: a taker fee of 0.2 percent on 120 credits is 120 times 0.002, which is 0.24 credits, so the buyer pays 120.24 credits in total.

3. Science proof

An exchange works because it gathers all bids and all asks into one order book, so a buyer and a seller find each other without searching the whole market. Matching by price first and then by time is fair and checkable: the best price trades first, and the earliest order at that price trades first. Custody separates holding from trading, so assets rest in wallets while orders rest in the book, and settlement moves only what a matched trade says to move. Fees pay for the book, the matching, and the audit trail, and every trade leaves a written record that can be checked afterwards. That is why a fictional exchange, with fictional assets and fictional credits, can show every step of real exchange design without trading anything real.

What a Digital Asset Exchange Is

JAH-DAEXS-000001 — A digital asset exchange is a market where fictional digital assets are listed and swapped: a trader swaps 10 Aurora Tokens for Harbor Coins at a fictional rate of 2 Harbor Coins per Aurora Token, and receives 20 Harbor Coins.

Math proof: A swap is a multiplication: 10 Aurora Tokens times a fictional rate of 2 Harbor Coins per Aurora Token is 20 Harbor Coins. The rate is a ratio, and the ratio times the amount gives the amount received.

Science proof: An exchange is a meeting place: buyers and sellers who would never find each other arrive at one book, and the book pairs them. That is why a market with many traders can price an asset faster than two people bargaining alone.

Runnable demo program
rate, amount = 2, 10
print(f"Swap {amount} Aurora Tokens at {rate} Harbor Coins each: {amount * rate} Harbor Coins")

Output:

Swap 10 Aurora Tokens at 2 Harbor Coins each: 20 Harbor Coins

A Wallet Holds the Assets

JAH-DAEXS-000003 — A wallet holds balances, one number per asset: 50 Aurora Tokens and 100 Harbor Coins. At fictional prices of 12 and 6 credits, the wallet holds 50 times 12 plus 100 times 6, which is 1200 credits of fictional value.

Math proof: A wallet total is a sum of products: 50 times 12 is 600 credits, 100 times 6 is 600 credits, and 600 plus 600 is 1200 credits.

Science proof: A wallet works because it keeps one balance per asset in one place, so a trader can see what can be spent before placing an order. That is why an exchange checks the wallet before it accepts any order.

Runnable demo program
balances = {'AUR': 50, 'HBR': 100}
prices = {'AUR': 12, 'HBR': 6}
total = sum(balances[a] * prices[a] for a in balances)
print(f"Wallet holds {balances['AUR']} AUR and {balances['HBR']} HBR: {total} credits")

Output:

Wallet holds 50 AUR and 100 HBR: 1200 credits

Science proof: A deposit works because the exchange records the arrival before it lets the assets be traded, so the wallet never shows money that has not arrived. That is why a deposit must be confirmed first and spent second.

The Order Book, Bids and Asks

JAH-DAEXS-000006 — The order book lists bids and asks for one fictional asset: bids of 11, 10 and 9 credits, and asks of 13, 14 and 15 credits. The best bid is 11 credits, the best ask is 13 credits, and the spread is 2 credits.

Math proof: The best bid is the largest bid: the largest of 11, 10 and 9 is 11 credits. The best ask is the smallest ask: the smallest of 13, 14 and 15 is 13 credits. The spread is 13 minus 11, which is 2 credits.

Science proof: An order book works because every order is visible in price order, so the best price is always on top and no trader has to guess where the market stands. That is why a book, not a private bargain, sets the price everyone sees.

Runnable demo program
bids = [11, 10, 9]
asks = [13, 14, 15]

Output:

Best bid 11 credits; best ask 13 credits; spread 2 credits

A Market Order

JAH-DAEXS-000007 — A market order buys at the best price available right now: buying 5 Aurora Tokens at the best ask of 13 credits costs 5 times 13, which is 65 credits.

Math proof: A market order is a multiplication at the best ask: 5 Aurora Tokens times 13 credits is 65 credits. The buyer pays whatever the best ask is, and the trade happens at once.

Science proof: A market order works because the buyer accepts the price the book already offers, so there is no waiting and no bargaining. That is why a market order is the fastest way to trade, and why its price can move when the book is thin.

Runnable demo program
amount, best_ask = 5, 13
print(f"Market buy {amount} Aurora Tokens at {best_ask} credits: {amount * best_ask} credits")

Output:

Market buy 5 Aurora Tokens at 13 credits: 65 credits

A Limit Order

JAH-DAEXS-000008 — A limit order buys only at its stated price or better: a buy of 5 Aurora Tokens with a limit of 12 credits waits, because the best ask is 13 credits, which is above the limit.

Math proof: A limit is a comparison: the order trades only when the best ask is at most the limit. Here 13 is greater than 12, so the order waits, and it trades only when an ask of 12 credits or less appears.

Science proof: A limit order works because the trader names the worst price they will accept, and the exchange refuses any worse price on their behalf. That is why a limit order protects the trader from a sudden jump in price.

Runnable demo program
limit, best_ask = 12, 13
print(f"Limit {limit} credits, best ask {best_ask} credits: {'trade now' if best_ask <= limit else 'wait for 12 or less'}")

Output:

Limit 12 credits, best ask 13 credits: wait for 12 or less

How Matching Works

JAH-DAEXS-000009 — Matching pairs a buy with a sell when the prices meet: a buy order for 5 Aurora Tokens at 13 credits meets a sell order for 5 Aurora Tokens at 13 credits, and the trade is 5 Aurora Tokens for 65 credits.

Math proof: A match is an equality of price and a minimum of amounts: the buy price 13 equals the sell price 13, so they match, and the traded amount is the smaller of 5 and 5, which is 5 Aurora Tokens, worth 5 times 13, or 65 credits.

Science proof: Matching works because the rule is the same for every order: price first, then time. That is why two strangers can trade without talking, and why the result can be checked by anyone who reads the book.

Runnable demo program
buy, sell, price = 5, 5, 13
traded = min(buy, sell)
print(f"Buy {buy} at {price} meets sell {sell} at {price}: trade {traded} Aurora Tokens for {traded * price} credits")

Output:

Buy 5 at 13 meets sell 5 at 13: trade 5 Aurora Tokens for 65 credits

The Spread Between Bid and Ask

JAH-DAEXS-000010 — The spread is the gap between the best bid and the best ask: with a best bid of 11 credits and a best ask of 13 credits, the spread is 2 credits and the midpoint is 12 credits.

Math proof: The spread is a subtraction and the midpoint is an average: 13 minus 11 is 2 credits, and 11 plus 13 divided by 2 is 12 credits. A narrow spread means buyers and sellers nearly agree; a wide spread means they do not.

Science proof: A spread exists because buyers want the lowest price and sellers want the highest, and the book shows the distance between them. That is why a spread that shrinks means the market is agreeing, and a spread that grows means it is unsure.

Runnable demo program
bid, ask = 11, 13
print(f"Bid {bid}, ask {ask}: spread {ask - bid} credits, midpoint {(bid + ask) / 2} credits")

Output:

Bid 11, ask 13: spread 2 credits, midpoint 12.0 credits

Maker Fees and Taker Fees

JAH-DAEXS-000011 — A maker adds an order to the book and a taker removes one, and each pays a different fee: on a fictional trade of 1000 credits, a maker fee of 0.1 percent is 1 credit and a taker fee of 0.2 percent is 2 credits.

Math proof: A fee is a rate times a value: 1000 times 0.001 is 1 credit for the maker, and 1000 times 0.002 is 2 credits for the taker. The maker pays less because the maker's order helps the book.

Science proof: Fees work because they pay for the exchange itself: the book, the matching, and the records. Charging the taker more and the maker less rewards the orders that give the market its prices.

Runnable demo program
value = 1000
print(f"Trade {value} credits: maker fee {value * 0.001} credits, taker fee {value * 0.002} credits")

Output:

Trade 1000 credits: maker fee 1.0 credits, taker fee 2.0 credits

A Worked Fee Example

JAH-DAEXS-000012 — A worked fee example, step by step: buying 10 Aurora Tokens at 12 credits is 120 credits, the taker fee of 0.2 percent is 0.24 credits, and the buyer pays 120.24 credits in total.

Math proof: The total is a value plus its fee: 10 times 12 is 120 credits, 120 times 0.002 is 0.24 credits, and 120 plus 0.24 is 120.24 credits. Every step is one multiplication or one addition, and each can be checked.

Science proof: A worked example works because it shows the arithmetic instead of hiding it, so a trader can repeat the same steps on any other trade. That is why an exchange that shows its fees earns trust, and one that hides them does not.

Custody: Who Holds the Keys

JAH-DAEXS-000013 — Custody is the question of who holds the keys: in this fictional study, the exchange holds the keys for 3 of 4 wallets, and 1 wallet is held by its owner alone.

Math proof: Custody is a count: 3 of 4 wallets are held by the exchange and 1 of 4 by the owner, so the exchange holds 75 percent of the wallets and the owner holds 25 percent.

Science proof: Custody matters because whoever holds the keys can move the assets, so the holder must be named and the rules must be written down. That is why an exchange that holds keys must also show its records, and why an owner who holds their own keys must guard them alone.

Runnable demo program

Hot Wallets and Cold Storage

JAH-DAEXS-000014 — A hot wallet is online and ready to pay, and cold storage is offline and hard to reach: of 1000 fictional Lantern Units, 900 sit in cold storage and 100 sit in a hot wallet, which is 90 percent cold and 10 percent hot.

Math proof: The split is a percentage: 900 divided by 1000 is 0.9, or 90 percent cold, and 100 divided by 1000 is 0.1, or 10 percent hot. The two parts add back to 1000 Lantern Units.

Science proof: Hot and cold storage work because they balance speed against safety: the hot wallet pays out quickly, and the cold store keeps most assets out of reach of any online attack. That is why an exchange keeps only a small share hot.

Security Checks Before a Trade

JAH-DAEXS-000015 — Before a trade, the exchange runs three checks: the wallet holds enough, the price is inside its limit, and the order is not too large. In the worked example all 3 checks pass, so the order is accepted.

Math proof: The checks are three yes-or-no questions joined by 'and': enough balance is true, price inside the limit is true, and size allowed is true, so all three are true and the order is accepted. One false answer would refuse the order.

Science proof: Checks work because they run before the trade, not after, so a bad order is stopped while it is still only an order. That is why an exchange checks first and matches second, and never the other way round.

Runnable demo program
checks = {'enough balance': True, 'price inside limit': True, 'size allowed': True}
print(f"{len(checks)} checks; passed {sum(checks.values())}: {'order accepted' if all(checks.values()) else 'order refused'}")

Output:

3 checks; passed 3: order accepted

Settlement: When a Trade Is Final

JAH-DAEXS-000016 — Settlement is the moment a trade becomes final, in two steps: first the orders match, then the assets move. In the worked example, 5 Aurora Tokens move from the seller's wallet to the buyer's wallet, and the trade is final.

Math proof: Settlement is two moves that must both happen: the seller's balance falls by 5 and the buyer's balance rises by 5, so the total of both wallets stays the same and only the owner changes.

Science proof: Settlement works because the move is recorded at the same time it happens, so the book, the wallets, and the audit trail all agree. That is why a trade is not final when it matches, but when the assets have moved and the record is written.

Runnable demo program
seller, buyer = 50, 20
seller -= 5
buyer += 5
print(f"Settled: seller {seller} AUR, buyer {buyer} AUR; total {seller + buyer} AUR")

Output:

Settled: seller 45 AUR, buyer 25 AUR; total 70 AUR

Math proof: The change is a subtraction: 12 minus 11 is 1 credit up. The candle range is also a subtraction: the high of 13 minus the low of 10 is 3 credits, and the close of 12 sits inside that range.

Science proof: Market data works because every trade leaves a price behind, and the ticker and the candle summarise those prices so a reader can see the day at a glance. That is why a market that publishes its data can be checked, and one that hides it cannot.

Math proof: The day's trades are a multiplication: 3 trades per hour times 8 hours is 24 trades. The 6 steps run in order, and each step must finish before the next begins.

Science proof: A trading day works because the same steps run in the same order every day, so every trader knows when the book opens, when orders are accepted, and when the report appears. That is why operations are a routine, not an improvisation.

Pausing Trading: Circuit Breakers

JAH-DAEXS-000019 — A circuit breaker pauses trading when prices move too fast: when the fictional price of Aurora Token falls 5 credits in one minute, past the limit of 4 credits, trading pauses for 5 minutes.

Math proof: The trigger is a comparison: a fall of 5 credits is greater than the limit of 4 credits, so the breaker trips, and trading pauses for 5 minutes while the book settles.

Science proof: A breaker works because a pause gives traders time to read the book instead of racing it, so a fast fall does not turn into a panic. That is why a market that can pause is steadier than one that cannot stop.

Keeping Records: The Audit Trail

JAH-DAEXS-000020 — The audit trail is a written line for every trade: each of the 24 fictional trades of the day writes 1 log line, so the day ends with 24 lines that any checker can read.

Math proof: The trail is a count: 24 trades times 1 line per trade is 24 lines. Each line names the trade, the price, and the time, so the whole day can be rebuilt from the lines alone.

Science proof: An audit trail works because a trade that is written down cannot be quietly changed, and a checker can follow the lines from the first trade to the last. That is why a market keeps its records in order, and never throws a line away.

Runnable demo program
trades = 24

Handling a Failed Order

JAH-DAEXS-000021 — A failed order is refused cleanly: an order to buy 100 Aurora Tokens is refused because the wallet holds only 50 credits of buying power, so 0 Aurora Tokens trade and the wallet is unchanged.

Math proof: The refusal is a comparison: the order needs 100 Aurora Tokens, the wallet can pay for only 50, and 100 is greater than 50, so the order fails and 0 trade. The wallet keeps its 50 credits of buying power.

Science proof: Refusing cleanly works because a failed order leaves no half-trade behind: nothing moves, nothing is owed, and the trader can try again with a smaller order. That is why a failed order is a message, not a mess.
