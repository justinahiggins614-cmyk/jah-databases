One electron, its rest energy, and the electric force that holds it in an atom, checked as a single mix.

An electron carries rest energy from its mass and feels the electric force of a nucleus. The question this record answers is whether that mix holds together: does the binding energy of the atom cover the work the electric force could do and the energy put into the electron, or does the electron escape? The mix is the smallest energy-force-mass combination in the program, and it sets the pattern every later mix follows: name the mass, name the energy, name the force, add the balance, and read the verdict before building anything on it.

History

The electron was identified by J. J. Thomson in 1897, and the hydrogen atom binding energy of 13.6 electron-volts was fixed by the Bohr model in 1913 and confirmed by spectroscopy. The rest energy E = m c^2 came from Einstein in 1905. This record joins those three settled facts into one mix.

  1. Name the mass, the energy form, and the force in the mix.
  2. Write the rest energy as m times c squared.
  3. Write the work as force times distance.
  4. Compare the binding energy with work plus input energy.
  5. Read the verdict from the sign of the margin.

Members

Combination rule

Verdict

stable — The atomic binding energy of 2.18e-18 J is far larger than the work the electric force can do over one atomic distance plus the one-electron-volt input, so the margin is positive and the electron stays bound. Conditions: Holds while the input energy stays below the binding energy; a photon above 13.6 eV would ionise the atom instead.

The science is the balance of three energies. Rest energy is mass times c squared, about 8.19e-14 J for one electron. Sunlight, radiation pressure, and a light sail mass, checked as a single mix that must hold while it pushes.

A solar sail is pushed by sunlight: photons carry momentum, and bouncing them off a thin sail gives a tiny force. The verdict is read from the numbers, not from hope, and the same check scales to any sail size. This record checks photon energy, radiation pressure force, and sail mass as one mix.

History

Radiation pressure was predicted by Maxwell in the 1860s and measured by Lebedev in 1900. Solar sailing was proposed by Tsiolkovsky and others in the early twentieth century, and the Japanese IKAROS craft demonstrated a solar sail in space in 2010.

Method

  1. Name the mass, the energy form, and the force in the mix.
  2. Write the sail rest energy as m times c squared.

  4. Compare the sail binding energy with work plus absorbed energy.

Members

Combination rule

stable — The sail material binding of 1.0e4 J is far larger than the radiation work over one metre plus the absorbed sunlight energy, so the margin is positive and the sail holds while it is pushed.
