History
Trade across borders has always travelled on paper: the merchant who sent goods by sea carried a bill that named the goods, the ship, and the buyer, so that the goods could be claimed at the far port. From those single bills grew the whole family of trade documents — invoices, packing lists, certificates, declarations — each naming one part of the truth about a shipment, so that the goods, the parties, and the terms could all be proved. Keeping trade documents means holding that family together per shipment, complete and in order. This database is built on that public practice of documenting every shipment true, with every document it needs.
