# Dictionary entries — The Trade Documentation Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## trade document

A written record about a shipment, such as an invoice or a bill of lading, naming the goods, the parties, and the terms, kept as part of the shipment's whole record.

## bill of lading

The document given by a carrier that names the goods received for carriage, the ship or vehicle, and the destination, serving as receipt and as proof of the carriage agreement.

## certificate of origin

The document stating where goods were made or grown, signed by the named authority, so that the origin of a shipment can be proved at the border.

## commercial invoice

The seller's written bill for a shipment, naming the goods, their quantities, their prices, and the buyer, so that the sale can be counted and the payment can be checked.

## packing list

The written list of what is packed in each parcel of a shipment, with quantities and weights, so that the shipment can be checked parcel by parcel when it arrives.

## customs declaration

The written statement given to the border authority, naming the goods, their value, and their origin, so that the shipment can pass the border lawfully.

## export licence

The written permission to send stated goods out of a country, naming the goods and the permission, kept with the shipment, so that the export can be shown lawful.

## import permit

The written permission to bring stated goods into a country, naming the goods and the permission, kept with the shipment, so that the import can be shown lawful.

## consignment

One shipment of goods sent together under one set of documents, named and counted as one, so that the whole shipment can be followed from sender to receiver.

## shipper

The party sending goods in a shipment, named in the documents with their address, so that every shipment can be traced to who sent it.

## consignee

The party a shipment is sent to, named in the documents with their address, so that the goods can be claimed by the right hands at the far end.

## goods code

The standard number given to a kind of goods, written on the documents, so that the same goods are counted the same way at every border they cross.
