# Dictionary entries — The Price Comparison Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## comparison

The act of laying the same product from different sellers side by side, on the same points, so that their differences can be seen in the same words.

## price

The amount asked for a product, recorded with its date and its seller, so that every price in the table can be traced to when and where it was recorded.

## offer

One seller's price for one product at one date, kept as a single record, so that the comparison is a table of offers, each with its own source.

## seller

The party offering a product at a stated price, named beside every offer, so that every price in the comparison can be traced to who asked it.

## baseline

The price a comparison is measured against, such as the lowest recorded price, stated before the table is read, so that every difference has the same starting point.

## unit price

The price of one unit of a product, such as one kilogram or one litre, counted the same way for every offer, so that different pack sizes can be compared fairly.

## total price

The whole amount a buyer would pay for an offer, including the stated charges beside the price, counted by the same rule for every seller in the table.

## price history

The kept record of a product's prices over time, in order, so that a price today can be read against the prices that came before it.

## price change

One movement in a product's price, recorded with its old price, its new price, and its date, so that the history shows when and how the price moved.

## like-for-like

The rule that only the same product, in the same amount and the same condition, may be compared, so that the table compares equals and never confuses unlike things.

## range

The span from the lowest to the highest price in a comparison, stated with both ends, so that a reader can see how far apart the sellers stand.

## snapshot

The table of prices as recorded at one date and time, kept whole, so that a comparison can be re-read later exactly as it stood when it was made.
