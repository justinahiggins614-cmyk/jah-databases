What Genetics Is

Genetics is the science of heredity: how the traits pass from the parents to the children, by the genes, in the DNA. The geneticist studies the genes, the chromosomes, and the inheritance, and how they make the living thing what it is. Genetics is the science of the code: the DNA is the instruction, and the living thing is the reading of it.

The human genome has about 20,000 genes, and about 3 billion letters of DNA: each gene has about 150,000 letters, on average. A child inherits half its genes from the mother, and half from the father: 10,000 from each, of the 20,000. Mendel crossed the pea plants in the 1860s, and genetics is about 160 years old in 2026. The DNA is the code: the 4 letters, A, T, C, and G, make the words, and the words make the genes, and the genes make the living thing.

DNA

DNA is the molecule of heredity: the double helix, made of 4 letters — A, T, C, and G — which carries the genes, and the instructions for the living thing. DNA is copied when the cell divides, and it is read when the protein is made, and it is passed from the parents to the children. DNA is the code of life, and it is the same code in every living thing, from the bacterium to the human.

The DNA has 4 letters, and they pair up: A with T, and C with G, so the pairs are 2. The human genome has about 3 billion letters, and about 20,000 genes: each gene has about 150,000 letters, on average. The DNA is about 2 metres long, in each cell, coiled into the nucleus, which is about 6 micrometres wide: the 2 metres fit in the 6 micrometres, by the coiling. Watson and Crick found the helix in 1953, and DNA is 73 years old as a structure in 2026. The DNA is the code, and it is the same in every living thing.

The human has 46 chromosomes, in 23 pairs: 22 pairs are the autosomes, and 1 pair is the sex chromosomes. A child gets 23 from the mother, and 23 from the father, and the 46 are the pairings. Inheritance

Inheritance is the passing of the genes from the parents to the children: the child gets half its genes from the mother, and half from the father, and the traits follow the genes. Inheritance is the pattern of the family: the eye colour, and the height, and the disease, which run in the family, because the genes run in the family. Inheritance is the genetics of the family, and the pedigree is its map.

Mendel's Laws

Mendel's laws are the laws of inheritance: the law of segregation, and the law of independent assortment, found by Gregor Mendel, in the pea plants, in the 1860s. The laws say that the genes come in pairs, and they separate when the gametes are made, and the pairs sort independently. Mendel's laws are the foundation of genetics: every inheritance is the laws, in action, and the ratios are the prediction.

Mendel counted 8,000 pea plants, and the ratios were 3 to 1: 6,000 round, and 2,000 wrinkled, in one cross. The Punnett square for two carriers has 4 boxes: 1 is the dominant pair, 2 are the mixed, and 1 is the recessive pair, so 3 show the trait, and 1 does not. Mendel published in 1866, and it was rediscovered in 1900, 34 years later. The laws are 2: the segregation, and the independent assortment, and they are the foundation of genetics. Dominant and Recessive Traits

Dominant and recessive traits are the two kinds of inheritance: the dominant trait appears with one copy of the gene, and the recessive trait needs two copies. The dominant gene is expressed, even when the other copy is different, and the recessive gene is hidden, unless both copies are the same. Dominant and recessive are the language of inheritance: the capital letter is the dominant, and the small letter is the recessive, and the Punnett square is the prediction.

Two carriers have 4 boxes in the Punnett square: 1 is affected, 2 are carriers, and 1 is neither, so the chance of an affected child is 1 in 4. A dominant parent with one copy has a 1 in 2 chance per child: each child has a half chance. The round pea is dominant, and the wrinkled is recessive: the round appears with one copy, and the wrinkled needs two. The capital letter is the dominant, and the small letter is the recessive: R is the round, and r is the wrinkled, and Rr is round, and rr is wrinkled. Dominant and recessive are the language: the letters are the alleles, and the square is the prediction.

Mutation

A mutation is a change in the DNA: a letter changed, added, or deleted, which changes the gene, and the protein, and the living thing. Mutations are the source of the variation: without them, there would be no new traits, and no evolution. Mutations are mostly harmful or neutral, and rarely helpful, and the helpful ones are the evolution, over generations.

The mutation rate is about 1 in a billion letters, per division, and the human genome has 3 billion letters, so each division has about 3 new mutations. A population of 1 billion bacteria has about 1 mutant per gene, per generation: the rate is low, but the population is large. The point mutation changes 1 letter, and the protein changes by 1 amino acid. The deletion removes the letters, and the gene is shortened. Mutations are the source of the variation: without them, there would be no new traits, and no evolution.
