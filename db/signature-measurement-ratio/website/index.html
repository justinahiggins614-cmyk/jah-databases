Section 1: Description and history

The Ratio Way measures the signature by proportions: every part compared with every other part, in exact pixel counts. A ratio needs no ruler and no assumed size: it is one counted number divided by another, so it holds at any render width. The tall strokes, the sweeps, and the dots are all counted from the file, and their ratios are the signature's proportions, written down so any reader can check them. Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history and description, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, a scientific equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

Ratios are the oldest way of comparing sizes: a builder who could not carry a ruler could still say one beam was twice another. The Ratio Way brings that habit to the signature, so its proportions can be written, sent, and checked without the file itself.

Section 2: Math proof

Worked arithmetic: 12,841 / 11,553 = 1.1115; 8,222 / 3,764 = 2.1844; 37,277 / 1,572,864 = 0.0237. The demo of every record prints the same total as its math proof, so a fresh run of any demo verifies its proof exactly.

Section 3: Science proof

ratio = part / other part. In plain words, this equation turns two counted quantities into one checkable number: with 12,841 over 11,553, the arithmetic gives 1.1115, and every record's demo prints that same kind of value, so the claim can be checked by arithmetic alone.
