History
Keeping the books is one of the oldest crafts of trade: merchants wrote down what came in and what went out, so that at the end of the day the money could be counted against the writing. From single lists grew the double-entry book, where every entry has its match, so that the books can be proved balanced and a missing entry shows itself. Accounting is the reading of those books — turning the entries into statements that say how a business stands. This database is built on that public practice of writing every transaction down once, in order, with its proof beside it.
