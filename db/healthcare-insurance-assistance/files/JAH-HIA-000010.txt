A provider network is the list of doctors, hospitals, and clinics that have agreed to care for a policyholder under the terms of a policy, written in the record with each provider named, so that which providers belong to the policy can be checked. The list is copied from the insurer as published, because a network written from memory may name a provider who has left it and the cover would then be read wrong. History
Health insurance has been sold for more than a century, and the policies that carry it have grown long and hard to read, with premiums, deductibles, copayments, networks, and exclusions written in terms that few policyholders know by heart. Assistance grew beside the policies: clerks, advisers, and public helpers who read the policy aloud, filled in the forms, and wrote down what was asked and what was answered. From the paper form filed in a drawer to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the help down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every act of healthcare insurance assistance true.
