# Dictionary entries — The Expense Tracking Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## expense

Money spent, or a cost incurred, for a stated purpose, recorded with its date, its amount, and its proof, so that the spending can be counted and checked.

## receipt

The written proof that a cost happened, showing what was bought, when, and for how much, kept with the expense it proves, so the cost can be shown, not merely claimed.

## category

One named purpose for spending, such as travel or supplies, so that costs of the same kind sit together and their total can be counted and compared.

## expense report

A written list of costs for a stated period, each with its date, its purpose, and its proof, added up and signed, so the period's spending can be checked as a whole.

## budget

A written plan of how much may be spent, on what, and over what period, kept beside the record of what was actually spent, so the plan and the truth can be compared.

## reimbursement

Paying back a person who spent their own money for the business, on the proof of the receipts, so the person is made whole and the cost is recorded once.

## petty cash

A small kept sum for small costs, recorded as it is spent, so that little costs are written down like big ones and none vanish for being small.

## recurring expense

A cost that returns on a regular pattern, such as a monthly charge, recorded each time it occurs, so the pattern can be seen and the total counted.

## per diem

A fixed daily amount allowed for costs while travelling, recorded by day, so that travel costs are counted by the day and not by guesswork.

## approval

The recorded agreement of the person who may allow a cost, given before the money is spent or after the proof is seen, and written beside the expense it allows.

## audit trail

The ordered chain of records from a cost to its proof to its approval, kept whole, so that any cost can be followed back to its beginning and checked.

## spending pattern

The shape of spending over time, read from the records in order, showing which purposes cost most and when, so the pattern can be judged and changed.
