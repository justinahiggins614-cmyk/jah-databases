# Dictionary entries — The Train Booking Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## train booking

The written record of a seat held on a train, naming the passenger, the train, and the fare, kept as the single true record of the booking. It is a record only: no live booking and no payment happens here.

## passenger

The person named on a booking as the one who will travel, recorded with the name written exactly as it stands on their travel document.

## journey

One travel from a departure station to an arrival station, named with its places and times, so that the booking can be read against the journey it rests on.

## train

One scheduled service on a railway, named by its number or its name, its stations, and its times, so that the booking can be read against the train it rests on.

## seat

The place on a train held by a booking, named by its carriage and its number, so that the passenger's place can be found and no seat is booked twice.

## carriage

One coach of a train, named by its letter or number, so that the seat held can be found among the coaches of the train.

## fare

The price stated for a train booking, recorded with its amount and what it covers, so that the cost of each booking can be counted and checked. No payment is taken here; the fare is a recorded figure only.

## departure station

The station a train leaves from, written on the booking, so that the start of the journey can be read from the record.

## arrival station

The station a train arrives at, written on the booking, so that the end of the journey can be read from the record.

## booking reference

The one code given to a booking when it is made, written on every message about it, so that the booking can be found in the record at any moment.

## ticket

The written proof of a booking, naming the passenger, the train, and the fare, kept with the booking record, so that the seat can be shown when asked.

## timetable

The written list of a railway's trains, with their stations and times, kept beside the booking, so that the times in the record can be checked against the trains as scheduled.
