1. Description

An integral calculator integrates functions by the power rule for integrals, and gives the area under a curve between two points. Type a function, and it shows the antiderivative, the substitution and the area. Everything runs in this browser; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

The integral of x squared is x cubed over 3: from 0 to 3 it is 9. The integral of x + 1 from 0 to 2 is 4, and the integral of 2x from 1 to 3 is 8. The area under y = x from 0 to 4 is 8, and under y = x squared from 0 to 2 it is 2.67. The integral of x from -1 to 1 is 0, because equal areas above and below cancel.

3. Science proof

An integral works because area can be added up: the antiderivative reverses differentiation, and a definite integral is the antiderivative at the top minus the bottom. Rectangles approximate the same area from below: 4 rectangles under y = x from 0 to 4 give 6, close to the true 8. That is why the calculator shows the antiderivative first, then the substitution.
