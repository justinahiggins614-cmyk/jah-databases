ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS: • On November 14, 2004, during training ~100 miles southwest of San Diego, the USS Princeton (CG-59) had been tracking unusual radar contacts for two weeks - objects appearing at 80,000 feet, descending rapidly to 20,000 feet, and hovering. • Commander David Fravor and Lt. Commander Alex Dietrich were vectored to investigate; Fravor reported a white, featureless, oval object about 40 feet long hovering over a disturbed patch of ocean, with no wings, tail fins, or exhaust plume. • Fravor said the object mirrored his descending spiral, then accelerated away; the Princeton's radar reacquired it 60 miles away less than a minute later. • A second Nimitz aircrew, including Lt. Commander Chad Underwood - whose fighter carried a FLIR infrared camera - recorded the now-famous 'FLIR1' video; Underwood coined the 'Tic Tac' nickname. The Pentagon released the video in 2017; Fravor testified about the incident before the House Oversight Committee on July 26, 2023. NAMED WITNESS REPORTS: • David Fravor (2004) — Retired U.S. Navy Commander and Black Aces squadron CO; visually observed the 40-foot white Tic Tac-like object for about five minutes and described its extraordinary maneuvers to '60 Minutes' and Congress. • Alex Dietrich (2004) — Retired U.S. Navy Lt. Commander; Fravor's wingman and corroborating eyewitness to the Tic Tac encounter. • Chad Underwood (2004) — U.S. Navy Commander; recorded the FLIR1 infrared video of the Tic Tac and coined the 'Tic Tac' description.
