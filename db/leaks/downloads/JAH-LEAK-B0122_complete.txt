ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • On June 24, 1947, private pilot Kenneth Arnold reported nine shiny objects flying past Mount Rainier, estimating their speed at a minimum of 1,200 mph (1,932 km/h) - the first post-WWII sighting to get nationwide news coverage.
  • Arnold described their motion as like 'a saucer if you skip it across water'; newspapers mangled this into the objects being shaped like saucers, coining the terms 'flying saucer' and 'flying disc'.
  • The report triggered the 1947 flying disc craze, with hundreds of sightings reported across the U.S. over the following two to three weeks.
  • The military said it had been conducting no test flights during the time of Arnold's sighting, though Arnold himself first suspected experimental military aircraft.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Kenneth Arnold (1947) — Experienced pilot and businessman; saw nine crescent-shaped objects weaving in formation near Mount Rainier and clocked them traveling about 50 miles in 1.5 minutes.
  • Unnamed prospector (1947) — A prospector on Mt. Adams reportedly saw the objects at around the same time as Arnold, noted in contemporary press coverage.

PUBLIC INVESTIGATIONS:
  • Project Sign / Project Grudge / Project Blue Book — U.S. Air Force, 1947-1969: Blue Book, the successor project, analyzed 12,618 cases (1952-1969) with 701 remaining unidentified; concluded no evidence of extraterrestrial vehicles or a national security threat.
