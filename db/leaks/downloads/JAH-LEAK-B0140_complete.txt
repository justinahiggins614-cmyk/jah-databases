ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • Erik Ludvigsen Pontoppidan's 'Natural History of Norway' (1752–53; English translation 1755) described the kraken as the world's largest animal, roughly 2.5 km in circumference, easily mistaken for an island.
  • The earliest written reference appears in Christen Jensøn's Norwegian glossary (1646).
  • On November 30, 1861, near the Canary Islands, the French aviso Alecton encountered a dying giant squid floating at the surface; Lt. Frédéric-Marie Bouyer filed an official report to the French Ministry of the Marine, and ensign Émile Rodriguez made a contemporaneous sketch.
  • In 1857, Japetus Steenstrup formally described the giant squid as Architeuthis dux — the real animal behind the legend.
  • Modern giant squid reach about 10.5 m (34 ft) — among the largest invertebrates on Earth, but a fraction of the kraken of legend.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Lt. Frédéric-Marie Bouyer (November 30, 1861) — Filed an official report on the Alecton crew's encounter with a giant squid near the Canary Islands
