ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • On July 8, 1947, the Roswell Army Air Field issued a press release announcing possession of a 'flying disc' recovered from a ranch near Roswell, New Mexico; the claim was retracted within a day.
  • The debris came from a complex uncrewed military balloon train operated from Alamogordo Army Air Field as part of the top-secret Project Mogul - a program to detect Soviet nuclear tests via sound waves in the upper atmosphere; specifically NYU Flight 4, launched June 4, 1947.
  • In the 1990s the U.S. Air Force published official reports (1994 and 1997) concluding the recovered material was consistent with a Project Mogul balloon, not a UFO.
  • The 1997 report ('Case Closed') concluded the 'alien body' accounts likely reflected witnesses misremembering anthropomorphic dummies used in 1950s high-altitude parachute tests.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • William 'Mac' Brazel (1947) — Rancher who found the metallic and rubber debris on his land and reported it, triggering the Army's involvement.
  • Jesse Marcel (1978) — Retired USAF Major; in 1978 claimed the Army's weather-balloon story was a cover and speculated the debris was extraterrestrial - the claim that revived the Roswell legend via the 1980 book 'The Roswell Incident'.

PUBLIC INVESTIGATIONS:
  • The Roswell Report — U.S. Air Force, 1994 and 1997: Debris was consistent with a Project Mogul balloon train; the 'bodies' accounts traced to misremembered 1950s anthropomorphic dummy tests. No evidence of extraterrestrial craft.
