ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • On August 15, 1977, Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope - a SETI instrument that could observe any given sky point for only 72 seconds as Earth rotated - detected a strong narrowband radio signal that filled the entire observation window.
  • Astronomer Jerry Ehman, reviewing printouts days later, circled the alphanumeric sequence '6EQUJ5' and wrote 'Wow!' in red pen, giving the signal its name.
  • The signal arrived at about 1420.4056 MHz, near the hydrogen line (21-cm) frequency proposed since 1959 as a logical interstellar broadcast band, and was 30 standard deviations above the background noise.
  • The signal never repeated despite decades of follow-up listening; its origin remains unexplained. A 2017 hypothesis (Antonio Paris) proposed hydrogen clouds from comets 266P/Christensen and 335P/Gibbs, but it remains disputed and unconfirmed.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Jerry Ehman (1977) — Ohio State astronomer who found the 72-second narrowband printout at Big Ear, circled it, and wrote 'Wow!'; he later concluded an extraterrestrial intelligence 'might' have sent it but remained cautious.
