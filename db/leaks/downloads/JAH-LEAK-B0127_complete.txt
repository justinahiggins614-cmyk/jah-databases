ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • Albert K. Bender founded the International Flying Saucer Bureau (IFSB), the first major civilian UFO club, in 1952, and abruptly shut it down in 1953 after claiming he was visited by three men in black who ordered him to stop publishing about flying saucers.
  • Gray Barker's 1956 book 'They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers' was the first to allege that 'Men in Black' cover up the existence of flying saucers; Barker recounted Bender's encounters and described the figures traveling in threes, wearing black suits and driving large black cars.
  • Bender did not tell his own full story for nine years; his 1962 book 'Flying Saucers and the Three Men' (co-published with Barker) proposed the visitors were themselves extraterrestrials.
  • The MIB legend grew from these 1950s accounts and became a staple of UFO folklore and later film.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Albert K. Bender (1953) — Founder of the IFSB; claimed in 1953 that three men in dark clothes visited him, communicated telepathically, and ordered him to dissolve his UFO organization.
  • Gray Barker (1956) — West Virginia writer and IFSB associate; published the first book describing the Men in Black and popularized the legend.
