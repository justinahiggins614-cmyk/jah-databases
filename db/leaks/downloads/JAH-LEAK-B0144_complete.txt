ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • Roy Chapman Andrews recorded the first Western account in 'On the Trail of Ancient Man' (1926), from tales told at a gathering of Mongolian officials — none of those present had ever seen the creature.
  • Mongolian Prime Minister Damdinbazar described it in 1922 as 'shaped like a sausage about two feet long, has no head nor leg and it is so poisonous that merely to touch it means instant death' — he did not claim to have seen one.
  • Czech researcher Ivan Mackerle's private 1990 expedition into the Gobi used motorbikes, vibration traps, and small explosives — and found no specimen.
  • A 1954 Soviet expedition collected eyewitness accounts but no specimen; Russian paleontologist Ivan Yefremov wrote that he heard the legend many times in 1946–49 but nobody claimed to have seen it.
  • In 1983, locals shown a Tartar sand boa (Eryx tataricus) identified it as the olgoi-khorkhoi.
