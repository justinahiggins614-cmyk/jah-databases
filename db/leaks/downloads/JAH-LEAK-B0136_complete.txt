ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • Bulldozer operator Jerry Crew found 16-inch (41 cm) footprints around his equipment at Bluff Creek, Humboldt County, California, in 1958; he cast one and took it to the Humboldt Times.
  • Humboldt Times reporter Andrew Genzoli's front-page story of October 6, 1958, coined the name 'Bigfoot' for the track-maker.
  • The Patterson–Gimlin film, shot October 20, 1967, at Bluff Creek by Roger Patterson and Robert 'Bob' Gimlin, runs 59.5 seconds over 954 frames and remains the most famous alleged Bigfoot footage.
  • After Ray Wallace's death, his family revealed he had faked tracks with carved wooden feet — a posthumous hoax confession behind some early Bluff Creek prints.
  • The word 'Sasquatch' was coined by J.W. Burns in a 1929 Maclean's article, adapted from the Halq'emeylem word sasq'ets ('hairy man').

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Roger Patterson and Robert 'Bob' Gimlin (October 20, 1967) — Filmed a walking bipedal figure at Bluff Creek; Patterson claimed it was a live Bigfoot
  • Jerry Crew (1958) — Discovered and cast 16-inch footprints at Bluff Creek

PUBLIC INVESTIGATIONS:
  • FBI Laboratory hair analysis — Federal Bureau of Investigation, Scientific and Technical Services Division (Assistant Director Jay Cochran Jr.), at the request of Peter Byrne of the Bigfoot Information Center and Exhibition, ?: Microscopic examination concluded the hairs were 'of deer family origin.'
