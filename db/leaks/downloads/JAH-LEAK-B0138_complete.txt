ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • The first wave of attacks came in March 1995 in Canóvanas, Puerto Rico: eight sheep were found dead with three puncture wounds in the chest and reportedly drained of blood.
  • Madelyne Tolentino's August 1995 sighting in Canóvanas became the foundational description — a ~4-foot bipedal creature with spines down its back — and her sketch circulated worldwide.
  • The name 'chupacabras' ('goat-sucker' in Spanish) was coined by Puerto Rican comedian Silverio Pérez.
  • Genetic sequencing of the 2007 Cuero, Texas, carcass (Texas State University + UC Davis, 2010) identified it as a coyote/Mexican wolf hybrid with sarcoptic mange.
  • Benjamin Radford's 'Tracking the Chupacabra' (2011) argued Tolentino's description was influenced by the 1995 film 'Species,' released shortly before her sighting.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Madelyne Tolentino (August 1995) — Sighting of a ~4-foot bipedal creature with spinal ridges; her sketch defined the chupacabra
  • Devin McAnally (2004) — Shot the carcass known as the Elmendorf Beast in Texas
  • Phylis Canion (2007) — Recovered the Cuero, Texas, carcass that was later DNA-tested

PUBLIC INVESTIGATIONS:
  • Genetic analysis of the Cuero carcass — Texas State University / University of California, Davis, ?: The Cuero carcass was a coyote–Mexican wolf hybrid with sarcoptic mange — not an unknown species.
