ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • THEORETICAL: Physicist Freeman Dyson formalized the concept in his 1960 'Science' paper 'Search for Artificial Stellar Sources of Infra-Red Radiation', proposing that an advanced civilization could surround its star with structures to capture its energy.
  • Dyson imagined a swarm of independently orbiting asteroid-sized habitats (a 'Dyson swarm'), not a solid shell; the popular solid-sphere image came later from science fiction writers.
  • A full Dyson structure would intercept starlight and re-radiate waste heat as far-infrared radiation - a signature detectable from interstellar distances, which is why the idea matters to SETI searches.
  • The concept was inspired by Olaf Stapledon's 1937 novel 'Star Maker'; building one would require dismantling an entire planet for material, far beyond current human capabilities.
