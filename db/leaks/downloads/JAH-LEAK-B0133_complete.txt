ESTABLISHED FACTS & MEASUREMENTS:
  • Allied aircraft pilots in World War II reported unidentified flying objects or mysterious aerial phenomena called 'foo fighters', formally reported from November 1944 onwards over the European and Pacific theaters.
  • One November 1944 night, a 415th Night Fighter Squadron Bristol Beaufighter crew - pilot Edward Schlueter, radar observer Donald J. Meiers, and intelligence officer Fred Ringwald - reported 'eight to 10 bright orange lights off the left wing... flying through the air at high speed' over the Rhine north of Strasbourg; neither airborne radar nor ground control registered anything.
  • Meiers coined the name 'foo fighters' from the nonsense word 'foo' in Bill Holman's 'Smokey Stover' firefighter cartoon strip.
  • Witnesses initially assumed the lights were secret enemy weapons; post-war study found the phenomenon never showed up on radar and no enemy weapon matched the reports.

NAMED WITNESS REPORTS:
  • Edward Schlueter (1944) — 415th Night Fighter Squadron pilot; reported the November 1944 encounter with 8-10 bright orange lights off the Beaufighter's left wing over the Rhine.
  • Donald J. Meiers (1944) — Radar observer on the same Beaufighter crew; coined the term 'foo fighters' for the unexplained lights.
  • Fred Ringwald (1944) — Intelligence officer on the same Beaufighter crew; co-witness to the orange-light encounter over the Rhine.

PUBLIC INVESTIGATIONS:
  • Robertson Panel — CIA, 1953: Explored prosaic explanations for the WWII foo fighter reports, including electrostatic phenomena similar to St. Elmo's fire, electromagnetic phenomena, and reflections of light from ice crystals.
