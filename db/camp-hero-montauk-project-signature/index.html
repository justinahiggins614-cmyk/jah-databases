Signature design. Design detail. The teaching model shows three lessons that real degaussing also teaches. Students who run the model with different coil settings learn all three lessons from the numbers rather than from a lecture.

A Signature warning network model links four nodes, two radars, one control center, and one interceptor base, and shows how a contact report travels from detection to decision.

Signature design. Each node adds a time cost, and the model prints the total warning time for a contact, showing why communications matter as much as radar range. The demo runs one contact through the chain and prints each leg's time and the total.

Use and limits. Use this model to teach network thinking about defense systems. Do not use it as a working defense design, because its times are teaching values.

Design detail. The network model's lesson is that warning time is a chain, and a chain is only as fast as its slowest leg. The total at the end is the only number that matters to the defended area, because an aircraft is intercepted on the total, not on any single leg.

JAH-CHMPS-000012 — Signature Source Criticism Ladder

A Signature source criticism ladder ranks evidence from strongest to weakest: contemporary documents, physical traces, contemporary photographs, later testimony, and retellings, and a claim must cite the highest available rung.

Signature design. The ladder is used by placing each source on its rung and then reading the claim's support from the top down: if only the bottom rungs hold sources, the claim is marked weakly supported. The demo ranks five example sources and prints the claim's verdict.

Use and limits. Use this ladder for any historical claim. Do not use it as a substitute for archival research, because ranking sources is only the first step of checking them.

Design detail. The ladder's rungs are ordered by how close a source stands to the event it describes. Contemporary documents stand highest because they were written when the event happened and for purposes other than telling the story, physical traces stand next because they cannot be misremembered, contemporary photographs stand next because they show but do not explain, later testimony stands lower because memory changes, and retellings stand lowest because they repeat rather than witness. A claim supported only from the bottom rungs is not necessarily false, but it is weakly supported, and the ladder's verdict says exactly that, no more and no less.

JAH-CHMPS-000014 — Signature Claim Table Builder

A Signature claim table builder takes a list of claims and prints a four-column table: claim, evidence needed, evidence offered, and verdict, with unverified as the default verdict.

Signature design. The builder never upgrades a verdict without a source: a claim with testimony only stays unverified, and only a claim with a document or a physical trace moves to supported. The demo builds a table for three example claims and prints their verdicts.

Use and limits. Use this builder for any contested topic. Do not use it as a verdict on any real claim, because its example claims are teaching material.

Design detail. The claim table's default verdict of unverified is a discipline, not a judgment. A new claim starts unverified because no evidence has been entered for it yet, and it moves only when a source is entered in the evidence column. Testimony moves a claim to weakly supported at most, because testimony alone cannot carry a claim about physical events, while a document or a physical trace can move it to supported. The table keeps every claim's history, so that a claim once marked supported can be returned to unverified if its source is later shown to be unreliable, and the table never deletes a claim to hide a change of verdict.

JAH-CHMPS-000016 — Signature Layered Interpretation Sign

A Signature layered interpretation sign presents one site in three labeled bands: documented history, present-day park, and legend, with the legend band marked as story in its heading.

Signature design. The sign's rule is that no band borrows another band's authority: the legend band never cites the history band, and the history band never cites the legend band. The demo prints a sign for an example site with its three bands and their labels.

Use and limits. Use this sign design for any contested heritage site. Do not use it as a sign for any real site, because its example text is invented.

Design detail. The layered sign's bands are separated by color and by heading so that a visitor cannot mistake one band for another even at a glance. The history band uses a plain heading and past-tense verbs, the park band uses present-tense verbs and directions, and the legend band uses a heading that contains the word story, so that every sentence in it is read as story. The sign's rule about borrowing authority is enforced by its writers: a legend sentence may not cite a history document, and a history sentence may not cite a legend book, and any draft that breaks the rule is rewritten before the sign is made.
