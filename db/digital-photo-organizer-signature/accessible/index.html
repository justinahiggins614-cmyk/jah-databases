    The digital photo organizer helps a person sort pictures into albums worth keeping, in plain words, with each album named by its subject, its time, and its place. Every album shown is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind of album, never a real picture of a real person. A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Garden Album, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Albums, in the Signature edition.

    History: Sorting pictures is an old habit: prints in a box, pages in an album, a name written on the back of each one. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by subject and a plain name for every album. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

    Proposed solution: Add a Signature tournament bracket with rounds, divisions, and a season; Add a Signature creator studio so visitors can build a new photo album; Add a Signature review step so every new photo album is checked before it is published; Add the Signature photo album in many languages, with the same rules in each; Add a Signature offline pack so the photo album works with no connection.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a list of generic kinds of albums, a clearly labeled sample album for each kind, a search box, and a review step before publishing. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex photo albums open.

    Verification path: A tournament runs a full season, a creator publishes a new photo album through the review step, and the offline pack is tried with no connection. The demo program prints the same total as the math proof, and every step is checked by a second reader.

    Science proof: total = count x each. In plain words, this equation turns counted quantities into one checkable number: with 23 items at 1 each, the arithmetic gives 23 records, and the demo prints that same total, so the claim can be checked by arithmetic alone.
