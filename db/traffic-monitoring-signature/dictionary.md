# Dictionary entries — The Traffic Monitoring Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## traffic monitoring

The watching and recording of vehicles on roads, with their numbers, their speeds, and their times, so that the state of the roads can be read as it is.

## traffic flow

The number of vehicles passing a point on a road in a stated time, written in the record, so that how busy the road is can be counted and compared.

## traffic count

One counted number of vehicles at a place and time, recorded with its place and its time, so that the count can be checked and repeated.

## congestion

The state of a road where vehicles move slowly because there are too many of them, recorded with the place, the time, and the speed, so that the slowing can be read plainly.

## traffic light

A signal at a road junction that tells traffic when to stop and when to go, named in the record with its place, so that the signals on a monitored road can be read.

## sensor

A device that counts or measures vehicles on a road, named in the record with its place and its kind, so that where each reading came from can be read.

## speed

How fast vehicles travel on a road, written in the record with its place and its time, so that the speed of the traffic can be read and compared.

## road junction

A place where two or more roads meet, named in the record, so that the traffic at the meeting of roads can be read apart from the traffic on the roads themselves.

## peak hour

The hour when the most vehicles use a road, written in the record with its time, so that the busiest hour can be read and compared with other hours.

## monitoring station

A fixed place where traffic is watched and recorded, named in the record, so that every reading can be traced to the station that took it.

## traffic report

The written account of the state of traffic on a road at a time, kept with the monitoring record, so that what was seen can be read after the fact.

## road network

All the roads of an area taken together, named in the record, so that one road's readings can be read against the whole network they belong to.
