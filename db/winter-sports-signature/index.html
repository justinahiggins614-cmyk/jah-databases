Alpine Skiing Rank 1 · simple · skiing · beginner

Signature alpine skiing is taught as turn shape before speed. A student learns to stop, then to turn, then to link turns, on a gentle slope, before any steep run, because speed control lives in the shape of the turn, not in braking. Our instructors teach carving as a quiet edge change, and they film students from the side so each skier can see whether their skis are truly parallel. Racing comes only when linked turns are reliable, and the hall's praise goes to the skier whose turns are round and calm at any speed.

Training

Sessions pair stopping drills, single turns, linked turns, and video review from the side.

Techniques

Signature basics are stopping, turning, linking turns, and carving through edge change.

Safety

Helmets are always worn, bindings are checked, and off-piste skiing requires avalanche training.

History

The Signature school follows alpine skiing from Alpine travel to resort sport and Olympic racing.

Working demo program

DAYS = [('Monday stopping and turning', 90), ('Wednesday linked turns', 90), ('Friday carving drills', 90), ('Sunday guided slope skiing', 120)]
print('Signature alpine skiing learning week')
total = sum(m for _, m in DAYS)
best = max(DAYS, key=lambda d: d[1])
for day, m in DAYS:
    print('%s: %d minutes' % (day, m))
print('Weekly total: %d minutes' % total)
print('Daily average: %.1f minutes' % (total / len(DAYS)))
print('Longest session: %s (%d minutes)' % (best[0], best[1]))

Cross-Country Skiing Rank 2 · simple · skiing · beginner

Signature cross-country skiing is taught as glide before effort. A student learns to balance on one ski and glide, then to add the pole plant, then to link strides, because a skier who cannot glide wastes every push. Our programme teaches classic technique first, since the track guides the ski, and skate technique only when balance is reliable. Waxing is taught as a practical skill from the first month, and long skis build slowly, adding distance week by week. The hall's praise goes to the skier whose stride looks effortless after an hour, not the skier who starts fastest.

Training

Sessions pair balance and glide drills, classic technique, skate technique, and gradually longer skis.

Techniques

Signature basics are the glide, the pole plant, the diagonal stride, and double poling.

Safety

Layers and weather checks are standard, skiers carry a phone and food on long outings, and groups stay together.

History

The Signature school follows the sport from Scandinavian winter travel to Olympic racing.

Working demo program

DIST_KM = 5.0
MINUTES = 30.0
print('Signature cross-country pace calculator')
pace = MINUTES / DIST_KM

print('Distance: %.2f km in %.1f minutes' % (DIST_KM, MINUTES))
print('Pace: %.2f minutes per km' % pace)
print('Average speed: %.2f km per hour' % speed)

Ski Jumping Rank 3 · simple · skiing · beginner

Signature ski jumping is taught as a landing before a flight. A student practises the telemark landing on flat ground, then from small jumps, and only then on a small hill, because a jump is only as good as its landing. Our coaches teach the inrun crouch as stillness, not speed, and the take-off as a forward lean timed to the table's edge. Flight position is built in stages, and hill size rises only when landings are consistently clean. The hall's praise goes to the jumper whose landing is quiet and balanced, whatever the distance.

Training

Sessions pair telemark landing drills, inrun crouch practice, small-hill jumps, and video review.

Techniques

Signature basics are the crouch, the take-off lean, the V-style flight, and the telemark landing.

Safety

Hill size rises only when landings are clean, and coaches supervise every jump.

History

The Signature school follows ski jumping from nineteenth-century Norway to the Four Hills Tournament.

A student learns to stop on each edge, then to link turns, then to carve, on a gentle slope, before any park feature is attempted, because every trick rests on edge control. Our instructors teach falling safely, with wrists protected and arms tucked, as a first lesson, so that falls stop being frightening. Park features are progressed by size, from small boxes to jumps, and only when riding is reliable. The hall's praise goes to the rider whose turns are smooth and whose landings are quiet.

Training

Techniques

Safety

Helmets and wrist guards are always worn, falling safely is taught first, and park features grow by size.

History

Biathlon Rank 5 · simple · skiing · beginner

Signature biathlon is taught as stillness inside speed. A student learns to ski, then to shoot, then to combine them, and the range routine is drilled until it survives a racing heart: ski in, settle, breathe out, shoot, leave. Our programme teaches shooting on the range only, with strict handling rules, and penalty loops are practised as part of racing, not as punishment. Breathing control is trained deliberately, since the last breath before a shot decides it. The hall's praise goes to the athlete who shoots clean after skiing hard, because that is the whole sport in one moment.

Training

Sessions pair ski technique, range routine drills, breathing control, and combined ski-and-shoot sessions.

Techniques

Signature basics are the range routine, breathing out before the shot, and steady trigger control.

Safety

Rifles are handled only on the range under supervision, and safety rules are taught before any shooting.

History

The Signature school follows biathlon from military ski patrols to Olympic competition.

Head-first position is built gradually, with neck strength work, and the hall's praise goes to the slider whose dive is quiet and whose run is calm.

Training

Short-Track Speed Skating Rank 10 · compounded · skating · intermediate

Signature short-track is taught as pack awareness before passing. A student learns to skate in a group, to hold a line through corners, and to read when a gap opens, before any passing is attempted, because a pass attempted blind is a fall for two skaters. Our sessions drill relay exchanges by push until the exchange is smooth at speed, and disqualification rules are taught as tactics: knowing what is illegal keeps a skater in the race. The hall's praise goes to the skater who passes cleanly and leaves room, not the skater who forces a gap.

Training

Sessions pair pack skating, cornering drills, relay exchange practice, and passing drills on open gaps.

Techniques

Signature basics are pack lines, deep cornering with hand-to-ice balance, drafting, and clean passing.

Safety

Helmets, gloves, and cut-resistant suits are standard, and rink padding protects falls.

History

The Signature school follows short-track from small-rink pack racing to Olympic competition.

Falls are treated as information, not failure, and the hall's praise goes to the skater whose programme flows as one piece, not as a list of tricks.

Training

Techniques

Signature basics are edge holding, clean turns, upright spins, and then jumps, in that order.

Safety

Curling Rank 12 · complex · snow · advanced

Signature curling is taught as weight before strategy. A student learns to slide and release a stone with consistent weight, then to sweep, then to call simple shots, because strategy built on unreliable weight is only guessing. Our programme teaches sweeping as judgement: sweepers watch the stone's line and speed and decide together, rather than sweeping on every shot. The skip's role is taught as communication, not command, and the hall's praise goes to the team whose stones finish where they were called, end after end.

Training

Sessions pair slide delivery drills, weight practice, sweeping judgement, and simple called shots.

Techniques

Signature basics are the slide, the release with turn, sweeping, and the draw shot.

Safety

Sliders are worn correctly, stones are handled with care, and players keep clear of moving stones.

History

The Signature school follows curling from Scottish lochs to Olympic competition.

Navigation and layers are taught from the first outing, and the hall's praise goes to the walker who finishes warm, dry, and unhurried, whatever the distance.

Training
