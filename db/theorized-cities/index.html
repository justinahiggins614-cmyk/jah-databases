Utopia Rank 1 · simple · beginner

Utopia is the island city of Thomas More's book Utopia, published in 1516, a philosophical work that imagines a society organized by reason, with shared goods, planned towns, and a capital, Amaurot. The book is a work of political philosophy and satire, not a building plan, and no Utopian city was built from it. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label, with source Thomas More, 1516, and status philosophical proposal. It cross-links the existing utopia database, which is not rebuilt here. Its districts are the capital, the planned towns, the farms, and the harbor. As a blueprint it is a planned-town model: a planned town can be built, but Utopia itself remains a philosophical design.

Arcosanti Rank 3 · simple · beginner

Arcosanti is an experimental town in Arizona, begun in 1970 from the designs of architect Paolo Soleri, built as an arcology: architecture joined with ecology, a dense, compact town meant to use less land and energy than a sprawling city. Unlike most records in this pair, Arcosanti is partially built and still inhabited and worked on, so its status is partially built and ongoing, not merely proposed. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label with that status stated plainly: the built portion is real, and the full planned city is not. Its districts are the built foundry and studios, the housing arcs, the public spaces, and the unbuilt planned arcs. Its technology level is Level 2, a prototype district, because a district exists and is tested, while the full arcology remains Level 3 work.

Broadacre City is Frank Lloyd Wright's proposal from the 1930s for a decentralized American city: one acre per family, homes spread across the countryside, connected by roads and cars, with small markets and civic centers instead of a dense downtown. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label, with source Frank Lloyd Wright, 1930s, and status proposed, never built as a whole. Its districts are the farmsteads, the market centers, the civic centers, and the road network. As a blueprint it is a low-density settlement model: its parts can be built, but the whole remains a proposal.

Garden City Rank 5 · simple · beginner

Garden City is Ebenezer Howard's proposal from 1898, in To-Morrow: A Peaceful Path to Real Reform, for planned towns that join town and country: a compact town ringed by farms and greenbelt, with homes, workshops, gardens, and a civic center. Unlike a purely paper proposal, Garden City was implemented in part: Letchworth Garden City and Welwyn Garden City in England were built on its principles, though neither matches the full diagram. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label with that status stated: implemented in part. Its technology level is Level 2, a prototype district, because built garden towns exist and can be studied, while the full diagram remains a proposal. Its districts are the civic center, the residential rings, the industrial edge, and the greenbelt.

It was never built as a whole, though its tower-in-park ideas influenced later housing estates. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label, with source Le Corbusier, 1930, and status proposed, never built as a whole. Its districts are the housing towers, the business district, the civic center, and the parkland and transport network. As a blueprint it is a tower-city model: towers in parkland can be built, but the whole ordered city remains a proposal.

Motopia Rank 8 · compounded · intermediate

Motopia is Geoffrey Jellicoe's proposal from 1961 for a city built for the motor age: homes and gardens on roof decks above roads, with cars parked beneath the living level and pedestrians kept apart from traffic. It was never built. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label, with source Geoffrey Jellicoe, 1961, and status proposed, never built. Its districts are the roof-garden homes, the road and parking levels, the civic decks, and the green approaches. As a blueprint it is a deck-city model: deck housing can be built, but the whole motor city remains a proposal.

Venus Project Rank 10 · compounded · intermediate

The Venus Project is Jacque Fresco's proposal for a resource-based economy and its cities: circular cities with a central dome, rings of housing and services, automated production, and high technology, designed to end scarcity. It has been presented in designs, models, and a research center in Florida, but no Venus Project city has been built. This record carries the THEORIZED/SPECULATIVE label, with source Jacque Fresco, and status proposed, never built as a city.
