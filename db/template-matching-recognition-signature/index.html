JAH-KW-TMPL-000101 — JAH's agreement ladder — three gates, climbed in order

This is JAH's own decision discipline for template matching, and it is deliberately a ladder rather than a single bar. Most matchers set one threshold and live with its mistakes; JAH's agreement ladder makes the candidate earn the decision three times. Rung 1 is suspicion: is the peak even worth examining (0.5)? Rung 2 is strength: is the agreement genuinely strong (0.8)? Rung 3 is uniqueness: is it clearly better than everything else (margin 0.25)? A candidate can fall off at any rung, and falling off early is cheap — the ladder quits examining losers.

The ordering is the invention. Strength before uniqueness would waste fine measurements on ambiguous scenes; uniqueness before strength would crown the best of a bad lot. JAH's order — suspicion, then strength, then uniqueness — spends precision only where it matters and refuses to decide whenever any rung fails. It is the two-gate rule of the public database, split into three rungs with an escape hatch at each.

The demo runs three scenes up the ladder: a true pattern climbs all three rungs (FOUND), a half-right distractor falls at rung 2 (strength), and a scene with two near-identical candidates falls at rung 3 (uniqueness). Three scenes, three stopping rungs, one honest ladder. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic images.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
NCC response maps; the ladder is JAH's decision discipline over any score.
The recipe
Rung 1: peak >= 0.5 (worth a closer look). Rung 2: peak >= 0.8 (strong agreement). Rung 3: peak - runner-up >= 0.25 (clearly the best). Fall off any rung and the verdict is NOT FOUND.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff the candidate climbs all three rungs. JAH's rule: a decision must survive suspicion, strength, AND uniqueness — in that order.

line-finder, corner-finder, cat-finder, face-finder

def convolve(image, kernel):
    kh, kw = len(kernel), len(kernel[0])
    rows, cols = len(image), len(image[0])
    out = []
    for i in range(rows - kh + 1):
        r = []
        for j in range(cols - kw + 1):
            s = 0
            for a in range(kh):
                for b in range(kw):
                    s += kernel[a][b] * image[i + a][j + b]
            r.append(s)
        out.append(r)
    return out

JAH-KW-TMPL-000102 — The mirror check — JAH's symmetry detector

JAH's mirror check starts from a practical annoyance: some patterns look almost the same flipped — a diamond, a square, a face — and a matcher that only runs the upright template cannot tell whether it found the pattern or its mirror image. His fix is to run both as rivals: score the scene with the template and with its horizontal flip, and read the gap. A clear winner means the pattern has a handedness and you just learned which way it faces; a tie means the pattern is symmetric, which is itself a useful fact about the object.

The check doubles as an orientation sensor. An arrow pointing right scores 1.0 with the right-template and 0.3 with the left-template — the mirror gap of 0.7 not only confirms the detection but reports the direction. JAH's rule turns the template bank's weakness (which variant matched?) into a feature (the winning variant IS the orientation measurement).

The demo runs the mirror check on an asymmetric arrow and a symmetric diamond. Arrow: T scores 1.00, mirror 0.31, gap 0.69 — ORIENTED, facing right. Diamond: T scores 1.00, mirror 1.00, gap 0.00 — SYMMETRIC, no facing to report. Two patterns, two verdicts, one extra template. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
NCC maps for the template and its mirror; the comparison is JAH's.
The recipe
Score the scene with T and with mirror(T). If T wins clearly, the pattern is oriented; if they tie, the pattern is symmetric — and symmetry is reported as a finding, not a failure.
The knowing-when rule
ORIENTED iff score(T) - score(mirror(T)) >= 0.2 at the peak; SYMMETRIC iff the gap < 0.05. The mirror is a second opinion that turns ties into information.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
corner-finder, face-finder, car-finder, arrow-finder

JAH-KW-TMPL-000103 — The ninety-percent knowing rule — JAH's bar

The public database sets its absolute bar at 0.8; JAH sets his at 0.9, and he has reasons. At 0.9 normalized correlation, the patch is the template up to brightness and a whisper of noise — unmistakable. Between 0.8 and 0.9 live the lookalikes: the similar-but-wrong, the half-occluded, the wishful. JAH's ninety-percent rule draws the line at unmistakable and lets the merely-likely wait for better evidence. It is a bar for decisions that matter — faces, identities, anything where a false FOUND costs more than a missed one.

The rule is deliberately asymmetric about error types. A missed detection can be retried next frame, with a better template, after cleaning the image; a false detection sends the system acting on a lie. JAH prices falsehoods above misses, so the bar sits high. For low-stakes matching — counting texture specks, say — he happily drops to 0.7; the ninety-percent rule is for when the decision has consequences.

The demo scores three scenes: a perfect copy (1.00, FOUND), a slightly noisy copy (0.91, FOUND — just over), and a lookalike (0.85, NOT FOUND). The 0.85 stings — it looks close — and the rule rejects it anyway. That sting is the rule working: 'close' is not 'known'. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic images.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
NCC scores; the 0.9 bar is JAH's chosen strictness for high-stakes matching.
The recipe
Set TAU_ABS = 0.9 — nine-tenths structural agreement. Anything less is NOT FOUND, no matter how good the margin. Reserve the strict bar for decisions that matter.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff peak >= 0.9 (plus the margin gate). JAH's reasoning: at 0.9 the pattern is unmistakable; between 0.8 and 0.9 it is merely likely, and 'likely' is not 'known'.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
face-finder, cat-finder, dog-finder, car-finder

JAH-KW-TMPL-000104 — The patience window — JAH's best-of-N frames

A single frame can lie — a noise spike lands on the pattern at the wrong moment, or a shadow eats it at the right one. JAH's patience window refuses to judge a scene by one frame: it watches N frames, scores each, and decides on the best. The logic is temporal margin, the same idea as the runner-up margin stretched across time: a true pattern repeats its evidence, noise does not. If the best of three frames cannot clear the bar, the pattern is not there.

The window length is the patience parameter, and JAH tunes it to the scene's rhythm: 3 frames for a still camera, more for a shaking one, fewer when latency matters. Too long a window and a passing pattern lingers in the verdict after it leaves; too short and the patience buys nothing. Three is his default — enough for noise to decorrelate, short enough to stay honest about motion.

The demo builds three noisy frames of one scene: the pattern scores 0.72 (shadow frame), 0.93 (clean frame), 0.68 (noise frame). Frame-by-frame judging with a 0.8 bar would say no, YES, no — flickering. The patience window takes the max, 0.93, and decides once: FOUND. The matcher waited for the evidence's best moment instead of judging its worst. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
NCC scores across a short frame window; the window length is JAH's patience parameter.
The recipe
Collect the peak score over N consecutive frames (here 3); decide on the maximum. A pattern that is truly present will show itself clearly in at least one frame; noise will not repeat.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff max-over-window >= bar: the decision waits for the evidence's best moment instead of judging its worst. Patience is temporal margin.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
face-finder, cat-finder, pedestrian-finder, car-finder

JAH-KW-TMPL-000105 — Edge-first agreement — JAH's two-domain rule

JAH's edge-first agreement treats pixels and edges as two independent witnesses that must corroborate each other. A bright blob can fake a high pixel correlation — brightness is easy to counterfeit — but it cannot fake the edge map, where only shape survives. Run the matcher in both domains; a FOUND needs both witnesses to point at the same location. One witness disagreeing is reasonable doubt, and the verdict is NOT FOUND.

The edge bar is deliberately lower (0.6 vs 0.8) because edge maps are sparse — accidental agreement is rarer, so less agreement is more meaningful. The location agreement is strict: same peak coordinates, not just 'somewhere nearby'. JAH's reasoning is legalistic on purpose: two witnesses, same story, same place, or no conviction.

The demo stages the attack: a scene with a bright blob (no X structure) where the true pattern should be. Raw pixel correlation: 0.99 of the true pattern's self-score — fooled, above the bar. Edge agreement at the same spot: 0.44 — the blob's square outline shares less than half the X's diagonal edges, witness two dissents. Verdict: NOT FOUND. Then the true patterned scene: pixels 1.00, edges 1.00 — FOUND, both witnesses agree. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic images.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Pixel NCC and edge-map NCC in parallel; agreement is JAH's confirmation.
The recipe
Match on pixels AND on the edge map independently. FOUND only if both domains clear their bars at the same location. Two witnesses, one verdict.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff (pixel_peak >= 0.8) AND (edge_peak >= 0.6) AND (same location): the shape must agree with itself in two independent descriptions.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
face-finder, car-finder, house-finder, box-finder

JAH-KW-TMPL-000106 — The corner census — JAH's structural pre-check

JAH's corner census is a bouncer for the matcher: before any expensive pixel comparison, count the corners. A template with 4 corners can only match a patch with 4 corners — so count the patch's corners first (cheap: edge pixels with perpendicular neighbors, the record-2 trick of the feature-hierarchy database) and skip the NCC entirely where the census disagrees. In the demo scene, 40 candidate patches shrink to 4 before a single correlation is computed.

The census is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one — passing it earns an examination, not a verdict. That is its strength: it is allowed to be crude, because the full gates still have the final word. False rejections are the only danger, so JAH counts generously (a wobbly corner still counts) and lets the strict gates downstream do the rejecting. Cheap filter, expensive decider, each doing what it is good at.

The demo builds a scene with one true square (4 corners), one triangle (3), one pentagon (5), and blank patches (0). The template's census is 4. Patches counted: only the square's patch passes the census; the rest are skipped without matching. Then NCC runs on the survivor: 1.00, FOUND. Structure first, pixels second — JAH's ordering. Signature-original, demonstrated on synthetic images.

JAH-KW-TMPL-000107 — The negative template — JAH's veto pattern

Every target has an evil twin: the pattern most likely to fool its matcher. For a face template it is a face-like blob of shadows; for JAH's arrow it is the arrow's own shaft without the head. His negative template names the twin explicitly and matches it alongside the target — and if the twin outscores the target, the detection is vetoed on the spot. It is a second matcher whose only job is to say 'you found ME instead'.

The veto inverts the usual threshold logic. Instead of asking 'is the target score high enough?', it asks 'is the target score higher than the most dangerous alternative?' — a comparative gate like the runner-up margin, but against a designed adversary rather than the scene's accidents. JAH's reasoning: false alarms have shapes, and the best defense against a shaped false alarm is to look for it directly.

The demo sets an X-target against a +-shaped negative (the classic confuser: similar energy, wrong structure). On the true X scene: target 1.00, negative 0.42 — FOUND. On the decoy +-scene: target 0.71 (above the 0.7 bar — the plain gate would fire!), negative 0.95 — VETO. The plain gate would have reported a false FOUND; the negative template caught the twin. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
NCC maps for both the target and the negative template; the veto compares them.

The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff (target passes its gates) AND (target_peak > negative_peak): the detection must beat its own evil twin. The veto is absolute.
