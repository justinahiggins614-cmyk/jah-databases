JAH-KW-CHAM-000101 — JAH's closeness budget — distance as money

The public gate averages per edge; JAH budgets the total. A 5-edge corner template gets 7.5 distance units — 1.5 per edge — to spend however it likes: four edges at distance 0 and one at 6 still fits (6 <= 7.5, FOUND), while five edges at 2 each overspends (10 > 7.5, REJECT). Closeness is money, and money is fungible. The average would say the same thing here, but the budget says it in JAH's language: spend, don't average.

The budget framing matters when templates have important and unimportant edges. Give the critical edges a sub-budget (the corner's tip: at most 3) and the decorative edges the remainder — the budget splits, the average cannot. JAH's recognition is full of sub-budgets: the important evidence gets its own allowance, and overspending the allowance is a veto even when the total fits.

The demo prices two placements of the corner template: exact-but-one (distances [0,0,0,0,6], total 6 <= 7.5, FOUND — one far edge forgiven) and uniformly-mediocre ([2,2,2,2,2], total 10 > 7.5, REJECT — nothing exact, everything meh). Same average-ish story, opposite verdicts from the budget's perspective: one perfect-ish placement beats five mediocre ones. Signature-original to JAH, demonstrated on synthetic distances.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Chamfer distances per template edge; the budget is JAH's accounting over them.
The recipe
Total budget = 1.5 x (number of edges). Sum the edges' distances; accept iff the sum fits the budget. One far edge is affordable if the rest are exact — closeness is fungible.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff total_distance <= 1.5 * n_edges: the template may spend its budget unevenly, but it may not overspend. JAH's single-number gate.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
corner-finder, polygon-finder, car-finder, house-finder

    kh, kw = len(kernel), len(kernel[0])
    rows, cols = len(image), len(image[0])

def chamfer_dt(edge):
    INF = 10**9
    h, w = len(edge), len(edge[0])
    d = [[0 if edge[i][j] else INF for j in range(w)] for i in range(h)]
    for i in range(h):
        for j in range(w):
            if i > 0: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i-1][j]+3)
            if j > 0: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i][j-1]+3)
            if i > 0 and j > 0: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i-1][j-1]+4)
            if i > 0 and j < w-1: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i-1][j+1]+4)
    for i in range(h-1, -1, -1):
        for j in range(w-1, -1, -1):
            if i < h-1: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i+1][j]+3)
            if j < w-1: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i][j+1]+3)
            if i < h-1 and j < w-1: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i+1][j+1]+4)
            if i < h-1 and j > 0: d[i][j] = min(d[i][j], d[i+1][j-1]+4)
    return d

JAH-KW-CHAM-000102 — The distance ledger — JAH's per-edge books

A chamfer score of 1.2 is a claim; JAH's distance ledger is the proof. Every template edge gets a line: its measured distance, its sub-budget (the critical tip edge: 3.0; the arm edges: 1.5 each... summing to the 7.5 total), and its balance. The audit reads the books: any edge over budget by more than tolerance fails the match, and the ledger names the offender. 'Edge 3 (tip) overspent by 4.0' is a diagnosis; 'score 2.1' is a shrug.

The sub-budgets encode JAH's knowledge of the template: the corner's tip is the critical feature — it gets a tight 3.0 because a corner whose tip is 6 away is not a corner — while the arm edges get 1.5 each as ordinary evidence. The ledger enforces the design: the important edges are held to their own standard, not hidden in the average.

The demo books a corner match: tip edge distance 1 (budget 3.0, balance +2.0), arms at 0, 1, 2, 0 (budgets 1.5 each) — total 4.0 <= 7.5, every sub-budget respected, LEDGER CLEAN, match certified. Then a tampered placement: tip at 6 (over by 3.0) — the ledger names edge 0 and fails the match, though the total (7.0) would have fit. The tip is not negotiable. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Per-edge chamfer distances; the ledger is JAH's bookkeeping over them.
The recipe
Book each edge: (edge_id, distance, sub_budget, balance). Audit: every balance >= -tolerance, total within budget. The ledger names which edge overspent, if any.
The knowing-when rule
MATCH CERTIFIED iff the ledger audits clean: no edge over its sub-budget by more than the tolerance, total within the closeness budget. Receipts for every unit spent.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
corner-finder, box-finder, car-finder, pedestrian-finder

JAH-KW-CHAM-000103 — The occlusion allowance — JAH's 30-percent rule

The public robust average handles occlusion by averaging the best 70%; JAH's occlusion allowance states it as policy: up to 30% of the template may be missing, excused in advance, no questions asked — but the remaining 70% must fit the budget at full strictness, and the 31st missing percent voids the match. The allowance is forgiveness with a published limit, not a statistical trick.

The policy framing changes the argument about a miss. 'The wheel is behind the trash can' is not an excuse to lower the bar; it is a claim on the allowance, and the allowance has 30% to spend. Spend it on the wheel (20% of the template's edges) and the match proceeds; spend it on the wheel AND the door (45%) and the match is void. Occlusion is budgeted, like everything else in JAH's system.

The demo tests a 10-edge car silhouette: 3 edges occluded (distances 12+), 7 visible at distances [0,1,0,2,1,0,1] totaling 5 <= 10.5 — FOUND, allowance spent 30%. Then 5 occluded: allowance exceeded — VOID, no matter how good the visible 5 are. The excuse has a limit, and the limit is 30%. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Per-edge distances; the allowance drops the worst 30% before budgeting.
The recipe
Sort edges by distance; discard the worst 30% as occluded; budget the remaining 70% at 1.5 each. The missing edges are not counted — they are excused, up to the allowance.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff (visible edges' total <= 1.5 * n_visible) AND (occluded fraction <= 0.3): the visible evidence must be strong, and the excuse has a limit.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
car-finder, pedestrian-finder, face-finder, cat-finder

JAH-KW-CHAM-000104 — The coarse-first search — JAH's pyramid discipline

The public pyramid is an optimization; JAH's coarse-first search is a discipline: NO full-resolution scoring without a coarse suspicion. It is the same legalism as his two witnesses — suspicion (coarse, cheap, loose bar 3.0) authorizes investigation (fine, expensive, strict bar 1.5), and investigation without suspicion is forbidden. The discipline guarantees the search never spends fine work on hopeless placements, not just usually but always.

The bar relationship is principled: 3.0 coarse units equal 1.5 full-res units (the pyramid halves distances), so the loose bar is the strict bar seen from far away — same physical tolerance, coarser ruler. A placement that cannot clear the loose bar when small cannot clear the strict bar when large; the logic is airtight, which is why the discipline never misses.

The demo runs the discipline on the 16x16 corner scene: 49 coarse placements scored, 2 suspects (coarse <= 3.0), 18 fine placements investigated in their neighborhoods, 1 convicted at 0.0. Brute force would have spent 196 fine scorings; the discipline spent 49 coarse + 18 fine. Suspicion is cheap; conviction is earned. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Coarse and fine distance transforms; the discipline orders their use.
The recipe
Coarse transform, coarse template, loose bar (3.0): collect suspects. Fine transform only on suspects' neighborhoods, strict bar (1.5): convict or release. Never score fine without a coarse suspicion.
The knowing-when rule
SUSPECT iff coarse <= 3.0; FOUND iff fine <= 1.5 with margin: suspicion is cheap and loose, conviction is expensive and strict. JAH's legalism, applied to search.
Used by (Tier-2/Tier-3 finders)
car-finder, pedestrian-finder, face-finder, house-finder

JAH-KW-CHAM-000105 — The direction oath — JAH's sworn orientations

The public directed chamfer prices orientation mismatch; JAH swears it as an oath. The difference: pricing lets a mismatched edge pay full distance and still pass if everything else is perfect; the oath makes the mismatch structural — a horizontal edge may ONLY consult the horizontal map, and what it finds there is the whole story. It is his arrangement-oath instinct (record: feature-hierarchy-recognition-signature #104) applied to pixels: sworn, not suggested.

The oath is stricter than the price, and JAH wants it strict: in street clutter, half the false matches his pedestrian finder ever suffered came from position-right-orientation-wrong placements that the priced version forgave. The oath forgives nothing — which is exactly right for evidence as cheap to check as orientation.

The demo re-runs the bar scene under the oath: horizontal template on the horizontal scene, consulting only the horizontal map: 0.0, FOUND. The same template's edges attempting the vertical scene consult the horizontal map — which is empty, no horizontal edges anywhere: 99.0, REJECTED — perjury, no credit for position. The oath held; the clutter lost. Signature-original to JAH.

The recognition principle

Which kernels feed it
Orientation-split distance transforms; the oath governs which map each edge may consult.
The recipe
Each template edge is sworn to its orientation bucket and may consult ONLY that bucket's map. Consulting another bucket is perjury: the distance reads full, no credit for position.
The knowing-when rule
FOUND iff the oath-keeping score <= 1.5: position and orientation must BOTH agree, and the oath makes orientation non-negotiable.

JAH-KW-CHAM-000106 — The clutter alarm — JAH's saturation tripwire

The public failure record diagnoses clutter after the fact; JAH's clutter alarm trips before any detection is trusted. The tripwire is the score map's median: in a clean scene the median placement is far from all edges (median ~9); in edge soup the median is low (~1.8) because edges are everywhere. Median under 2.5 means the scene is saturated — every placement scores well, so no placement means anything — and the alarm voids the entire search: UNMATCHABLE, no detections, no appeals.

The median is the right statistic because it is peak-proof: one true object in the scene does not move the median, so the alarm does not punish scenes that contain real targets among moderate clutter.     h, w = len(edge), len(edge[0])
    d = [[0 if edge[i][j] else INF for j in range(w)] for i in range(h)]

The distance transform is an optimization — two passes of min-plus arithmetic standing in for true nearest-edge distances. Optimizations get audited. JAH's budget audit re-prices the winning placement the slow honest way: for each template edge, brute-force the nearest scene edge and measure the true distance. The two prices must agree within 10%; if they do not, the transform lied (a bug, a degenerate scene) and the match is void — no matter how good the score looked.

The audit is deliberately expensive and deliberately rare: it runs only on the winner, once, after the gates pass. One slow verification amortized over thousands of fast scorings — the same economics as his ladder audit (rung-by-rung verification only where it matters).
