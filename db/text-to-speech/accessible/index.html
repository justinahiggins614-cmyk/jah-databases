The Text-to-Speech Database (Public)

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. Text-to-speech reads written words aloud with a voice, so text can be heard instead of read. Type or paste text, pick a voice, set the speed and pitch, and press Speak. The voices are the browser's own; nothing is uploaded and no sign-in is needed.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced.

Full record index: https://justinahiggins614-cmyk.github.io/jah-databases-data-2/data/text-to-speech/index.jsonl.gz

  1. What Text-to-Speech Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Text-to-speech reads written words aloud with a voice, so text can be heard instead of read. The sentence 'Hello, world' is spoken as 2 words.

    Math proof: Speech length follows text length: at about 120 words a minute, 60 words take about 30 seconds, 150 words take about 75 seconds, and 300 words take about 2 minutes 30 seconds. Speed is a rate: a rate of 2 speaks twice as fast, so 60 words take about 15 seconds. Volume is a fraction of full: 0.5 is half as loud as 1.

    Science proof: Text-to-speech works because written words can be turned into sounds by rule: letters map to sounds, punctuation marks pauses, and numbers and dates are read in words. A voice is a set of recorded sounds joined together, and speed and pitch change how fast and how high they are played. That is why the same text can sound different in different voices, and why hearing text helps a reader who cannot see the page.
