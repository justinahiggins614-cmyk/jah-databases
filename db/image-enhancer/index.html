1. Description

An image enhancer changes a picture's pixels to make it clearer: brighter, darker, more contrast, sharper, or inverted, all by real pixel maths, drawn on a canvas. The seeded 4 by 2 picture is drawn on the page; you can also upload your own picture, which is read in the browser and never leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Brightening by 20: 100 becomes 120, 120 becomes 140. Contrast 1.5 times: 100 becomes 86, 160 becomes 176. Gamma 0.5 brightens 100 to 159. Inverting 100 gives 155. Clipping: 250 plus 20 is 255, not 270. Before and after: the average brightness goes from 130 to 150.

3. Science proof

Enhancement works because each pixel is a number, and changing the numbers changes the picture: adding 20 brightens, stretching away from the middle adds contrast, averaging neighbours blurs. Values are clipped to 255, so nothing overflows. That is why the enhancer shows the numbers before and after, so every change can be read.
