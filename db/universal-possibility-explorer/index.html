1. Description

A universal possibility explorer maps the branches a choice can take, with chances and outcomes for each branch, so the best path can be seen and compared. This is a simulation and design-study tool, classified FRONTIER: it explores stated possibilities with stated chances, and it never claims to explore all possibilities or to predict the future.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A possibility space grows by multiplication: with b choices at each of n steps, the number of complete paths is b to the power n. With 2 choices at each of 3 steps there are 2 times 2 times 2, which is 8 complete paths. Probabilities add to one: if the branches from one choice have chances 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2, they sum to 1.0, so every outcome is counted once and none is missed. The chance of one complete path is the product of the chances along it: 0.5 then 0.5 then 0.5 gives 0.125, or 1 in 8. An expected value is the sum of each outcome times its chance: outcomes worth 10, 4 and 0 with chances 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 give 5 plus 1.2 plus 0, which is 6.2.

3. Science proof

Exploring possibilities works because a human mind can hold only a few branches at once, while a written tree can hold hundreds. Writing each branch down, with its chance and its outcome, turns guessing into comparison: the best path can be seen, not just felt. Probabilities here are estimates built from stated assumptions, not measurements of the future, so every result must be read with its assumptions beside it. That is why a possibility explorer is a simulation and design-study tool: it shows what follows from the assumptions, and it is honest about what it does not know.

6. Use

Use it to study a decision before you make it. Write the branches, give each branch a chance, give each outcome a value, and read the tree: the most likely path, the expected value, and the rare branch that still matters. Everything runs in this browser, so nothing leaves the page. Remember the limit: the explorer shows what follows from the chances you state. It does not explore all possibilities, and it does not predict the future.
