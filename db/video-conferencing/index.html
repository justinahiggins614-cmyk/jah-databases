1. Description

Video conferencing lets people in different places talk face to face on screen. The demo on this page is a LOCAL DEMO ONLY: it previews your camera where the browser allows it and shows simulated participants, with no server and no real call. A real multi-party call needs a server, and this page honestly says so. Nothing is recorded and nothing is uploaded.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A call is counted in people and minutes: 3 people show 3 pictures, and a meeting of 4 people and 30 minutes is 120 person-minutes. Three calls of 20 minutes each total 60 minutes. A call that drops at 15 minutes of 60 ends 45 minutes early. Leaving takes 4 people to 3.

3. Science proof

Video conferencing works because a camera and a microphone turn a person into a picture and sound, and a server carries those between everyone in the call. The local demo on this page does the first half only: it can preview your own camera, and it shows simulated people so the grid can be tried. That is why the page labels itself a local demo and makes no real call.
