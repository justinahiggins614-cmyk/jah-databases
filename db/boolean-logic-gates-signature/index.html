A Signature design for a Boolean playground: two values, a truth table, and a function counter a reader can explore.

The Signature Boolean playground design asks how to teach the two values without a lecture, and answers with a playground: two buttons, 0 and 1, a table that fills itself as the reader tries inputs, and a counter that shows how many functions exist for the chosen input count. The design question is what the playground shows first: the answer is the counting, 1 input gives 4 functions, 2 inputs give 16, 3 inputs give 256, so the reader sees the space of functions grow before meeting any single gate. The design also plans the table as the definition: every function the playground shows is its full truth table, never a name alone, so the reader learns that a gate is its table. This record designs the playground, the counter, and the table-first rule.

A reader opens the Signature playground and picks two inputs. The table shows four rows, 00, 01, 10, 11, and the counter reads 16 functions. The reader tries the AND function and the table fills 0, 0, 0, 1. The reader tries XOR and the table fills 0, 1, 1, 0. The reader sees that each function is a column of four outputs, and that the sixteen columns are all the two-input functions there are.

History

A Signature design for a surface sorter: answer orientable and chi, and the sorter names the surface.

The Signature surface sorter design asks how to classify a surface by answering two questions, and answers with a sorter: is the surface orientable, and what is its Euler characteristic. The design question is what the sorter shows: the answer is the kind, so orientable with chi 0 gives the torus, non-orientable with chi 0 gives the Klein bottle, and orientable with chi -2 gives the genus-2 surface. The design also plans the three kinds as the sorter's outputs: sphere, connected sum of g tori with chi = 2 - 2g, and connected sum of k projective planes with chi = 2 - k, so every closed surface lands in exactly one bin. This record designs the sorter, the two questions, and the three bins.

A reader feeds the Signature sorter a surface that is orientable with chi 0: the sorter computes genus 1 from 2 - 2g = 0 and names it a torus. The reader feeds a surface that is non-orientable with chi 0: the sorter computes k = 2 from 2 - k = 0 and names it a Klein bottle. The same characteristic, two different surfaces, told apart by the orientability question, which is the classification in action.
