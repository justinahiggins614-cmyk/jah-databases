# Dictionary entries — The Accounting and Bookkeeping Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## bookkeeping

The daily work of recording transactions in the books, each with its date, its amount, and its account, so that nothing is written twice and nothing is left out.

## accounting

The work of reading the books and turning their entries into statements that say how a business stands, what it owns, what it owes, and what it earned.

## ledger

The book in which transactions are gathered by account, so that the whole history of each account can be read in one place, in order, from its first entry.

## journal

The book of first entry, where each transaction is written down as it happens, with its date, its accounts, and its amounts, before it is posted to the ledger.

## double entry

The rule that every transaction is entered twice, once as a debit and once as a credit, in equal amounts, so that the books can be proved balanced.

## debit

One side of a double entry, recorded in the left column of an account, raising some accounts and lowering others by the rules of the system.

## credit

The other side of a double entry, recorded in the right column of an account, matching the debit in amount, so that the two sides of the books stay equal.

## trial balance

The list of all account balances at a moment, added up to show that debits equal credits, proving the books balance before the statements are drawn.

## financial statement

A written statement drawn from the books, such as the income statement or the balance sheet, saying how the business stands over a stated period.

## asset

Something a business owns that has value, such as money, stock, or equipment, recorded in its own account and counted in the balance sheet.

## liability

Something a business owes, such as a bill unpaid or a loan, recorded in its own account, so that what is owed is never hidden from what is owned.

## equity

What is left of a business after what it owes is taken from what it owns, recorded as the owners' share, and shown in the balance sheet as the balancing figure.
