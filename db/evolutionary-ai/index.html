John Holland and the Genetic Algorithm JAH-EAI-000003 · simple

Evidence: established The claims in this record are documented in the cited sources; numbers in the ledger are a model, labeled as such.

John Holland, a physicist and computer scientist at the University of Michigan, gave evolutionary computation its first general theory. His 1975 book Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems defined the genetic algorithm: a population of strings, scored by fitness, selected in proportion to fitness, and varied by crossover and mutation. The book also gave the field its central theorem, the schema theorem, which explains why the algorithm processes many patterns at once.

History

Holland studied physics, then moved to Michigan, where he worked on adaptive systems through the 1960s and early 1970s. His students carried the genetic algorithm into conferences and applications: the first International Conference on Genetic Algorithms met in 1985, ten years after the book, and the field grew from there into optimization, design, and machine learning. Holland also helped found the Santa Fe Institute's complex-systems work.

Skeptical analysis

Forecasts of a steady field are easy to make and easy to be wrong about: a single new method, a hardware shift, or a funding wave can change the pace. The honest forecast is a range, not a date, and this record keeps its scenarios as scenarios, scored and labeled, not as promises.
