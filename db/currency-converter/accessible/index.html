  1. Converting One Amount Between Two Currencies

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The currency converter turns an amount in one currency into the matching amount in another, using a sample illustrative rate that is shown in plain sight and labeled as a sample, never as a live market rate. A visitor who wants converting one amount between two currencies needs one honest number: how much an amount in one currency is worth in another, with the rate shown in plain sight. Without a converter that shows its rate, the visitor cannot tell whether the figure is fair, and a hidden rate can quietly take a cut. This record states the design of a converter that shows a sample illustrative rate, labels it clearly as a sample and never as a live market rate, and converts with plain arithmetic anyone can repeat. One amount and one rate is the simplest conversion, so the converter must show the amount, the sample rate, and the result together, with the rate labeled as a sample. This is a simple record: one idea and one calculation, with nothing extra to learn. Worked example: in a website built this way, converting one amount between two currencies runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Changing money is an old trade: coins weighed at a market stall, rates written on a board, a fee taken in plain sight. The website form grows out of that trade, adding a converter that shows its rate before it converts, so a visitor can check the arithmetic.
