JAH-R20-USIM-000001 — Simulation framework — colliding at cosmic scale

SIMULATION — Signature-labeled. Universe-scale collision scenarios computed with standard, working physics (gravity, general relativity results as public formulas, conservation laws). Computed scenarios, not observed events and not predictions. Standard physics only; no speculative axioms. The Big Bang is treated as the public cosmological model, not as a 'collision' claim beyond the standard description.

At cosmic scale the collider is gravity. This record defines the framework every scenario in this database uses. The physics ladder: Newtonian gravity for galaxy dynamics (accurate where speeds are non-relativistic and fields are weak), the public results of general relativity for compact objects (orbital decay by gravitational waves, the quadrupole formula, merger energetics as measured by LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA), and the public Lambda-CDM cosmological parameters for anything involving the expanding universe. Each scenario states which rung it stands on.

Three scenario classes. Class A: galaxy mergers — gas-rich and gas-poor, computed with dynamical friction timescales and star-formation bursts. Class B: cluster mergers — the Bullet Cluster class, where dark matter, gas, and galaxies separate, computed with standard gravitational lensing mass maps as the public reference. Class C: compact-object mergers — black hole and neutron star binaries, using the public gravitational-wave catalog's measured masses and radiated energies as calibration.

The honesty header is the same as the other simulation databases: inputs are public astronomical measurements or stated hypotheticals, computation is standard physics, results are scenario outcomes. The generator extends by varying mass ratios, impact parameters, and redshifts within stated ranges.

JAH-R20-USIM-000002 — Scenario: Milky Way-Andromeda merger — Milkomeda

SIMULATION — Signature-labeled. Universe-scale collision scenarios computed with standard, working physics (gravity, general relativity results as public formulas, conservation laws). Computed scenarios, not observed events and not predictions. Standard physics only; no speculative axioms. The Big Bang is treated as the public cosmological model, not as a 'collision' claim beyond the standard description.

The nearest future giant merger in the public record: Andromeda (M31) approaches the Milky Way at ~110 km/s and the pair is expected to merge in ~4.5 billion years. Inputs: Milky Way ~1.5 x 10^12 solar masses (with dark halo), Andromeda ~1.5-2 x 10^12, separation 2.5 million light-years. Assumption: the public orbital solution (mostly radial infall with some tangential uncertainty); the simulation explores the tangential-velocity uncertainty as variants.

Computation: dynamical friction bleeds orbital energy into the dark halos over the first pass; the galaxies interpenetrate, tidal tails spray stars across megaparsecs, gas clouds collide and trigger starbursts, and the central supermassive black holes (4 x 10^6 and ~10^8 solar masses) sink to the centre and eventually merge. The product: a giant elliptical galaxy — "Milkomeda" — with most star formation quenched. Stellar collisions themselves are essentially nil: stars are too small and far apart; galaxies merge by gravity, not by touch.

Variants: higher tangential velocity gives a later, more grazing merger; a gas-rich variant (if both galaxies retained more gas) gives a stronger starburst. All variants are labeled scenarios; the 4.5 Gyr figure is the public estimate.

JAH-R20-USIM-000003 — Scenario: Bullet Cluster class — clusters colliding

SIMULATION — Signature-labeled. Universe-scale collision scenarios computed with standard, working physics (gravity, general relativity results as public formulas, conservation laws). Computed scenarios, not observed events and not predictions. Standard physics only; no speculative axioms. The Big Bang is treated as the public cosmological model, not as a 'collision' claim beyond the standard description.

The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-558) is the public archetype: two galaxy clusters collided ~150 million years ago at ~4,700 km/s, and the components separated — galaxies and dark matter sailed through (seen by lensing), while the hot X-ray gas slammed and shocked (seen by Chandra). Inputs for the scenario class: cluster masses ~10^14-10^15 solar masses, collision speeds of thousands of km/s, gas temperatures of 10^7-10^8 K.

Computation: the gas shock heats to tens of millions of kelvin and radiates X-rays; the dark-matter halos, interacting only by gravity, pass through nearly undisturbed — which is why the lensing mass peaks sit ahead of the gas. The scenario explores mass ratios from 1:1 to 10:1 and impact parameters from head-on to grazing, each producing a different shock morphology and separation distance, all computed with standard hydrodynamics-plus-gravity.

The database treats this class carefully: the Bullet Cluster's separation of dark matter and gas is public evidence for dark matter that modified-gravity alternatives must also explain. The simulations do not take sides — they compute what standard gravity plus dark matter predicts for each variant, labeled as scenarios.

JAH-R20-USIM-000004 — Scenario: black-hole mergers — GW150914 class

SIMULATION — Signature-labeled. Universe-scale collision scenarios computed with standard, working physics (gravity, general relativity results as public formulas, conservation laws). Computed scenarios, not observed events and not predictions. Standard physics only; no speculative axioms. The Big Bang is treated as the public cosmological model, not as a 'collision' claim beyond the standard description.

The public LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA catalog gives this scenario class its calibration: GW150914 (2015) merged black holes of ~36 and ~29 solar masses, radiating ~3 solar masses of energy as gravitational waves — peak power exceeding the combined light of all stars in the observable universe, for a fifth of a second. Inputs: component masses, spins, and orbital eccentricity from the public catalog's parameter ranges.

Computation uses the public results of numerical relativity as formulas: the inspiral chirps upward in frequency (the "chirp mass" sets the rate), the merger rings down to a Kerr black hole, and the radiated fraction is ~3-5 percent of the total mass for comparable-mass mergers. The scenario explores the catalog's range: GW190521's ~150-solar-mass remnant (an intermediate-mass black hole, public), neutron-star mergers like GW170817 (with its public kilonova and heavy-element production), and hypothetical supermassive mergers for future LISA.

The simulation's rule: every variant's masses and spins are drawn from or near the public catalog's measured ranges, and extrapolations (supermassive, primordial) are labeled as such. Standard general relativity throughout — the same theory the catalog's measurements confirmed.

JAH-R20-USIM-000005 — Scenario: the early universe — the ultimate hot dense state

SIMULATION — Signature-labeled. Universe-scale collision scenarios computed with standard, working physics (gravity, general relativity results as public formulas, conservation laws). Computed scenarios, not observed events and not predictions. Standard physics only; no speculative axioms. The Big Bang is treated as the public cosmological model, not as a 'collision' claim beyond the standard description.

The furthest the collider concept stretches: the first microseconds after the Big Bang, when the entire observable universe was a quark-gluon plasma at trillions of kelvin — the state ALICE and RHIC recreate in miniature. This scenario is built strictly from the public cosmological model (Lambda-CDM): inputs are the measured CMB temperature (2.725 K), the Hubble constant (~70 km/s/Mpc), and the light-element abundances from Big Bang nucleosynthesis.

Computation: run the expansion backward with the Friedmann equations (standard cosmology) and the temperature scales as 1/size. At ~10^-6 seconds the universe was QGP; at ~1 second neutrinos decoupled; at ~3 minutes nucleosynthesis forged hydrogen, helium, and trace lithium — predictions that match observation to percent precision, which is why the model is trusted. The scenario presents the timeline as the public model's account, with the honest boundary marked: before ~10^-12 seconds the physics is extrapolated, and the database labels that region as beyond established physics rather than filling it with speculation.

The Signature concept layer is confined to one labeled section: the observation that every collider in this whole lane is, in a sense, a time machine to this era — and that the progression from cosmic QGP to laboratory QGP is the deepest connection between the databases.
