1. Description

An image format converter changes a picture from one format to another, and changes its pixels for real: grey, resize, flip, rotate and crop, all on the pixel list, drawn on a canvas. The seeded 4 by 2 picture is drawn on the page; you can also upload your own picture, which is read in the browser and never leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A tiny picture 4 by 2 has 8 pixels. A picture of 8 pixels with 3 channels is 24 bytes. RGB to grey: [255, 0, 0] becomes 85. Resizing 4 by 2 to 2 by 1 gives 2 pixels. A bigger picture 8 by 8 has 64 pixels. With 8 bits per channel and 3 channels, each pixel is 24 bits.

3. Science proof

A format converter works because a picture is a list of pixels, and each pixel is three numbers: red, green and blue. Converting changes the numbers: grey averages the three, resizing picks a new grid, flipping reverses a row. That is why the converter shows the pixel list before and after, so every change can be read.
