  1. A First Membership Site With Three Plain Pages

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The membership website builder lets a person build a small site for their members: plain pages, a member list with names written out, and a clear statement of what members receive, so members always know what they joined and what it gives them. A first membership site with three plain pages needs plain rules a new visitor can follow, and the count is written beside each membership site so a reader can see it at a glance. Without plain rules, a visitor must guess at every step, and a wrong guess wastes the time the tool was meant to save. The rules must be checked by a second reader before they are published, because a mistake in plain words is a mistake every visitor will follow. When the rules are clear and checked, the tool does its job quietly and the visitor gets on with the work. Worked example, Signature design: the same membership site is rebuilt from the Signature pattern, so a first membership site with three plain pages runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Clubs with members are an old habit: a book of members, a list of what members receive, a gathering place kept for members only. The website form grows out of that habit, adding plain pages and a member list a person can join by choice. Lists and guides for membership sites are an old habit: a notebook page, a card file, a notice pinned to a board. This record follows that habit, writing the membership site down in plain words so a reader can check it.
