# Dictionary entries — The Identity and Permissions Center Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## identity

The answer to who someone is, as a system records it: a name, an account, and the proof that ties them together.

## account

One person's named place in a system, holding their settings, their roles, and their history of sign-ins.

## role

A named bundle of permissions, such as reader or editor, given to a person so their access can be managed as one thing.

## permission

One allowed action on one thing, such as reading one file or changing one record.

## least privilege

The rule that a person or program gets only the permissions it needs for its work, and nothing more.

## authentication

Proving who you are, by something you know, something you hold, or something you are.

## authorization

Deciding what an authenticated person may do, by checking their roles and permissions.

## session

One signed-in visit, from sign-in to sign-out, during which the system remembers who you are.

## delegation

Letting another person act on your behalf for a limited time and a limited set of actions.

## revocation

Taking a permission or a sign-in away, immediately, so it can no longer be used.

## audit trail

The ordered list of who did what and when, kept so that any change can be traced back.

## consent

A person's clear agreement to one specific use of their identity or their data, which can be withdrawn.
