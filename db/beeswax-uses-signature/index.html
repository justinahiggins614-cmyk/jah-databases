Beeswax Candles simple · candles · beginner

The Signature honey-gold candle is an original Signature design: a rolled beeswax candle with a hand-combed honeycomb texture pressed into the outer sheet, a double cotton wick for a broad steady flame, and a beeswax drip collar molded at the base. It is a Signature original, not a historical candle style, and it is made to show off the wax itself rather than to copy any traditional candle.

Materials

  • Signature-filtered beeswax sheets with a pressed honeycomb texture
  • Double cotton wick, braided
  • Beeswax drip-collar mold
  • Double boiler with thermometer
  • Comb-texture roller or stamp
  • Kitchen scale

Method

  1. Warm a beeswax sheet gently until it bends without cracking, then press the honeycomb texture into the outer face with the roller.
  2. Lay the double wick along one short edge and roll the sheet firmly, keeping the edge straight so the candle stands level.
  3. Mold a drip collar from softened wax around the base and smooth the join with a warm spoon.
  4. Trim both wicks to the same 6 mm length, level the base, and cure for a day before the first burn.
  5. Burn the Signature candle in a holder that catches drips, and trim both wicks before each lighting.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Never leave a burning candle unattended, and keep candles away from curtains and drafts.

Yield

Six pillar candles, 7 cm across and 15 cm tall, use about 3.5 kg of wax including a waste allowance; see the demo.

Variations

  • Dipped tapers in pairs for the table.
  • Molded pillars with a wide, slow flame.
  • Rolled honeycomb sheets in natural gold or ivory.
  • Scent-free plain beeswax, since the wax itself carries the scent.

Working demo program

import math

TITLE = "Beeswax Candles (Signature)"
COUNT = 6
DIAMETER_CM = 7.0
HEIGHT_CM = 15.0
BURN_G_PER_HOUR = 7.0
WASTE_PCT = 10.0


def main():
    r = DIAMETER_CM / 2.0
    vol = math.pi * r * r * HEIGHT_CM
    weight = vol * 0.96
    total = weight * COUNT * (1.0 + WASTE_PCT / 100.0)
    burn = weight / BURN_G_PER_HOUR
    print(TITLE + " - candle wax calculator")
    print("One candle: %.1f g of wax" % weight)
    print("Burn time per candle: %.1f hours" % burn)
    print("Wax for %d candles (with %d%% waste allowance): %.1f g" % (COUNT, int(WASTE_PCT), total))

Beeswax Food Wraps simple · food storage · beginner

The Signature wrap set is an original Signature design: three wraps in graduated sizes with a stitched cotton border that stops fraying, a beeswax-and-resin coating mixed for a firmer seal, and a small stamped honeycomb mark in one corner of each wrap. It is a Signature original, not a traditional wrap pattern, and the stitched border is what sets it apart from plain cut wraps.

Materials

  • Organic cotton with a stitched border
  • Food-grade beeswax (E901)
  • Pine resin for grip
  • Jojoba oil
  • Baking paper, iron, scissors

Method

  1. Stitch a narrow border around each cloth piece before coating, so the edges cannot fray in washing.
  2. Coat with the Signature blend of beeswax, resin, and jojoba oil, melted evenly into the cloth under baking paper.
  3. Stamp the honeycomb mark into one corner while the wrap is still warm, using a cool metal stamp pressed lightly.
  4. Test the seal by wrapping a bowl and pressing the edges down with warm hands; add a little more wax and reheat any edge that will not hold.
  5. Cool flat, wash in cool water, and re-wax when the seal weakens after months of use.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Food-grade separation: only food-grade beeswax (E901) touches food. Wax of unknown grade, colored craft wax, and wax used for candles, polish, or display models is never used on food, and food equipment is never shared with non-food wax work. Wraps are not used on raw meat or fish, and they are never put in a microwave, oven, or dishwasher, where heat melts the coating.

Yield

One set of small, medium, and large wraps uses about 43 g of wax; see the demo.

Variations

  • Small wraps for half lemons and jars.
  • Medium wraps for sandwiches and bowls.
  • Large wraps for loaves and platters.
  • Re-waxing a tired wrap with fresh pellets instead of throwing it away.

    print("Wax for one set of three wraps: %.1f g" % total_wax)
    print("Wraps from a 500 g block of wax: %d" % int(500.0 / (total_wax / 3.0)))

The Signature aged-wheel seal is an original Signature design: a two-coat beeswax seal with a beeswax medallion pressed into the top of the wheel, carrying the aging date and the cheese style in stamped letters. It is a Signature original, not a traditional cheese-wax style, and the medallion is a label that peels away with the coating.

Materials

  • Food-grade beeswax (E901)
  • Food-grade plant oil
  • Stamp set for the medallion
  • Brush and dipping pot
  • Thermometer

Method

  1. Brush the wheel clean and dry, then brush on a first thin coat of melted food-grade wax, covering the edges where the wheel meets its flat faces.
  2. Let the first coat set fully, then brush a second thin coat over the whole wheel for a complete seal.
  3. While the second coat is still soft, press a thin beeswax medallion onto the top and stamp it with the aging date and style.
  4. Cool the wheel fully before moving it to the aging shelf, keeping it cool and humid while it ages.
  5. Peel medallion and coating away together before cutting and eating the cheese.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Food-grade separation: only food-grade beeswax (E901) touches food. Wax of unknown grade, colored craft wax, and wax used for candles, polish, or display models is never used on food, and food equipment is never shared with non-food wax work. Wax that has coated a cheese is never re-melted for food use, and moldy wax is discarded.

Yield

A 20 cm wheel takes about 130 g of wax for two coats; see the demo.

Variations

  • Red or yellow wax for farmhouse styles.
  • Clear natural wax that shows the rind beneath.
  • Wax over cloth binding for long-aged wheels.

Variations

Lip Balm and Salves simple · cosmetics · beginner

The Signature honey-lip balm is an original Signature design: a beeswax balm with a small amount of honey folded in at the end of melting, a touch of cocoa butter for a chocolate note, and a beeswax seal stamped on the tin lid. It is a Signature original, not a traditional balm recipe, and the honey is there for flavor and feel, not for any health claim.

Materials

  • Beeswax (cosmetic grade)
  • Cocoa butter
  • Jojoba oil
  • Honey (a small amount)
  • Tins with stamped lids

Method

  1. Weigh the wax, cocoa butter, and jojoba oil in the ratio 1 part wax to 1 part butter to 2 parts oil, and melt them together in a double boiler until clear.
  2. Take the pot off the heat and fold in a small amount of honey, stirring until it is fully mixed, working quickly before the mixture starts to set.
  3. Pour carefully into tins, filling each one without spilling over the rim.
  4. While the balm cools, press a beeswax seal onto each tin lid.
  5. Let the balms set fully, cap them, label with date and ingredients, and store in a cool place.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Keep balm ingredients cosmetic-grade, patch-test any new balm on a small area of skin first, and never share lip balm tubes. Honey in balm is a flavor ingredient; this record makes no health claim for it.

Yield

A 120 g batch fills 25 standard tubes; see the demo.

Variations

  • A firmer balm for winter, with more wax.
  • A softer balm for summer tubes, with more oil.
  • Tinted balm with a pinch of cosmetic-grade mica.
  • Unscented balm for sensitive skin.

Lotions and Creams compounded · cosmetics · intermediate

The Signature whipped honey cream is an original Signature design: an emulsified beeswax cream whipped while cooling for a light, mousse-like texture, with a small amount of honey in the water phase and a beeswax seal under the jar lid. It is a Signature original, not a traditional cream formula, and the whipping step is what gives it its airy texture.

Materials

  • Beeswax (cosmetic grade)
  • Jojoba oil
  • Distilled water with a little honey
  • Plant emulsifier
  • Preservative
  • Sterilized jars

Method

  1. Heat the wax, jojoba oil, and emulsifier to about 70 degrees Celsius, and heat the water phase with its little honey to the same temperature.
  2. Pour the water phase into the oil phase slowly, stirring constantly, and blend in short bursts until the mixture thickens and turns opaque.
  3. While the cream cools, whip it gently at intervals so air is folded in and the texture turns light and mousse-like.
  4. Add preservative at the maker's recommended temperature, stirring it in evenly.
  5. Pour into sterilized jars, press a thin beeswax seal under each lid, label with date and ingredients, and keep cool.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Creams with a water phase need a proper preservative and clean equipment, or they grow microbes; never sell or share unpreserved cream, and patch-test new creams on a small area of skin.

Yield

A 200 g batch is 30 g wax, 50 g oil, and 120 g water phase; see the demo.

Variations

  • A rich night cream with more wax and oil.
  • A light day lotion with more water.
  • A hand cream with extra butter for dry hands.
  • An unscented cream for sensitive skin.

Encaustic Painting compounded · art · advanced

The Signature layered-light panel is an original Signature design: an encaustic work built from alternating clear and pigmented layers, with a honeycomb pattern incised into the warm surface and filled with a contrasting color, so light passes through the clear layers and glows against the pattern. It is a Signature original, not a traditional encaustic style.

Materials

  • Beeswax and damar resin medium
  • Dry pigments
  • Rigid panel
  • Incising tools
  • Heat gun

Method

  1. Melt the medium and mix the pigmented colors, keeping each color in its own heated tin.
  2. Lay down alternating clear and pigmented layers, fusing each one with a heat gun until the surface just glistens, until the panel has depth.
  3. While the top layer is warm, incise a honeycomb pattern with the tools.
  4. Fill the incised lines with a contrasting pigmented medium, working it in while the surface is still warm.
  5. Fuse lightly once more, cool fully, and polish the surface with a soft cloth to bring out the layered glow.

Safety

Hot wax handling: melt beeswax in a double boiler or a dedicated wax melter, never over a direct flame and never in a pot used for food afterward unless it is food-grade equipment kept for that purpose. Keep a lid nearby to smother any flare, keep water away from hot wax, and keep children and pets out of the work area. Beeswax melts near 62 to 64 degrees Celsius and can burn skin badly while liquid. Encaustic work needs ventilation, since overheated wax and some pigments give off fumes; never heat wax above its smoking point, and keep the heat gun moving so no spot scorches.

Yield

One 30 by 40 cm panel at 1 mm thickness needs about 115 g of medium; see the demo.

Variations

  • Thin translucent glazes over a drawing.
  • Thick impasto layers carved with tools.
  • Photo transfer sealed under clear medium.
  • Mixed media with collage sealed in wax.

Batik Resist Dyeing compounded · textile art · intermediate

The Signature honeycomb batik is an original Signature design: a batik cloth patterned with a large honeycomb grid, where each cell is waxed and dyed in turn so the finished cloth reads as a field of colored hexagons with crackle lines running through them. It is a Signature original, not a traditional batik motif, and the grid is drawn with a ruler and a hexagonal template.

Materials

  • Beeswax blend for resist
  • Cotton cloth
  • Hexagonal template and ruler
  • Tjanting tool
  • Fabric dyes

Method

  1. Wash and dry the cloth, then draw the honeycomb grid on it with the template and ruler.
  2. Melt the resist wax and wax the cells that should hold the lightest color first, using the tjanting tool.
  3. Dye the cloth in the lightest color, rinse, and dry it.
  4. Wax the next set of cells and dye darker, working cell by cell until the grid is complete.
  5. Iron out the wax between newspaper sheets and wash the cloth in hot soapy water to finish.

Safety
