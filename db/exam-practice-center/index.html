Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

An exam practice website: each practice set is a list of invented sample questions written for this center, never taken from a real exam, with the answer and a plain-words explanation shown after the learner answers. A first practice set in arithmetic must be small enough to finish in a few minutes and clear enough to understand at once: ten invented questions about adding and taking away, with the answers explained in plain words. The center must give the questions one at a time, a timer the learner can see, and an answer key that shows the working, not just the answer. Without a small first set, a learner faces a hard set on the first visit and leaves before learning anything.

Small first practice sets are as old as arithmetic books, which have always begun with adding two numbers.

Without a timer, a practice set has no honest end: one learner takes ten seconds per question and another takes ten minutes, and their scores are compared as if the sets were the same. The center must fix the length of every set, so a fast answer earns more points than a slow one and every learner faces the same pressure. Without a timer, the best strategy is to wait and think forever, and the practice loses its tension. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.
