    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The public knowledge question exchange is a public place to ask a plain question and receive a plain, checked answer, with every question and answer open for all to read. Asking a plain question in public takes courage and a clear form: write the question in one sentence, say what you already know, and say what you want to find out, so an answerer can help at once. Without a plain form, a reader must guess how to ask, and guessing how to ask is a poor way to learn.     The public knowledge question exchange is a public place to ask a plain question and receive a plain, checked answer, with every question and answer open for all to read. It deals in public knowledge only: questions anyone may ask and answers anyone may check, with no private messages and no personal data. A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, Asking a Plain Question in Public, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Questions and Answers.

    History: Asking a question in public and receiving a plain answer is an old habit: a question asked in a hall, an answer written on a noticeboard, a fact checked in a book. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a public list of questions and a mark for answers that have been checked. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

    Proposed solution: Add a tournament bracket with rounds, divisions, and a season; Add a creator studio so visitors can build a new question exchange; Add a review step so every new question exchange is checked before it is published; Add the question exchange in many languages, with the same rules in each; Add an offline pack so the question exchange works with no connection.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a public list of questions, plain answers with their sources written out, a mark for checked answers, and a review step before publishing. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex question exchanges open.
