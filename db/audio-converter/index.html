1. Description

An audio converter changes sound from one format to another, keeping its samples. The demo on this page converts a small sound sample locally: 441 samples of a sine wave, downsampled and counted in the browser. A full transcode needs a backend, and that is BLOCKED; this is a local partial demo, and it says so. You can also upload your own clip, which is read in the browser and never leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A clip at 44100 samples a second for 10 seconds has 441000 samples. A clip of 441000 samples at 16 bits each is 882000 bytes. Halving 44100 samples a second gives 22050 samples a second, and 441000 samples become 220500 samples. Stereo to mono halves 882000 bytes to 441000 bytes. Downsampling 8 samples keeps 4 samples: 0, 87, 87 and 0.

3. Science proof

An audio converter works because sound is samples in order, and each sample is a number of bits: 441000 samples at 16 bits each is 882000 bytes, and halving the sample rate halves the samples to 220500. That is why the local demo converts a small sample list and shows the count, and says plainly that a full transcode needs a backend and is BLOCKED.
