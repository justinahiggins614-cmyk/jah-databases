3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Example — the first record’s formal equation: V = pi * (d/2)^2 * h

The demo computes the grinding chamber as a cylinder. With diameter 70.0 mm the radius is 35.0 mm, so the chamber volume is pi times radius squared times the chamber depth of 30.0 mm, which the program reports in cubic millimetres and cubic centimetres. The screen area uses the same circle formula with the screen diameter of 62.0 mm. Dividing the tooth count of 56 by the chamber floor area gives teeth per square centimetre, a check that the teeth are spread evenly rather than crowded. Each of the 8 turns in the worked example moves the teeth past each other once, so the total cutting passes are the tooth count times the turns, and the program prints the volume and area so the reader can repeat the arithmetic with a pocket calculator. The formulas are shown in full, the numbers come from the specification in this record, and the demo prints both runs with SHA-256 digests so the reader can confirm the computation is deterministic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation.

Each of the 48 record pages carries its own full math proof — standard math plus a metamath gloss, stated, not machine-checked — with its demo program verifying the numbers on two runs.

4. Science proof

Shear, not crushing, is the working principle. Opposing diamond teeth pass each other with a small clearance, and the edges cut dried flower along its natural fracture planes. Aluminium 6061 is chosen because it is light, machines cleanly, and its anodised surface resists corrosion from plant resins. The teeth stay sharp because the load is spread across 49 teeth and each tooth meets only a small piece of herb at a time. Ground particles fall through sized holes in the grinding plate by gravity once they are smaller than the hole diameter, which is why a dozen steady turns produces a fairly uniform particle size instead of powder. The 100-micron mesh in a four-piece grinder is a sieve: particles finer than 100 microns, including the small resin glands called trichomes, pass through, while larger ground herb stays above. Friction is low because the herb is dry and brittle, and the magnet lid keeps the halves aligned so the teeth never clash metal on metal. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Skeptical limit, stated in every record: the science proof explains the physics of the mechanism, not a verdict on the practice; it makes no claim about health, safety, or benefit, and gives no advice about how much of anything to use. Dimensions describe a common example drawn from the cited sources; makers vary sizes, materials, and tolerances.
