Section 1
Description and history

The Marijuana Supplies Database is the public record of the supplies and equipment used to prepare, store, measure, and clean marijuana supplies — grinders, vaporizers, pipes, bongs, bowls, rolling supplies, storage, scales, cleaning tools, ashtrays, and grow equipment sold as products. It holds 48 full public records: 24 from Wave 1 (grinding, vaporizing, smoking, rolling, storage, measuring, cleaning, concentrates) and 24 from Wave 2 (bong types, bowl types, ashtrays, smoke-shop tools, and grow equipment as products). Every record is a complete file: description, history, specification (materials, dimensions, mechanism, use, care, variants), science proof, math proof, a working demo, a full program, a boundless generator (scales A1/A2/A3, ceiling 1,000,000,000 scales per file, generated on demand — not stored files), a file AI, and cited sources. A Signature edition (marijuana-supplies-signature, JAH-MJSS-000001–000048) sits beside it: 48 Signature-made designs of the same supplies, labeled design-study throughout, never presented as real products.

The history of these supplies is the history of small hand tools. The herb grinder as a two-half cylindrical shredder is documented in the early 1900s, with William Wingfield and John Balding of Boort, Victoria, Australia patenting a novel grinding device in 1905, and the form was popularised in the 1960s as cannabis use spread; early grinders were simple wood or metal cylinders, and the modern four-piece aluminium grinder with a kief screen is a later refinement of that same two-half idea (record JAH-MJS-000001, citing Wikipedia — Herb grinder, opened live and spot-checked by the package coordinator). The manually-operated cigarette-rolling machine behind the rolling-machine record has a real expired patent, US3011498A (1959 filing, roller + apron + gummed-edge reservoir), cited by record JAH-MJS-000010 and opened live at Google Patents by the coordinator. Each of the other 46 records carries its own history in the same pattern: the public form, how it changed, and the sources behind each stage. The database does not invent that history; it records what the cited sources establish and says plainly where a figure is a typical value or a midpoint between guides that disagree.

What this website is for: one public place where every supply in the database can be found, read in full, checked by running its demo, scaled by its generator, and asked about through its AI — with the Signature designs one click away and clearly labeled as designs.

Section 3
Math proof (standard math + metamath)

The database's mathematics is worked per record from each record's own dimensions, and it is checkable with a pocket calculator. The worked example below is the Manual Herb Grinder (JAH-MJS-000001), the first record; every other record follows the same pattern with its own formula (its formal_equation and scientific_equation fields).

Standard math — grinding-chamber volume (cylinder):

V = π · (d / 2)² · h

With the record's dimensions d = 63.0 mm, h = 28.0 mm:

  • r = d / 2 = 31.5 mm
  • V = π · 31.5² · 28.0 = 87,282.87 mm³ = 87.28 cm³

Screen area (circle): A_screen = π · (d_screen / 2)² = π · 28.0² = 2,463.01 mm² (screen diameter 56.0 mm).

Tooth density: 49 teeth ÷ (π · 31.5² / 100) cm² = 1.57 teeth per cm².

Cutting passes: 49 teeth × 12 turns = 588 passes in the worked example.

The demo program (demos/grinder_public_demo.py in this pilot) computes exactly these numbers, runs twice, and prints matching SHA-256 digests with "Deterministic: MATCH". Its stdout is recorded in PILOT-REPORT.md.

Section 4
Science proof

The physics in this database is the physics of small hand tools, worked per record. The governing principles, each established in its record and its cited sources:

  • Shear, not crushing (grinders): opposing diamond teeth pass each other with a small clearance, and the edges cut dried flower along its natural fracture planes. Aluminium 6061 is light, machines cleanly, and its anodised surface resists corrosion from plant resins. The load spreads across 49 teeth, so each tooth meets only a small piece at a time. Ground particles fall through sized holes by gravity once smaller than the hole diameter, which is why a dozen steady turns gives a fairly uniform particle size instead of powder. A 100-micron mesh is a sieve: particles finer than 100 microns pass through, larger ground herb stays above (JAH-MJS-000001).
  • Water filtration and cooling (bongs): water absorbs heat well — a millilitre of water takes far more heat to warm by one degree than a millilitre of glass — and a beaker's wide base holds more water than any other single-chamber shape, so smoke has more water to bubble through and more room to cool; the low, wide footprint keeps the centre of gravity low (JAH-MJS-000025, citing Thick Ass Glass and Dankstop).
  • Ground-glass joints (bowls and bongs): the taper itself is the seal — two ground surfaces pressed together by friction, with no gasket and no thread; joints are 14 mm or 18 mm tapers (JAH-MJS-000025, JAH-MJS-000031).
  • Skeptical limit, stated in every record: the science proof explains the physics of the mechanism, not a verdict on the practice; it makes no claim about health, safety, or benefit, and gives no advice about how much of anything to use. Dimensions describe a common example drawn from the cited sources; makers vary sizes, materials, and tolerances.
