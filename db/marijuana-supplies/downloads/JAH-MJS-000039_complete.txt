SCIENCE PROOF
A silicone ashtray works by heat tolerance, elasticity, and a contact seal. Silicone rubber tolerates continuous heat up to about 230 degrees C, the figure the cited supplier guides give, because its silicon-oxygen backbone does not break down the way a carbon backbone does, so a hot ember on the surface chars the ash but not the dish. Its density is near 1.15 grams per cubic centimetre and its specific heat near 1.46 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish and lid of about 124 grams store roughly 36,200 joules when heated by 200 kelvin, and its thermal conductivity is low, so the outside stays cool to touch while the inside takes the heat. Silicone is elastic, so the lid lip stretches over the rim and springs back to seal, and it is non-porous, so tar and smell sit on the surface instead of soaking in. The dish is a shallow cylinder, so its capacity is pi times radius squared times depth, and the lid is a flat disc whose volume is pi times radius squared times thickness.
