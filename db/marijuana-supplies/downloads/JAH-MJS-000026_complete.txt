The Straight-Tube Bong

The straight-tube bong is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a straight-tube bong that works by smoke from the bowl travels down a stem below the water line in a single straight cylinder, bubbles up through the water, and is drawn straight to the mouthpiece with very little drag. It is made from borosilicate glass tube and flat foot, glass downstem and bowl, water as the filtration medium, and a common example measures 320 mm tall, tube 50 mm diameter, flat foot 70 mm diameter, water depth 45 mm. The straight-tube bong is the oldest and simplest water-pipe shape: one cylinder from foot to mouthpiece, standing on a flat disc base. It descends from the bamboo water pipes of Asia and the hookah, and the word bong itself is adapted from the Thai baung, meaning a wooden cylinder or pipe cut from bamboo. A 1981 United States patent (4,253,475) describes the classic form in detail: a single vertical pipe about 1.5 inches in diameter sealed at the bottom, a bowl on a tube leading in near the bottom, water just above the junction, and a small hole above the water line to clear the chamber. Glass straight tubes became common in the twentieth century as borosilicate glass made heat-resistant water chambers practical. Trade guides describe the straight tube as the simplest design there is, with the fastest clear and the least drag, and the easiest shape to clean. SCIENCE PROOF
The straight tube works by keeping the smoke path short and straight. A cylinder has the same cross-section all the way up, so the smoke meets no narrowing, no bend, and no extra chamber on its way to the mouthpiece, and the draw feels direct because the pressure drop along a straight smooth tube is small. The water volume is the cylinder cross-section times the water depth, and because the tube is narrow the water volume is small: about 88 millilitres in the common example, against about 233 millilitres for a beaker of similar height. That small water volume is both the strength and the weakness of the shape: less water means less drag and a faster clear, but also less cooling, because the smoke spends less time in contact with water. The draw pressure at the downstem tip is water density times gravity times depth, only a few hundred pascals, so the pull needed to start the bubbles is small. Borosilicate glass is used for the same reason as in the beaker: low thermal expansion at the hot bowl joint and a clean flavour. Multiplying that area by the water depth of 45.0 mm gives the water volume, and multiplying it by the full height of 320.0 mm gives the total tube volume, both reported in millilitres. Subtracting the water volume from the total tube volume gives the chamber air volume above the water. import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 50.0; h = 320.0; water_h = 45.0; foot = 70.0
    area = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2
    water_mm3 = area * water_h
    total_mm3 = area * h
    air_mm3 = total_mm3 - water_mm3
    pressure_pa = 1000.0 * 9.81 * (water_h / 1000.0)
    return {"water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "tube_volume_mL": round(total_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "chamber_air_mL": round(air_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "draw_pressure_Pa": round(pressure_pa, 2), "water_depth_mm": water_h, "tube_diameter_mm": d}
def main():
