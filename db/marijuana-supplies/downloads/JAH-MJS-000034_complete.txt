The ash-catcher bowl works by putting a small chamber between the bowl and the bong, so ash and coarse debris fall into the chamber instead of entering the main water. It is made from borosilicate glass, with a built-in flower bowl on top and a 14 mm male joint at 90 degrees below, and a common example has a bowl about 20 mm across and 16 mm deep, above a catcher chamber about 30 mm across that holds about 8.5 millilitres of water at a 60 percent fill. Smoke passes from the bowl down through the chamber, where the water and the chamber walls catch ash, and then into the bong. The dry form of the same piece uses no water at all and catches ash in the dry chamber. SCIENCE PROOF
An ash catcher works by gravity and by impaction, two pieces of physics that need no electronics. Ash particles leaving the bowl are heavier than smoke, so when the smoke stream turns and slows in the wider catcher chamber, the ash falls out of the stream and lands in the water or on the chamber floor, while the gas keeps moving. The chamber cross-section is about 707 square millimetres, far wider than the 14 mm joint, so the smoke slows by roughly the ratio of the areas and the particles have time to drop. Water in a wet catcher adds a second trap: particles that reach the water surface are wetted and stay there instead of bouncing back into the stream. Borosilicate glass matters because it has a low coefficient of thermal expansion, about 3.3 times ten to the minus six per kelvin, so it tolerates the hot bowl end and the cool joint without cracking, and because glass is chemically inert it adds no flavour of its own. The bowl is a shallow cylinder, so its volume is pi times radius squared times depth, and the chamber is a wider cylinder whose water volume is its fill fraction times its full volume. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

HISTORY
The ash catcher is a recent addition to bong design, because it answers a cleaning problem that only appears once a bong is used often: ash and loose material travel down the downstem and foul the main water chamber, so the whole piece needs cleaning after every few sessions. The first ash catchers were separate glass chambers that sat between the bowl and the bong, and the ash-catcher bowl folds the two parts into one piece by building the bowl into the top of the catcher chamber. The combined form spread through smoke shops in the 2010s as borosilicate glassblowing made the extra chamber cheap to produce, and it is now sold in 14 mm and 18 mm joints at 45 and 90 degree angles to match beaker and straight-tube bongs. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. The sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage, and the history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

import hashlib, math
def compute():
    bowl_d = 20.0; bowl_h = 16.0; ch_d = 30.0; water_h = 20.0; fill = 0.60; pack = 0.15; joint = 14.0
    bowl_mm3 = math.pi * (bowl_d / 2.0) ** 2 * bowl_h
    bowl_cm3 = bowl_mm3 / 1000.0
    capacity_g = bowl_cm3 * pack
    ch_mm3 = math.pi * (ch_d / 2.0) ** 2 * water_h
    ch_cm3 = ch_mm3 / 1000.0
    water_ml = ch_cm3 * fill
    trap_mm2 = math.pi * (ch_d / 2.0) ** 2
    return {"bowl_volume_mm3": round(bowl_mm3, 2), "bowl_volume_cm3": round(bowl_cm3, 2), "herb_capacity_g": round(capacity_g, 2), "chamber_volume_cm3": round(ch_cm3, 2), "water_ml": round(water_ml, 2), "water_mass_g": round(water_ml, 2), "trap_area_mm2": round(trap_mm2, 2), "joint_mm": joint}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['bowl_volume_mm3', 'bowl_volume_cm3', 'herb_capacity_g', 'chamber_volume_cm3', 'water_ml', 'water_mass_g', 'trap_area_mm2', 'joint_mm']:
