The Pipe Cleaning Kit
Record JAH-MJS-000017 — public
JCC v1 code: JAHDB/marijuana-supplies#JAH-MJS-000017

The Pipe Cleaning Kit is a cleaning and maintenance supply for smoking pipes, and this record describes the standard kit as it is actually sold and used. The kit contains bristle pipe cleaners, soft cotton pipe cleaners, a shank brush, a 3-in-1 Czech-style pipe tool (tamper, reamer, pick), a bowl scraper and a lint-free cloth, held in a zippered case. Its parts are made from cotton fibre, nylon bristles, twisted steel wire, stainless steel, brass, beechwood handle, chosen because each material suits its own job: cotton absorbs, nylon scrubs, steel scrapes without bending, and brass works on stubborn buildup without gouging. The kit measures cleaners 150 mm long; shank brush 120 mm long with a 12 mm brush head; 3-in-1 tool 95 mm long; case 180 mm by 120 mm by 35 mm. In use, the twisted-wire cleaner carries absorbent cotton through the stem airway, the bristle cleaner scrubs the airway wall, the shank brush sweeps the shank, and the reamer shaves the carbon cake inside the bowl back to an even layer. That combination is what makes the kit more than a loose handful of tools: every step of a full clean, from the first dry pass through the stem to the last wipe of the outside, has a tool made for it. Routine use is simple. After the pipe has cooled, run a dry cleaner through the stem until it comes out clean, brush the shank, ream the bowl only when the cake is thicker than about 1.5 mm, and wipe the outside with the cloth. Care of the kit matters as much as care of the pipe. Wash the shank brush in warm soapy water and let it dry, replace pipe cleaners after one use, oil the wooden handle lightly, and keep the steel tools dry so they do not rust. The common variants are the travel kit with 10 cleaners; bench kit with 50 cleaners and an adjustable reamer; glass-only kit with nylon brushes and no metal reamer. Legal note: laws on cannabis and cannabis accessories vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

SCIENCE PROOF
The science of this supply is the science of dissolving, scrubbing, and separating, and each part of the kit uses a different piece of it. Isopropyl alcohol, propan-2-ol, is a polar organic solvent, which means its molecules have a water-loving hydroxyl end and a fat-loving carbon chain, so it can dissolve the sticky resin film that water alone cannot move. Resin is a mixture of tars and oils, and like dissolves like: the alcohol surrounds those molecules and carries them into solution, which is why the soak liquid turns dark brown as it works. Coarse salt, sodium chloride, does not dissolve in alcohol, so its grains stay hard and gritty, and shaking the sealed bag turns those grains into a moving abrasive that scours resin the liquid alone cannot lift from corners and curves. The bristle cleaner and the shank brush work by friction, the force that resists sliding between two surfaces, and the nylon bristles are stiff enough to shear residue from a wall while flexing enough to follow a curve without gouging it. Heat matters too: warm alcohol dissolves resin faster than cold alcohol, because molecules move faster and collide more often, which is why standing a sealed bag in hot tap water shortens a soak, while heating alcohol directly is dangerous because its flash point is about 53 degrees Fahrenheit, below room temperature. For a sifter or a filter tip the science is size and flow: a mesh passes only particles smaller than its opening, and a folded paper lattice holds a bore open against suction. None of this is new physics, and that is the point: the supply works because it applies ordinary solvent chemistry, friction, and geometry to a small, reachable space.

HISTORY
Pipe cleaners began as craft supplies, lengths of wire twisted around cotton, and pipe smokers adopted them because nothing else could follow the narrow curved airway of a stem. The Czech 3-in-1 tool, combining tamper, reamer and pick in one folding piece, became the standard pocket companion for the same reason: one tool answered three small jobs. Over time makers added the shank brush, a short stiff brush sized for the shank bore, and bristle cleaners, in which nylon bristles are twisted into the cotton to give the cleaner a scrubbing edge. Modern kits gather all of these into one case so that routine cleaning, which used to mean hunting through drawers for a cleaner and a pick, becomes a single short routine done in the same place every time.

GRIP_MM = 30
STEM_AIRWAY_MM = 120
BRUSH_DIA_MM = 12
BRUSH_LEN_MM = 25
CLEANERS_PER_KIT = 24

def compute(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    residue = 100.0
    passes = 0
    total_cleaner_mm = 0
    while residue > 1.0 and passes < 12:
        removed = residue * rng.uniform(0.35, 0.55)
        residue -= removed
        passes += 1
        total_cleaner_mm += CLEANER_LENGTH_MM
    reach = CLEANER_LENGTH_MM - GRIP_MM
    coverage = round(100.0 * reach / STEM_AIRWAY_MM, 1)
    brush_area = round(math.pi * BRUSH_DIA_MM * BRUSH_LEN_MM, 1)
    out = {

        "usable_reach_mm": reach,
        "airway_length_mm": STEM_AIRWAY_MM,
        "airway_coverage_percent": coverage,
        "passes_to_clean": passes,
        "residue_remaining_percent": round(residue, 2),
        "cleaner_length_used_mm": total_cleaner_mm,
        "brush_contact_area_mm2": brush_area,
        "cleaners_in_kit": CLEANERS_PER_KIT,
    }
    return out
