The Recycler Bong
Record JAH-MJS-000028 — public
JCC v1 code: JAHDB/marijuana-supplies#JAH-MJS-000028

The recycler bong is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a recycler bong that works by smoke and water circulate together in a loop: the percolator diffuses smoke in the main chamber, an uptake tube carries water and smoke up to a second chamber where they separate, and a drain tube returns the water to the main chamber. It is made from borosilicate glass main chamber, secondary chamber, uptake tube and drain tube, showerhead percolator, glass downstem and bowl, water as the filtration and circulation medium, and a common example measures 340 mm tall, main chamber 300 ml, secondary chamber 150 ml, uptake tube 10 mm diameter, drain tube 8 mm diameter, water 180 ml in total. The recycler bong is a recent design, developed in the 2010s as glassblowers working on concentrate rigs looked for a way to keep water moving instead of letting it sit still. The idea borrows from the Klein bottle, a mathematical shape with no inside or outside, and some recycler designs are still called Klein recyclers for the sealed loop their glass forms. Early recyclers were small dab rigs; the loop layout then spread to flower bongs, with two chambers joined by an uptake tube and a drain tube so water circulates while smoke travels to the mouthpiece. Trade guides describe the loop as doing three jobs at once: it keeps the percolator covered, it keeps water off the user's lips, and it keeps smoke bubbles surrounded by water longer. The design is now a recognised type in its own right, sold alongside beakers and straight tubes. SCIENCE PROOF
The recycler works by keeping water in motion. In a plain bong the water sits still and the smoke passes through it once; in a recycler the draw creates a pressure difference that pulls water up the uptake tube into the secondary chamber, where the water and the smoke separate because water is far denser than smoke and falls back under gravity. The drain tube carries that water back to the main chamber, so the percolator never runs dry during a long pull, which is the failure that makes a plain percolator gurgle and splash. The loop time is the loop water volume divided by the draw flow rate: at a draw of 1.2 litres per minute the 180 millilitres of water in the loop takes about nine seconds to make one full circuit. The uptake tube must be wider than the drain tube, because it carries water and smoke together while the drain carries water alone, and a drain that is too narrow will back up and flood the upper chamber. The smoke is not filtered a second time by the loop: the loop's jobs are to keep the percolator covered, to keep water off the lips, and to keep each bubble surrounded by water longer, which is where the extra cooling comes from. import hashlib, math
def compute():
    main_mL = 120.0; sec_mL = 60.0; up_d = 10.0; dr_d = 8.0; up_len = 180.0; dr_len = 150.0; draw = 1.2
    up_area = math.pi * (up_d / 2.0) ** 2
    dr_area = math.pi * (dr_d / 2.0) ** 2
    loop_mL = main_mL + sec_mL
    flow_mL_s = draw * 1000.0 / 60.0
    loop_time = loop_mL / flow_mL_s
    up_vol = up_area * up_len / 1000.0
    return {"main_water_mL": main_mL, "secondary_water_mL": sec_mL, "loop_water_mL": round(loop_mL, 2), "uptake_area_mm2": round(up_area, 2), "drain_area_mm2": round(dr_area, 2), "loop_time_s": round(loop_time, 2), "uptake_volume_mL": round(up_vol, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
