SCIENCE PROOF
A drying rack works by giving air access to material from every direction at once. Each tier is a flat disc of open mesh, so air passes through the floor of the chamber as well as around its sides, and the material is spread in a single layer instead of piled in a heap where the middle stays damp. The mesh is non-toxic polyester, chosen because it is breathable, washable, and strong enough to hold a load without stretching, and the frame is flexible steel wire, chosen because it holds the disc open under load and still folds flat for storage. HISTORY
The drying rack is a hanging tower of mesh tiers that holds plant material in the air so it can dry evenly, and it descends from the herb-drying frames and net bags that gardeners have used for centuries. The modern form is a collapsible cylinder of polyester mesh over a flexible steel wireframe, with zippered chambers stacked one above another and a hook at the top so the whole tower hangs from a bar. The standard example in this record is the six-tier rack, 600 millimetres across and 1,194 millimetres long when hanging, with each chamber 171 millimetres high; a four-tier version, 457 millimetres across, fits a 2 by 2 tent, and eight-tier versions reach 1,700 millimetres.
