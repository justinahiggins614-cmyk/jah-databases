The Dab Tool Set
Record JAH-MJS-000021 — public
JCC v1 code: JAHDB/marijuana-supplies#JAH-MJS-000021

The Dab Tool Set is a cleaning and maintenance supply for smoking pipes, and this record describes the standard kit as it is actually sold and used. The kit contains five interchangeable tools on one handle: a flat paddle, a pointed pick, a scoop, a spatula blade and a shovel, plus a glass bubble carb cap, a silicone mat and a zippered case. Its parts are made from grade 2 titanium tips, stainless steel handle, borosilicate glass carb cap, food-grade silicone mat, chosen because each material suits its own job: cotton absorbs, nylon scrubs, steel scrapes without bending, and brass works on stubborn buildup without gouging. The kit measures handle 100 mm long; each tool head 30 mm long; paddle 8 mm wide; pick tip 1 mm across; carb cap 25 mm across; case 170 mm by 110 mm by 30 mm. In use, the tool tip picks up, scoops or spreads a measured piece of concentrate and carries it to the heated banger, and the carb cap then covers the banger to restrict airflow, lower the pressure inside, and direct vapour across the hot surface. That combination is what makes the kit more than a loose handful of tools: every step of a full clean, from the first dry pass through the stem to the last wipe of the outside, has a tool made for it. Routine use is simple. Choose the tip that matches the concentrate texture, paddle for crumbly material and pick for sticky material, load the banger without touching the hot glass, cap it, and wipe the tip clean with a cotton swab while it is still warm. Care of the kit matters as much as care of the pipe. Wipe tips after every session, soak metal tips in isopropyl alcohol when residue hardens, never use a steel tool on a hot quartz banger with force, since a hard scrape can chip it, and store tips in the case so the fine points do not bend. The common variants are the single titanium dabber; dabber and carb cap combo tool; multi-tool folding set; glass sword dabber with a titanium tip. Legal note: laws on cannabis and cannabis accessories vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

SCIENCE PROOF
The science of this supply is the science of dissolving, scrubbing, and separating, and each part of the kit uses a different piece of it. Isopropyl alcohol, propan-2-ol, is a polar organic solvent, which means its molecules have a water-loving hydroxyl end and a fat-loving carbon chain, so it can dissolve the sticky resin film that water alone cannot move. Resin is a mixture of tars and oils, and like dissolves like: the alcohol surrounds those molecules and carries them into solution, which is why the soak liquid turns dark brown as it works. Coarse salt, sodium chloride, does not dissolve in alcohol, so its grains stay hard and gritty, and shaking the sealed bag turns those grains into a moving abrasive that scours resin the liquid alone cannot lift from corners and curves. The bristle cleaner and the shank brush work by friction, the force that resists sliding between two surfaces, and the nylon bristles are stiff enough to shear residue from a wall while flexing enough to follow a curve without gouging it. Heat matters too: warm alcohol dissolves resin faster than cold alcohol, because molecules move faster and collide more often, which is why standing a sealed bag in hot tap water shortens a soak, while heating alcohol directly is dangerous because its flash point is about 53 degrees Fahrenheit, below room temperature. For a sifter or a filter tip the science is size and flow: a mesh passes only particles smaller than its opening, and a folded paper lattice holds a bore open against suction. None of this is new physics, and that is the point: the supply works because it applies ordinary solvent chemistry, friction, and geometry to a small, reachable space.

HISTORY
Dabbing began with improvised tools, paper clips and dental picks, because the job is awkward: move a sticky piece of concentrate onto a surface hot enough to vaporise it without burning a finger. Dedicated dabbers replaced them as soon as concentrates became common, and makers settled on titanium and stainless steel because both survive heat, wipe clean, and do not flake into the material. The carb cap arrived as the second half of the set, a small lid that sits on the banger after loading and traps heat while steering air across the surface, which lets the concentrate vaporise more completely at lower temperatures. Kits then gathered the pieces together, interchangeable tips for different concentrate textures, a cap, a mat to catch drips, and a case, so that a full session needs nothing borrowed from another drawer.
