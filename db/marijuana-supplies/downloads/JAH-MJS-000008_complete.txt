SCIENCE PROOF
A cone is a frustum, a cone with its tip cut off, and its shape does real work. The wide end holds more material than the narrow end, so the contents pack more densely toward the filter and more loosely at the tip, which helps the roll burn evenly from the wide end down. The filter tip, or crutch, is a rolled cylinder of paper: it holds the cone open, stops loose material from reaching the mouth, and gives the user something cool to hold. The paper is a trapezoid before rolling, cut so the gum edge runs straight and the opposite edge runs at an angle, which is what makes the finished tube taper instead of running parallel. Gum arabic seals the seam as it dries. The volume of a frustum is one third of pi times height times the sum of the two end radii squared plus their product, and multiplying that volume by the packing density of ground herb gives an estimate of the mass the cone can hold.
