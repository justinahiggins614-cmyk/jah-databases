The Isopropyl Alcohol Cleaning Soak
Record JAH-MJS-000018 — public
JCC v1 code: JAHDB/marijuana-supplies#JAH-MJS-000018

The Isopropyl Alcohol Cleaning Soak is a cleaning and maintenance supply for smoking pipes, and this record describes the standard kit as it is actually sold and used. The kit contains a bottle of 91% or 99% isopropyl alcohol, coarse kosher salt, a sealable bag or lidded container, pipe cleaners and cotton swabs. Its parts are made from isopropyl alcohol (propan-2-ol), coarse kosher salt (sodium chloride), low-density polyethylene bag, borosilicate glass pipe, chosen because each material suits its own job: cotton absorbs, nylon scrubs, steel scrapes without bending, and brass works on stubborn buildup without gouging. The kit measures soak container 200 mm by 150 mm by 80 mm; 500 ml bottle of 91% isopropyl alcohol; 1 tablespoon (about 18 g) of coarse salt per soak. In use, isopropyl alcohol is a polar solvent that dissolves the sticky resin film, while coarse salt stays undissolved in the alcohol and acts as a moving abrasive that scours resin the liquid alone cannot lift. That combination is what makes the kit more than a loose handful of tools: every step of a full clean, from the first dry pass through the stem to the last wipe of the outside, has a tool made for it. Routine use is simple. Let the pipe cool, tap out loose ash, place it in the bag, pour in enough alcohol to submerge it, add about a tablespoon of coarse salt, seal the bag, shake for one to two minutes, soak for 30 minutes to 12 hours, then rinse with warm water until no alcohol smell remains. Care of the kit matters as much as care of the pipe. Let the pipe dry completely before any use, since alcohol left inside is flammable; store alcohol away from flames, lighters and heat; never heat alcohol on a stove or in a microwave; never use this soak on silicone, which the alcohol degrades, or on wood, which it can crack and strip. The common variants are the quick 15-minute shake clean for light buildup; overnight soak for a neglected piece; alcohol-only soak without salt for delicate thin glass; baking-soda and vinegar backup method when no alcohol is available. Legal note: laws on cannabis and cannabis accessories vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

SCIENCE PROOF
The science of this supply is the science of dissolving, scrubbing, and separating, and each part of the kit uses a different piece of it. Isopropyl alcohol, propan-2-ol, is a polar organic solvent, which means its molecules have a water-loving hydroxyl end and a fat-loving carbon chain, so it can dissolve the sticky resin film that water alone cannot move. Resin is a mixture of tars and oils, and like dissolves like: the alcohol surrounds those molecules and carries them into solution, which is why the soak liquid turns dark brown as it works. Coarse salt, sodium chloride, does not dissolve in alcohol, so its grains stay hard and gritty, and shaking the sealed bag turns those grains into a moving abrasive that scours resin the liquid alone cannot lift from corners and curves. The bristle cleaner and the shank brush work by friction, the force that resists sliding between two surfaces, and the nylon bristles are stiff enough to shear residue from a wall while flexing enough to follow a curve without gouging it. Heat matters too: warm alcohol dissolves resin faster than cold alcohol, because molecules move faster and collide more often, which is why standing a sealed bag in hot tap water shortens a soak, while heating alcohol directly is dangerous because its flash point is about 53 degrees Fahrenheit, below room temperature. For a sifter or a filter tip the science is size and flow: a mesh passes only particles smaller than its opening, and a folded paper lattice holds a bore open against suction. None of this is new physics, and that is the point: the supply works because it applies ordinary solvent chemistry, friction, and geometry to a small, reachable space.

HISTORY
Rubbing alcohol has been a household solvent for more than a century, and glass pipe owners adopted it because resin, the sticky tar-like film left behind after use, dissolves in alcohol while water barely touches it. The salt addition came from kitchen practice: coarse salt does not dissolve in alcohol, so it stays gritty and works like sand in a bottle. Guides converged on the same recipe, 91% or 99% alcohol with about a tablespoon of coarse salt in a sealed bag, shaken and then left to soak, because it uses only drugstore supplies and needs no special equipment. The method spread through forums and care guides until it became the default deep-clean for glass, with the warnings attached later: alcohol is flammable, it ruins silicone and wood, and a piece must be fully dry before it is used again.

import hashlib, json, math, random

ALCOHOL_PERCENT = 91
ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML = 250
SALT_GRAMS = 18
SOAK_LIGHT_MIN = 30
SOAK_HEAVY_MIN = 720
SHAKE_SECONDS = 90

def compute(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    water_fraction = 1.0 - ALCOHOL_PERCENT / 100.0
    pure_alcohol_ml = round(ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML * ALCOHOL_PERCENT / 100.0, 1)
    water_ml = round(ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML * water_fraction, 1)
    salt_ratio = round(SALT_GRAMS / ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML, 3)
    k = 0.02 + 0.0006 * ALCOHOL_PERCENT
    eff_light = round(100.0 * (1.0 - math.exp(-k * SOAK_LIGHT_MIN)), 1)
    eff_heavy = round(100.0 * (1.0 - math.exp(-k * SOAK_HEAVY_MIN)), 1)
    evap_ml_per_hour = round(ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML * 0.04 * rng.uniform(0.9, 1.1), 1)
    shakes = SHAKE_SECONDS // 30
    out = {
        "alcohol_percent": ALCOHOL_PERCENT,
        "solution_volume_ml": ALCOHOL_VOLUME_ML,
        "pure_alcohol_ml": pure_alcohol_ml,
        "water_in_solution_ml": water_ml,
        "salt_grams": SALT_GRAMS,
        "salt_grams_per_ml": salt_ratio,
        "shake_blocks_of_30s": shakes,
        "resin_dissolved_light_soak_percent": eff_light,
        "resin_dissolved_heavy_soak_percent": eff_heavy,
        "evaporation_ml_per_hour": evap_ml_per_hour,
    }
    return out
