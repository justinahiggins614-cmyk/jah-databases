SCIENCE PROOF
A spoon pipe is a direct-flow combustion pipe with a manual air valve, the carb. Lighting the herb starts combustion, and the draw creates a pressure drop along the stem: air at atmospheric pressure enters through the bowl, passes over the burning herb, and the hot gases travel down the stem to the mouth. Covering the carb forces all incoming air through the bowl, which keeps the herb burning and fills the stem and bowl chamber with smoke. Releasing the carb opens a low-resistance path for fresh air, which sweeps the stored smoke into the mouth in one clear. Borosilicate glass matters because it has a low coefficient of thermal expansion, so it tolerates the hot bowl end and the cool mouthpiece end without cracking, and because glass is chemically inert, it adds no flavour of its own. The bowl is a shallow cylinder, so its volume is pi times radius squared times depth, and the stem is a long narrow cylinder whose volume sets how much smoke the pipe holds between the bowl and the mouth.
