Filter Tips (Crutches)

Filter Tips (Crutches) is a cleaning and maintenance supply for smoking pipes, and this record describes the standard kit as it is actually sold and used. The kit contains a booklet of 50 pre-cut unbleached hemp-fibre tips, each strip 50 mm long and 15 mm wide, plus 10 pre-rolled tips and a folding guide card. Its parts are made from unbleached hemp-fibre paper, 150 gsm card-weight paper, vegetable-based gum on pre-rolled tips, chosen because each material suits its own job: cotton absorbs, nylon scrubs, steel scrapes without bending, and brass works on stubborn buildup without gouging. The kit measures strip 50 mm long by 15 mm wide; finished tip 6 mm in diameter and 15 mm long; booklet 55 mm by 40 mm; pack of 50 tips. In use, the strip is folded into an accordion of three or four folds and rolled into a cylinder, and the folded core acts as a lattice that holds the mouthpiece open under suction while blocking loose plant matter. That combination is what makes the kit more than a loose handful of tools: every step of a full clean, from the first dry pass through the stem to the last wipe of the outside, has a tool made for it. Routine use is simple. Fold one end of the strip back and forth three or four times into a w or m shape, roll the rest of the strip tightly around the folds, place the finished tip at the mouthpiece end of the rolling paper, and build the roll around it. Care of the kit matters as much as care of the pipe. Keep tips dry, since damp paper collapses; use one tip per roll and discard it afterwards; do not use glossy, plastic-coated or adhesive-coated paper, which can release unwanted substances when heated. The common variants are the pre-rolled tips; extra-wide tips for large rolls; slim tips at 5 mm diameter; reusable glass tips; wooden tips. SCIENCE PROOF
The science of this supply is the science of dissolving, scrubbing, and separating, and each part of the kit uses a different piece of it. Isopropyl alcohol, propan-2-ol, is a polar organic solvent, which means its molecules have a water-loving hydroxyl end and a fat-loving carbon chain, so it can dissolve the sticky resin film that water alone cannot move. Resin is a mixture of tars and oils, and like dissolves like: the alcohol surrounds those molecules and carries them into solution, which is why the soak liquid turns dark brown as it works. Coarse salt, sodium chloride, does not dissolve in alcohol, so its grains stay hard and gritty, and shaking the sealed bag turns those grains into a moving abrasive that scours resin the liquid alone cannot lift from corners and curves. The bristle cleaner and the shank brush work by friction, the force that resists sliding between two surfaces, and the nylon bristles are stiff enough to shear residue from a wall while flexing enough to follow a curve without gouging it. Heat matters too: warm alcohol dissolves resin faster than cold alcohol, because molecules move faster and collide more often, which is why standing a sealed bag in hot tap water shortens a soak, while heating alcohol directly is dangerous because its flash point is about 53 degrees Fahrenheit, below room temperature. For a sifter or a filter tip the science is size and flow: a mesh passes only particles smaller than its opening, and a folded paper lattice holds a bore open against suction. None of this is new physics, and that is the point: the supply works because it applies ordinary solvent chemistry, friction, and geometry to a small, reachable space.

HISTORY
The crutch, also called a filter tip or simply a tip, appeared wherever hand-rolled joints were smoked, because a roll without one has two faults: the mouth end collapses when it gets damp, and loose material reaches the mouth. Early rollers tore strips from card and rolled them into spirals, but the spiral leaves a hole in the middle that lets plant matter through. The accordion fold, three or four folds pressed into a W or M shape before rolling, fixed that: the folded core forms a lattice that blocks particles while leaving air channels open. Brands such as RAW later standardised the idea into pre-cut booklets of unbleached hemp-fibre paper, and pre-rolled tips and reusable glass tips followed. The name itself is rolling slang, a crutch being the support the joint leans on, and the distinction matters: a paper crutch is a structural spacer, not a cigarette-style filter, since it does not absorb compounds from the smoke passing through it.
