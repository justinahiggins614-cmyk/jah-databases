The Beaker Bong
Record JAH-MJS-000025 — public
JCC v1 code: JAHDB/marijuana-supplies#JAH-MJS-000025

The beaker bong is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a beaker bong that works by smoke from the bowl travels down a stem below the water line in a wide flared base, bubbles up through the water, collects in the tall neck, and is drawn to the mouthpiece. It is made from borosilicate glass tube, base, downstem, and bowl; plain water as the filtration medium; optional ice pinches in the neck, and a common example measures 300 mm tall, flared base 100 mm diameter at the foot, neck 50 mm diameter, water depth 40 mm in the base. The beaker bong takes its shape from the laboratory beaker, the wide-based glass vessel chemists have used since the nineteenth century, and from the water pipe itself, whose word comes from the Thai baung, a wooden cylinder or pipe cut from bamboo. Glass water pipes became common in the twentieth century as borosilicate glass made a heat-resistant, clean-tasting water chamber practical, and a 1981 United States patent (4,253,475) describes the classic form in detail: a single vertical pipe sealed at the bottom, a bowl on a tube leading in near the bottom, and water just above the junction. The beaker base was one of the first variations makers tried, because the flared foot spreads the weight low and wide. Trade guides still call the beaker the most stable shape and the usual first bong, and the wide base holds more water than a straight tube of the same height. SCIENCE PROOF
The beaker works by geometry. Its base is a frustum, a cone with its tip cut off, so its volume grows faster than its height: the wide bottom holds most of the water while the narrow top keeps the neck slim. Water volume is the frustum volume up to the water line, and the water depth matters because the downstem must end below the surface or smoke will not bubble at all. Bubbles rising through water cool the smoke by contact: each bubble is a sphere of hot gas, and the smaller the bubble the larger its surface area for its volume, so more of the heat passes into the water. The wide base also lowers the centre of gravity, which is why the beaker resists tipping: the mass of water sits low and wide, so the tipping moment needed to lift one edge is larger than for a straight tube of the same height. Borosilicate glass is used because it expands very little when heated, so the hot bowl joint does not crack the cold base, and because it does not give flavour to the smoke. The neck above the water is a reservoir: its volume sets how much smoke one draw can hold before the chamber must be cleared. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

"""Demo: beaker bong water and chamber volumes — The Beaker Bong."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    R = 50.0; r = 25.0; base_h = 70.0; water_h = 40.0; neck_d = 50.0; neck_h = 230.0
    r_water = R - (R - r) * (water_h / base_h)
    water_mm3 = math.pi * water_h / 3.0 * (R * R + R * r_water + r_water * r_water)
    base_mm3 = math.pi * base_h / 3.0 * (R * R + R * r + r * r)
    neck_mm3 = math.pi * (neck_d / 2.0) ** 2 * neck_h
    air_mm3 = (base_mm3 - water_mm3) + neck_mm3
    return {"water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "base_volume_mL": round(base_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "neck_volume_mL": round(neck_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "chamber_air_mL": round(air_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "water_depth_mm": water_h, "base_diameter_mm": 2 * R}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["water_volume_mL", "base_volume_mL", "neck_volume_mL", "chamber_air_mL", "water_depth_mm", "base_diameter_mm"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
