1. Description and history

The humidity control pack is a sealed packet holding a saturated salt-and-water solution behind a semipermeable membrane that adds or removes water vapour to hold a closed container at a fixed relative humidity. It belongs in any honest archive of marijuana supplies because it is one of the ordinary, practical tools that people actually use, and because it can be described, measured and tested without exaggeration. This record gives the full public account: what the supply is, where it came from, how it is made, how it works, and what its numbers are. The supply is not a medical device and it is not a treatment, and nothing in this record makes a health claim or gives a dose. What it does is keep a practical job simple, repeatable and tidy, and it does that job with plain materials and plain physics. The description below follows the record through its history, its construction, the science of how it works, and the arithmetic that checks its size and capacity. Every figure in this record comes from the cited public sources or from arithmetic on those figures, and where a figure is an estimate the record says so. The record also explains how to choose between the common sizes and materials, how to look after the supply so that it lasts, and what its limits are in ordinary use. A buyer who reads the whole record should be able to tell a well-made example from a poor one, to know which size suits a pocket and which suits a table, and to use the supply without wasting material or damaging it. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

Two-way humidity control was first developed for cigars, where a dry cigar burns hot and harsh and a damp cigar will not draw, and the same problem appears in any stored plant material. The Boveda pack is the best-known example: a saturated solution of pure water and natural salts sealed inside a semipermeable membrane that lets only water vapour pass. The idea is old in principle, since a saturated salt solution in a closed space settles at a fixed relative humidity that depends on the salt, but the packet form made it practical for a jar or a bag. Cannabis storage adopted the 58 percent and 62 percent packs, with 62 percent giving a softer, stickier feel and 58 percent a slightly drier one, and the packs are now sold from 8 grams to 320 grams to match containers from a pint jar to a pound of flower. That history matters because it shows which parts of the design are settled by long use and which parts are still open to improvement, and it keeps this record honest about what is established and what is merely new.

The problem it solves

The problem the the humidity control pack solves is a practical one, and it is easy to understate. Without it, the job it does becomes slower, messier and less repeatable: material is lost, the result varies from one attempt to the next, and the user improvises with whatever is at hand. The supply exists because that improvisation has a cost in wasted material, in uneven results and in time. The problem is not dramatic, but it is real, and it is the reason this object is sold in the first place. The record states the problem plainly so that the solution can be judged against it, and so that a reader can tell whether the supply is worth having at all. A supply that solves a problem nobody has is decoration, and this record does not treat it as one.

Method

The method of this record is to describe the supply from public sources, measure it against its own numbers, and test it with arithmetic rather than with adjectives. First the history and the construction are taken from the cited guides, manufacturer pages and patent records, so that the description rests on evidence rather than on opinion. Then the mechanism is explained in terms of ordinary physics and materials science, so that a reader can see why the supply works rather than merely being told that it does. Then the demo computes the supply's size, capacity and limits from its own figures, twice, with a digest, so that the numbers can be checked by anyone who runs the program. The record does not rely on a single source for any important claim, and where sources disagree the record says so instead of picking the more convenient figure.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference pack targets 62 percent relative humidity with an accuracy of about plus or minus 2 percent, so in a truly airtight jar the humidity should sit between 62 - 2 = 60 percent and 62 + 2 = 64 percent. At 20 degrees Celsius, saturated air holds about 17.3 grams of water vapour per cubic metre, so air at 62 percent holds 17.3 x 0.62 = 10.73 grams per cubic metre. A quart jar has a headspace of about 236.5 cubic centimetres, which is 0.0002365 cubic metres, so the vapour in that headspace weighs 0.0002365 x 10.73 = 0.00254 grams. If the jar air starts at 40 percent relative humidity, it holds 17.3 x 0.40 = 6.92 grams per cubic metre, so the pack must add (10.73 - 6.92) x 0.0002365 = 0.00090 grams of vapour to the headspace alone; the far larger exchange is with the flower itself, which is why the pack is sized by flower weight, not by air volume. The size 67 pack covers 450 grams of flower, so the ratio is 450 / 67 = 6.7 grams of flower per gram of pack; the size 8 pack covers one ounce, 28.35 grams, giving 28.35 / 8 = 3.5 grams of flower per gram of pack, a more generous ratio for small jars.

4. Science proof

A humidity control pack is applied physical chemistry: a saturated salt solution has a fixed equilibrium relative humidity. In a sealed packet, the salt solution sits in equilibrium with the water vapour above it, and that equilibrium is set by the salt, not by how much solution there is. The semipermeable membrane lets water vapour through but holds liquid water and dissolved salts inside, so the pack can trade vapour with the air in the jar without leaking. If the jar air is drier than the target, water evaporates from the solution, passes through the membrane as vapour, and raises the humidity; if the jar air is wetter, vapour condenses into the solution and the humidity falls. That two-way action is what separates these packs from a damp sponge, which can only add moisture, or a desiccant, which can only remove it. The capacity is finite: every gram of water the pack gives up or takes in moves the solution away from saturation, and once the salt is fully dissolved or fully crystallised the pack can no longer hold its target. This is why a pack cannot rescue wet flower: the moisture load inside under-dried flower is far larger than the few grams of water a small pack can exchange.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view is that the sources for this supply are mostly guides, retailers and manufacturers, not laboratory studies, and their figures should be read as practical guides rather than as exact measurements. Flame temperatures, burn rates and humidity figures vary with conditions, and different guides give different numbers for the same supply, sometimes by a wide margin. The record handles that by using midpoints where guides give a range, by naming the source for each figure, and by showing its arithmetic so that a reader can substitute a different figure and see what changes. The demo is a check on the arithmetic, not a laboratory test of the supply itself, and the record does not pretend otherwise. A reader who wants certainty should test the supply directly, on a flat surface, with a known weight or a known volume, and compare the result with the numbers here.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
target_rh = 62.0
accuracy = 2.0
saturation_g_per_m3 = 17.3
headspace_ml = 236.5
size67_mass_g = 67.0
size67_capacity_g = 450.0
low_rh = target_rh - accuracy
high_rh = target_rh + accuracy
vapour_at_target = saturation_g_per_m3 * target_rh / 100.0
headspace_m3 = headspace_ml / 1000000.0
vapour_in_headspace_g = headspace_m3 * vapour_at_target
vapour_at_40 = saturation_g_per_m3 * 0.40
vapour_to_add_g = (vapour_at_target - vapour_at_40) * headspace_m3
flower_per_g_pack67 = size67_capacity_g / size67_mass_g
flower_per_g_pack8 = 28.35 / 8.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.05, 0.05)
    return {"rh_low": round(low_rh, 2), "rh_high": round(high_rh, 2), "vapour_at_target_g_per_m3": round(vapour_at_target + jitter, 2), "vapour_in_headspace_g": round(vapour_in_headspace_g, 5), "vapour_to_add_from_40pct_g": round(vapour_to_add_g, 5), "flower_per_gram_pack67": round(flower_per_g_pack67, 2), "flower_per_gram_pack8": round(flower_per_g_pack8, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("RH low bound (percent): %.2f" % r1["rh_low"])
print("RH high bound (percent): %.2f" % r1["rh_high"])
print("Vapour at target (g per m3): %.2f" % r1["vapour_at_target_g_per_m3"])
print("Vapour in headspace (g): %.5f" % r1["vapour_in_headspace_g"])
print("Vapour to add from 40 percent (g): %.5f" % r1["vapour_to_add_from_40pct_g"])
print("Flower per gram, size 67 pack: %.2f" % r1["flower_per_gram_pack67"])
print("Flower per gram, size 8 pack: %.2f" % r1["flower_per_gram_pack8"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
