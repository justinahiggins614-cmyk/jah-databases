1. Description and history

The carb cap is one of the eight supplies built in Wave 2 Lane B of the marijuana supplies pair, a smoke-shop supply with a full description, a specification, a science proof, a math proof, a working demo, and a boundless generator. The carb cap works by covering the quartz banger during a dab, trapping heat and restricting airflow so the concentrate vaporizes at a lower temperature instead of being pulled out half-done. It is made from borosilicate glass or quartz, shaped as a bubble dome about 30 mm across with a single 2 mm directional air hole, sized to fit a 25 mm flat-top banger. Dropping the concentrate into the hot banger and capping it at once traps the hot air above the floor, and the small inlet sets up a swirling current that moves heat and oil around the bucket. A directional opening can be turned slowly so the airflow pushes melted concentrate across the hottest part of the floor. The parts fit together in a fixed order, and each part has one job: to hold the material, to apply the mechanism, or to carry the result to the user. Because the parts are simple and the mechanism is thermal rather than electronic in its basic form, the supply can be understood by inspection, measured with a ruler, and checked by the arithmetic in this record. This record also gives the materials and design, a build-it-yourself section, 3D-printing directions, and a use program, with scale variants at pocket, standard, and workshop sizes. The carb cap is a young accessory, because it only became necessary when quartz bangers replaced domed nails on dab rigs in the 2010s. Early dabbing used a dome and nail, where the dome itself trapped the vapour, but the open quartz banger that followed had no lid at all, so vapour escaped and the concentrate pooled half-vaporized on the hot floor. Glass workers answered with a small lid, borrowing the name carburetor from engines because, like a carburetor, it controls airflow and pressure in a chamber. The first caps were simple glass discs; then came bubble caps with a directional hole, spinner caps with angled holes that spin terp pearls, and directional caps that aim air across the banger floor. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. The sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage, and the history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

Materials and design

The carb cap is a heat-and-seal design, and the material choice is the design. Borosilicate glass is the common choice because it is transparent, so the user can see the concentrate move, and because its low thermal expansion lets it sit on a banger at several hundred degrees without cracking. Quartz is the upgrade: it holds heat longer than borosilicate, so the cap itself stays hot through the dab, but it costs more and is harder to shape. Titanium caps exist for rigs where breakage matters more than visibility, and they tolerate the same heat with no risk of shattering. The joint is not a ground taper but a flat seal: the cap rim must sit flat on the banger rim without rocking, and the tolerance is the flatness of that rim, checked by a light tap test. The inlet hole is the tuning: a 2 mm hole holds more heat and gives a denser pull with more drag, while a wider hole cools faster and draws easier. The handle is the grip: bubble caps are held by the dome, directional caps by a short stem, and a cap that is hard to hold is hard to turn. The finish is clear glass or quartz with a polished rim, and the design varies by cap type, bubble, directional, spinner, dome, and disc, while the working principle stays the same.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = (2/3) * pi * r^3

V is the dome volume in cubic millimetres; r is the cap radius in millimetres.

The demo treats the cap dome as a hemisphere and the glass shell as the difference between two hemispheres. With a cap diameter of 30.0 mm the radius is 15.0 mm, so the dome volume is two thirds pi times 3,375, about 7,069 cubic millimetres. The wall is 2.0 mm thick, so the inner radius is 13.0 mm and the shell volume is two thirds pi times the difference of the cubes, about 2,463 cubic millimetres, or 2.47 cubic centimetres. At a borosilicate density of 2.23 grams per cubic centimetre the mass is 5.50 grams, and multiplying by the specific heat of 0.83 and a 175 kelvin rise gives about 799 joules to warm the cap. The inlet hole is 2.0 mm across, so its area is pi times 1 squared, 3.14 square millimetres, against a banger mouth area of pi times 12.5 squared, 490.87 square millimetres, a ratio of 0.64 percent, which is why the draw feels restricted. 4. Science proof

A carb cap works by turning surface heating into surface plus air heating, and by lowering the pressure inside the banger. When concentrate first touches a hot banger it is heated by conduction, direct contact with the hot surface, like food on a hot pan. Without a cap the hot air rises straight out and cool air rushes in, so only the surface does the work. With a cap on, the banger becomes a mostly sealed space, the trapped air heats up, and as the user draws, that hot air circulates over the concentrate, so it is heated by convection as well. The cap also restricts airflow, which lowers the air pressure inside the chamber and lowers the boiling point of the concentrate, so it vaporizes at 350 to 450 degrees Fahrenheit instead of 500 to 600 degrees, the range the cited guides give. Borosilicate glass matters because its low thermal expansion, about 3.3 times ten to the minus six per kelvin, lets the cap sit on a hot banger without cracking, and quartz holds heat longer than borosilicate. The dome is a hemisphere, so its volume is two thirds pi times radius cubed, and the inlet is a small circle whose area sets the draw resistance. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

Build it yourself

There is no genuine home build for a carb cap, and this record says so plainly: shaping a bubble dome in borosilicate or quartz needs a glassblowing torch, an annealing kiln, and a trained hand, and glassblowing is a skilled trade, not a home craft. The closest genuine maker activity is making a cap holder and a fit check, and it works like this. Start by measuring the banger rim with calipers: the cap must match the banger diameter, 20, 25, or 30 mm, and the rim must be flat. Take a bought glass bubble cap and set it on the banger cold, then give it a light tap: it should sit flat without rocking, and a slow test pull should meet resistance, which is the seal working. If it rocks, the rim is not flat and the cap will leak vapour. Then make the accessory a home printer can do: a PETG cap holder, a small cradle with a 30 mm pocket that holds the cap upright on a table so it cannot roll and chip its rim. Print it flat, check the pocket with calipers, and keep the cap in the holder between dabs. That holder is the build; the glass cap itself is bought, not made at home.

3D-print version

The carb cap itself is not 3D-printable in glass or quartz on a home printer, and this record says so plainly: home printers extrude plastic filament, and no plastic survives contact with a banger at several hundred degrees, so a printed cap would melt and give off fumes. What is printable is the accessory that goes with it: a PETG cap holder and dab-tool rest. Print it in PETG, which tolerates warm water and cleaning better than PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, a 0.2 mm layer height, 20 percent gyroid infill, and 3 perimeters, giving a wall about 1.2 mm thick. Print the holder flat on its base with no supports, the cap pocket facing up, at a nozzle temperature of 235 degrees C and a bed temperature of 80 degrees C, with the fan at 50 percent. Print time (estimate): about 40 minutes for the holder at standard size. Post-processing is simple: remove any brim, check the 30 mm pocket with calipers, and sand the rim lightly if it is sharp. Unmodeled factors: wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing all change the real print time and strength, so the estimate is not a validated print job; measure the printed pocket before trusting it with a real glass cap.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: carb cap dome volume and inlet ratio — The Carb Cap."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    cap_d = 30.0; wall = 2.0; inlet_d = 2.0; banger_d = 25.0; rho = 2.23; cp = 0.83; dT = 175.0
    r = cap_d / 2.0; ri = r - wall
    dome_mm3 = (2.0 / 3.0) * math.pi * r ** 3
    shell_mm3 = (2.0 / 3.0) * math.pi * (r ** 3 - ri ** 3)
    shell_cm3 = shell_mm3 / 1000.0
    mass_g = shell_cm3 * rho
    heat_J = mass_g * cp * dT
    inlet_mm2 = math.pi * (inlet_d / 2.0) ** 2
    mouth_mm2 = math.pi * (banger_d / 2.0) ** 2
    ratio_pct = 100.0 * inlet_mm2 / mouth_mm2
    return {"dome_volume_mm3": round(dome_mm3, 2), "shell_volume_cm3": round(shell_cm3, 2), "cap_mass_g": round(mass_g, 2), "heat_to_175C_J": round(heat_J, 2), "inlet_area_mm2": round(inlet_mm2, 2), "banger_mouth_mm2": round(mouth_mm2, 2), "inlet_ratio_pct": round(ratio_pct, 2), "banger_mm": banger_d}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['dome_volume_mm3', 'shell_volume_cm3', 'cap_mass_g', 'heat_to_175C_J', 'inlet_area_mm2', 'banger_mouth_mm2', 'inlet_ratio_pct', 'banger_mm']:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Use, as a program

To use the carb cap, heat the banger to the target range, 350 to 500 degrees Fahrenheit, drop the concentrate onto the floor, and cap it at once, before the vapour can escape. Draw slowly and steadily, and turn or tilt the cap so the directional hole aims air at any concentrate that has not melted. Lift the cap when the vapour stops, and let the cap cool on its holder before cleaning it. The program in this record computes what the cap is and how it seals: the dome volume from its diameter, the glass shell volume from the wall thickness, the mass from the borosilicate density, the heat needed to warm the cap, and the inlet area against the banger mouth area, which is the ratio that sets the draw resistance. Run the demo to see the numbers, then change one input at a time — a wider cap, a bigger inlet hole, a thicker wall — and watch the mass and the inlet ratio move. The program is the use check: if the inlet ratio it reports is far above one percent, the cap will not hold heat, and if it is far below, the draw will choke, so the reader should re-measure the cap before trusting it.
