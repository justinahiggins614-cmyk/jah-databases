1. Description and history

The glass bowl (14mm/18mm male/female) is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a glass bowl (14mm/18mm male/female) that works by the bowl holds the material in its cup while air drawn through the hole in the bottom of the cup carries smoke down the ground-glass joint and into the water pipe. It is made from borosilicate glass bowl, ground-glass joint, stainless-steel screen (optional), and a common example measures 14 mm male joint, cup 22 mm diameter at the rim and 18 mm deep, hole 3 mm at the bottom of the cup, stem 35 mm long with an 8 mm bore. The glass bowl is the small detachable cup that holds the material on a water pipe, and its history is the history of the ground-glass joint, borrowed from laboratory glassware. Two tapered glass surfaces, one male and one female, meet and seal by friction alone, with no threading, gasket, or sealant, and the joint size names the nominal diameter where the two pieces meet. Three sizes cover almost the whole market, 10 mm, 14 mm, and 18 mm, with 14 mm the everyday standard on most beakers and straight tubes and 18 mm common on larger bongs. The sizes are not interchangeable: a 14 mm bowl drops loosely into an 18 mm joint and leaks air, and an 18 mm bowl will not fit a 14 mm joint at all. Adapters can bridge a mismatch, but the joint itself sets which bowls, downstems, and ash catchers fit a piece. Materials and design

Borosilicate glass is chosen for the bowl because the cup sits where the material burns, and a glass that tolerates heat without cracking is what survives that spot. The cup wall is made thicker at the rim than at the bottom, because the rim is handled and the bottom is protected by the stem, and a thick rim resists the chips that end a bowl's life. The joint is ground, not smooth: the frosted surface is what grips the mating joint, and a polished joint would slip and leak. A stainless-steel screen can be fitted over the hole, because steel tolerates the heat at the bottom of the cup and its mesh stops fine particles without blocking the airflow. The joint is the bowl: a ground-glass taper, 14 mm or 18 mm, male or female, sealing by friction with no gasket and no thread. A male bowl slides into a female joint, and a female bowl slides over a male joint, and the size and the gender must both match. The tolerance that matters is the taper angle and the ground length: a taper ground too short will rock, and a taper forced into the wrong size can wedge and crack the downstem. Adapters exist that step an 18 mm joint down to a 14 mm bowl, but the adapter adds a second joint and a second place to leak. The outside is left smooth and clear so the cup can be seen and the pack checked at a glance, and the rim is fire-polished so it has no sharp edge. Coloured glass versions add a coloured frit to the outside of the cup, never to the inside, so the colour never touches the smoke path. Variants change the joint size, the gender, the cup shape, and the colour, but the cup and the ground taper stay the same in every version.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = pi * h / 3 * (R^2 + R*r + r^2)

V is the volume of a frustum in cubic millimetres; h is the frustum height in millimetres; R is the wide-end radius in millimetres; r is the narrow-end radius in millimetres.

The demo treats the cup as a frustum: pi times the cup depth of 18.0 mm divided by 3, times the sum of the top radius squared, the top radius times the bottom radius, and the bottom radius squared, with a top radius of 11.0 mm and a bottom radius of 4.0 mm. Dividing by 1000 gives cubic centimetres, and multiplying by the packing density of 0.15 grams per cubic centimetre gives the estimated capacity in grams. The joint areas are circles: a 14 mm joint has an area of pi times 7 squared, and an 18 mm joint has an area of pi times 9 squared, and the ratio of the two areas is about 1.65, so the larger joint passes the same draw with less restriction. The hole area is pi times the hole radius squared, with a hole diameter of 3.0 mm, and the stem volume is pi times the bore radius squared times the stem length of 35.0 mm. 4. Science proof

The glass bowl works by two simple ideas: a cup and a taper. The cup is a frustum, wide at the rim and narrow at the bottom, so the material sits in a cone that funnels smoke toward the hole. The hole at the bottom is small, 3 millimetres in the common example, so ground material cannot fall straight through, and a screen can be fitted over it to stop finer particles. The taper is the ground-glass joint: two tapered surfaces pressed together seal by friction, because the taper angle makes the contact area large and the glass faces grip each other. The joint size sets the airflow ceiling: an 18 mm joint has about 1.65 times the cross-sectional area of a 14 mm joint, so the same draw meets less restriction through the larger joint, and 18 mm bowls usually have bigger cups. Borosilicate glass is used because the cup sits where the material burns, and a glass that tolerates heat without cracking is what survives that spot. The stem below the cup carries the smoke into the downstem, and its bore sets how easily the smoke flows onward. Build it yourself

This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. A glass bowl cannot be made at home: blowing a borosilicate cup with a ground-glass taper is a skilled glassblowing trade, and grinding the taper needs a lathe and abrasive, not a home tool. The closest genuine maker activity is fitting and maintaining a bought bowl, and making a screen for it. Buy a bowl in the joint size and gender that matches the downstem, and check the fit dry: it should seat firmly without wobble and lift out cleanly. Then cut a screen from stainless-steel mesh: measure the inside diameter of the cup bottom, cut a disc a millimetre wider so it springs into place, and press it flat with a blunt tool so it sits level over the hole. Test the screen by blowing gently through the stem from below; air should pass freely and the screen should not lift. Replace a bowl with a chipped joint, because a chip will cut the mating joint and leak air. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

3D-print version

Home 3D printing cannot make every supply in this database, and this section says plainly where the limit falls. A glass bowl is not 3D-printable at home: no home filament prints glass, and a printed plastic bowl would soften and give off fumes where the material burns, so it must never be used as a bowl. The printable accessory is a protective bowl cap and a storage tray for spare bowls. Print the cap in PETG, which tolerates warm water and does not crack as easily as PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 perimeters, and 25 percent gyroid infill, and print the tray in the same material with separate recesses for each bowl. Print both flat on their bottom faces with no supports. Print time (estimate): 30 minutes for the cap and 2 hours for the tray, at the settings given. Unmodelled factors that change real print time and strength are wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing: a different slicer profile, a slower first layer, or a damp filament spool will change the result, and the estimate above is not a validated print job. Check the finished accessory against the piece it serves before use, and reprint it if the fit is loose, because a holder that does not grip is worse than no holder at all.

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: glass bowl cup volume and joint areas — The Glass Bowl (14mm/18mm male/female)."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    joint = 14.0; R = 11.0; r = 4.0; h = 18.0; hole = 3.0; s_len = 35.0; s_bore = 8.0; rho = 0.15
    cup_mm3 = math.pi * h / 3.0 * (R * R + R * r + r * r)
    cup_cm3 = cup_mm3 / 1000.0
    cap = cup_cm3 * rho
    a14 = math.pi * (14.0 / 2.0) ** 2
    a18 = math.pi * (18.0 / 2.0) ** 2
    ratio = a18 / a14
    hole_area = math.pi * (hole / 2.0) ** 2
    stem_mm3 = math.pi * (s_bore / 2.0) ** 2 * s_len
    return {"cup_volume_cm3": round(cup_cm3, 2), "capacity_g": round(cap, 2), "joint_area_14mm_mm2": round(a14, 2), "joint_area_18mm_mm2": round(a18, 2), "area_ratio": round(ratio, 2), "hole_area_mm2": round(hole_area, 2), "stem_volume_mL": round(stem_mm3 / 1000.0, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["cup_volume_cm3", "capacity_g", "joint_area_14mm_mm2", "joint_area_18mm_mm2", "area_ratio", "hole_area_mm2", "stem_volume_mL"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
