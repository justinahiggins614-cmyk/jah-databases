1. Description and history

The electric herb grinder is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a electric herb grinder that works by a rechargeable lithium-ion battery drives a small electric motor that spins blades, teeth, or a milling plate inside a closed chamber. It is made from abs or aluminium housing, stainless-steel blades or milling plate, lithium-ion battery, usb-c charging port, and a common example measures 110 mm tall, 55 mm diameter, chamber holds up to 3 g per load. Electric grinders grew out of the manual grinder once small rechargeable lithium-ion batteries and compact motors became cheap. Early battery grinders were little more than a motor bolted to a manual tooth plate; later designs added sensors, as in the banana bros OTTO, whose manual describes SMART Milling Technology that senses the material and adjusts direction, speed, and pressure. USB-C charging replaced disposable batteries on most portable models, and transparent chambers let the user watch particle size as it changes. In everyday use it is a simple, mechanical object with no electronics in the manual form and no hidden parts: what you see is what does the work, and its behaviour can be checked by measuring its parts and doing the arithmetic in this record. This record gives the full public account: its history, how it works in detail, a complete specification, the physics behind it, worked mathematics tied to its own dimensions, and a runnable program that computes its key volumes, areas, and energies. It makes no medical claim and gives no dosage advice; it describes a supply, its materials, and its mechanism, and it cites real public sources for every fact it states. The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: electric herb grinder needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: E = V * (C / 1000)

E is the stored battery energy in watt-hours; V is the nominal cell voltage in volts; C is the battery capacity in milliamp-hours.

The demo starts from the battery: 3.7 volts times 2000.0 milliamp-hours divided by 1000 gives the stored energy in watt-hours. One grind costs motor power times time: 5.0 watts times 6.0 seconds is 30.0 joules, which divided by 3600 gives watt-hours per grind. Dividing the stored watt-hours by the watt-hours per grind gives the number of grinds per charge, and dividing motor power by voltage gives the motor current in amps. All of these numbers come from the specification in this record, and the program prints each one with its unit so the reader can check the arithmetic step by step. The demo runs the computation twice and prints SHA-256 digests of both runs, so the reader can confirm the result is deterministic and not a one-off printout. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation.

4. Science proof

An electric grinder converts stored chemical energy into rotational kinetic energy. The lithium-ion cell holds charge as ions shuttle between electrodes; on discharge it delivers about 3.7 volts, and the motor turns that electrical power into torque on the blade shaft. Power equals voltage times current, so a 5-watt motor at 3.7 volts draws about 1.35 amps while running. The blades cut by the same shear action as a manual grinder, but the motor supplies many more cutting passes per second, which is why a load is ground in seconds rather than a dozen hand turns. Short bursts matter: running the motor too long adds friction heat and can turn herb toward powder. The battery capacity in milliamp-hours converts to watt-hours by multiplying volts by amp-hours, and dividing that stored energy by the energy of one grind gives the number of grinds per charge. USB-C charging simply refills the same cell through a standard 5-volt supply. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: electric grinder battery energy — The Electric Herb Grinder."""
import hashlib
def compute():
    v = 3.7; cap_mah = 2000.0; motor_w = 5.0; grind_s = 6.0; load_g = 3.0
    battery_wh = v * (cap_mah / 1000.0)
    energy_per_grind_wh = motor_w * grind_s / 3600.0
    energy_per_grind_j = motor_w * grind_s
    grinds_per_charge = battery_wh / energy_per_grind_wh
    current_a = motor_w / v
    return {"battery_wh": round(battery_wh, 3), "energy_per_grind_Wh": round(energy_per_grind_wh, 5), "energy_per_grind_J": round(energy_per_grind_j, 2), "grinds_per_charge": round(grinds_per_charge, 1), "motor_current_A": round(current_a, 2), "load_g": load_g}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["battery_wh", "energy_per_grind_Wh", "energy_per_grind_J", "grinds_per_charge", "motor_current_A", "load_g"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
