1. Description and history

The gravity bong is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a gravity bong that works by moving water creates suction that pulls air through the bowl into a chamber, and pushing the chamber back down into the water forces the collected smoke out through the opening in one steady delivery. It is made from plastic 2-litre bottle with its bottom cut off, plastic bucket or larger container, metal or glass bowl fitted to the bottle cap, aerator screen, water as the displacement medium, and a common example measures 2-litre bottle chamber about 280 mm tall and 105 mm diameter, bucket larger than the bottle, lift height 180 mm. The gravity bong comes from do-it-yourself culture rather than from a glass workshop. The bucket bong is made from two containers, a smaller bottle with its bottom cut off and a bowl fitted to its cap, set inside a larger container of water, and the waterfall bong from a single bottle with a small hole near its base. Both use water displacement instead of the lungs to move air, and both are documented in the same public account that notes neither actually fits the strict definition of a bong, because the smoke is not bubbled through water to cool and filter it. The device is also called a GB, a grav, a geeb, or a bucket bong, and the common pairing in guides is a 2-litre bottle as the chamber with a 16 to 20 ounce bottle, or a bucket, as the water container. Glass gravity bongs are also sold, but the homemade bottle-and-bucket version remains the form most people picture. This record gives the full public account: its history, how it works, a complete specification, the physics behind it, worked mathematics, and a runnable program that computes its key figures. It makes no medical claim and gives no dosage advice; it describes a supply and its mechanism, and it cites real public sources for every fact it states. Materials and design

Plastic is chosen for the homemade gravity bong because it is the material at hand: a 2-litre bottle is a ready-made chamber with a neck that takes a bowl, and a bucket is a ready-made water container taller than the chamber. The bottle must be taller than it is wide, so the lift height gives a useful volume, and its plastic must be stiff enough not to collapse when the vacuum forms inside it. The bowl is metal or glass because it sits where the material burns: plastic would soften there. Water is the displacement medium because it is heavy and free-flowing: a litre of water weighs a kilogram, so lifting the bottle out of it takes real effort, and that effort is what creates the suction. The joints on a gravity bong are improvised, and the record is honest about that. The bowl is fitted to the bottle cap through a hole cut in the centre of the cap, and the fit is sealed by the cap's own thread and by the screen inside the bowl, not by a ground-glass taper. The tolerance that matters is the hole in the cap: too large and the bowl wobbles and leaks air, too small and it cannot be threaded in. The bucket has no joint at all: the bottle simply slides inside it, and the clearance between the bottle and the bucket wall sets how smoothly the chamber rises and falls. The outside is left as the bottle and bucket came, because the device is assembled from containers, not finished as a product. The cut edge of the bottle bottom must be smoothed, because a rough edge can cut a hand and can catch on the bucket wall and tilt the chamber. Variants change the chamber size, the bucket size, and whether the drain hole of the waterfall version is plugged with a rubber stopper or a finger, but the two-container layout and the displacement principle stay the same in every version.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: v = sqrt(2 * g * h)

v is the drain speed in metres per second; g is the acceleration due to gravity, 9.81 metres per second squared; h is the height of the water column in metres.

The demo uses two ideas. First, the displaced volume is the bottle cross-section times the lift height: pi times the bottle radius squared, with a bottle diameter of 105.0 mm, times the lift of 180.0 mm, which gives the smoke volume drawn into the chamber in millilitres. Second, the waterfall drain speed follows Torricelli's law: the square root of twice gravity times the water column height of 200.0 mm. Multiplying that speed by the drain hole area, from a hole diameter of 6.0 mm, gives the drain flow in millilitres per second, and dividing the chamber volume of 2000.0 mL by that flow gives the drain time in seconds. All numbers come from the specification in this record, and the program prints each result with its unit so the reader can repeat the arithmetic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation. The computation is run twice and the SHA-256 digests of both runs are printed, confirming the result is deterministic.

4. Science proof

The gravity bong works by displacement, not by suction from the lungs. When the bottle is lifted out of the bucket, water leaves the inside of the bottle, and the space the water leaves must be filled by something: the only opening is through the bowl, so air is pulled across the burning material and into the chamber. The volume drawn in is the cross-section of the bottle times the lift height, which is why a wide bottle lifted a short way draws the same volume as a narrow bottle lifted further. Pushing the bottle back down reverses the process: the water level inside rises, the pressure in the chamber rises above the outside air, and the smoke is forced out through the opening. The waterfall version works the same way in reverse: water drains out through a hole near the base, and the speed of the draining water follows Torricelli's law, the square root of twice gravity times the height of the water column. The water never filters the smoke in either version, because nothing bubbles through it: it only moves air, so the draw is not cooled or diffused the way a water pipe cools it. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Build it yourself

This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. The gravity bong is the one item in this lane with a genuine home build, and the steps below are the real ones from the public guides. Take a 2-litre plastic bottle and cut its bottom off cleanly, then smooth the cut edge with sandpaper so it cannot cut a hand. Take the bottle cap and cut a small hole in its centre, then thread a metal bowl or a hex-bit socket through the hole from the outside and fit an aerator screen inside the bowl. Fill a bucket or larger container with water, screw the cap onto the bottle, and set the bottle into the bucket so it is submerged to its neck. Light the bowl while lifting the bottle slowly and evenly until it is about to come out of the water, then hold it still, remove the bowl, place the mouth over the opening, and push the bottle back down steadily while breathing in. The common pairing in guides is a 2-litre bottle as the chamber and a 16 to 20 ounce bottle or a bucket as the water container. Replace a bottle that is cracked or cloudy, and never lift the chamber so far that it leaves the water entirely. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

3D-print version

Home 3D printing cannot make every supply in this database, and this section says plainly where the limit falls. A plastic bottle and bucket gravity bong is not itself 3D-printable, because the chamber is a ready-made bottle and the water container is a bucket, and printing either would be slower and weaker than using the containers. The printable accessory is a guide ring and a bowl-cap adapter for the bucket version. Print the guide ring in PETG, which tolerates warm water and does not crack as easily as PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 perimeters, and 25 percent gyroid infill, so the bottle rises and falls straight instead of tilting. Print the cap adapter in the same material at the same settings, sized to the bottle cap, and fit it by hand rather than forcing it. Print time (estimate): 1 hour 20 minutes for the guide ring and 35 minutes for the adapter, at the settings given. Unmodelled factors that change real print time and strength are wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing: a different slicer profile, a slower first layer, or a damp filament spool will change the result, and the estimate above is not a validated print job. Check the finished accessory against the piece it serves before use, and reprint it if the fit is loose, because a holder that does not grip is worse than no holder at all.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: gravity bong displaced volume and waterfall drain rate — The Gravity Bong."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    vol = 2000.0; d = 105.0; lift = 180.0; hole = 6.0; col = 200.0
    displaced = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * lift / 1000.0
    v = math.sqrt(2.0 * 9.81 * (col / 1000.0))
    hole_area = math.pi * (hole / 2.0) ** 2
    flow = hole_area * v * 1000.0 / 1000.0
    drain_time = vol / flow
    return {"chamber_volume_mL": vol, "displaced_volume_mL": round(displaced, 2), "lift_mm": lift, "drain_velocity_m_s": round(v, 2), "drain_flow_mL_per_s": round(flow, 2), "drain_time_s": round(drain_time, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["chamber_volume_mL", "displaced_volume_mL", "lift_mm", "drain_velocity_m_s", "drain_flow_mL_per_s", "drain_time_s"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
