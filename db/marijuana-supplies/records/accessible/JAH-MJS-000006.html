1. Description and history

The water pipe (bong) is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a water pipe (bong) that works by smoke from the bowl travels down a stem below the water line, bubbles up through the water, collects in the chamber, and is drawn to the mouth when the carb is opened or the stem is lifted. It is made from borosilicate glass tube and base, glass downstem and bowl, water as the filtration medium, and a common example measures about 300 mm tall, tube 50 mm diameter, water depth about 50 mm in the base. The bong descends from the hookah and from bamboo water pipes of Asia; the word itself is adapted from the Thai word baung, meaning a wooden cylinder or pipe cut from bamboo. Glass bongs became common in the twentieth century as borosilicate glass made a heat-resistant, clean-tasting water chamber practical. A 1981 United States patent (4,253,475) describes the classic form in detail: a single vertical pipe about 1.5 inches in diameter sealed at the bottom, a bowl on a tube leading in near the bottom, water just above the junction, and a small hole above the water line to clear the chamber. In everyday use it is a simple, mechanical object with no electronics in the manual form and no hidden parts: what you see is what does the work, and its behaviour can be checked by measuring its parts and doing the arithmetic in this record. This record gives the full public account: its history, how it works in detail, a complete specification, the physics behind it, worked mathematics tied to its own dimensions, and a runnable program that computes its key volumes, areas, and energies. It makes no medical claim and gives no dosage advice; it describes a supply, its materials, and its mechanism, and it cites real public sources for every fact it states. Laws on cannabis and cannabis supplies vary by jurisdiction, so readers should check local law before buying, possessing, or using this supply. The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: water pipe (bong) needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = pi * (d/2)^2 * h

V is the volume of a cylindrical section in cubic millimetres; d is its diameter in millimetres; h is its height or depth in millimetres.

The demo treats the tube as a cylinder. The cross-sectional area is pi times the tube radius squared, with a tube diameter of 50.0 mm. Multiplying that area by the water depth of 50.0 mm gives the water volume, and multiplying it by the height above the water gives the chamber volume, both reported in millilitres. A single bubble is treated as a sphere with diameter 4.0 mm: its volume is four thirds pi times radius cubed, and its surface area is four pi times radius squared. The draw pressure is water density times gravity times depth, which the program reports in pascals. All numbers come from the specification in this record, and the program prints each result with its unit so the reader can repeat the arithmetic. It runs the computation twice and prints SHA-256 digests of both runs. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation.

4. Science proof

A bong is a gas-washing bottle. Smoke is a mixture of hot gases and small solid particles, and bubbling it through water does two things at once. First, the water is a heat sink: smoke leaving the bowl is hot, and contact with water at room temperature transfers heat out of the gas, so the smoke that reaches the mouth is cooler. Second, the water is a trap: heavier particles such as ash have too much inertia to follow the gas around a bubble wall and strike the water, and some water-soluble molecules dissolve. Smaller bubbles work better because surface area grows as bubble size shrinks: for the same volume of gas, many small bubbles expose far more gas-water interface than a few large ones, which is why diffused downstems and percolators with many small slits are used. The chamber above the water is a reservoir, and the water level sets the pressure the user must overcome: the draw must lift the water column by the depth of the downstem below the surface, a small hydrostatic pressure of water density times gravity times depth. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: water pipe water and chamber volumes — The Water Pipe (Bong)."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    tube_d = 50.0; height = 300.0; water_h = 50.0; down_d = 12.0; bubble_d = 4.0
    area_mm2 = math.pi * (tube_d / 2.0) ** 2
    water_mm3 = area_mm2 * water_h
    chamber_h = height - water_h
    chamber_mm3 = area_mm2 * chamber_h
    bubble_r = bubble_d / 2.0
    bubble_vol_mm3 = (4.0 / 3.0) * math.pi * bubble_r ** 3
    bubble_area_mm2 = 4.0 * math.pi * bubble_r ** 2
    pressure_pa = 1000.0 * 9.81 * (water_h / 1000.0)
    return {"water_volume_mm3": round(water_mm3, 1), "water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 1), "chamber_volume_mL": round(chamber_mm3 / 1000.0, 1), "bubble_volume_mm3": round(bubble_vol_mm3, 2), "bubble_surface_mm2": round(bubble_area_mm2, 2), "draw_pressure_Pa": round(pressure_pa, 1)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["water_volume_mm3", "water_volume_mL", "chamber_volume_mL", "bubble_volume_mm3", "bubble_surface_mm2", "draw_pressure_Pa"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
