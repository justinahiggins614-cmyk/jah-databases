Glass jars for preserving food are an old technology: the mason jar, with its two-piece lid of a flat disc and a screw band, was patented in the nineteenth century and became the standard home canning jar because glass does not react with food and the lid seals tightly. Cannabis storage adopted the same jar, because the problems are the same: air, light, heat and moisture all degrade the contents over time. Guides now call the mason jar the gold standard for cannabis storage, and makers have added amber and violet glass, rubber gaskets and silicone sleeves to block ultraviolet light. The jar itself has not changed much in a century; what changed is the understanding of what to put inside it, including humidity packs and the rule to fill the jar no more than three quarters full so air can circulate. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.

The problem it solves

The design problem for the Signature the airtight glass storage jar is the same problem the public version faces, stated as a design brief. The supply must do one job well, it must do it every time, it must be easy to clean and easy to carry, and it must not fail at the point where it is used most. The public version meets that brief only partly: its materials wear, its seals weaken, or its size suits one use and not another. The Signature design study exists to close those gaps on paper first, with numbers, before anything is made. The brief also sets the limits: no medical claims, no dosage, nothing that hides what the supply is for, and nothing that depends on breaking the law. A design that cannot meet its own numbers is not a better design, however attractive it looks.

Method

The Signature method is to start from the public supply, keep the mechanism that works, and redesign the parts that wear out or waste material. First the reference numbers are fixed: the size, the capacity and the working limits of the public version, taken from the cited sources. Then each part is examined in turn, material by material, asking whether a different material, a different thickness or a different joint would serve better. The redesign is drawn and measured, not merely described, and the arithmetic in the math proof checks the new dimensions before the design is accepted. Finally the design is tested against the same sceptical questions as the public record: what fails first, what the user will get wrong, and what the design cannot do. Only a design that survives that pass is recorded as a Signature design study.

Section 3
Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The quart jar holds 946 millilitres, which is 946 cubic centimetres. Filled to the recommended 75 percent, the contents occupy 946 x 0.75 = 709.5 cubic centimetres and the headspace air is 946 - 709.5 = 236.5 cubic centimetres. Treating the jar as a cylinder 9.5 centimetres in diameter, its radius is 4.75 centimetres and its cross-sectional area is pi times radius squared: 3.1416 x 4.75 x 4.75 = 70.9 square centimetres. Dividing volume by area gives the height: 946 / 70.9 = 13.3 centimetres, close to the measured 14 centimetres once the shoulder and base thickness are allowed for. At 20 degrees Celsius, air at 62 percent relative humidity holds about 10.7 grams of water vapour per cubic metre; the headspace is 236.5 cubic centimetres, which is 0.0002365 cubic metres, so it holds 0.0002365 x 10.7 = 0.00253 grams of water vapour, about two and a half milligrams. The pint jar at 473 millilitres is exactly half the quart, and the half-gallon at 1893 millilitres is 1893 / 946 = 2.0 times the quart.

Section 4
Science proof

An airtight glass jar protects its contents by controlling four enemies: air, light, heat and moisture. Oxygen in air slowly oxidises cannabinoids, so limiting air exchange slows that loss; the lid gasket is the seal that does it, compressing against the glass rim so that air can only enter when the jar is opened. Glass itself is a nearly perfect barrier: it is non-porous, so water vapour and oxygen do not diffuse through the walls the way they slowly do through plastic, and it is chemically inert, so it neither absorbs terpenes nor gives off flavours. Light is the worst enemy of all: a 2019 study in the Journal of Pharmacy and Pharmacology found that light, particularly ultraviolet, was the single greatest factor in cannabinoid degradation across the storage conditions tested, because ultraviolet photons carry enough energy to break chemical bonds. Amber glass absorbs most ultraviolet light before it reaches the contents, which is why it protects better than clear glass. Static electricity matters too: plastic builds up charge and pulls fine trichomes off flower, while glass does not. The jar does not stop time, but by holding the air still, dark and at a steady humidity, it slows every decay process at once.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view of this design study is that a drawing is not a product, and a design that has never been built has never failed in the ways real products fail. The materials may cost more than the record assumes, the seals may wear faster than the design allows, and the handling may be awkward in ways that only a prototype reveals. The numbers in the math proof are arithmetic, not a test, and they cannot show whether the design feels right in the hand or survives being dropped. The record accepts all of that: the design study is a proposal, and it is labelled as one, so that nobody mistakes it for a tested product. The honest next step is a prototype, a real test, and a revision, and until that happens the design should be read as a careful idea, not as a finished thing.

import hashlib, json, math, random
volume_ml = 946.0
fill_fraction = 0.75
diameter_cm = 9.5
vapour_g_per_m3 = 10.7
contents_ml = volume_ml * fill_fraction
headspace_ml = volume_ml - contents_ml
radius_cm = diameter_cm / 2.0
cross_area_cm2 = math.pi * radius_cm * radius_cm
height_cm = volume_ml / cross_area_cm2
headspace_m3 = headspace_ml / 1000000.0
vapour_g = headspace_m3 * vapour_g_per_m3
half_gallon_ratio = 1893.0 / 946.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.1, 0.1)
    return {"contents_ml": round(contents_ml, 2), "headspace_ml": round(headspace_ml, 2), "jar_height_cm": round(height_cm + jitter, 2), "headspace_water_vapour_g": round(vapour_g, 5), "pint_volume_ml": 473.0, "half_gallon_to_quart_ratio": round(half_gallon_ratio, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Contents at 75 percent (ml): %.2f" % r1["contents_ml"])
print("Headspace (ml): %.2f" % r1["headspace_ml"])
print("Jar height from volume (cm): %.2f" % r1["jar_height_cm"])

Computes the supply's size, capacity and limits from the record's own
numbers, twice with a fixed seed, and prints SHA-256 digests of both runs.
Standard library only; no network. Run: python3 program.py
"""


class SupplyModel:
    """Model of the the airtight glass storage jar."""

    def run(self):
        import hashlib, json, math, random
        volume_ml = 946.0
        fill_fraction = 0.75
        diameter_cm = 9.5
        vapour_g_per_m3 = 10.7
        contents_ml = volume_ml * fill_fraction

        radius_cm = diameter_cm / 2.0

        half_gallon_ratio = 1893.0 / 946.0

            jitter = rng.uniform(-0.1, 0.1)
            return {"contents_ml": round(contents_ml, 2), "headspace_ml": round(headspace_ml, 2), "jar_height_cm": round(height_cm + jitter, 2), "headspace_water_vapour_g": round(vapour_g, 5), "pint_volume_ml": 473.0, "half_gallon_to_quart_ratio": round(half_gallon_ratio, 2)}
        r1 = run_once(42)
        r2 = run_once(42)
        d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
        d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
        print("Contents at 75 percent (ml): %.2f" % r1["contents_ml"])
        print("Headspace (ml): %.2f" % r1["headspace_ml"])
        print("Jar height from volume (cm): %.2f" % r1["jar_height_cm"])
        print("Headspace water vapour (g): %.5f" % r1["headspace_water_vapour_g"])
        print("Pint jar volume (ml): %.2f" % r1["pint_volume_ml"])
        print("Half-gallon to quart ratio: %.2f" % r1["half_gallon_to_quart_ratio"])
        print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
        print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
        print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    SupplyModel().run()
