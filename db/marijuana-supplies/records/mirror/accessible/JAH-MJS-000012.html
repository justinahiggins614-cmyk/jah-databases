1. Description and history

The Signature the hemp wick is a Signature-made design study of a length of hemp twine coated in beeswax that burns slowly and steadily and is used to carry a small flame to a target instead of holding a lighter against it, drawn as a design for the Signature edition of the marijuana supplies database. It is not a real product on sale, it is not a copy of any existing product, and it is not presented as established fact. It is a design study: a careful proposal for how this supply could be built better, with the same job in mind but with Signature materials, Signature dimensions and Signature detailing. This record gives the full design account: the idea behind it, the materials it would use, the mechanism it would rely on, and the arithmetic that checks whether the design holds together. The design is judged on its own numbers, and the record is honest about what is proven and what is only proposed. Nothing here is a medical claim and nothing here gives a dose. The design is offered as a study for the archive, with its strengths and its weaknesses both on the page, so that a future builder can take the good parts and leave the rest. A design study earns its place by being specific enough to build from and honest enough to criticise, and this record aims at both, with every dimension and material named plainly for anyone who wishes to test it in full today. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

Hemp has been twisted into cord and rope for thousands of years, and coating cord in wax to slow its burning is as old as the candle, where the wick draws melted wax up by capillary action and burns it. Hemp wick as a smoking accessory is a modern revival of that old idea: hemp twine is coated in beeswax so that it burns slowly enough to be useful as a portable flame. It spread through head shops and glassware shops as an alternative to holding a butane lighter directly against a bowl, and it is now sold in balls and spools from one foot to 250 feet long, in several thicknesses. The appeal is simple and old-fashioned: a slow, cool, clean-burning flame that can be lit once and carried, pinched out and relit many times. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.

The problem it solves

The design problem for the Signature the hemp wick is the same problem the public version faces, stated as a design brief. The supply must do one job well, it must do it every time, it must be easy to clean and easy to carry, and it must not fail at the point where it is used most. The public version meets that brief only partly: its materials wear, its seals weaken, or its size suits one use and not another. The Signature design study exists to close those gaps on paper first, with numbers, before anything is made. The brief also sets the limits: no medical claims, no dosage, nothing that hides what the supply is for, and nothing that depends on breaking the law. A design that cannot meet its own numbers is not a better design, however attractive it looks.

Method

The Signature method is to start from the public supply, keep the mechanism that works, and redesign the parts that wear out or waste material. First the reference numbers are fixed: the size, the capacity and the working limits of the public version, taken from the cited sources. Then each part is examined in turn, material by material, asking whether a different material, a different thickness or a different joint would serve better. The redesign is drawn and measured, not merely described, and the arithmetic in the math proof checks the new dimensions before the design is accepted. Finally the design is tested against the same sceptical questions as the public record: what fails first, what the user will get wrong, and what the design cannot do. Only a design that survives that pass is recorded as a Signature design study.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference ball holds 20 feet of wick and published guides report about 2 to 3 minutes of burn per foot, so this record uses the midpoint of 2.5 minutes per foot. Total burn time is 20 x 2.5 = 50 minutes of flame. If each lighting uses about 3 centimetres of wick, and 20 feet is 20 x 30.48 = 609.6 centimetres, the ball gives 609.6 / 3 = 203 lightings. At 2.5 minutes per foot, one foot is 30.48 centimetres, so the burn rate along the wick is 30.48 / 2.5 = 12.19 centimetres per minute, or 12.19 / 60 = 0.203 centimetres per second. A 3-centimetre lighting therefore lasts 3 / 0.203 = 14.8 seconds of flame. Guides put the wick flame at about 1650 degrees Fahrenheit and a butane flame at about 3500 degrees Fahrenheit, so the difference is 3500 - 1650 = 1850 degrees, and the wick flame is 1650 / 3500 = 0.47, or 47 percent, of the butane temperature. A 250-foot spool would give 250 x 2.5 = 625 minutes, or 10.4 hours, of flame.

4. Science proof

Hemp wick works exactly like a candle, and the physics is capillary action plus a wax fuel. Hemp fibres are hollow tubes, and liquid beeswax climbs up them by capillary action, the same force that pulls water up a paper towel. At the burning tip the wax vaporises and burns in air, giving off carbon dioxide and water vapour; the heat of that flame melts more wax just below the tip, which is drawn up and burned in turn. Raw hemp string without wax flares and burns out quickly because the cellulose fibres themselves are the fuel and they burn fast. The beeswax coating changes the balance: wax burns more slowly than loose fibre, it coats the fibres so oxygen reaches them more slowly, and it acts as a heat sink that keeps the burning zone small. That is why the flame is cooler and steadier than a butane jet, which mixes gas and air violently and burns at thousands of degrees. The cooler flame is the point of the product: it lights the target without the fierce, wide heat of a lighter flame, and because no butane is burned at the target, no unburned fuel gas passes over it. The wax also waterproofs the twine, so a damp wick still lights.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view of this design study is that a drawing is not a product, and a design that has never been built has never failed in the ways real products fail. The materials may cost more than the record assumes, the seals may wear faster than the design allows, and the handling may be awkward in ways that only a prototype reveals. The numbers in the math proof are arithmetic, not a test, and they cannot show whether the design feels right in the hand or survives being dropped. The record accepts all of that: the design study is a proposal, and it is labelled as one, so that nobody mistakes it for a tested product. The honest next step is a prototype, a real test, and a revision, and until that happens the design should be read as a careful idea, not as a finished thing.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
ball_ft = 20.0
burn_min_per_ft = 2.5
cm_per_ft = 30.48
lighting_cm = 3.0
wick_flame_f = 1650.0
butane_flame_f = 3500.0
total_minutes = ball_ft * burn_min_per_ft
ball_cm = ball_ft * cm_per_ft
lightings = ball_cm / lighting_cm
burn_cm_per_min = cm_per_ft / burn_min_per_ft
burn_cm_per_s = burn_cm_per_min / 60.0
seconds_per_lighting = lighting_cm / burn_cm_per_s
spool_minutes = 250.0 * burn_min_per_ft
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.1, 0.1)
    return {"total_burn_minutes": round(total_minutes, 2), "lightings_per_ball": round(lightings, 2), "burn_rate_cm_per_min": round(burn_cm_per_min + jitter, 2), "seconds_per_lighting": round(seconds_per_lighting, 2), "wick_to_butane_temp_ratio": round(wick_flame_f / butane_flame_f, 2), "spool_burn_hours": round(spool_minutes / 60.0, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Total burn time (minutes): %.2f" % r1["total_burn_minutes"])
print("Lightings per ball: %.2f" % r1["lightings_per_ball"])
print("Burn rate (cm per minute): %.2f" % r1["burn_rate_cm_per_min"])
print("Seconds per lighting: %.2f" % r1["seconds_per_lighting"])
print("Wick to butane temperature ratio: %.2f" % r1["wick_to_butane_temp_ratio"])
print("250 ft spool burn time (hours): %.2f" % r1["spool_burn_hours"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
