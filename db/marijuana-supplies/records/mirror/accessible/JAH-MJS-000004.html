1. Description and history

The Signature Zephyr Desktop Vaporizer is a Signature-made design study for desktop vaporizer, a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — mains power heats air in a large aluminium heat exchanger, and an air pump pushes that hot air through a filling chamber of ground herb into a detachable balloon, or through a whip tube — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature aluminium twin heat exchanger; stainless-steel chamber; food-grade balloon; mains heater and pump. The design is sized at 210 mm tall x 210 mm diameter; 120 w heater; balloon holds about 3.5 litres, which sets every number in this record, from the volumes and areas in the demo to the scale variants at the end. This record is a design study, not a real manufactured product, and nothing here claims the design has been built, tested, or sold. What it does offer is a complete, checkable design: a full specification, the physics of how it would work, worked arithmetic tied to its own dimensions, a runnable program that computes those numbers, and three boundless scale variants. Readers can follow the design from first principles, repeat the arithmetic by hand, run the program, and see exactly where each number comes from. The Signature edition exists alongside the public edition so the archive holds both what exists in the world and what the Signature system designs next, clearly labelled, so the two are never confused. Like every Signature record, it is filed as a design study, with its own generator and its own file AI, and it marches with the rest of the database toward a million records.

The desktop vaporizer was defined by the Storz & Bickel Volcano, introduced in 2000 and the benchmark for the category ever since. The Volcano Classic is a purely electromechanical machine: an analog dial, a heater, and an air pump, with no screen or app. It filled a detachable balloon, called a bag, with vapour, and the vapour stayed fresh in the balloon for up to 8 hours according to the maker. The later Volcano Hybrid added a digital display, faster heat-up, app and Bluetooth control, and a whip tube for direct draw alongside the balloon. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. Museums, trade guides, and manufacturer manuals preserve the steps of that change, and the sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage. The history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: desktop vaporizer needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V_balloon = Q_air * t_fill

V_balloon is the filled balloon volume in litres; Q_air is the airflow rate in litres per minute; t_fill is the fill time in minutes.

The demo uses two simple products. First, heat-up energy is heater power times time: 120.0 watts times 150.0 seconds, which the program reports in joules and in watt-hours after dividing by 3600. Second, the filled balloon volume is airflow times fill time: 7.0 litres per minute times 30.0 seconds divided by 60 gives the filled volume in litres, and dividing that by the balloon volume of 3.5 litres gives the fill fraction. 4. Science proof

A desktop vaporizer is a forced-air convection system. Mains electricity heats a large aluminium block, the heat exchanger, which stores thermal energy and holds a steady temperature while air passes over it. The air pump drives room air through that block, so the air leaves at the set temperature, then flows through the filling chamber where it gives up heat to the herb and picks up vapour. Because the herb never touches the heater, the temperature the herb sees is the temperature of the air, which is why the result repeats from session to session. The balloon is a simple reservoir: vapour collects in it, cools slightly as it waits, and the valve mouthpiece releases vapour only when the user draws. Filling volume is airflow rate times fill time, and the heater energy is power times time. The large heat exchanger is the reason a desktop unit holds temperature better than a small portable: its thermal mass resists the cooling effect of each draw of air. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: desktop vaporizer balloon fill — The Desktop Vaporizer."""
import hashlib
def compute():
    heater_w = 120.0; heatup_s = 150.0; airflow = 7.0; fill_s = 30.0; balloon_l = 3.5; temp_c = 190.0
    heatup_energy_j = heater_w * heatup_s
    fill_min = fill_s / 60.0
    filled_l = airflow * fill_min
    fill_fraction = filled_l / balloon_l
    fills_per_hour = 3600.0 / fill_s
    return {"heatup_energy_J": round(heatup_energy_j, 1), "heatup_energy_Wh": round(heatup_energy_j / 3600.0, 2), "filled_volume_L": round(filled_l, 2), "balloon_volume_L": balloon_l, "fill_fraction": round(fill_fraction, 2), "target_temp_C": temp_c}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["heatup_energy_J", "heatup_energy_Wh", "filled_volume_L", "balloon_volume_L", "fill_fraction", "target_temp_C"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
