1. Description and history

The Signature Metal Ashtray with Poker is a Signature-made design study for metal ashtray with poker, a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — ash and spent material fall into the larger Signature dish; rim rests hold a cigarette or joint by gravity; the brass poker stirs ash and clears clogged bowls — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature stainless steel dish with a brass poker; brushed finish; no plastic in the ash path. The design is sized at dish 120 mm across and 35 mm deep; wall 2.5 mm thick; poker 5 mm across and 140 mm long; about 500 grams total, which sets every number in this record, from the metal ashtray and poker volumes and heat storage in the demo to the scale variants at the end. Like every Signature record, it sits beside its public twin in the same pair, so the reader can compare the public form with the Signature reworking side by side.

The metal ashtray with a poker is the working ashtray of bars, casinos, and patios, because metal is the only ashtray material that cannot break, cannot crack from heat, and can be hosed down at the end of the night. The poker, a slim steel rod kept in a clip or socket on the rim, is the tool that stirs ash, clears a clogged pipe bowl, and pushes spent material off the rests. The form descends from the heavy cast-iron and brass ashtrays of the early twentieth century, when ashtrays were made as luxury objects in metal, and it survives today in stainless steel because steel does not rust outdoors. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. The sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage, and the history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: metal ashtray with poker needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. Materials and design

The metal ashtray is a steel design, and the choice is between stainless steel, brass, and cast iron. Stainless steel is chosen here because it is rustproof, easy to clean, and strong at a 2.5 mm wall, while brass is heavier and prettier but tarnishes, and cast iron is heavier still and rusts unless it is oiled. The wall is 2.5 mm thick, which is the working compromise: thinner walls dent and ring when tapped, thicker walls survive any drop but make the dish heavy to carry. The poker is the same steel, 5 mm across, with a flattened tip for stirring and a loop or ball handle so it cannot slip through the clip. The joint between dish and poker is not a joint at all but a clip or socket on the rim, and its tolerance is the grip of the clip, tight enough to hold the poker in wind and loose enough to pull it free with one hand. The finish is brushed or polished steel, and the design varies by dish size, by the number of rests, and by whether the poker clips to the rim or sits in a socket, while the working principle stays the same.

4. Science proof

A metal ashtray works by conductivity, mass, and corrosion resistance. Stainless steel has a density near 7.9 grams per cubic centimetre and a specific heat near 0.50 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish and poker of about 500 grams store roughly 50,000 joules when heated by 200 kelvin, and its thermal conductivity near 16 watts per metre kelvin spreads heat quickly through the wall, so the whole dish warms evenly instead of cracking at a hot spot. Steel cannot crack from thermal shock the way glass and ceramic can, because it yields instead of fracturing, and its chromium oxide skin keeps it from rusting outdoors, which is why the same dish works on a patio in rain. The poker is a simple lever: a 140 mm rod with a 5 mm diameter gives the hand enough reach and stiffness to stir ash and clear a bowl without bending. The dish is a shallow cylinder, so its capacity is pi times radius squared times depth, and the poker is a long thin cylinder whose volume is pi times radius squared times length. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

Build it yourself

There is no genuine home build for a metal ashtray with poker, and this record says so plainly: spinning or pressing a steel dish and drawing a steel poker need a metal workshop, and sheet-metal work is a skilled trade, not a home craft. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling and fitting a bought dish and poker, and it works like this. Start with a bought stainless steel ashtray, 120 mm across, and a bought steel poker, 5 mm across and 140 mm long, and check that the poker fits the rim clip without rattling. If the dish has no clip, make one: bend a 20 mm strip of 1 mm stainless steel around the poker to form a loop, drill two 3 mm holes in the rim, and rivet the loop in place with 3 mm blind rivets, checking that the poker slides free with one hand. Then make the accessory a home workshop can do: a wooden base board, cut to a 160 mm square from 12 mm hardwood, sanded smooth and oiled, that sits under the dish and keeps it from sliding or scratching the table. Then make the accessory a home printer can do: a PETG poker holster that clips to the board and holds the poker upright. That clip, board, and holster are the build; the steel dish and poker themselves are bought, not made at home.

3D-print version

The metal ashtray itself is not 3D-printable in steel on a home printer, and this record says so plainly: home printers extrude plastic filament, and no plastic survives contact with hot embers or the weight of a steel dish, so a printed ashtray would melt and give off fumes. What is printable is the accessory that goes with it: a PETG poker holster that clips to the base board and holds the poker upright. Print it in PETG, which tolerates warm water and cleaning better than PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, a 0.2 mm layer height, 20 percent gyroid infill, and 3 perimeters, giving a wall about 1.2 mm thick. Print the holster upright on its base with supports inside the poker socket only, at a nozzle temperature of 235 degrees C and a bed temperature of 80 degrees C, with the fan at 50 percent. Print time (estimate): about 45 minutes for the holster at standard size. Post-processing is simple: remove the supports from the socket, check the 5 mm bore with calipers, and sand the rim lightly if it is sharp. Unmodeled factors: wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing all change the real print time and strength, so the estimate is not a validated print job; measure the printed bore before trusting it with a real steel poker.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: metal ashtray and poker volumes and heat storage — Signature Metal Ashtray with Poker."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 120.0; h = 35.0; wall = 2.5; pk_d = 5.0; pk_l = 140.0; rho = 7.9; cp = 0.50; dT = 200.0
    cap_mm3 = math.pi * ((d - 2.0 * wall) / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    cap_cm3 = cap_mm3 / 1000.0
    outer_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * (h + wall)
    dish_cm3 = (outer_mm3 - cap_mm3) / 1000.0
    dish_g = dish_cm3 * rho
    pk_mm3 = math.pi * (pk_d / 2.0) ** 2 * pk_l
    pk_cm3 = pk_mm3 / 1000.0
    pk_g = pk_cm3 * rho
    total_g = dish_g + pk_g
    heat_J = total_g * cp * dT
    return {"capacity_mm3": round(cap_mm3, 2), "capacity_cm3": round(cap_cm3, 2), "dish_volume_cm3": round(dish_cm3, 2), "dish_mass_g": round(dish_g, 2), "poker_volume_cm3": round(pk_cm3, 2), "poker_mass_g": round(pk_g, 2), "total_mass_g": round(total_g, 2), "heat_to_200C_J": round(heat_J, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['capacity_mm3', 'capacity_cm3', 'dish_volume_cm3', 'dish_mass_g', 'poker_volume_cm3', 'poker_mass_g', 'total_mass_g', 'heat_to_200C_J']:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Use, as a program

To use the metal ashtray with poker, set it on a flat surface, rest the cigarette or joint in a rim rest between draws, and tap ash into the dish. Use the poker to stir ash, to clear a clogged pipe bowl, and to push spent material off the rests, then clip the poker back into its socket. Empty the dish when the ash reaches a third of the depth, while the embers are out, and wash it with warm soapy water, because steel releases tar easily and does not hold smell.
