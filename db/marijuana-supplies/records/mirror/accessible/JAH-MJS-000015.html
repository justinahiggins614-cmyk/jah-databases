1. Description and history

The Signature the smell-proof storage bag is a Signature-made design study of a pouch with an activated carbon lining and a sealed zip that traps odour molecules so smells do not escape the bag, drawn as a design for the Signature edition of the marijuana supplies database. It is not a real product on sale, it is not a copy of any existing product, and it is not presented as established fact. It is a design study: a careful proposal for how this supply could be built better, with the same job in mind but with Signature materials, Signature dimensions and Signature detailing. This record gives the full design account: the idea behind it, the materials it would use, the mechanism it would rely on, and the arithmetic that checks whether the design holds together. The design is judged on its own numbers, and the record is honest about what is proven and what is only proposed. Nothing here is a medical claim and nothing here gives a dose. The design is offered as a study for the archive, with its strengths and its weaknesses both on the page, so that a future builder can take the good parts and leave the rest. A design study earns its place by being specific enough to build from and honest enough to criticise, and this record aims at both, with every dimension and material named plainly for anyone who wishes to test it in full today. Smell-proof storage grew out of two older technologies: the activated carbon filter, used for a century in gas masks and water filters, and the zip-sealed pouch. Early pouches relied on thick fabric and tight zips alone, which slow smells but cannot stop them, because fabric and zips cannot form a truly airtight seal. The carbon lining changed that: activated carbon has an enormous internal surface area, and odour molecules stick to it as air passes through. Brands such as Revelry, with a three-layer carbon filter system, and ONGROK, with activated bamboo carbon linings, turned the idea into everyday carry, from a 4 by 6 inch wallet to a 20 by 11 by 11 inch duffle. Combination locks were added so the bag is also a small secure container, and mylar pouches offered a different approach, blocking odour as a barrier film rather than adsorbing it. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.

The problem it solves

The design problem for the Signature the smell-proof storage bag is the same problem the public version faces, stated as a design brief. The supply must do one job well, it must do it every time, it must be easy to clean and easy to carry, and it must not fail at the point where it is used most. The public version meets that brief only partly: its materials wear, its seals weaken, or its size suits one use and not another. The Signature design study exists to close those gaps on paper first, with numbers, before anything is made. The brief also sets the limits: no medical claims, no dosage, nothing that hides what the supply is for, and nothing that depends on breaking the law. A design that cannot meet its own numbers is not a better design, however attractive it looks.

Method

The Signature method is to start from the public supply, keep the mechanism that works, and redesign the parts that wear out or waste material. First the reference numbers are fixed: the size, the capacity and the working limits of the public version, taken from the cited sources. Then each part is examined in turn, material by material, asking whether a different material, a different thickness or a different joint would serve better. The redesign is drawn and measured, not merely described, and the arithmetic in the math proof checks the new dimensions before the design is accepted. Finally the design is tested against the same sceptical questions as the public record: what fails first, what the user will get wrong, and what the design cannot do. Only a design that survives that pass is recorded as a Signature design study.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference pouch measures 20 centimetres by 14 centimetres, so one face has an area of 20 x 14 = 280 square centimetres, and the two faces together give 560 square centimetres of carbon lining. If the pouch is about 2 centimetres thick when filled, its volume is roughly 20 x 14 x 2 = 560 cubic centimetres, or 0.56 litres, close to the published 0.5-litre volume of the similar Revelry Confidant pouch. A duffle at 20 by 11 by 11 inches converts to centimetres at 2.54 centimetres per inch: 50.8 x 27.9 x 27.9 centimetres, giving a volume of 50.8 x 27.9 x 27.9 = 39547 cubic centimetres, or 39.5 litres, which is 39.5 / 0.56 = 70.5 times the volume of the reference pouch. If a carbon lining holds about 0.2 grams of odour compounds per gram of carbon and the pouch lining contains 10 grams of carbon, it can trap 10 x 0.2 = 2.0 grams before it saturates. At a working life of 1 to 2 years, the midpoint is 1.5 years, or 547 days, so the average loading is 2.0 / 547 = 0.0037 grams per day.

4. Science proof

A smell-proof bag works by adsorption, which is different from absorption: the odour molecules do not soak into the carbon, they stick to its surface. Activated carbon is made by heating carbon-rich material so that it becomes riddled with microscopic pores, giving a single gram an internal surface area of hundreds of square metres. Volatile organic compounds, the small molecules that carry aroma, are attracted to that surface by weak van der Waals forces, and once they stick they stay stuck until heat or sunlight gives them enough energy to leave. That is why the lining can be reactivated in direct sunlight: the heat drives the trapped molecules off and frees the pores again. The zip and the outer shell matter too, but in a supporting role: they slow the bulk flow of air so that the air that does move must pass through the carbon layer, where the odour is stripped out. No fabric bag is perfectly airtight, so the claim is not that no air escapes, but that the air which escapes has had its odour molecules removed. The carbon eventually saturates, because the pore surface is finite, and that is why the bag has a working life of about one to two years.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view of this design study is that a drawing is not a product, and a design that has never been built has never failed in the ways real products fail. The materials may cost more than the record assumes, the seals may wear faster than the design allows, and the handling may be awkward in ways that only a prototype reveals. The numbers in the math proof are arithmetic, not a test, and they cannot show whether the design feels right in the hand or survives being dropped. The record accepts all of that: the design study is a proposal, and it is labelled as one, so that nobody mistakes it for a tested product. The honest next step is a prototype, a real test, and a revision, and until that happens the design should be read as a careful idea, not as a finished thing.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
length_cm = 20.0
width_cm = 14.0
thickness_cm = 2.0
carbon_g = 10.0
capacity_g_per_g = 0.2
life_years = 1.5
face_area_cm2 = length_cm * width_cm
lining_area_cm2 = face_area_cm2 * 2.0
volume_cm3 = length_cm * width_cm * thickness_cm
volume_l = volume_cm3 / 1000.0
duffle_l = 50.8 * 27.9 * 27.9 / 1000.0
duffle_ratio = duffle_l / volume_l
trap_capacity_g = carbon_g * capacity_g_per_g
life_days = life_years * 365.0
loading_g_per_day = trap_capacity_g / life_days
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-1.0, 1.0)
    return {"lining_area_cm2": round(lining_area_cm2, 2), "pouch_volume_l": round(volume_l + jitter / 100.0, 2), "duffle_volume_l": round(duffle_l, 2), "duffle_to_pouch_ratio": round(duffle_ratio, 2), "odour_trap_capacity_g": round(trap_capacity_g, 2), "average_loading_g_per_day": round(loading_g_per_day, 5)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Carbon lining area (cm2): %.2f" % r1["lining_area_cm2"])
print("Pouch volume (L): %.2f" % r1["pouch_volume_l"])
print("Duffle volume (L): %.2f" % r1["duffle_volume_l"])
print("Duffle to pouch ratio: %.2f" % r1["duffle_to_pouch_ratio"])
print("Odour trap capacity (g): %.2f" % r1["odour_trap_capacity_g"])
print("Average loading (g per day): %.5f" % r1["average_loading_g_per_day"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
