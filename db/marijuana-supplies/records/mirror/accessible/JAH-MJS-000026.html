1. Description and history

The Signature Lance Straight Tube is a Signature-made design study for straight-tube bong, a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — smoke from the bowl travels down a stem below the water line in a single straight cylinder, bubbles up through the water, and is drawn straight to the mouthpiece with very little drag — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature borosilicate glass with a thickened 5 mm foot ring; glass downstem and bowl. The design is sized at 340 mm tall; tube 55 mm diameter; flat foot 80 mm diameter; water depth 50 mm, which sets every number in this record, from the volumes and areas in the demo to the scale variants at the end. The straight-tube bong is the oldest and simplest water-pipe shape: one cylinder from foot to mouthpiece, standing on a flat disc base. It descends from the bamboo water pipes of Asia and the hookah, and the word bong itself is adapted from the Thai baung, meaning a wooden cylinder or pipe cut from bamboo. A 1981 United States patent (4,253,475) describes the classic form in detail: a single vertical pipe about 1.5 inches in diameter sealed at the bottom, a bowl on a tube leading in near the bottom, water just above the junction, and a small hole above the water line to clear the chamber. Glass straight tubes became common in the twentieth century as borosilicate glass made heat-resistant water chambers practical. Trade guides describe the straight tube as the simplest design there is, with the fastest clear and the least drag, and the easiest shape to clean. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. Trade guides and manufacturer manuals preserve the steps of that change. The history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: straight-tube bong needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

Materials and design

This is a Signature design study, not a real product. Borosilicate glass is chosen for the tube because a straight cylinder is the easiest shape to blow evenly, so the wall thickness stays uniform from foot to mouthpiece and the piece does not develop a thin spot that cracks first. The foot is a separate disc fused to the bottom of the tube, and it is made wider than the tube so the footprint is larger than the smoke path: the wider the foot, the harder the piece is to tip. Water is the filtration medium, and its depth is set by the downstem length: the tip must end below the surface, and the depth is kept shallow so the draw pressure stays low and the clear stays fast. The joints are the same ground-glass tapers as on the beaker, 14 mm or 18 mm, sealing by friction with no gasket and no thread. The downstem enters through a joint in the tube wall at about 45 degrees, and its tip ends below the water line. The tolerance that matters is the fit of the foot to the tube: the fused joint must be even all the way around, or the tube will lean and the water line will sit higher on one side. The mouthpiece rim is fire-polished so it has no sharp edge. The outside is left smooth and clear so the water level and the smoke can be seen, and the straight wall makes cleaning simple: a brush or a shake of salt and alcohol reaches every part of the inside. Variants change the wall thickness, the foot width, and whether ice pinches are pressed into the tube, but the single straight cylinder stays the same in every version, which is what makes the shape the easiest to clean and the fastest to clear.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = pi * r^2 * h

V is the volume of a cylinder in cubic millimetres; r is the cylinder radius in millimetres; h is the cylinder height in millimetres.

The demo treats the tube as a cylinder. The cross-sectional area is pi times the tube radius squared, with a tube diameter of 55.0 mm. Multiplying that area by the water depth of 50.0 mm gives the water volume, and multiplying it by the full height of 340.0 mm gives the total tube volume, both reported in millilitres. Subtracting the water volume from the total tube volume gives the chamber air volume above the water. The draw pressure is water density times gravity times depth: 1000 kilograms per cubic metre times 9.81 metres per second squared times the water depth in metres, which the program reports in pascals. All numbers come from the specification in this record, and the program prints each result with its unit so the reader can repeat the arithmetic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation. 4. Science proof

The straight tube works by keeping the smoke path short and straight. A cylinder has the same cross-section all the way up, so the smoke meets no narrowing, no bend, and no extra chamber on its way to the mouthpiece, and the draw feels direct because the pressure drop along a straight smooth tube is small. The water volume is the cylinder cross-section times the water depth, and because the tube is narrow the water volume is small: about 88 millilitres in the common example, against about 233 millilitres for a beaker of similar height. That small water volume is both the strength and the weakness of the shape: less water means less drag and a faster clear, but also less cooling, because the smoke spends less time in contact with water. The draw pressure at the downstem tip is water density times gravity times depth, only a few hundred pascals, so the pull needed to start the bubbles is small. Borosilicate glass is used for the same reason as in the beaker: low thermal expansion at the hot bowl joint and a clean flavour. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Build it yourself

As a design study, the Signature Lance Straight Tube has not been built; the steps below describe the closest genuine maker activity for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. A straight-tube bong cannot be made at home: blowing a straight borosilicate cylinder with a fused foot and a ground joint is a skilled glassblowing trade, and no home method produces it safely. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought kit and making a carry sleeve for it. Buy a straight tube, a downstem, and a bowl in matching joint sizes, and check the fit dry before any water goes in: the downstem should seat firmly without wobble, and its tip should end below the intended water line. Then make a sleeve from a length of thick felt or neoprene: measure the tube circumference, cut the material to wrap once around with a small overlap, and stitch or glue a hook-and-loop strip along the seam so the sleeve opens flat. The sleeve protects the glass in storage and gives a steady grip. Test the fit by sliding the sleeve on and off the dry tube; it should move freely without catching on the joint. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

3D-print version

As a design study, the Signature Lance Straight Tube has not been printed or built; the directions below are for the printable accessory for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. Home 3D printing cannot make every supply in this database, and this section says plainly where the limit falls. A glass straight-tube bong is not 3D-printable at home: no home filament prints glass, and a printed plastic tube would soften near the hot bowl joint and would not seal to a glass downstem. The printable accessory is a foot collar and grip sleeve for the tube. Print the collar in PETG, which tolerates warm water and does not crack as easily as PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 perimeters, and 25 percent gyroid infill, and print a separate TPU grip sleeve at 0.2 mm layers with 15 percent infill so it stays flexible. Print the collar flat on its bottom face with no supports, and the sleeve upright with a brim to stop it lifting. Print time (estimate): 1 hour 40 minutes for the collar and 2 hours 10 minutes for the sleeve, at the settings given. Unmodelled factors that change real print time and strength are wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing: a different slicer profile, a slower first layer, or a damp filament spool will change the result, and the estimate above is not a validated print job. Check the finished accessory against the piece it serves before use, and reprint it if the fit is loose, because a holder that does not grip is worse than no holder at all.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: straight-tube bong water and chamber volumes — Signature Lance Straight Tube."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 55.0; h = 340.0; water_h = 50.0; foot = 80.0
    area = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2
    water_mm3 = area * water_h
    total_mm3 = area * h
    air_mm3 = total_mm3 - water_mm3
    pressure_pa = 1000.0 * 9.81 * (water_h / 1000.0)
    return {"water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "tube_volume_mL": round(total_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "chamber_air_mL": round(air_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "draw_pressure_Pa": round(pressure_pa, 2), "water_depth_mm": water_h, "tube_diameter_mm": d}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["water_volume_mL", "tube_volume_mL", "chamber_air_mL", "draw_pressure_Pa", "water_depth_mm", "tube_diameter_mm"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
