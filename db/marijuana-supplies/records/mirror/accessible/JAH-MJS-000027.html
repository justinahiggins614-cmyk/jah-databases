1. Description and history

The Signature Grove Tree Perc is a Signature-made design study for percolator bong (tree/arm), a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — smoke from the bowl is forced through a tree percolator of hollow branching arms, each cut with slits, so it breaks into many small bubble streams in the water before rising to the mouthpiece — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature borosilicate glass with reinforced arm roots; 12-arm tree percolator; glass downstem and bowl. The design is sized at 400 mm tall; tube 65 mm diameter; 12-arm tree percolator, each arm 45 mm long with 8 slits; water depth 55 mm, which sets every number in this record, from the volumes and areas in the demo to the scale variants at the end. The percolator bong grew out of the plain water pipe once glassmakers learned to fuse extra chambers and arms inside the tube. The tree percolator, a central stem with branching hollow arms cut with slits, was one of the earliest percolator designs, and trade guides still describe it as the most universal percolator style, usually built with four to twelve arms. The idea is old in outline: breaking one large bubble into many small ones increases the surface where smoke meets water, which is the same diffusion principle used in laboratory glassware and aquarium air stones. Percolator bongs became widely sold in the 2000s and 2010s as glassblowing workshops competed on diffusion designs, adding honeycomb discs, showerheads, and matrix grids alongside the tree. The tree arm remains popular because it diffuses strongly while keeping the draw open. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. Trade guides and manufacturer manuals preserve the steps of that change. The history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

Materials and design

This is a Signature design study, not a real product. Borosilicate glass is chosen for the percolator because the arms must be blown thin and hollow and then cut with slits, and only a glass that tolerates heat and holds a fine edge will keep those slits open and even. The central stem carries the smoke down to the arms, and each arm is a hollow tube open at its slits, so the smoke has nowhere to go but out through the water. The tube wall is made thicker than on a plain bong, because the fused percolator adds weight inside the tube and the piece is handled more during cleaning. Water is the filtration medium, and its level must cover the arms: if an arm sits above the water, its slits pass smoke without diffusing it. The joints are the same ground-glass tapers as on the beaker, 14 mm or 18 mm, sealing by friction with no gasket and no thread. The percolator itself has no joint: it is fused into the tube while the glass is hot, so the arms and the stem are one piece of glass with the tube. The tolerance that matters is the slit size: slits cut too wide pass large bubbles and diffuse poorly, and slits cut too narrow clog quickly and tighten the draw. The downstem seats into the base joint at about 45 degrees, and its tip ends below the water line. The outside is left smooth and clear so the percolator can be seen working and the water level read at a glance. The arms are annealed slowly after they are cut, so the glass around each slit does not hold stress that would crack later. Variants change the arm count, the slit count, and whether a second percolator is stacked above the first, but the tree shape, a central stem with branching arms, stays the same in every version.

4. Science proof

The tree percolator works by diffusion, which is the splitting of one flow into many small flows. Smoke enters the central stem and is forced out through the slits in each hollow arm, so instead of one large bubble rising from the downstem, dozens of small bubbles rise at once from every arm. A small bubble has more surface area for its volume than a large one, so more of the smoke touches water, and more heat passes into the water before the bubble reaches the surface. The total slit area sets how easily smoke gets out: eight arms with six slits each give forty-eight streams, and the sum of the slit areas is the opening the smoke must pass through. If the slits clog with resin, the opening shrinks, the draw tightens, and the diffusion falls, which is why tree percs must be cleaned often. The arms are fragile because they are thin hollow glass with slits cut into them, and a knock that a plain tube would survive can snap an arm. The percolator sits above the base water so the smoke is diffused twice: once at the downstem and once at the arms. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Build it yourself

As a design study, the Signature Grove Tree Perc has not been built; the steps below describe the closest genuine maker activity for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. A percolator bong cannot be made at home: fusing a tree percolator with hollow slitted arms inside a glass tube is a skilled glassblowing trade, and no home method produces it safely. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought kit and making a cleaning stand for it. Buy a percolator tube, a downstem, and a bowl in matching joint sizes, and check the fit dry before any water goes in: the downstem should seat firmly without wobble, and the water must cover the percolator arms when filled. Then make a drying stand from a block of hardwood: drill a hole the diameter of the tube, cut a slot for the joint, and sand it smooth so the piece rests upright while it drains after cleaning. Test the stand by setting the dry bong in it and pressing the rim gently from the side; it should not rock, and the arms should not touch the wood. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: tree percolator slit area and chamber volumes — Signature Grove Tree Perc."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    arms = 12; slits = 8; w = 1.2; l = 9.0; d = 65.0; h = 400.0; water_h = 55.0
    slit_area = w * l
    total_slit = arms * slits * slit_area
    streams = arms * slits
    area = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2
    water_mm3 = area * water_h
    chamber_mm3 = area * h
    return {"arms": arms, "slits_per_arm": slits, "slit_area_mm2": round(slit_area, 2), "total_slit_area_mm2": round(total_slit, 2), "bubble_streams": streams, "water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "chamber_volume_mL": round(chamber_mm3 / 1000.0, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["arms", "slits_per_arm", "slit_area_mm2", "total_slit_area_mm2", "bubble_streams", "water_volume_mL", "chamber_volume_mL"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
