Cardiac Catheterization Lab

The cath lab threads catheters through wrist or groin arteries to the heart, injecting dye to find blockages. Interventional cardiologists open them with balloons and stents. It is where heart attacks are stopped in real time.

PCI for Heart Attack

Percutaneous coronary intervention inflates a balloon in the blocked artery and leaves a stent to hold it open. Done within 90 minutes of arrival, it saves heart muscle by the minute. Most patients go home in days.

Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting

CABG reroutes blood around blocked arteries using the patient's own vessels, performed by cardiac surgeons on bypass. It treats severe multi-vessel disease that stents cannot. Recovery takes weeks, but results last decades.

Echocardiography

Echo uses ultrasound to watch the heart beat in real time — valves, chambers, pumping strength. It is painless, radiation-free, and answers most cardiac questions. Every heart failure patient gets one.

Stress Testing

Stress tests watch the heart work hard — on a treadmill or with drugs — while ECG and imaging look for starving muscle. A positive test points to blockages needing a cath. It stratifies risk safely.

Telemetry beams every heartbeat to a central station watched around the clock. Dangerous rhythms trigger instant alarms and rapid response. It guards post-heart-attack and post-procedure patients continuously.

Heart Failure Management

Heart failure care balances fluids with diuretics, strengthens the pump with guideline drugs, and monitors weight daily. Nurses teach the 'call for 3 pounds in a day' rule. Good management keeps patients home, not hospitalized.

Electrophysiology and Ablation

EP studies map the heart's electrical circuits, then ablation burns or freezes the misfiring spots. It cures many arrhythmias like atrial flutter and SVT. Patients often leave the next day, rhythm fixed.

Pacemakers and ICDs

Pacemakers keep slow hearts beating on time; ICDs shock deadly rhythms back to life. Implanted under the collarbone in under an hour, they are checked wirelessly for years. They are among medicine's most successful devices.

TAVR Valve Replacement

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement threads a new valve through the leg artery, no open chest needed. High-risk patients who could never survive surgery get new valves and go home in days. It revolutionized valve care.

Cardiac rehab is supervised exercise, education, and counseling after heart events — and it cuts mortality. Patients rebuild strength over 36 sessions while learning heart-healthy living. Every eligible patient should be referred.

Door-to-Balloon Time

The clock from hospital arrival to opened artery must stay under 90 minutes. EDs, cath labs, and EMS drill this handoff relentlessly. Each 30-minute delay raises mortality — speed is muscle saved.

Atrial Fibrillation Care

AFib's chaotic rhythm raises stroke risk fivefold, so blood thinners are the cornerstone. Rate or rhythm control, ablation, and lifestyle changes complete care. Many patients live fully active lives with managed AFib.

Valvular Heart Disease

Diseased valves — stenotic or leaky — are repaired or replaced surgically or via catheter. Echo tracks severity over years until intervention time. Valve disease is increasingly a catheter, not surgical, fix.

Cardiac Surgery ICU

After open-heart surgery, patients recover in a dedicated ICU with chest tubes, pacing wires, and hour-by-hour weaning. Nurses watch for bleeding, arrhythmias, and tamponade. Most transfer to step-down within days.

Anticoagulation Management

Blood thinners prevent clots in AFib, valves, and DVT — but raise bleeding risk. Warfarin needs INR checks; newer agents need kidney monitoring. Pharmacists and clinics keep the balance safe.

GRACE and TIMI scores turn ECG, troponin, and history into risk numbers for acute coronary syndromes. High scores mean early invasive strategy. They make triage decisions objective and fast.

Cardiogenic Shock Care

When the heart cannot pump enough, shock teams deploy balloon pumps, Impella devices, or ECMO to support circulation. It is the ICU's highest-stakes cardiology care. Early recognition and mechanical support save lives.
