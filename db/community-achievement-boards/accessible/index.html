  1. A Board That Shows Member Progress in Plain Sight

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    Community achievement boards are public boards where a community shows what its members have done: badges for finished tasks, streaks for returning members, and a season board everyone can follow. A community where nobody can see what anyone has done feels quiet and still, because good work happens in corners and nobody knows to praise it. A board that shows member progress in plain sight changes that: every finished task, every badge, every streak is written where any member can read it, under the name or pen name the member chose. Without a public board, members work alone, new members cannot see what is possible, and the community has no shared story of its own progress to tell. Worked example: in a website built this way, a board that shows member progress in plain sight runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Public boards of achievement are an old habit: a noticeboard with names in gold, a school honors list, a club trophy shelf, a parish roll of good deeds. The website form grows out of that habit, adding badges a member can earn in a day, a streak anyone can follow, and a season board that gives the whole year its shape. Public boards of progress are an old habit, from school honors lists to workshop walls where finished work was pinned for all to see.

    Proposed solution: Name the achievement board and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every achievement board the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one achievement board without signing in; Check the achievement board works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a public board, a badge system, a weekly challenge, and a review step before any badge is published. Nothing else is needed to open the first achievement board.
