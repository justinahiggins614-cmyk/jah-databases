  1. An Alert for One Good and One Target Price

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    Price drop alerts tell a shopper when the example price of a generic kind of good falls below a target price the shopper chose, and stay quiet the rest of the time. An alert with no target price is noise, because it speaks whenever any price moves, and a shopper who hears every move soon stops listening. An alert for one good and one target price speaks only when the example price of that good falls below the price the shopper chose, in plain numbers, so the shopper is told at the right moment and not before. Without a target price, the alert shouts at every small change, and the shopper who is shouted at daily turns the alert off. Worked example: in a website built this way, an alert for one good and one target price runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Waiting for a price to fall is an old shopping habit: a shopper who asks a stallholder to keep a good aside, a neighbor who knocks when the price drops, a note left on a shop door. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a target price a shopper chooses, an alert that arrives only when the price falls, and a list the shopper can edit freely. Target price alerts grew out of the old habit of telling a stallholder the price at which a good becomes worth buying.
