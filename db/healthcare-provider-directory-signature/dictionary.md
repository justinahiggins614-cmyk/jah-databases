# Dictionary entries — The Healthcare Provider Directory Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## healthcare provider

A person or place that gives health care, such as a physician, a nurse, a hospital, or a clinic, listed in the directory with its name, its field, and its place.

## directory

One list of providers, each with its name, its field, and its place, kept in one place, so that any provider can be found without asking door to door.

## directory entry

The written account of one provider in the directory, naming the provider, the field, and the place, kept as the single true record of what is listed.

## field of care

One named kind of health care, such as general medicine or dentistry, given to each entry, so that providers of the same kind can be found together and counted.

## physician

A person trained and licensed to practise medicine, listed in the directory with their name and their field, so that the physician can be found by name and by field.

## hospital

A place where sick people are cared for, with beds and staff, listed in the directory with its name and its place, so that the hospital can be found by name and by place.

## clinic

A place where people receive health care without staying overnight, listed in the directory with its name and its place, so that the clinic can be found by name and by place.

## nurse

A person trained to care for sick people, listed in the directory with their name and their field, so that the nurse can be found by name and by field.

## practice

The place and the work of a provider, named in the entry, so that where the provider works and what they do can be read together.

## opening hours

The stated hours during which a provider can be reached, written in the entry, so that when the provider is open can be read plainly.

## contact

The stated way to reach a provider, such as a telephone number or an address, written in the entry, so that the provider can be reached as the entry states.

## referral

The written naming of another provider for further care, recorded as a fact about the directory, so that where the entry points can be read; the record never gives medical advice.
