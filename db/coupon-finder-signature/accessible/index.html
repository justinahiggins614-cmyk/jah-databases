The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Coupon List for One Kind of Good, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Checklist With Review Before Launch, in the Signature edition.

    History: Coupons are an old shopping habit: a paper slip cut from a newspaper, a stamp collected at a shop, a voucher handed out at a fair. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search box that finds a coupon by kind of good, a clear expiry date, and a saving amount a shopper can compare. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

    Proposed solution: Add a Signature tournament bracket with rounds, divisions, and a season; Add a Signature creator studio so visitors can build a new coupon search; Add a Signature review step so every new coupon search is checked before it is published; Add the Signature coupon search in many languages, with the same rules in each; Add a Signature offline pack so the coupon search works with no connection.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a coupon list for generic kinds of goods, a search box, plain terms, and a clear expiry date. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex coupon searchs open.

    Verification path: A tournament runs a full season, a creator publishes a new coupon search through the review step, and the offline pack is tried with no connection. The demo program prints the same total as the math proof, and every step is checked by a second reader.

    Science proof: total = count x each. In plain words, this equation turns counted quantities into one checkable number: with 23 items at 1 each, the arithmetic gives 23 records, and the demo prints that same total, so the claim can be checked by arithmetic alone.

    Math proof: Worked arithmetic: 23 x 1 = 23. The demo sets count to 23 and each to 1, multiplies them, and prints Total: 23, so a fresh run of the demo verifies this proof exactly.
