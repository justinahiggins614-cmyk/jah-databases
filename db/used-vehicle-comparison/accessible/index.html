Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, Used Vehicles Side by Side on Price and Age, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, The Vehicle Comparison Championship Format.

    History: Buyers have always compared used vehicles side by side, from classified pages in a paper to walking a row of cars on a lot. The website form grows out of that habit, adding one plain table where every vehicle shows the same facts, so an honest comparison takes minutes instead of days. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
