  1. Describing the Look You Like in Plain Words

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The personal style assistant is a plain website where a person describes the look they like in their own words and gets gentle suggestions for putting outfits together from clothes they already own, with no pressure to buy anything. A look that is never described is a look that cannot be found, because the assistant has nothing to work from and the visitor has no way to say what they want. Describing the look in plain words, such as plain, bright, warm, or neat, gives the assistant a starting point it can truly use. Without plain words, the assistant guesses, and a guess about taste is wrong more often than it is right. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. Describing a look in plain words is an old habit of friends at the mirror, who have always said what they like before choosing.

The website form grows out of that habit, adding a look book of plain examples, a suggestion list, and a rule that every suggestion starts from what the visitor already owns. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
