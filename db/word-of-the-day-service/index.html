Section 1: Description and history

The word-of-the-day service sends one word each morning: the word, its meaning in plain words, one example sentence, and one question that uses the word. A word a day is an old habit: desk calendars, newspaper columns, and word lists kept in notebooks. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a shared day so every reader meets the same word, and an archive of every past day. Every record below carries its own history section, so the description and the history of each design are told together, in full sentences and paragraphs, never as stubs.

Section 2: Math proof

Every record carries a worked arithmetic proof: a count multiplied by a per-item amount gives a total, and the demo program prints that same total, so a fresh run of the demo verifies the proof exactly. A Word About Morning Light Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A word-of-the-day website: one word each morning, with its meaning in plain words, one example sentence, and one question that uses the word. Words are picked for everyday use, checked for plain meaning before publishing, and the archive lets a reader browse every past day. This record covers A Word About Morning Light.

A word about morning light must be one word, told in plain words: the word, its meaning, one example sentence, and one question that uses it. The word must be useful in everyday speech, because a word nobody can use is a word nobody will remember. Without the example, the word is only a definition, and a definition alone is hard to keep. This record gives the word, its meaning, its example, and its question. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a word about morning light runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the rules of this database applied throughout.

History

A word a day is an old habit: desk calendars, newspaper columns, and word lists kept in notebooks. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a shared day so every reader meets the same word, and an archive of every past day. Words about morning light grew out of the old habit of greeting the day with a word worth carrying into it.

Proposed solution

Name the word of the day and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every word of the day the same fair start: the same inputs, the same steps, the same result; Let a visitor try one word of the day without signing in; Check the word of the day works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

Feasibility
