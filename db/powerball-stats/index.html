Powerball Game Overview and Current Matrix

JAH-PBLS-000001 | powerball statistics record | simple

Powerball is a multi-state lottery game in the United States, overseen by the Multi-State Lottery Association and sold in 45 states plus the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. A play consists of five white balls drawn from a pool of 69 numbers and one red Powerball drawn from a pool of 26 numbers. A ticket costs 2 dollars per play, and the jackpot starts at 20 million dollars in annuity value and grows when no ticket matches all six numbers.

The matrix is the foundation of every statistic in this database. Five white balls from 69 give 11,238,513 possible white-ball sets, and 26 possible Powerballs multiply that total to 292,201,338 possible combinations. Every odds figure, tier probability, and expected-value illustration in this database is computed from that matrix, so a change in the matrix would change every figure. The matrix stated here was verified against current official publications on 2026-10-26.

Exact Powerball Jackpot Odds by Combinatorics

JAH-PBLS-000002 | powerball statistics record | simple

The jackpot odds follow directly from counting. The number of ways to choose five white balls from 69, where order does not matter, is C(69, 5) = 11,238,513. Each white-ball set can pair with any of 26 Powerballs, so the total number of distinct plays is 11,238,513 times 26 = 292,201,338. Exactly one of those combinations matches the drawn combination, so the jackpot odds for one play are 1 in 292,201,338.

That figure is a property of the matrix, not of any ticket, number choice, or past result. Buying more plays adds combinations covered, but each individual play still carries the same 1 in 292,201,338 chance, and no selection method changes the count. The demo recomputes the figure from the combination formula so that the arithmetic can be checked without trusting the text.

All Powerball Prize-Tier Odds

JAH-PBLS-000003 | powerball statistics record | simple

Powerball pays nine prize tiers. The jackpot tier requires matching all five white balls and the Powerball. The second tier, matching all five white balls without the Powerball, pays 1 million dollars. The remaining tiers pay 50,000 dollars for four white balls plus the Powerball, 100 dollars for four white balls or for three white balls plus the Powerball, 7 dollars for three white balls or for two white balls plus the Powerball, and 4 dollars for one white ball plus the Powerball or for the Powerball alone. The overall chance of winning any prize is 1 in 24.87.

Each tier odds figure is computed by counting: ways to pick the matched drawn numbers, times ways to pick the unmatched numbers from the undrawn remainder, times the Powerball factor (1 for a matched Powerball, 25 for an unmatched Powerball). The demo prints every tier from that counting, and the figures match the published tier table. Prize amounts are the published United States amounts; other jurisdictions may pay different lower-tier amounts, so the amounts must be checked against the rules that apply to the ticket in question.

Power Play Option Mechanics: General Information

JAH-PBLS-000004 | powerball statistics record | simple

Power Play is an optional add-on that costs 1 dollar extra per play. When Power Play is added, a multiplier is drawn for the drawing, and non-jackpot prizes are multiplied by that multiplier. The available multipliers are 2x, 3x, 4x, 5x, and 10x, with the 10x multiplier available only when the advertised annuity jackpot is 150 million dollars or less. Power Play does not apply to the jackpot prize.

Two Power Play effects deserve emphasis. First, the Match 5 prize of 1 million dollars becomes 2 million dollars under Power Play regardless of the multiplier drawn, rather than being multiplied. Second, Power Play changes prize amounts, not odds: the chance of matching any tier is exactly the same with or without Power Play, because the multiplier is applied after the draw. This record gives general information about the option only.

Powerball Matrix History: The 2015 Change

JAH-PBLS-000006 | powerball statistics record | compounded

The current Powerball matrix dates from October 2015. The change was announced on 4 October 2015 and was in force from 7 October 2015: the white-ball pool expanded from 59 to 69 numbers, and the Powerball pool shrank from 35 to 26 numbers. The stated purpose, in the Louisiana Lottery notice of the change, was to create larger average jackpots and more winning experiences.

The arithmetic effect was twofold. Jackpot odds lengthened from about 1 in 175.2 million to 1 in 292.2 million, because the larger white-ball pool added more than 117 million combinations. At the same time, the chance of winning any prize improved from about 1 in 32 to about 1 in 25, because the smaller Powerball pool made matching the Powerball alone more likely. The Match 4 plus Powerball prize also rose from 10,000 dollars to 50,000 dollars at the same change. Earlier matrices, such as 5 of 59 plus 1 of 35 from January 2012, are part of the same history, but any frequency count that mixes matrices must be split at the change, since numbers added in 2015 could not appear before it.

Powerball Frequency Method With a Simulated-Set Demo

JAH-PBLS-000008 | powerball statistics record | compounded

The frequency method for Powerball counts how many times each white ball and each Powerball has appeared in a chosen set of draws, with the two pools counted separately. Mixing the pools would be an error, because a white ball and a Powerball are drawn from different sets and have different ranges. A sound frequency table therefore has two sections: white balls 1 to 69 and Powerballs 1 to 26, each with its own counts and its own window.

Powerball Hot Numbers Method

JAH-PBLS-000009 | powerball statistics record | compounded

A Powerball hot number is a number near the top of a frequency table for a chosen window, counted separately for white balls and for Powerballs. The method is the same tally as the frequency method, read from the top: rank the white balls by count, rank the Powerballs by count, and show the counts beside the ranks so that the size of any lead is visible.

The caution is identical to the flagship caution. A white ball that led a past window has the same chance in the next draw as every other white ball, and a Powerball that led a past window has the same chance as every other Powerball. Powerball Cold and Overdue Numbers Method

JAH-PBLS-000010 | powerball statistics record | compounded

A Powerball cold number sits near the bottom of a frequency table for a chosen window, and an overdue number has a long gap since its last appearance. Both measures are computed separately for white balls and for Powerballs, because the pools differ in size and the expected gap differs: a given white ball appears on average once in about 13.8 draws, while a given Powerball appears on average once in 26 draws. A gap that looks long for a white ball may be ordinary for a Powerball.

Neither measure changes the next draw. A cold or overdue white ball has the same 5-in-69 chance of appearing in the next draw as any other white ball, and a cold or overdue Powerball has the same 1-in-26 chance as any other Powerball. The demo uses clearly labeled simulated draws and reports gaps for both pools, so that the different expected gaps can be compared directly.

Powerball Rollover and Jackpot Growth Patterns

JAH-PBLS-000011 | powerball statistics record | compounded

When no ticket matches all five white balls and the Powerball, the Powerball jackpot rolls over and grows for the next drawing. The jackpot starts at 20 million dollars in annuity value after a win, and each rollover adds an amount driven by ticket sales and the game rules. There is no cap on the jackpot, which is why rollover runs can reach hundreds of millions or billions of dollars. The record run cited in this database lasted 41 drawings before the 2.04 billion dollar jackpot of 7 November 2022 was won.

Rollover length cannot be forecast. Each drawing is random, and the chance that a rollover continues at any single drawing depends on how many distinct combinations are covered by tickets sold, which changes with sales. Powerball Expected Value: General Educational Information Only

JAH-PBLS-000012 | powerball statistics record | complex

Expected value for a Powerball ticket is computed by multiplying each prize by its probability and adding the results, then comparing the sum with the 2 dollar ticket price. At the starting jackpot of 20 million dollars, using the published fixed prizes and ignoring taxes, annuity discounting, and jackpot sharing, the expected value is about 0.39 dollars per ticket, well below the 2 dollar price. That gap is the normal state of a lottery ticket: the average ticket loses money, while a rare ticket wins a large prize.

A larger jackpot raises expected value, because the jackpot term grows while the fixed-prize terms stay the same, but the calculation must then account for sharing: a large jackpot attracts many tickets, so a win is more likely to be split. Taxes and the cash-versus-annuity choice lower the value further. This record gives general educational information about the method only and gives no advice about any individual choice to play.

Powerball Number-Pool Structure: White Balls and Powerball

JAH-PBLS-000013 | powerball statistics record | complex

The Powerball pool structure is two pools, not one. The white-ball pool holds numbers 1 to 69, and five distinct white balls are drawn from it. The Powerball pool holds numbers 1 to 26, and one Powerball is drawn from it. A single number value can therefore appear in both roles in the same drawing, for example as a white ball and as the Powerball, without any contradiction, because the draws come from separate sets.

The two-pool structure determines every probability in the game. A given white ball appears in a drawing with probability 5 in 69, and a given Powerball appears with probability 1 in 26. The jackpot probability is the product of the white-ball combination count and the Powerball count, and the overall any-prize probability of 1 in 24.87 is the sum of the nine tier probabilities. Any analysis that treats the six drawn numbers as one pool of six from a single range is wrong for Powerball.

Fallacies Specific to Powerball

JAH-PBLS-000015 | powerball statistics record | complex

Several fallacies attach specifically to Powerball because of its two pools. The first is treating a Powerball number as due or hot based on white-ball counts, or the reverse: the pools are separate, so a count from one pool says nothing about the other. The second is reading the Powerball alone as a shortcut: matching only the Powerball wins the lowest tier, but it does not bring a ticket closer to the jackpot in the next drawing, because draws are independent.

The third fallacy concerns popular number patterns, such as calendar dates. Popular patterns do not change the chance of matching, but they do raise the chance of sharing a prize when a win happens, because more tickets carry the same pattern. The fourth fallacy is jackpot-chasing arithmetic: a larger jackpot does not make any single combination more likely, even though it changes the expected-value illustration. Each fallacy fails for the same reason: lottery outcomes are random and each drawing is independent.

Powerball Compared With Mega Millions

JAH-PBLS-000016 | powerball statistics record | complex

Mega Millions is the other large United States multi-state game, and the comparison is instructive because the games are similar in structure and different in detail. Mega Millions uses five white balls from 70 plus one Mega Ball from 24, draws on Tuesdays and Fridays at 11:00 p.m. Eastern Time, costs 5 dollars per play under the format introduced on 8 April 2025, and starts its jackpot at 50 million dollars. Its jackpot odds are 1 in 290,472,336, close to Powerball 1 in 292,201,338, and its overall any-prize odds are about 1 in 23, compared with Powerball 1 in 24.87.

The differences matter for reading statistics. Mega Millions includes a built-in multiplier on every play under the current format, rather than an optional add-on, and its starting jackpot is higher while its ticket price is higher. A frequency table, tier table, or expected-value illustration built for one game cannot be carried over to the other without recomputing from the other game matrix.
