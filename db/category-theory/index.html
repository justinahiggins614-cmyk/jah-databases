Categories and Morphisms

JAH-CAT-000002 · categories and morphisms

Description: A category is defined by its objects and its morphisms, the arrows between them. For each pair of objects A and B there is a collection Hom(A,B) of all morphisms from A to B, an identity morphism for each object that does nothing, and a composition law that follows one arrow by another. Sets with functions, groups with homomorphisms, and spaces with continuous maps are all categories. JAH-CAT-000004 · natural transformations between functors

The Yoneda Lemma

JAH-CAT-000005 · the Yoneda lemma

Description: The Yoneda lemma is the fundamental lemma of category theory: it says an object is known by its relationships. For any category C, any object A, and any functor F from C to sets, the natural transformations from Hom(A,-) to F are in natural bijection with the elements of F(A). Hom(A,-) is the functor sending each object X to the set of arrows from A to X, and the bijection says that compatible families of maps out of it are exactly the elements of F(A). The lemma was proved by Nobuo Yoneda in 1954. Forgetful Functors

JAH-CAT-000024 · forgetful functors

Problem: How can structure be forgotten and rebuilt? A group is a set with an operation, and the problem is to say precisely what is lost when the operation is dropped, and what is gained when a free group is built on a set. The problem matters because free constructions are the standard way to build structure, and forgetful functors are their partners.

Method: The method is the adjunction: the forgetful functor U from groups to sets has the free group functor F as its left adjoint, so maps from a free group to a group correspond to maps from the set to the underlying set. The same pattern holds for rings, modules, and vector spaces, and the forgetful functor is faithful in each case, so no two distinct structure-preserving maps become equal when forgotten.
