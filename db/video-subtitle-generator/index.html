1. Description

A video subtitle generator writes timed words for a video: a transcript in, an SRT file out, with a number and a time for every line. The demo on this page generates subtitles in the browser, with no server and no connection. Everything runs in this browser; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Two subtitles cover 8 seconds: 4 seconds each. A time of 65 seconds is 1 minute and 5 seconds. Two videos: 3 subtitles and 2 subtitles, 5 subtitles in all. A long video: 60 seconds needs 15 subtitles at 4 seconds each.

3. Science proof

A subtitle generator works because a video is words at times, and each subtitle is a number, a time and words: the first subtitle starts at 1 second and ends at 4 seconds, and two subtitles cover 8 seconds at 4 seconds each. A time shift of 2 seconds moves 1 second to 3 seconds. That is why the local demo shows the transcript and the timed file together, and runs 0 generations on a server.

4. Working demo

Try it: video subtitle generator (writes timed subtitles)

Type transcript lines, one per line, press Generate, and read the SRT file. Each line gets 4 seconds, starting at 1 second. The demo runs 0 generations on a server: it runs in the browser.
