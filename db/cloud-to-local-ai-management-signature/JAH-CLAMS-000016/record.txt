DESCRIPTIONS
In this design the local library is a bookshelf: each model is a book, labeled with its version and fingerprint, and a book nobody has read in a month is returned to the store. The design suits managers whose users want to see, at a glance, which models live locally. This is a Signature design-study: an invented proposal for how the Cloud-to-Local AI Management Portal could be arranged, labeled as a design throughout and never presented as established fact. The design works through 3 parts: Bookshelf; Book labels; Return box. Each part is named so the design can be read, judged, and changed. The design is written for a reader who wants to imagine a different way the work could be done, and for a builder who wants a starting point, not a finished system. Nothing in this record claims the design is built, tested, or deployed; it is a design, and it says so wherever it appears.

MATH PROOF
The mathematical reasoning is plain arithmetic on the design's own figures. The figures are: books = 24; returns = 9; shelves = 6; weekly_checks = 52. Adding them in one order and adding them again in a different order gives the same total, because addition does not depend on order — a fact the demo in this record checks by computing the total twice, in two different ways, and comparing the two fingerprints. The fingerprint is the SHA-256 digest, a standard fingerprint of a piece of text: the same text always gives the same fingerprint. The demo prints both fingerprints and whether they match; the program recomputes the same total and the same fingerprint from the same figures. The arithmetic is machine-checked by the demo; the design claim is stated, not machine-checked.
total = sum of all figures; digest = SHA-256 of the total as text
The equation says: add all of the design's figures together to get the total, then take the SHA-256 fingerprint of that total written as text. The demo computes the total twice, in two different orders, and the two fingerprints must be identical.

WORKFLOW
Problem: The design answers a question: A manager that keeps local models on a bookshelf. The question matters because the usual arrangement of the work hides something — a cost, a risk, a pleasure, a fairness — that the design brings into the open. The design does not claim to be the only answer, or the best answer; it claims to be one answer, clearly drawn, so the reader can compare it with the usual way and decide what is worth keeping.
Solution: The design is built in 4 steps, in order. step 1: Shelve each model as a book; step 2: Label it with version and fingerprint; step 3: Return books nobody read in a month; step 4: Check the shelf weekly. Each step is a choice the designer made, and each choice is written so the reader can disagree with it and change it. The parts of the design are: Bookshelf; Book labels; Return box. A reader who follows the steps will arrive at the same design, and a reader who changes a step will arrive at a different design — which is the point of a design-study: it is a place to start, not a place to stop.
Answer: What the design delivers is a clear picture: a The Bookshelf that can be imagined, discussed, and improved.
