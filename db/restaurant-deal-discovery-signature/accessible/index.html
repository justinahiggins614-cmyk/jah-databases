Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Deal List for One Kind of Meal, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Checklist With Review Before Launch, in the Signature edition.

    History: Finding a meal deal is an old habit: a chalkboard outside a kitchen, a lunch special written on a card, a neighbor who knows which kitchen offers a plain meal at a fair price. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search box that finds a deal by kind of meal, clear valid days, and a saving amount a diner can compare. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
