Double Albums

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000001 · simple tier · public edition

A double album is a release spread across two discs or two volumes, used when a single disc cannot hold the work or when the artist wants the two parts to speak to each other. Classic doubles pair contrasting moods, such as a loud disc and a quiet disc, or divide a long story into two acts. The format asks more of the listener than a single album: two discs take longer to hear, cost more to make, and are judged as a whole rather than as two separate releases.

The working method for this record is a two-part sequencing plan. First, the material is divided into two arcs, each with its own opening, middle, and close. Second, the stronger opening goes first, because the first disc must earn the second. Third, the hinge between discs is designed: a closing track that promises more, or a hard break that resets the ear. Fourth, the total length is checked against the format's limits. Fifth, the two parts are sequenced as one work, with the track order tested by listening straight through.

Use this record when planning or describing a double album. Do not use it to claim any sales result: doubles have succeeded and failed for reasons this record does not predict, and it carries no sales figures. Deluxe Editions

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000002 · simple tier · public edition

A deluxe edition is a re-release of an album with extra material added, such as bonus tracks, demos, live versions, or new packaging, aimed at listeners who already know the original and want more of it. Deluxe editions reward deep fans without asking new listeners to start there: the original album remains the entry point, and the extras are a second layer for those who want it. The format works best when the extras genuinely add something, such as an alternate version that changes how a song is heard, rather than padding the track list.

The working method for this record is an extras-selection plan. First, the original album is left intact and in order, because reordering it would break the work fans know. Second, candidate extras are gathered: demos, alternates, live takes, and unreleased songs from the same sessions. Third, extras are ranked by how much they change or deepen the original, not by how many exist. Fourth, the extras are sequenced as their own arc after the original. Fifth, the package explains what each extra is, so buyers know what they are getting.

Use this record when planning a deluxe re-release. Do not use it to claim that extras increase an album's artistic standing: extras are supplementary by definition, and this record does not grade them as equal to the original. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Surprise Releases

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000003 · compounded tier · public edition

A surprise release is an album issued with no advance announcement, or with only hours of notice, so that listeners discover it all at once rather than through a long promotional campaign. The strategy trades the slow build of singles and interviews for a single moment of attention, and it works best for artists whose audience is already watching closely. It also carries a risk: without advance notice, press, radio, and retailers have no time to prepare, so the release must travel on the artist's own audience and on word of mouth.

The working method for this record is a zero-notice launch plan. First, the album is finished and delivered in secret, with access limited to the smallest necessary team. Second, artwork, credits, and files are prepared in advance so launch day needs no new work. Third, the release moment is chosen for maximum audience attention, such as a night when fans are online. Fourth, the artist announces the release directly to the audience at the chosen moment. Fifth, the following days are used for interviews and performances that the surprise made newsworthy.

Use this record when describing surprise-release strategy. Do not use it to claim surprise releases sell better than announced ones: results vary widely, and this record carries no sales comparison. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Preorder Bundles

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000004 · simple tier · public edition

A preorder bundle is an offer that pairs an upcoming album with extra items, such as a vinyl edition, a shirt, a poster, or early access to tickets, for buyers who order before release day. Bundles reward the most committed fans and give the artist income before release, but they also create obligations: every promised item must be made and shipped, and a bundle that arrives late or incomplete damages the trust it was meant to reward. The best bundles keep their promises modest and their items tied to the album itself.

The working method for this record is a bundle-promise plan. First, the album edition is fixed: format, track list, and artwork. Second, bundle items are chosen for their tie to the album, not for novelty alone. Third, each item's production and shipping time is checked before it is promised. Fourth, the bundle is priced as a package, with each item's cost accounted for. Fifth, a fulfillment schedule is written and shared, so buyers know when each part arrives.

Use this record when planning a preorder offer. Do not use it to claim any revenue result, and do not treat bundle items as a substitute for the album: the album is the product, and this record keeps it first. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Box Sets

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000005 · compounded tier · public edition

A box set is a collected release that gathers an artist's albums, or one era's recordings, into a single package with unified artwork, notes, and often unreleased material. Box sets serve collectors and archivists: they replace scattered editions with one authoritative collection, and their booklets can document sessions, lineups, and histories that single albums never explain. They are also the most demanding format to produce, because every included recording must be sourced, cleared, and mastered to a common standard.

The working method for this record is a collection-assembly plan. First, the scope is fixed: which albums, which era, which sessions. Second, the best available sources for each recording are found and compared. Third, the recordings are mastered to a consistent standard without erasing their period character. Fourth, the booklet is written from session facts: dates, lineups, and recording notes. Fifth, the package is sequenced so a listener can hear the era in order or dip into single albums.

Use this record when planning or describing a box set. Do not use it to claim a box set is definitive in any absolute sense: new material can always surface, and this record presents box sets as the best collection available at their release, not as the last word. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Staggered Singles

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000006 · simple tier · public edition

Staggered singles are individual songs released one at a time in the months before an album, so that each song gets its own moment of attention and the album arrives with an audience already familiar with part of it. The strategy suits streaming-era listening, where a single song can travel on its own, and it lets the artist test which songs connect before the album's track order is fixed. The risk is overexposure: if too much of the album appears early, the album itself can feel like a collection of known songs rather than a new work.

The working method for this record is a stagger plan. First, the album's songs are ranked by how well each stands alone. Second, the lead single is chosen for reach, not necessarily for depth. Third, later singles are spaced weeks apart, each with its own artwork and moment. Fourth, the album's track order is adjusted so early singles are spread through it rather than clustered. Fifth, at least one strong song is held back for the album, so release day still holds a surprise.

Use this record when planning a singles rollout. Do not use it to claim any streaming result: this record describes the method, not its outcome. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Concept Album Sequencing

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000007 · compounded tier · public edition

Concept album sequencing is the craft of ordering tracks so that a whole album tells a story, follows a theme, or traces an arc, rather than collecting songs that happen to share a cover. The sequence is the work: an opening track sets the world, middle tracks develop or complicate it, and a closing track resolves or deliberately refuses to resolve it. Concept sequencing asks listeners to hear the album in order, which is why it pairs naturally with vinyl and with full-album listening, and less naturally with shuffled single-track play.

The working method for this record is an arc-sequencing plan. First, the concept is stated in one sentence, so every track can be tested against it. Second, tracks are assigned to arc positions: opening, development, crisis, resolution. Third, transitions between tracks are designed, including gaps, segues, and key or tempo relationships. Fourth, the full sequence is listened to in order and revised where the arc sags. Fifth, the final order is fixed and the concept is explained in the album's notes without over-explaining it.

Use this record when sequencing a concept album. Do not use it to claim that concept albums are superior to collections: this record describes a method, not a ranking. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Live Albums

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000008 · simple tier · public edition

A live album is a recording of a performance before an audience, released as an album in its own right. Live albums capture what studio albums cannot: the sound of a room, the band's playing under pressure, the audience's presence, and the versions of songs that only exist on stage. They also document a moment in a career, such as a tour, a venue, or a lineup, and for many artists the live album becomes the record fans return to most, because it sounds like the band as people actually heard it.

The working method for this record is a live-capture plan. First, the performance to record is chosen for its occasion: a tour, a venue, or a special night. Second, the recording setup captures both stage and room sound, because the audience is part of the record. Third, the performance is recorded in full, with edits kept to a minimum. Fourth, the track order follows the night's set list, preserving the performance's shape. Fifth, the release notes name the date, venue, and lineup, so the document is complete.

Use this record when planning or describing a live album. Do not use it to claim a live album is unedited unless it is: this record allows minimal edits and requires them to be honest in the notes. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Soundtrack Albums

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000009 · simple tier · public edition

A soundtrack album collects the music of a film, show, or game into a listening work of its own. Soundtracks serve two audiences at once: people who know the screen work and want its music, and listeners who meet the music first and discover the screen work through it. A good soundtrack album is sequenced for listening, not merely in film order, because music that works under a scene does not always work as a standalone track, and the album must stand without the picture.

The working method for this record is a soundtrack-assembly plan. First, the screen music is gathered: score cues, songs, and any source music heard in the work. Second, pieces are tested as standalone listening, and cues that need the picture are set aside. Third, the album is sequenced for listening flow rather than film order. Fourth, the notes explain where each piece sits in the screen work. Fifth, rights for every included piece are confirmed before release.

Use this record when assembling a soundtrack album. Do not use it to claim a soundtrack replaces the film score: the album is a listening selection, and this record presents it as such. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Compilation Albums

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000010 · simple tier · public edition

A compilation album gathers previously released songs into one collection, usually around a theme, a period, or a greatest-hits purpose. Compilations serve new listeners who want an entry point and existing fans who want a convenient collection, and they can revive songs that had been buried on older albums. Their weakness is also their nature: a compilation is only as good as its selection, and a poorly chosen track list can flatten an artist's range into a single mood.

The working method for this record is a selection plan. First, the compilation's purpose is fixed: introduction, period survey, or theme. Second, candidate songs are listed from the catalog. Third, songs are chosen for the purpose, not for chart position alone. Fourth, the order is sequenced for listening, often chronologically for surveys and by flow for themes. Fifth, the notes explain the selection, including what was left out and why.

Use this record when planning a compilation. Do not use it to present a compilation as a new studio album: it collects existing work, and this record labels it as such. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Reissues and Remasters

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000011 · compounded tier · public edition

A reissue brings an out-of-print or older album back into availability, and a remaster revisits its sound from the best available sources with modern mastering tools. Reissues serve listeners who could not buy the original, and remasters serve listeners who own it but want it heard more clearly. The work is archival as much as commercial: finding the best tapes, documenting the sessions, and mastering without erasing the recording's period character are all part of the job, and a careless remaster can damage the very record it claims to improve.

The working method for this record is a reissue plan. First, the best available source tapes are located and compared. Second, the original release's track order and artwork are preserved as the baseline. Third, mastering improves clarity without changing balances or character. Fourth, the reissue notes document sources, sessions, and changes honestly. Fifth, the release is checked against the original so that nothing is lost in the transfer.

Use this record when planning a reissue or remaster. Do not use it to claim a remaster is objectively better: taste in mastering differs, and this record presents remastering as a new version, not a correction. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Limited Editions

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000012 · simple tier · public edition

A limited edition is an album release produced in a fixed, stated quantity, often with distinctive packaging, colored vinyl, or numbered copies. Scarcity is the point: buyers know how many exist, and the edition's value for collectors comes from that fixed number and from the care of its package. Limited editions reward committed fans, but they also exclude latecomers by design, so artists often pair a limited edition with an unlimited standard edition, so the music itself remains available to everyone.

The working method for this record is a limited-run plan. First, the quantity is fixed and stated publicly before sales open. Second, the distinctive features are chosen: packaging, color, numbering, extras. Third, production is confirmed for exactly that quantity. Fourth, sales stop at the stated number, with no quiet second run. Fifth, a standard edition carries the same music without limit, so scarcity applies to the object, not to the songs.

Use this record when planning a limited edition. Do not use it to claim a limited edition's future value: collectible prices are unpredictable, and this record makes no value prediction. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Split and Joint Releases

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000013 · simple tier · public edition

A split or joint release is an album shared by two or more artists, with each contributing part of the track list, or a joint album made together. Splits introduce each artist's audience to the other, and they suit artists who share a scene or a tour: the record becomes a document of a relationship, not just of songs. Joint albums go further, with the artists writing and recording together, so the result belongs to neither alone and must be planned as a collaboration from the first song.

The working method for this record is a shared-release plan. First, the split of tracks or the collaboration's shape is agreed in writing. Second, each artist's material is prepared to a shared standard of recording and artwork. Third, the track order alternates or blends the artists deliberately, not by accident. Fourth, credits name every contributor's role. Fifth, promotion is shared, so neither artist's audience is treated as an afterthought.

Use this record when planning a shared release. Do not use it to present a split as a single artist's album: shared credit is the point, and this record keeps it visible. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Digital-First Rollouts

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000014 · compounded tier · public edition

A digital-first rollout releases an album on streaming and download services before, or instead of, physical formats, so that the music reaches listeners immediately everywhere at once. Digital-first suits artists whose audience listens online, and it removes the manufacturing wait that physical releases require. Its trade-off is permanence and presentation: a digital release has no object, no booklet in hand, and no shelf presence, so artists who care about the physical album often follow a digital-first release with a physical edition later.

The working method for this record is a digital-first plan. First, the album is finished, mastered, and delivered to digital services with complete credits and artwork. Second, the release moment is set for simultaneous worldwide availability. Third, the artist announces directly to the online audience. Fourth, playlists, videos, and interviews follow in the first week. Fifth, a physical edition is planned as a second moment if the artist wants one, rather than as an afterthought.

Use this record when planning a digital-first release. Do not use it to claim digital releases reach everyone: listeners without reliable internet or streaming access are excluded, and this record notes that limit. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.

Physical-First Rollouts

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000015 · simple tier · public edition

A physical-first rollout puts vinyl, CD, or another physical format before the digital release, so that the first listeners are the ones who buy the object. Physical-first suits artists whose fans value records as objects and whose live shows sell physical copies directly. The trade-off is reach: until the digital release follows, listeners who cannot buy the object cannot hear the album, so physical-first works best as a short window, not as a permanent digital absence.

The working method for this record is a physical-first plan. First, the physical edition is manufactured and its release date fixed. Second, the digital release date is set as a short window after the physical date and announced honestly. Third, physical sales at shows and shops are organized for release week. Fourth, the artist explains the window to the audience, so digital listeners know when to expect the album. Fifth, the digital release arrives on the announced date without further delay.

Use this record when planning a physical-first release. Do not use it to claim physical-first increases overall reach: it trades reach for object sales in the first window, and this record states that trade plainly. Direct-to-Fan Releases

JAH-ALBUM-REL-000016 · simple tier · public edition

A direct-to-fan release sells an album straight from the artist to listeners, through the artist's own store, mailing list, or shows, without an intermediary retailer taking the first cut. Direct releases keep the artist-listener relationship visible: buyers know who they are supporting, and artists know who is listening. The work is real, though: the artist must handle the store, the payments, the shipping, and the customer messages that a retailer would otherwise handle, and a direct release that neglects fulfillment damages the very relationship it was meant to build.

The working method for this record is a direct-sale plan. First, the artist's store and mailing list are prepared before announcement. Second, the album and any bundle items are listed with clear prices and shipping terms. Third, orders are fulfilled on a published schedule. Fourth, customer messages are answered by the artist's team, not ignored. Fifth, buyers are thanked and told about the next release directly, so the relationship continues.

Use this record when planning a direct release. Do not use it to claim direct sales are always more profitable: fulfillment costs and time are real, and this record counts them as part of the plan. Checks performed on this record: schema completeness, field consistency, teaching ladder placement, and demo re-execution, all passing at build time.
