The Eye-Controlled Computing Platform Database

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. The eye-controlled computing platform lets a visitor steer a computer with looks: a steady look to open, a slow blink to confirm, a look away to pause. It is an assistive design study, and it never claims to read a mind or to judge a person's health.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to compounded to complex.

  1. A Look to Open a Page

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The eye-controlled computing platform lets a visitor steer a computer with looks: a steady look to open, a slow blink to confirm, a look away to pause. It is an assistive design study, and it never claims to read a mind or to judge a person's health. A steady look that opens a page, with a plain sign the page is open. The design for this record must state the idea in plain sentences, name what a visitor does first, and say what good looks like when it works, so a reader can judge the design without building anything. Without that plain statement, the idea stays vague, a visitor cannot tell what the design promises, and a promising idea is lost because nobody could explain it in words a newcomer can follow. A reader who can follow the design in words can also check it, and a design that can be checked is a design worth building. Worked example: in a website built this way, a look to open a page runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Following a look is an old habit: a glance toward a door, a long look at a picture, a look away when the talk is done. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain guide for each gaze command and a rest mark that stops them all. A Look to Open a Page grows out of an older habit: following a look to see what a friend was seeing, and the website form adds a plain list, a search, and a written record for it.

    Proposed solution: Name the gaze command and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every gaze command the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one gaze command without signing in; Check the gaze command works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a list of generic gaze commands, a plain description for each command, a practice guide, a rest mark, and a review step before publishing. Nothing else is needed to open the first gaze command.
