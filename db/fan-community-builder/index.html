A Welcome Page Written by the Fans Themselves Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A builder website: a fan starts a community by naming the shared interest in plain words, choosing a welcome message, and opening a notice board where members post news, pictures they made themselves, and plans for group events. Every post carries the name of the member who wrote it, every community has written rules and volunteer moderators, and nothing is shared outside the community unless the members agree. The builder names no real fan group, copies no real community, and presents no sample member as a real person. This record covers A Welcome Page Written by the Fans Themselves.

A welcome page names the shared interest in plain words, greets a new member by name, and shows the club rules where every member can read them, so a stranger knows at once what the club loves and how it behaves. The design is stated in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits: what a visitor sees first, what they can do next, and what happens after they finish. A fan community built this way stays open and kind, with every post carrying its writer's name and every rule written down where members can read it.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a welcome page written by the fans themselves runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
