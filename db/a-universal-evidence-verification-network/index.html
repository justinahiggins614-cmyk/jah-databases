This record covers A Page for One Evidence Check.

A page for one evidence check is the smallest page the network can publish now: one claim, its evidence for and against, and its verdict, all on one page a reader can finish in one sitting. The page must be written for a new reader, with no jargon and no assumed knowledge, because a page that assumes knowledge shuts out the reader it was made for. Without plain pages, the network is a list of verdicts with nothing behind them, and verdicts with nothing behind them are a poor way to judge.

An evidence verification network website: a visitor enters a plain claim, and the network shows the evidence for it and against it, each piece in plain sentences, with a plain verdict of supported, contradicted, or undecided. Every claim and verdict shown is a clearly labeled illustrative sample written for this database, never a verdict about a real claim, and the network cites no external source as fact. This record covers A Check That Costs Nothing to Run.

A check that costs nothing to run gives every reader a real place to weigh claims for free, with no payment, no sign-in, and no private messages. The network must keep its cost at nothing, because a price would shut out the readers who most need a plain verdict. Without a free network, a reader must pay to check, and paying to check is a poor way to spread honest verdicts. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a check that costs nothing to run runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Checking a claim against evidence is an old habit: a fact checked in a book, a witness asked twice, a list of reasons for and against written on one page. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain verdict and the evidence on both sides. Free checks are an old habit, from a claim weighed in a hall to a verdict given freely on a noticeboard.

Proposed solution

Combine two evidence checks into one visit: a warm-up and a main round; Let a visitor bring a friend: two players, one evidence check, one shared score; Add a daily evidence check that everyone shares on the same day; Add a streak that rewards a visitor for coming back; Add a community vote that picks the next evidence check.

This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.
