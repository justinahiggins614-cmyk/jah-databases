1. Description

Speech-to-text listens to spoken words and writes them down, so speech can be read, searched and kept. Press Start and speak, and the words appear as they are heard. It runs only where the browser supports live speech recognition; where it does not, the page says so plainly and writes nothing. This page stores nothing it hears and uploads no file.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Speech is measured in words per minute: at 150 words a minute, 10 minutes hold about 1,500 words. Accuracy is a fraction: 98 of 100 words right is 98 percent, with 2 words wrong. Confidence is a number per word: a word at 0.9 is kept, a word at 0.4 is flagged for checking.

3. Science proof

Speech-to-text works because spoken sounds can be matched to words, and the words that follow help choose between sounds that are alike. Interim results show words as they are heard, and final results settle when the speaker pauses. That is why a quiet room gives clearer results than a noisy one, and why a transcript is always checked before it is trusted.

6. Use

Press Start and speak clearly; the words appear as they are heard, then settle into final words. Press Stop to end, and edit the transcript if a word was heard wrongly. If the browser does not support live recognition, the page says so and nothing is written.
