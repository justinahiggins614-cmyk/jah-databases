The Speech-to-Text Database (Public)

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. Speech-to-text listens to spoken words and writes them down, so speech can be read, searched and kept. Press Start and speak, and the words appear as they are heard. It runs only where the browser supports live speech recognition; where it does not, the page says so plainly and writes nothing. This page stores nothing it hears and uploads no file.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced.

Full record index: https://justinahiggins614-cmyk.github.io/jah-databases-data-2/data/speech-to-text/index.jsonl.gz

  1. What Speech-to-Text Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Speech-to-text listens to spoken words and writes them down, so speech can be read, searched and kept. Ten spoken words become 10 written words.

    Math proof: Speech is measured in words per minute: at 150 words a minute, 10 minutes hold about 1,500 words. Accuracy is a fraction: 98 of 100 words right is 98 percent, with 2 words wrong. Confidence is a number per word: a word at 0.9 is kept, a word at 0.4 is flagged for checking.

    Science proof: Speech-to-text works because spoken sounds can be matched to words, and the words that follow help choose between sounds that are alike. Interim results show words as they are heard, and final results settle when the speaker pauses. That is why a quiet room gives clearer results than a noisy one, and why a transcript is always checked before it is trusted.
