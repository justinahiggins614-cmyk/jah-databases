A picture guessing website: a picture is shown cropped, blurred, or in small pieces, the player types a guess in plain words, clues are offered one at a time, and points are given for early correct guesses. The game is fair play only, with no real-money play of any kind, and every picture is either a public-domain image, an original drawing made for the game, or a picture the player supplied themselves. This record covers A Picture Guessing Round in Five Clues.

A picture guessing round needs exactly the right number of clues: too few and nobody can guess, too many and the answer is given away before the round is fun. Five clues give the round its shape, because each clue can reveal a little more of the picture while still leaving the guess to the player. Without a fixed clue count, rounds are uneven: one player guesses from a single corner of a picture while another needs the whole image, and their scores cannot be compared fairly.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a picture guessing round in five clues runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

Five clues became the natural shape once players compared scores and asked why one round was harder than another.

This record covers Scoring That Rewards Early Guesses.

Scoring is what turns guessing into a game, and the score must reward the early guess more than the late one, or the best strategy is to wait until the picture is fully revealed. A fair score gives the most points for a guess after the first clue and fewer points with every clue that follows, so bold players are rewarded and careful players still earn something. Without that slope, every player waits, every round drags, and the game loses its tension.
