Definition: The British Museum is a public museum in Bloomsbury, London, holding about eight million objects that record two million years of human history and culture. Because the collection is so large, the museum is best understood as two things at once: a public gallery and a vast study collection, used by scholars from every country.

History: The museum began with the collection of the physician and collector Hans Sloane, who left about 71,000 objects, books, and manuscripts to the nation when he died in 1753. The Rosetta Stone arrived in 1802, and the Parthenon sculptures were bought in 1816. The present Greek Revival building, designed by Robert Smirke, rose between 1823 and 1852.
