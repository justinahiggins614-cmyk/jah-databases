  1. What a Tutor Does

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. A tutor explains an idea, asks a question, and checks the answer, one step at a time. A tutor explains fractions, asks 3 questions, and checks the answers.

    Math proof: A quiz score is right answers divided by all questions. With 4 of 5 right, the score is 4 over 5, which is 0.8, or 80 percent. Mastery is a threshold: 9 of 10 right, twice in a row, is 90 percent twice. A level rises after two right answers in a row, and falls after a miss.

    Science proof: A tutor works because learning is built in small steps with quick checks: an idea is explained, a question tests it, and a mistake is explained at once, while the working is still fresh. Repeating a missed question on later days moves it into lasting memory, which is why spaced repetition beats cramming.
