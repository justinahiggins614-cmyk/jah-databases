History
Governments have bought goods and works in the open since the first towns posted notices asking builders to bid for a wall or a road, first in notices nailed to the market cross, then in printed gazettes of tenders and awards, and now in registers that hold the tender notice, the bid, the award, and the contract side by side. Public procurement opportunities are the chances a government publishes for firms to supply what it needs, so that any supplier can read what is wanted and when. From the printed gazette kept in the library to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the opportunity down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every public procurement opportunities record true.
