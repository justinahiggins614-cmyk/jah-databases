1. A Three-Panel Joke That Lands

JAH-CSCIS-000001 — simple

The comic strip creator turns one idea into a short comic: panels in the right order, characters a reader can recognize at a glance, speech bubbles that read in order, and a punchline that lands. A joke told in the wrong order does not land: the punchline comes first and the setup comes last, the reader is confused instead of amused, and the comic fails at the one thing it was made to do. A creator that builds every joke in three panels, with the setup on the left, the turn in the middle, and the punchline on the right, gives the joke its order back: the reader follows the story panel by panel, the turn surprises them, and the punchline lands. The three-panel shape is the oldest joke shape in comics, and the creator keeps it, because it works: setup, turn, punchline, and nothing else.
