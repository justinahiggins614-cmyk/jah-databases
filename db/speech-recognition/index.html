What Speech Recognition Is JAH-SPREC-000001 · simple

Evidence: established The claims in this record are documented in the cited sources; numbers in the ledger are a model, labeled as such.

Speech recognition, also called automatic speech recognition (ASR), is the technology that converts spoken language into written text. A microphone captures sound, the system extracts acoustic features from the waveform, an acoustic model scores which speech sounds are likely, a language model scores which word sequences are likely, and a decoder searches for the most probable transcript. The result is ordinary text that other software can search, store, or act on.

History

Speech recognition began as a laboratory curiosity in the 1950s, grew through hidden Markov models in the 1970s and 1980s, became a consumer product with dictation software in the 1990s, and was transformed by deep learning in the 2010s. Each era is covered in its own record in this pair; this record states the definition they all share.

Hidden Markov Models JAH-SPREC-000003 · simple

Evidence: established The claims in this record are documented in the cited sources; numbers in the ledger are a model, labeled as such.

A hidden Markov model (HMM) is a statistical model in which the system moves through hidden states and each state produces observable sounds. In speech recognition, the hidden states are speech sounds and the observations are acoustic features. HMMs powered speech recognition from the 1970s into the 2010s: they modelled how sounds follow one another, and the Viterbi algorithm found the most likely state sequence for a given utterance.

History

HMMs were developed in the late 1960s and applied to speech in the 1970s, notably at IBM and at Carnegie Mellon University. The DARPA Speech Understanding Research program of the 1970s funded systems such as Harpy, and by the 1980s HMMs with Gaussian mixture observation models were the standard approach, refined through the 1990s in systems such as CMU Sphinx.

A hidden Markov model (HMM) is a statistical model in which the system moves through hidden states and each state produces observable sounds. In speech recognition, the hidden states are speech sounds and the observations are acoustic features. The Worked Solution
