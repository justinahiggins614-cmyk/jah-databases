JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000001 — Plasma Computer Generation — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The plasma computer is a future machine whose working medium is ionized gas — plasma — rather than solid silicon. Plasma is often called the fourth state of matter: a gas heated or driven so hard that electrons tear free of their atoms, leaving a mixture of ions and free electrons that responds collectively to electric and magnetic fields. Generation is the first piece of the stack: every computation begins by turning neutral feed gas into a controlled, pure plasma volume. Real-world plasma sources are mature technology — neon signs, fluorescent lamps, semiconductor etchers, and fusion experiments all generate plasma daily. The computing use is new, but the generation physics is established.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000002 — Plasma Computer Generation — Radio-frequency capacitive generation

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A radio-frequency capacitive discharge drives ionization with alternating electric fields, typically at the industrial standard frequency of 13.56 megahertz. Electrons are light and follow the fast field, slamming into neutral atoms hard enough to knock more electrons free — an avalanche that sustains the plasma. Capacitive discharges run at modest pressures (roughly 1 to 1000 pascals), are simple to build, and are the workhorse of plasma processing. For a plasma computer, RF capacitive sources are the natural 'power switch' piece: cheap, well understood, and easy to array across many generation cells.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000003 — Plasma Computer Generation — Inductive and microwave generation

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Inductive coupling drives current loops inside the gas without electrodes touching the plasma, and microwave sources push densities higher still. Electron cyclotron resonance (ECR) sources use a magnetic field tuned so electrons orbit at exactly the microwave drive frequency — at 2.45 gigahertz that resonance field is about 0.0875 tesla — and resonant electrons absorb energy extremely efficiently, producing dense, clean plasmas used in semiconductor manufacturing and space thrusters. A plasma computer's high-performance generation pieces would likely be inductive or microwave driven, trading circuit complexity for density and purity.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000004 — Plasma Computer Generation — Direct-current breakdown and the Paschen law

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The oldest plasma generation method is simply applying enough voltage to break gas down. The Paschen law states the breakdown voltage depends on the product of pressure and electrode distance, not on either alone, and the curve has a minimum: for air the minimum is about 327 volts at a pressure-distance product near 0.57 torr-centimetres. Below or above that product, breakdown needs more voltage. This matters for computer design because it sets the minimum ignition energy of every cell: the generation stage must deliver at least the Paschen voltage for its geometry, with margin for gas mixtures and surface conditions.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000005 — Plasma Computer Generation — Saha ionization and temperature targets

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The Saha equation predicts what fraction of a gas is ionized at a given temperature and density: hotter and more diffuse gas ionizes more completely. Full thermal ionization needs stellar temperatures (tens of thousands of kelvin), but a computing plasma does not need the heat of the sun — non-thermal discharges keep electrons hot while the bulk gas stays near room temperature, giving useful ionization fractions with modest power. The design target for generation pieces is a chosen electron density (for example 10^16 to 10^19 per cubic metre), because density sets the plasma frequency, and the plasma frequency is the machine's natural clock.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000006 — Plasma Computer Generation — Debye length and collective behavior

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Ionized gas only counts as plasma when it behaves collectively: charges shield each other over a characteristic distance called the Debye length, lambda_D = sqrt(epsilon_0 k T / n e^2). A volume is a true plasma when it spans many Debye lengths and contains many particles per Debye sphere. For a computing plasma at 10^18 electrons per cubic metre and electron temperature near 3 electron-volts, the Debye length is about 13 micrometres — so a millimetre-scale compute cell spans roughly 75 Debye lengths and behaves as genuine plasma. Generation pieces must verify this condition, not just make ions.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000007 — Plasma Computer Generation — Generation safety, purity, and control

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A computing plasma must be pure: impurity atoms (sputtered wall material, water vapor, pump oil) ionize at different energies, radiate away energy, and poison logic operations. Control knobs are drive power, gas pressure, flow rate, and magnetic bias. Safety engineering handles high voltage, radio-frequency exposure, ultraviolet emission, and vacuum integrity. Diagnostics — Langmuir probes (small wires that measure current versus voltage), spectroscopy, and interferometry — are part of the generation piece, because an ungaged plasma is an uncontrolled plasma.

JAH-PLASMA-GEN-000008 — Plasma Computer Generation — Generation in the full stack

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Generation feeds containment: it must deliver plasma at the density, temperature, and purity the containment stage accepts, with smooth handoff geometry so the plasma never touches a solid surface. Standard interfaces are a density setpoint, a temperature window, a gas species list, and a flow rate. Beginner exercise: pick one feed gas (argon is the classic teaching choice), compute its Paschen minimum for a 1-centimetre gap, and size an RF drive that reaches that voltage with margin. Once generation is understood as a contract — inputs of gas and power, outputs of plasma at spec — the rest of the stack becomes composable.
