  1. What a Business-in-a-Box Kit Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A business-in-a-box kit is a complete starter set for a new small business: a plan, a name, a budget, a price list, a marketing plan and a record book, all in one place. The kit in this database holds 24 parts, and part 1 is the box itself.

    Math proof: Counting is addition: 24 parts, each counted once, make one complete kit. If 1 part is missing, 24 minus 1 leaves 23 parts, so the kit is incomplete until that part is added.

    Science proof: A starter kit works because a new owner faces too many first decisions at once, and a written kit moves those decisions from memory into a list that can be checked off one by one. Each finished part is visible proof of progress, and visible progress keeps a new owner going.

  2. The Business Plan in One Page

    Record 2 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A one-page business plan states what the business sells, who buys it, what it costs to run, and what it must earn, in four short paragraphs. A plan for a market stall selling jam reads: jam for neighbours, 40 jars a week, costs 120 dollars a week, earnings 200 dollars a week.

    Math proof: Profit is earnings minus costs: 200 dollars earned minus 120 dollars spent leaves 80 dollars profit in a week.

    Science proof: A written plan works because a goal held only in the head changes every time it is remembered, while a goal written down stays still long enough to be measured. Writing the four facts on one page forces the owner to choose, and a choice made can be tested.

  3. Choosing a Business Name

    Record 3 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A good business name is short, easy to say, easy to spell, and free to use, so customers remember it and no one else owns it. The name 'Sunny Jam' passes all four checks: two words, plain spelling, and no other jam seller using it in town.

    Math proof: Four checks, each a yes or a no: a name passes only when all four are yes, so 4 out of 4 is a pass and 3 out of 4 is not a pass.

    Science proof: A name works as a memory hook: short, plain words are easier to recall and repeat than long or tricky ones, and a customer who can say the name can recommend the business to a neighbour.

  4. Choosing a Business Structure

    Record 4 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A business structure is the legal shape of the business: a sole trader works alone, a partnership shares the work, and a company is a separate legal person. A jam seller working alone starts as a sole trader, and the kit records that choice with its reason. This is general information only, not legal advice.

    Math proof: One owner, one share of the profit: 80 dollars profit split 1 way is 80 dollars; split 2 ways between partners is 40 dollars each.

    Science proof: A structure works by drawing a clear line around who is responsible for what: when the line is written down before trading starts, later arguments about money and decisions have an answer already agreed.

  5. Registration Checklist

  6. Startup Budget (Worked Example)

The worked budget buys a stall table for 150 dollars, jars and labels for 120 dollars, a licence for 60 dollars and ingredients for 170 dollars, a total of 500 dollars.

    Science proof: A budget works by turning a vague worry about money into a list of named items with prices: once each item has a price, the total is a fact, and a fact can be saved toward with open eyes.

  7. Pricing Your Product or Service

    Record 7 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A price must cover the cost of making the product and leave a profit, or the business loses money on every sale. A jar of jam costs 3 dollars to make and sells for 5 dollars, leaving 2 dollars profit on each jar.

    Math proof: Price minus cost equals profit: 5 minus 3 equals 2 dollars per jar. Selling 40 jars gives 40 times 2, or 80 dollars profit.

    Science proof: A price works as a signal as well as a sum: too low, and customers doubt the quality; too high, and they walk past. A price built from cost plus a fair profit can be explained to any customer who asks.

  8. The Marketing Plan

The jam stall's plan reaches 3 places: the Saturday market, the town noticeboard, and word of mouth from happy customers.

  9. Building a Sales Funnel

    Record 9 of 24. working. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. A sales funnel is the path from stranger to customer: people hear about the business, some visit, some buy, and some come back. Of 100 people who hear about the jam stall, 20 visit, 10 buy, and 5 come back the next week.

    Math proof: Conversion is a fraction: 10 buyers out of 100 who heard is 10 over 100, or 10 percent. Of the 10 buyers, 5 return, which is 5 over 10, or 50 percent.

    Science proof: A funnel works because buying is a chain of small yeses: each step asks for a little more trust than the last, and measuring each step shows exactly where the chain breaks.

  10. Daily Operations

    Record 10 of 24. working. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. Daily operations are the repeated jobs that keep the business running: opening, serving, cleaning, counting the money, and closing. The jam stall's day has 5 jobs, and the day is done only when all 5 are checked off.

    Math proof: Five jobs a day, 6 days a week: 5 times 6 is 30 jobs a week.

    Science proof: A routine works because a repeated job done the same way every day becomes a habit, and a habit costs almost no thought: the owner can spend the saved thought on customers instead of remembering what comes next.

  12. Bookkeeping Basics

    Science proof: Bookkeeping works because memory of money fades fast: a dollar written down on the day it moves is a fact, while a dollar remembered a month later is a guess, and guesses cannot be added up.

  17. Cash Flow and the Break-Even Point

    Record 17 of 24. advanced. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. Cash flow is money moving in and out over time, and the break-even point is where sales exactly cover costs. The stall's fixed costs are 120 dollars a week and each jar earns 2 dollars profit, so it must sell 60 jars a week to break even.

    Math proof: Break-even is fixed costs divided by profit per sale: 120 divided by 2 is 60 jars.

    Science proof: A break-even point works as a single clear target: below it the business loses money, above it the business gains, so the owner knows exactly how many sales the week must hold.

  18. Suppliers and Inventory

    Record 18 of 24. advanced. Feasibility: buildable-now. Classification: PROVEN. Suppliers sell the business what it needs, and inventory is the stock kept on hand so the business never runs out. The stall keeps 100 jars in stock, uses 40 a week, and orders more when 20 remain, so the shelf is never empty.

    Math proof: Stock lasts stock divided by weekly use: 100 jars divided by 40 a week is 2 and a half weeks. Reordering at 20 jars leaves half a week of cover.

    Science proof: A reorder point works because deliveries take time: ordering when stock is low but not empty means the new stock arrives before the old stock runs out, and the shelf is never empty on market day.

Reinvesting profit means putting part of what the business earned back into the business, instead of taking it all out. The stall earns 80 dollars profit a week and reinvests half, 40 dollars, in new jars and a bigger sign.
