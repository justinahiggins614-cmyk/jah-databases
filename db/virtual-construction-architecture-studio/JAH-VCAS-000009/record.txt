DESCRIPTIONS
The cost estimate counts the building from the model: every wall, window, and pipe measured, priced, and added up, so the team knows the price before the building is built. The estimate is drawn from the model's quantities — the area of walls, the number of windows, the length of pipes — multiplied by the prices in the material library. The team checks the quantities by hand on a sample of items, because a model that counts wrong will price wrong. The estimate is shown with its uncertainty: a range, not a single number, and the reasons the price could move. When the design changes, the estimate is remade, so the client always knows the price of the building they are looking at. The Cost Estimate from the Model stage works through 4 parts: Quantity take-off; Price table; Uncertainty range; Re-estimate log. Each part is named, tested, and filed, so the work can be checked by a stranger. This record specifies the stage completely: what it is for, how it is done, how it is tested, what it costs in time and attention, and what can go wrong. The specification is written for a builder who has never done this stage before, and for a reviewer who must judge whether it was done properly. Inside the Virtual Construction and Architecture Studio, Cost Estimate from the Model sits between the stages that come before it and the stages that come after it, and it must fit both: what it receives must be in the shape it expects, and what it hands on must be in the shape the next stage expects. Nothing in this record is a claim that the system it specifies is deployed, live, or finished; it is a specification, documented from public knowledge and established practice, and the sources at the end of this record are the public pages it was checked against.

WORKFLOW
Problem: The problem Cost Estimate from the Model solves is simple to state and hard to do well: Counting what the building will cost, from the model itself. Without this stage, the work goes wrong in a particular way. The failure shows up late, when it is expensive to fix, and it shows up as a surprise, because nothing was recorded that would have warned anyone. The stage exists to move the failure earlier, where it is cheap, and to make it visible, where it can be fixed. The problem is not that people are careless; it is that a job with many parts and many hands needs a fixed shape, or the parts drift apart. Cost Estimate from the Model is that fixed shape for this part of the work.
Solution: The worked solution runs in 5 steps, in order. step 1: Measure quantities from the model; step 2: Price them from the material library; step 3: Check a sample by hand; step 4: Show the price as a range with reasons; step 5: Remake the estimate when the design changes. Each step has a visible result: something written, something checked, or something handed on, so the stage can be stopped and inspected at any point. The steps are done by the person or program named in the record, and each step is checked before the next begins, because a mistake carried forward is a mistake multiplied. The parts of the stage are: Quantity take-off; Price table; Uncertainty range; Re-estimate log. When a step fails, the worker stops, fixes the failure, and re-runs the step; the stage never skips a failed step and hopes the next one covers it. The whole method is written so that two workers following it would produce the same result, and a reviewer following it could find where a result came from.
Answer: The answer this stage delivers is a finished piece of work that the next stage can use without asking questions: the Cost Estimate from the Model is complete, checked, and filed. The checks that prove it are: Quantities come from the model; A sample is checked by hand; The price is shown as a range. When those checks pass, the stage is done, and the proof is the record itself — the checks, the dates, and the names. The figures that describe this stage are: estimate_range_pct = 8; items_priced = 1240; re_estimates = 12; sample_checked = 124. Those numbers are the record's own checkable figures, and the demo in this record recomputes them from the figures alone, so any reader can verify them without trusting the record's word. A stage whose checks pass is a stage the factory will stand behind.
Applies: Cost Estimate from the Model applies wherever the Virtual Construction and Architecture Studio is used: whenever a new specialist, a new routing, a new room, a new building, or a new property must pass through this stage of the work. It applies when the work is done for the first time and when it is done for the hundredth time, because the stage exists to make the hundredth time as careful as the first.
