JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000001 — Plasma Computer Containment — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Containment is the piece that makes 'no solid state' literal: hot, reactive plasma cannot be allowed to touch walls, because contact cools it, contaminates it, and destroys the computation. Instead, electromagnetic fields form invisible bottles. A charged particle in a magnetic field spirals around field lines (the Lorentz force), so a well-shaped field is a wall made of pure force. Fusion research has spent seventy years perfecting containment — tokamaks and stellarators hold plasmas at hundreds of millions of degrees — and the computing application borrows that entire science at far gentler conditions.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000002 — Plasma Computer Containment — Magnetic confinement basics

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The Lorentz force F = q(v cross B) bends charged particles into helical orbits around magnetic field lines. Particles stream freely along the field but are trapped across it, so the design problem is closing the field lines into loops — the torus of a tokamak — or plugging the ends of an open bottle with magnetic mirrors. Field strength sets the orbit size: at 1 tesla, a 3-electron-volt electron's orbit is a fraction of a millimetre, far smaller than any compute cell. Proven machines (tokamaks, stellarators, reversed-field pinches) are the reference library for every containment piece.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000003 — Plasma Computer Containment — Larmor radius and cyclotron frequency

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Two numbers govern every magnetic design. The cyclotron frequency omega_c = qB/m is how fast a particle circles the field (for electrons at 1 tesla, about 1.76e11 radians per second). The Larmor radius r_L = v_perp / omega_c is the orbit size (about 0.4 millimetres for a 3-eV electron at 1 tesla). Good confinement needs r_L much smaller than the cell: the rule of thumb is the cell should span at least ten Larmor radii. These two formulas size every magnet in the containment piece.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000004 — Plasma Computer Containment — Magnetic pressure and plasma beta

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A magnetic field pushes back on plasma with magnetic pressure B^2/(2 mu_0) — at 1 tesla that is about 400,000 pascals, four atmospheres. The ratio of plasma pressure to magnetic pressure is called beta; stable confinement keeps beta below roughly one, so the field dominates. Computing plasmas are cold and tenuous, so beta is naturally tiny — containment for computing is far easier than for fusion, and millitesla fields often suffice. The beta budget is part of every containment piece's data sheet.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000005 — Plasma Computer Containment — Electrostatic and radio-frequency confinement

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Not all bottles are magnetic. Electrostatic traps (Penning and Paul traps) hold ions with static and oscillating electric fields and are proven technology — Paul traps confine single ions for quantum computing. Radio-frequency ponderomotive forces push plasma away from high-field regions, making RF 'invisible walls'. These methods shine at small scales: a Paul-trap-like lattice could confine thousands of micro plasma cells on a chip-sized plate, each cell an independent compute unit.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000006 — Plasma Computer Containment — Magnetic mirrors and the loss cone

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A magnetic mirror reflects particles from regions of stronger field, so a bottle with strong fields at both ends traps most particles in the middle. But particles moving nearly parallel to the field, inside the 'loss cone' of velocity angles, escape out the ends — the classic leak of mirror machines. Fixes include plugging the ends with electrostatic potentials (tandem mirrors) or accepting the leak and continuously refuelling. Containment pieces specify their loss rate the way a bucket specifies its leak: as particles lost per second per cell.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000007 — Plasma Computer Containment — Instabilities and how to damp them

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Plasma confined by fields can go unstable: kink, sausage, and drift waves grow by feeding on field and pressure gradients, and magnetohydrodynamics catalogs them all. The defense is a layered one: design field shapes that are inherently stable (magnetic shear, minimum-B wells), sense growing modes with probes, and kill them with feedback coils. Computing plasmas are gentle — low beta, modest gradients — so the instability zoo is mostly a solved problem at these conditions, but the monitoring piece stays mandatory: an undetected mode scrambles logic.

JAH-PLASMA-CONTAIN-000008 — Plasma Computer Containment — Containment in the full stack

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Containment sits between generation and logic: it accepts plasma at the generation contract (density, temperature, purity) and delivers stable, addressable cells to logic. Its interfaces are a cell map (which volumes are confined and stable), a stability margin (distance to the nearest instability threshold), and a loss/refuel rate. Beginner exercise: for a 1-millimetre argon cell at 10^18 per cubic metre, compute the Larmor radius at 10 millitesla and confirm it is far smaller than the cell. That single calculation is the seed of every containment design.
