Automated Guided Vehicles in Logistics is a public record about Driverless carts that follow wires, magnets or lasers through a warehouse. Automated guided vehicles, called AGVs, are driverless carts that carry loads along fixed paths in warehouses and factories, following wires, magnetic tape or laser reflectors. AGVs have carried loads since the 1950s, and modern ones navigate by laser and cameras, so they can change routes without rebuilding the floor. A fleet controller assigns jobs to AGVs, plans their paths and keeps them from blocking each other at crossings. AGVs are used for moving pallets, towing trains of carts and feeding assembly lines, where the route is the same every day. Vehicle Routing Optimisation

Finding the shortest set of routes that serves every delivery.

Vehicle Routing Optimisation is a public record about Finding the shortest set of routes that serves every delivery. The vehicle routing problem asks for the cheapest set of routes that delivers to every customer, starting and ending at a depot, with limits on vehicle capacity and driver time. It is a hard problem: the number of possible route sets grows explosively with customers, so practical systems use heuristics that find good routes quickly rather than perfect ones. Common heuristics are savings, which merges routes that share a road, and local search, which swaps customers between routes until no swap helps. Routing software saves fuel and time every day, and it is the core of delivery, refuse collection and field service planning. The Travelling Salesperson Problem in Logistics

One vehicle, many stops, and the shortest possible tour.

The Travelling Salesperson Problem in Logistics is a public record about One vehicle, many stops, and the shortest possible tour. The travelling salesperson problem asks for the shortest tour that visits every stop once and returns home, and it is the single-vehicle version of the routing problem. The problem is famous because it is easy to state and hard to solve exactly: for twenty stops there are already more tours than a computer can list. Practical systems solve it with heuristics and with exact solvers for small cases, and the Concorde solver has solved tours with tens of thousands of stops. Every delivery driver, meter reader and repair technician lives a version of this problem every day. Last-Mile Delivery Automation

Last-Mile Delivery Automation is a public record about The final, hardest step: from the depot to the customer's door. Last-mile delivery is the final step from a local depot to the customer's door, and it is the most expensive part of delivery, often more than half the total cost. Automation attacks the last mile with delivery robots, drones, smart lockers and route planning, because the last mile has many small stops close together. Smart lockers let one driver serve many customers at one stop, and delivery robots handle short trips in neighbourhoods and campuses. The trade is access: robots need pavements, doors and permissions, so the last mile is as much a legal problem as a technical one. Inventory Optimisation in Logistics

Holding just enough stock: not so much it wastes money, not so little it loses sales.

Inventory Optimisation in Logistics is a public record about Holding just enough stock: not so much it wastes money, not so little it loses sales. Inventory optimisation finds the right amount of stock to hold: enough to meet demand, but not so much that money sits on shelves. The classic rule is the economic order quantity, which balances the cost of ordering against the cost of holding stock, and modern systems extend it to many products and warehouses. Optimisation also decides where stock should live: fast sellers near the customer, slow sellers in one central store. Good inventory control is measured by service level, the share of orders filled at once, and by turns, how often stock sells through. Autonomous Truck Platooning

Trucks driving in a tight convoy, linked by radio, to save fuel.

Autonomous Truck Platooning is a public record about Trucks driving in a tight convoy, linked by radio, to save fuel. Truck platooning links several trucks into a tight convoy by radio, so the following trucks brake and accelerate with the leader and ride in its slipstream. The following trucks save fuel by drafting, and the convoy uses less road space than the same trucks driving apart. Platooning is a step toward self-driving trucks: the leader is driven by a person, while the followers are automated, which is safer than full automation today. The challenges are radio reliability, mixed traffic cutting into the convoy, and rules that differ between countries. Intermodal Freight Automation is a public record about Containers that move by ship, train and truck without being opened. Intermodal freight moves containers by ship, train and truck without opening them, so the same box travels the whole journey and only the vehicle changes. Intermodal freight is cheaper and cleaner than road-only transport for long distances, since rail and ship carry more per unit of fuel. Supply Chain Resilience

Supply chains that bend in a crisis instead of breaking.

Supply Chain Resilience is a public record about Supply chains that bend in a crisis instead of breaking. Supply chain resilience is the ability of a supply chain to keep delivering when a supplier fails, a port closes or a demand surge hits. Resilient chains keep spare suppliers, safety stock and alternative routes, so one failure never stops the whole chain. Stress tests simulate failures, such as losing a supplier or a route, and measure how much delivery falls, which shows where the chain is fragile. Resilience costs money, since spares and stock are idle in good times, but it pays in a crisis, when a fragile chain stops and a resilient one keeps moving. Slug

Manifest
The master list of every database in the network, kept in data/manifest.json.

The type name of a record: the database slug with spaces instead of hyphens.
Identifier prefix
The short code at the start of every record identifier in a database.
Generator

Seed
The starting number given to a generator.
Generator gate
