History
Lawyers have searched the books for authority since the first case reports were printed, because a claim without a source behind it carries no weight. Legal research grew beside the books: indexes and digests, then databases, that let a researcher trace a question from a statute to a case to a commentary. From the hand-written notebook of citations to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the research down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every legal research true. It is a record of published sources only, and it gives no advice about any question.
