# Dictionary entries — The Legal Research Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## legal research

The recorded search of published legal sources for a named question, carried out so that the statutes, the cases, and the commentaries can be read from the record rather than remembered.

## statute

A written law passed by a legislature, kept in the record with its citation, so that the law can be read as it was written.

## case law

The body of past court decisions that later courts may read, kept in the record so that the published reasoning behind a decision can be followed.

## precedent

A past decision that a later court may follow, kept in the record with its citation, so that the authority can be traced to its paper.

## legal database

A kept store of statutes, cases, and commentaries that can be searched, named in the record with the sources it holds, so that where the research looked can be read plainly.

## citation

The published reference to a statute, case, or book, kept in the record so that the source can be found again in the published books.

## commentary

The published writing of a scholar about the law, kept in the record with its author and its citation, so that the view can be traced to its writer.

## digest

The ordered summary of case law by subject, kept in the record so that the cases on one question can be found in one place.

## authority

A published source that a court or a researcher may rely on, named in the record with its citation, so that the weight behind a finding can be read plainly.

## research question

The stated question that a piece of legal research sets out to answer, written in the record so that the scope of the search can be read plainly.

## finding

The written answer that legal research gives to its question, kept in the record with the sources behind it, so that the answer can be checked against its authorities.

## research memo

The written note that sets out a research question, the sources searched, and the findings, kept so that the whole research can be read in one place.
