History
Courts have called on people of special knowledge since the first trial needed a physician to explain a wound or a smith to explain a blade. Expert witnesses grew beside the courts: men and women whose trade or study let them speak to facts the court could not judge alone, listed by field so that any court could find the right voice. From the handwritten roll of learned witnesses to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the listing down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every expert witness directory entry true. It is a directory of published listings only, and it gives no advice about any case.
