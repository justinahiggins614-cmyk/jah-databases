  1. A Gathering With a Written Agenda

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    Virtual meetups are online gatherings where people meet at a set time to talk, learn, or make things together, with a host, a written agenda, and a public record of what happened. A gathering with a written agenda lets every guest know what will happen and when, because a guest who can read the agenda can choose the parts that matter to them. The agenda should name each part, its time, and its speaker, all in plain words. Without a written agenda, guests sit through the parts they do not need and miss the parts they came for. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. Worked example: in a website built this way, a gathering with a written agenda runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Meeting at a set time is an old habit, from the club night to the evening class to the town hall. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a gathering that can be joined from anywhere, a written agenda, and a public record. Writing the agenda down is an old habit of good meetings, where the order of business was always stated before it began.

    Proposed solution: Name the online gathering and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every online gathering the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one online gathering without signing in; Check the online gathering works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a gathering schedule, a host role, a written agenda, and a public record. Nothing else is needed to open the first online gathering.

    Verification path: A visitor opens one online gathering, follows the rules in plain sentences, and finishes it without help. The demo program prints the same total as the math proof, and a second visitor repeating the steps gets the same result.
