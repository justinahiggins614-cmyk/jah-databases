Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A price alert website: a shopper picks a generic kind of good, such as a cotton shirt, a wooden chair, or a steel kettle, and a target price, and the alert arrives only when the example price falls below that target. Each alert shows the old price and the new price in plain numbers, an alert list can be edited freely, and alerts never shout: they arrive once, quietly, and stop when the shopper says stop. No real store is named, no real brand is named, and no money changes hands on this site: it is a design study with example prices only. This record covers An Alert for One Good and One Target Price.

An alert with no target price is noise, because it speaks whenever any price moves, and a shopper who hears every move soon stops listening. An alert for one good and one target price speaks only when the example price of that good falls below the price the shopper chose, in plain numbers, so the shopper is told at the right moment and not before. Without a target price, the alert shouts at every small change, and the shopper who is shouted at daily turns the alert off.

This record covers An Alert That Shows the Old Price and the New Price.

An alert that says only that a price fell tells the shopper nothing about how far it fell, and a fall of one is not worth the same as a fall of ten. An alert that shows the old price and the new price in plain numbers lets the shopper judge the fall at a glance and decide whether it is worth acting on. Without both prices, the shopper must look the old price up elsewhere, and the moment to act passes while the shopper is still looking.

History

Waiting for a price to fall is an old shopping habit: a shopper who asks a stallholder to keep a good aside, a neighbor who knocks when the price drops, a note left on a shop door. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a target price a shopper chooses, an alert that arrives only when the price falls, and a list the shopper can edit freely. Alerts with both prices grew out of market tallies, where a stallholder said the old price and the new price together.
