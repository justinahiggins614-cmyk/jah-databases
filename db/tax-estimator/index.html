A Rough General Estimate in Plain Steps Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A tax website: a visitor types a plain yearly income figure, and the estimator shows a rough general estimate step by step, with every figure labeled an estimate and a plain note that tax rules change and differ by place. The estimator gives general information only, never advice for a specific person's return, and it never asks for tax identifiers, account numbers, or any real tax data. This record covers A Rough General Estimate in Plain Steps.

A rough general estimate in plain steps lets a learner see how a tax estimate is built: the income figure, the illustrative rate, and the estimate at the end, each on its own line. The steps must be plain, because an estimate with no steps is a number nobody can check. Without the steps, the learner must trust a single figure, and a single figure hides a mistake that plain steps would show. All figures are general information only, never advice for a specific person's return.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a rough general estimate in plain steps runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

Worked estimates are an old habit of plain guides, where every figure was written on its own line so the sum could be checked twice.
