Volleyball Rank 1 · simple · team sport · beginner

Volleyball is a team net sport in which two teams of six players hit a ball over a high net, trying to ground it on the opponent's court. Each team may touch the ball up to three times before returning it, and the usual pattern is a pass to the setter, a set to an attacker, and a spike over the net. A match is played as best of five sets, with sets to 25 points and a deciding set to 15, and a team must win by two clear points. Players rotate positions when they win the serve, so everyone serves, passes, and attacks in turn, and a specialist libero may replace back-row players for defence. Volleyball rewards timing, jumping, and communication, because the ball moves fast and no player can hold it.

History

Volleyball was invented in 1895 in Massachusetts as a gentler indoor game, and it grew into an Olympic sport with indoor and beach forms.

Rules

Two teams of six hit a ball over a net, scoring when the ball lands on the opponent's court or the opponents fail to return it, with sets played to 25 points.

Techniques

Key skills are the serve, pass, set, spike, and block, combined in a three-touch pattern: pass, set, attack.

Handball Rank 2 · simple · team sport · beginner

Handball is a fast indoor team sport in which two teams of seven players pass, dribble, and throw a ball into the opponent's goal. Players may hold the ball for only three seconds and take only three steps without dribbling, so play moves quickly and passing is constant. A match lasts two 30-minute halves, and goals are frequent, with top matches often producing more than fifty goals in total. Attackers work against a six-player defence and a goalkeeper, using jump shots from nine metres, breakthroughs between defenders, and fast breaks after a save or turnover. Handball demands speed, jumping, throwing power, and teamwork, and its rules keep the game flowing with few stoppages.

History

Modern team handball developed in northern Europe in the early twentieth century and became an Olympic sport in 1972 for men and 1976 for women.

Rules

Two teams of seven play on an indoor court, passing and throwing a ball into the opponent's goal, with matches of two 30-minute halves.

Techniques

Players use jump shots, fast breaks, and set plays against a six-player defence, with a goalkeeper guarding a goal three metres wide.

Netball Rank 3 · simple · team sport · beginner

Netball is a team ball sport played mainly by women, though men's and mixed netball are growing, in which two teams of seven try to score by shooting a ball through a high ring with no backboard. The court is divided into thirds, and each position may enter only certain thirds, so a centre, wing attack, goal attack, goal shooter, wing defence, goal defence, and goal keeper each have a defined zone. Players may not dribble or run with the ball; they must pass within three seconds and land with a footwork rule that stops them stepping with the landing foot. Only the goal shooter and goal attack may shoot, and only from inside the goal circle. Netball rewards precise passing, sharp footwork, and positional discipline, and its lack of contact keeps the game fast and skilful.

History

Netball grew from early women's basketball in the 1890s, with its own rules formalised in England, and it is now played widely across the Commonwealth.

Rules

Two teams of seven play on a court divided into thirds, with restricted positions, no dribbling, and shooting only from the goal circle.

Techniques

Passing, footwork, and positional play dominate, since players may hold the ball for three seconds and must stay within their assigned zones.

Korfball Rank 4 · simple · team sport · beginner

Korfball is a mixed-gender team sport from the Netherlands in which two teams of eight, four men and four women each, try to throw a ball through a basket mounted on a pole. The basket has no backboard and stands 3.5 metres high, and players may not run with the ball, dribble, or make physical contact, so scoring depends on passing, movement, and shooting skill. Players are divided into attack and defence zones and swap roles after every two goals scored by their team, and men mark men and women mark women. A match lasts two halves, and the team with the most goals wins. Korfball is unusual in deliberately designing equality into its rules, with mixed teams and matched marking as core principles rather than extras.

History

Korfball was invented in the Netherlands in 1902 by Nico Broekhuysen as a mixed-gender sport, and it remains the best-known mixed team ball sport.

Rules

Two mixed teams of eight, four men and four women, score by throwing a ball through a basket on a pole, with no running with the ball and no physical contact.

Techniques

Players shoot from distance, use passing and movement to create space, and swap attack and defence zones after every two goals.

Dodgeball Rank 5 · simple · team sport · beginner

Dodgeball is a team elimination sport in which two teams throw soft balls at opponents, trying to hit them and eliminate them, while dodging incoming throws and catching balls to eliminate the thrower. In most organised rule sets, a caught ball revives an eliminated teammate, so catching is as valuable as throwing, and matches are played in timed games with the team holding more players at the end declared the winner. Players may block with a ball they hold, and they must stay within their court, with stepping out eliminating them in many versions. Dodgeball is accessible because it needs little equipment and no specialist ground, and it rewards agility, throwing accuracy, and awareness of the whole court.

History

Dodgeball grew from playground games into organised leagues with written rules, national associations, and world championships.

Rules

Two teams throw balls at opponents to eliminate them, while dodging, catching, and blocking; a caught ball eliminates the thrower and revives a teammate in many rule sets.

Techniques

Throwing accuracy, dodging, catching, and court awareness matter most, with teamwork in targeting and ball retrieval.

Ultimate Frisbee Rank 6 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Ultimate, often called Ultimate Frisbee, is a team sport played with a flying disc, in which two teams of seven try to score by catching the disc in the opponent's end zone. The player holding the disc may not run with it and must pass within a set time, while teammates cut and move to get open, much like a passing game in soccer or basketball. Ultimate is unusual in having no referees in most play: players call their own fouls and resolve disputes by discussion, under a principle called Spirit of the Game, which prizes honesty and respect alongside competition. A match is played to a target score, often 15, with a time cap, and the sport is played on grass, beach, and indoors.

History

Ultimate was invented by students in New Jersey in 1968 and grew into a worldwide sport governed by the World Flying Disc Federation.

Rules

Two teams of seven pass a flying disc up the field to score in an end zone, with no running with the disc and self-officiating under the Spirit of the Game.

Techniques

Throwing forehands and backhands, cutting to get open, and zone or man defence shape play, with layout catches for spectacular defensive plays.

Softball Rank 7 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Softball is a bat-and-ball team sport closely related to baseball, played with a larger ball, underarm pitching, and a smaller field. Two teams take turns batting and fielding over seven innings, scoring runs by hitting the ball and running around four bases before being put out. Fastpitch softball, the elite form, features pitchers throwing underarm at high speed with rise balls, drop balls, and change-ups, while slowpitch softball uses a high arcing pitch and is popular recreationally. Fielders wear gloves, catchers wear protective gear, and batters may use slap hitting to place the ball and beat out a throw. Softball is played by men and women, with women's fastpitch its most prominent international form.

History

Softball began in Chicago in 1887 as an indoor version of baseball and became an Olympic sport, with fastpitch the elite form.

Rules

Two teams of nine or ten play seven innings, with underarm pitching from a flat circle, a larger ball, and a smaller field than baseball.

Techniques

Hitting, slap hitting, base running, and pitching with rise, drop, and change-up pitches define the game.

Baseball Rank 8 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Baseball is a bat-and-ball sport in which two teams of nine take turns batting and fielding over nine innings. The pitcher throws overarm from a mound toward home plate, the batter tries to hit the ball into fair territory, and runners advance around four bases to score runs. Three outs end a team's half-inning, and the team with more runs after nine innings wins, with extra innings if tied. Pitchers use fastballs, curveballs, sliders, and change-ups to deceive batters, fielders position themselves for likely hits, and managers make substitutions and tactical calls throughout the game. Baseball's pace, with pauses between pitches, gives it a strategic depth that rewards patience and knowledge of the game's many situations.

History

Baseball developed in the United States in the nineteenth century from earlier bat-and-ball games, with the Knickerbocker rules of 1845 an early codification.

Rules

Two teams of nine play nine innings, with pitchers throwing overarm, batters hitting, and runs scored by circling four bases.

Techniques

Pitching, hitting, fielding, and base running are the core skills, with strategy in pitch selection, defensive shifts, and substitutions.

Field Hockey Rank 9 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Field hockey is a team stick sport played on turf, in which two teams of eleven use curved sticks to hit a hard ball into the opponent's goal. Players may use only the flat side of the stick, may not use their feet or body to control the ball deliberately, and score only from inside the shooting circle. A match is played in four quarters of 15 minutes, and penalty corners, awarded for defensive fouls in the circle, are a major scoring weapon, with drag flicks struck at high speed toward goal. Goalkeepers wear helmets, pads, and kickers and may use their body to stop shots. Field hockey is fast, skilful, and widely played, with strong traditions in Europe, Asia, Australia, and Africa.

History

Modern field hockey was codified in England in the nineteenth century and became an Olympic sport in 1908 for men and 1980 for women.

Rules

Two teams of eleven play on turf with curved sticks, scoring by hitting the ball into the opponent's goal, in four quarters of 15 minutes.

Techniques

Dribbling, passing, tackling with the flat side of the stick, drag flicks at penalty corners, and goalkeeping in full protective gear define play.

Ice Hockey Rank 10 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Ice hockey is a team sport played on ice, in which two teams skate and use sticks to shoot a rubber puck into the opponent's goal. Six players per team are on the ice at once, including a goaltender, with rolling substitutions from the bench so players take short, intense shifts. A match is played in three 20-minute periods, and the team with more goals wins, with overtime and shootouts deciding tied games in many competitions. Body checking is allowed in the men's game, and players wear helmets, pads, and gloves for protection. Ice hockey is one of the fastest team sports, with the puck moving at high speed, play changing direction in seconds, and goaltenders making saves that decide championships.

History

Ice hockey developed in Canada in the nineteenth century from stick games on ice and became a major professional and Olympic sport.

Rules

Two teams of six on the ice, including a goaltender, skate and pass a puck, scoring in the opponent's goal, in three 20-minute periods.

Techniques

Skating, stickhandling, passing, shooting, body checking, and goaltending combine at high speed on a rink.

Water Polo Rank 11 · compounded · team sport · intermediate

Water polo is a team sport played in a pool, in which two teams of seven, including a goalkeeper, try to throw a ball into the opponent's goal. Players swim, tread water using the eggbeater kick, and pass and shoot while opponents wrestle for position, and only the goalkeeper may touch the ball with two hands. A match is played in four quarters of eight minutes, with a shot clock forcing teams to shoot within a set time, and exclusions sending a player out for 20 seconds after a major foul. Water polo demands swimming fitness, upper-body strength, and ball skill, all while staying afloat, which makes it one of the most physically demanding team sports.

History

Water polo developed in Britain in the nineteenth century as a water version of rugby and football, and became an Olympic sport in 1900.

Rules

Two teams of seven in the water, including a goalkeeper, pass and shoot a ball into the opponent's goal, in four quarters of eight minutes.

Techniques

Swimming, treading water, passing, shooting, and wrestling for position combine, with players using an eggbeater kick to stay upright.

Lacrosse Rank 12 · complex · team sport · advanced

Lacrosse is a team stick sport in which players use a stick with a netted pocket to carry, pass, catch, and shoot a ball into the opponent's goal. It descends from stickball games played by Indigenous peoples of North America, and it was codified in Canada in the nineteenth century before spreading worldwide. In the field game, ten players per team are on the field, including a goalkeeper, and the game is played in four quarters with face-offs, fast breaks, and set attacks. Players cradle the ball in the pocket while running, dodge defenders, and shoot at high speed, while defenders check sticks and bodies within the rules. Lacrosse combines the speed of hockey, the passing of basketball, and the strategy of soccer.

History

Lacrosse descends from stickball games played by Indigenous peoples of North America and was codified in Canada in the nineteenth century.

Rules

Two teams use sticks with a netted pocket to carry, pass, and shoot a ball into the opponent's goal, in four quarters.

Techniques

Cradling, passing, dodging, shooting, and checking combine speed, hand-eye coordination, and teamwork.

Rugby League Rank 13 · complex · team sport · advanced

Rugby league is a team sport played with an oval ball, in which two teams of thirteen try to score tries by grounding the ball in the opponent's in-goal area. A team in possession has six tackles to advance up the field, restarting play with a play-the-ball after each tackle, and must hand over possession if it fails to score within six tackles. Tries are worth four points, with goals after tries and penalty goals adding two points each, and drop goals adding one. A match lasts two 40-minute halves, and the game is known for hard running, strong tackling, and fast play-the-balls that keep the contest moving. Rugby league is strongest in England, Australia, New Zealand, and the Pacific Islands.

History

Rugby league split from rugby union in England in 1895 over payments to players, and developed its own rules and professional leagues.

Rules

Two teams of thirteen play two 40-minute halves, with six tackles to advance before handing over possession, and tries worth four points.

Techniques

Running, passing, tackling, kicking, and set plays from scrums and play-the-balls shape the game.

Rugby Union Rank 14 · complex · team sport · advanced

Rugby union is a team sport played with an oval ball, in which two teams of fifteen try to score tries by grounding the ball in the opponent's in-goal area. Tries are worth five points, conversions two, penalty goals three, and drop goals three. Unlike rugby league, union contests possession continuously through rucks, mauls, scrums, and lineouts, so a team can keep the ball for long phases if it wins those contests. A match lasts two 40-minute halves, and positions range from props and hookers in the scrum to wings and fullbacks in open play. Rugby union is played worldwide, with a World Cup every four years as its premier tournament, and it prizes strength, speed, tactical kicking, and teamwork in equal measure.

History

Rugby union was codified in England in 1871, grew into a worldwide amateur and then professional sport, and holds a World Cup every four years.

Rules

Two teams of fifteen play two 40-minute halves, with tries worth five points, scrums, lineouts, rucks, and mauls contesting possession.

Techniques

Running, passing, kicking, tackling, scrummaging, lineout jumping, and tactical kicking combine in a game of continuous contest for the ball.

Sepak Takraw Rank 15 · complex · team sport · advanced

Sepak takraw is a team sport from Southeast Asia in which two teams of three play on a court similar in size to a badminton court, hitting a rattan or synthetic ball over a net. Players may use their feet, head, knees, and chest, but not their hands, and each team may touch the ball up to three times before returning it. The sport's signature move is the bicycle kick spike, in which a player leaps, twists in the air, and strikes the ball over the net with the foot. Matches are played in sets, and the sport is governed internationally with Asian Games and world championship competition. Sepak takraw demands flexibility, jumping, timing, and extraordinary coordination.

History

Sepak takraw descends from kick volleyball games in Southeast Asia and is now an international sport with Asian Games and world championships.

Rules

Two teams of three play on a badminton-sized court over a net, striking a rattan or synthetic ball with feet, head, knees, and chest, but not hands.

Techniques

Acrobatic bicycle kicks, blocks at the net, and precise serves define the sport, which demands flexibility, timing, and aerial skill.

Bandy Rank 16 · complex · team sport · advanced

Bandy is a team winter sport played on ice, in which two teams of eleven skate on a field about the size of a soccer pitch and use curved sticks to hit a small ball into the opponent's goal. It resembles soccer on skates, with offside rules, corner strokes, and free strokes similar to soccer's set pieces, but played at skating speed. A match lasts two 45-minute halves, and goals are frequent because the field is large and the ball moves fast on ice. Bandy is strongest in Russia, Sweden, Finland, and Norway, where it has professional leagues and a world championship. It demands skating endurance, long accurate passing, and the ability to shoot and defend while moving at speed on ice.

History

Bandy developed in England in the nineteenth century as hockey on ice and is now strongest in Russia, Sweden, Finland, and Norway.

Rules

Two teams of eleven skate on a large ice field, using curved sticks to hit a ball into the opponent's goal, in two 45-minute halves.

Techniques

Skating endurance, long passing, shooting, and goalkeeping on a field the size of a soccer pitch define the game.
