The Numerical Simulation Database (Public)

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. A numerical simulation steps through a changing system one small step at a time: pick a step, press Run, and read the result. A simulation steps y from 1.0 to 1.1 in one step of 0.1. A falling ball after 2 seconds moves at 19.6 metres per second. Everything runs in this browser; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced.

  1. What a Numerical Simulation Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. A numerical simulation steps through a changing system one small step at a time. A simulation steps y from 1.0 to 1.1 in one step of 0.1

    Math proof: A simulation steps y from 1.0 to 1.1 in one step of 0.1. Two Euler steps: y goes 1.0 to 1.1 to 1.21. Euler with 100 steps of 0.01 gives 2.7048. A smaller step of 0.05 gives 1.05. After 5 steps of 0.2 the value is 2.49.

    Science proof: A numerical simulation works because a small step is plain arithmetic, and many small steps add up to the whole change: a pendulum of length 1 metre has period 2.01 seconds, and a ball thrown at 10 metres per second at 45 degrees lands 10.2 metres away. A cup at 100 degrees cools one step to 92.0 degrees. That is why the local demo shows the steps and the result together, and runs 0 simulations on a server.
