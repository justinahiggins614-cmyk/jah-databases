Mecca at a Glance Rank 2 · simple

Mecca, officially Makkah al-Mukarramah, is a city in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia and the holiest city in Islam. The 2022 census put the city population at 2,385,509, making it the third largest city in Saudi Arabia, and the city stands about 277 metres above sea level in a narrow valley about 73 kilometres inland from Jeddah. At its centre stands the Grand Mosque, Masjid al-Haram, which encloses the Kaaba, the direction of prayer for Muslims worldwide. Published capacity figures for the mosque vary by what is counted: about 1.5 million worshippers for the main complex in some published summaries, about 1.85 million after the Third Saudi Expansion according to Saudipedia, and up to 3.0 million in a widely reproduced infobox figure that counts the widest complex. This record reports that range rather than picking one figure as settled. Entry to Mecca is restricted to Muslims. That restriction is stated here as a fact, plainly and without further comment.

import math
MECCA_LAT = 21.3891
MECCA_LON = 39.8579
def qibla(lat, lon):
    p1 = math.radians(lat); p2 = math.radians(MECCA_LAT)
    d = math.radians(MECCA_LON - lon)
    y = math.sin(d) * math.cos(p2)
    x = math.cos(p1) * math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.cos(d)
    b = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360.0) % 360.0
    a = math.sin((p2 - p1) / 2.0) ** 2 + math.cos(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.sin(d / 2.0) ** 2
    dist = 6371.0 * 2.0 * math.asin(math.sqrt(a))
    return b, dist

Geography Overview Rank 3 · simple

Mecca sits in a valley in the Sarawat mountain foothills of western Saudi Arabia, inland from the Red Sea coast, in a hot and arid climate with very low rainfall and high summer temperatures. Published city coordinates are about 21.4225 degrees north and 39.8233 degrees east, the elevation is about 277 metres, and the city area is published as 1,200 square kilometres with 760 square kilometres of land. The valley setting shapes the city: steep ground limits flat building land, seasonal rain can run fast through wadis, and the road to Jeddah, about 73 kilometres away, is the main link to the Red Sea coast and its airport. The demo in this record measures great-circle distances and bearings from several cities to the Mecca reference point given in this lane, 21.3891N 39.8579E. That reference point differs slightly from the published city coordinates, because published sources give slightly different points for a whole city, and the demo states its point so its numbers can be reproduced exactly.

History Overview Rank 4 · simple

Mecca began as a trade town in a valley on routes linking the south of the Arabian Peninsula with the Levant, and it grew around a sanctuary and a water source that made a desert stopping place viable. In the early Islamic period of the 7th century CE the city became the centre of a new religious community, and from then on its history is inseparable from pilgrimage, scholarship, and the care of the sanctuary. Later centuries brought Umayyad and Abbasid building works, Ottoman roofing and arcade works in the 1570s, and then the Saudi-era expansions that began in 1955 and continued through the King Fahd works of the 1980s and the King Abdullah works launched in 2011. The full dated sequence lives in the history pair, which this record points to rather than duplicating. Where early origins are reported in tradition rather than in dated documents, the history pair labels them tradition, and this overview does the same.

Grand Mosque Overview Rank 5 · simple

The Grand Mosque, Masjid al-Haram, is the largest mosque in the world and the mosque that encloses the Kaaba at its centre. Its published site area is about 356,000 square metres for the historic core, while the total built-up area after the Third Saudi Expansion is published as 1.47 million square metres including service buildings, courtyards, bridges, and terraces. Capacity figures therefore vary: about 820,000 worshippers after the King Fahd expansion in published older summaries, about 1.5 million in other published summaries, about 1.85 million after the Third Saudi Expansion according to Saudipedia, and up to 3.0 million in the widest infobox count. A reader who compares two figures should ask which area each figure counts. The mosque is open around the clock, and its courtyards, prayer halls, minarets, and the Mataf around the Kaaba are described in full in the Grand Mosque pair. Entry to Mecca is restricted to Muslims, and that restriction applies to the mosque and the city alike.

Kaaba Overview Rank 6 · simple

The Kaaba is a near-cubic stone structure at the centre of the Grand Mosque, draped in a black covering called the Kiswah, with a gilded door set above ground level on one side. Published dimensions are about 12.86 metres long, 11.03 metres wide, and 13.1 metres high, built of stone with marble and limestone, and published coordinates are 21.4225 degrees north and 39.82617 degrees east. Muslims around the world face toward the Kaaba in prayer, and that direction, the qibla, is a fact about religious practice that can be observed and measured from any place on earth. Pilgrims circle the Kaaba seven times counterclockwise in the rite of tawaf, walking in the open Mataf area around it. The building's origin and early rebuilding are reported in Islamic tradition, and the Kaaba pair keeps those tradition accounts separate from the measured facts given here. The interior is a small room opened only rarely, and this record does not describe its interior beyond what published summaries state in general terms.

Pilgrimage Overview Rank 7 · compounded

Pilgrimage to Mecca takes two main forms: Hajj, the annual pilgrimage performed in a set season, and Umrah, the lesser pilgrimage that can be performed at other times of year. Both include tawaf, circling the Kaaba seven times, and sa'i, walking between the hills of Safa and Marwa, and Hajj adds rites at Mina, Arafat, and Muzdalifah outside the city. Published figures show the scale of the operation: after the Third Saudi Expansion, the hourly capacity for Umrah pilgrims is published as rising from about 44,000 to about 118,000 people per hour, and the Masa'a gallery between Safa and Marwa is published as 57,000 square metres holding about 70,000 people. Pilgrims arrive mainly through Jeddah, and the whole movement is managed as a crowd-science problem, which this suite treats in its own record. Entry to Mecca is restricted to Muslims, and this record states that fact plainly. The festival-hajj and holiday-hajj-pilgrimage records are cross-linked here, not rebuilt.

Mecca Today Overview Rank 9 · compounded

Modern Mecca is a large city of about 2.39 million residents by the 2022 census, with a metropolitan area of about 2.43 million, and it functions as both a resident city and a pilgrimage city at the same time. Its modern infrastructure includes the Grand Mosque expansions, the road and rail links toward Jeddah, hotels and services for pilgrims, hospitals, universities, and municipal services for a population that swells sharply in pilgrimage season. The tension between the two roles is the city's defining modern fact: streets, water, transport, and housing must serve everyday residents and, at peak times, millions of visitors as well. The clock tower complex beside the mosque is a visible marker of the modern skyline, and the city's time zone is UTC+03:00. Entry to Mecca is restricted to Muslims, and that restriction shapes who can live in, work in, and visit the city. Water and Environment Overview Rank 10 · compounded

Water has always been the limiting fact of Mecca. A valley city in an arid climate cannot grow without a reliable supply, and Mecca's history is in large part the history of wells, cisterns, aqueducts, and, in modern times, pipelines and desalination bringing water from the Red Sea coast. The Zamzam well inside the Grand Mosque complex is the most famous source, and its origin is reported in tradition, while its modern management as a pumped and distributed supply is a documented fact. Modern Mecca also faces the environmental facts of heat, dust, flash-flood risk in wadis after rare heavy rain, and the waste and water load of pilgrimage peaks. This record points to the history pair's water history record for the full sequence from early wells to modern supply, and to a later Zamzam companion pair in the suite for the well itself, rather than duplicating either here. What is not in the public record about early water volumes is labeled as not in the public record, not estimated silently.

Crowd Science Overview Rank 11 · compounded

Crowd science is the discipline that lets millions of people move through one mosque complex without crushing, and Mecca is one of its most studied cases. The published engineering answers are visible in the building itself: one-way flows, separated levels, wide ramps and escalators, the Mataf's open circuit around the Kaaba, the multi-level Masa'a gallery between Safa and Marwa, and the Jamarat bridge outside the city for the stoning rite. Published throughput figures show the scale of the design problem: Umrah hourly capacity is published as rising from about 44,000 to about 118,000 people per hour after the Third Saudi Expansion. The demo in this record is a simple throughput model, lanes multiplied by hourly rate multiplied by hours, and it is labeled as a model, because a model is not an observed count. Real crowd management also uses scheduling, grouping, signage, and stewarding, and the Grand Mosque pair's crowd-management record gives the operational detail.

Botany Overview Rank 12 · complex

The plants of Mecca are the plants of a hot, arid valley: drought-tolerant shrubs and trees, acacia-type thorn trees on wadi banks, palms where water is available, and seasonal herbs that appear after rain and vanish in the dry months. The valley's water points, gardens, and irrigated plots support more green cover than the surrounding rocky hills, and the contrast between irrigated ground and dry hillside is the basic pattern a visitor sees. This record does not present a full species census, because no such census is cited in the sources used here, and that gap is labeled as not in the public record rather than filled with plausible names. Cultivated plants in the modern city include date palms and ornamental plantings maintained with irrigation, and their presence is a fact about modern urban planting, not a claim about ancient flora. Readers who want a planting list for a garden should treat this record as a starting pattern, not as a survey.

Architecture Overview Rank 13 · complex

Mecca's architecture is dominated by the Grand Mosque, and the mosque's architecture is a layering of centuries: an open courtyard around the Kaaba, Ottoman arcades and domes from the 1570s works, the Saudi expansions that wrapped new construction around the older mosque from 1955 onward, and the modern towers and service buildings that now surround the complex. The Kaaba itself is the contrasting element, a small near-cube of stone against a vast roofed complex, and its published dimensions of about 12.86 by 11.03 by 13.1 metres make the contrast measurable. Beyond the mosque, the city mixes older valley neighbourhoods with modern high-rise hotels and housing built for pilgrimage peaks, and the clock tower complex is the most visible modern landmark. Materials in the mosque include stone, marble, and modern concrete and steel, and the Grand Mosque pair gives the material and engineering detail. This record contains no architectural drawings of any person and no image of any kind, and its descriptions cover buildings and ground only.

import math
MECCA_LAT = 21.3891
MECCA_LON = 39.8579
def qibla(lat, lon):
    p1 = math.radians(lat); p2 = math.radians(MECCA_LAT)
    d = math.radians(MECCA_LON - lon)
    y = math.sin(d) * math.cos(p2)
    x = math.cos(p1) * math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.cos(d)
    b = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360.0) % 360.0
    a = math.sin((p2 - p1) / 2.0) ** 2 + math.cos(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.sin(d / 2.0) ** 2
    dist = 6371.0 * 2.0 * math.asin(math.sqrt(a))
    return b, dist
CITIES = [('Riyadh', 24.7136, 46.6753), ('Medina', 24.5247, 39.5692), ('Dubai', 25.2048, 55.2708), ('Istanbul', 41.0082, 28.9784)]
print('Mecca reference point: %.4fN, %.4fE' % (MECCA_LAT, MECCA_LON))
for name, lat, lon in CITIES:
    b, d = qibla(lat, lon)
    print('%s: qibla bearing %.2f degrees, distance %.1f km' % (name, b, d))

Honesty Labeling Guide Rank 15 · complex

Every record in this suite carries one of three labels, and this guide defines them so that a reader never has to guess which kind of statement they are reading. Fact means a statement that can be checked against a published measurement, a census figure, a dated building work, or another public record, and the record names its source. Tradition means a statement reported as part of Islamic tradition, presented as tradition, not as a measured fact, and where traditions differ in detail, the record says that traditions differ rather than settling the difference. Interpretation means an original reading, design study, or model built for this database, labeled as such, never presented as an authentic public record. A single record can contain all three kinds of sentence, and when it does, each sentence's kind is stated or is clear from its source line. Gaps are labeled as not in the public record, never filled by speculation, and no record in this suite invents doctrine, invents a quotation, or presents a generated design as a public fact. Entry restrictions are stated as facts, plainly, with no further comment.
