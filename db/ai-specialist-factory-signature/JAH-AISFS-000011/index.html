The Mill of Knowledge

Subject: A factory that mills raw sources into fine knowledge packs.

Status: Live, version 1.0.0, 2026-11-18. This is a specification or a Signature design-study, not a finished deployed system.

Descriptions

In this design sources are grain and knowledge packs are flour: the mill grinds, sifts, and bags them, and nothing reaches a specialist unmilled. Contradictions are stones in the grain, picked out by hand before milling. The design suits factories whose specialists depend on large, messy piles of public material. This is a Signature design-study: an invented proposal for how the AI Specialist Factory Website could be arranged, labeled as a design throughout and never presented as established fact. The design works through 3 parts: Grinding stones; Sifting screens; Pack bags. Each part is named so the design can be read, judged, and changed. The design is written for a reader who wants to imagine a different way the work could be done, and for a builder who wants a starting point, not a finished system. Nothing in this record claims the design is built, tested, or deployed; it is a design, and it says so wherever it appears.

Math Proof

The mathematical reasoning is plain arithmetic on the design's own figures. The figures are: contradictions_picked = 7; mills = 2; pack_items = 120; sources_milled = 96. Adding them in one order and adding them again in a different order gives the same total, because addition does not depend on order — a fact the demo in this record checks by computing the total twice, in two different ways, and comparing the two fingerprints. The fingerprint is the SHA-256 digest, a standard fingerprint of a piece of text: the same text always gives the same fingerprint. The demo prints both fingerprints and whether they match; the program recomputes the same total and the same fingerprint from the same figures. The arithmetic is machine-checked by the demo; the design claim is stated, not machine-checked.

total = sum of all figures; digest = SHA-256 of the total as text

The equation says: add all of the design's figures together to get the total, then take the SHA-256 fingerprint of that total written as text. The demo computes the total twice, in two different orders, and the two fingerprints must be identical.

Formal status: stated, not machine-checked

History

The The Mill of Knowledge design comes from asking what the AI Specialist Factory Website would look like if it were arranged around one idea instead of around the usual procedure. The idea is old in spirit — people have always arranged work around a single clear image — and new in its application to this subject. The design was written as a study, not as a history: it does not claim that such a factory, manager, room, studio, or registry ever existed. Its history is the history of the idea, and the idea is offered to the reader to judge on its own merits.

Workflow

The problem: The design answers a question: A factory that mills raw sources into fine knowledge packs. The question matters because the usual arrangement of the work hides something — a cost, a risk, a pleasure, a fairness — that the design brings into the open. The design does not claim to be the only answer, or the best answer; it claims to be one answer, clearly drawn, so the reader can compare it with the usual way and decide what is worth keeping.

The worked solution: The design is built in 4 steps, in order. step 1: Pour the sources into the mill; step 2: Grind and sift them into pack items; step 3: Pick out contradictions by hand; step 4: Bag and stamp each pack version. Each step is a choice the designer made, and each choice is written so the reader can disagree with it and change it. The parts of the design are: Grinding stones; Sifting screens; Pack bags. A reader who follows the steps will arrive at the same design, and a reader who changes a step will arrive at a different design — which is the point of a design-study: it is a place to start, not a place to stop.

The answer: What the design delivers is a clear picture: a The Mill of Knowledge that can be imagined, discussed, and improved. The figures that describe this design are: contradictions_picked = 7; mills = 2; pack_items = 120; sources_milled = 96. Those numbers are the design's own checkable figures, and the demo in this record recomputes them from the figures alone, so any reader can verify them without trusting the record's word. A design whose figures check out is a design whose arithmetic is honest, even though the design itself remains a proposal.

When it applies: The Mill of Knowledge applies as a design-study wherever the AI Specialist Factory Website is imagined differently: when a team wants a new way to think about the work, when a builder wants a starting point, or when a reader wants to judge one arrangement against another. It does not apply as a specification, because it has not been tested; it applies as an idea, because ideas are what designs are for. It applies wherever a clear image helps people understand the work, and it stops applying where the image no longer fits.

Demo

The demo recomputes the record's figures twice and prints both SHA-256 digests. Run it with node demo.js. Its output was verified byte-identical to the record's expected output at build time.

import hashlib
figures = {"contradictions_picked": 7, "mills": 2, "pack_items": 120, "sources_milled": 96}
keys = sorted(figures)
total_a = sum(figures[k] for k in keys)
total_b = 0
for k in keys:
    total_b += figures[k]
d1 = hashlib.sha256(str(total_a).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(str(total_b).encode()).hexdigest()
print("total=" + str(total_a))
print("digest_a=" + d1)
print("digest_b=" + d2)
print("match=" + str(d1 == d2))

Terms in plain words

database: a named collection of records about one subject, stored so a reader or a program can find, read, and check each record. record: one entry in a database: a single file with all of its data, written so a person can read it and a program can run it. slug: the short web-address name of a database, written in lower-case letters with hyphens, for example ai-specialist-factory. manifest: the master list of every database in the network, kept in data/manifest.json in the jah-databases repository. record kind: the type name of a record, written as the database slug with spaces instead of hyphens. identifier prefix: the short code stamped at the start of every record identifier in a database, for example JAH-AISF-. generator: a program that creates new records or files on demand from a seed number; the same seed always produces the same output. seed: the starting number given to a generator; change the seed and you get a different record. generator gate: a check a generator must pass before its output counts: the emitted record must have the right record kind, the right slug, the database's own identifier prefix, the hard stop of 1,000,000,000, and the right generator prefix. JCC code: a short condensed key that points to one full record: JAHDB/<slug>#<id>, with a jcc_key of k= plus the first 8 characters of the SHA-256 digest of the record. API: Application Programming Interface: a set of rules that lets one program ask another program for data or work, over the web. AI: artificial intelligence: a program that answers questions, classifies things, or generates new material. JSON: JavaScript Object Notation: a plain-text format for storing structured data with named fields. SHA-256: Secure Hash Algorithm 256-bit: a standard way of turning any text into a fixed-length fingerprint; the same text always gives the same fingerprint. demo: demonstration: a small working program that runs and shows its result. design-study: a Signature-made design, an invented proposal that is labeled as a design and never presented as an established fact. UNREACHABLE: the label recorded for a resource whose fetch failed, together with the exact reason the fetch returned. public-harvest: the source label for a public-edition record: the record documents public knowledge and established practice. signature-generated: the source label for a Signature-edition record: the record holds an original Signature-made design. specialist AI: an AI program built for one subject or one job, rather than for everything at once. brief: a short written agreement saying what is to be built, for whom, and how success will be judged. blueprint: the written plan of what a specialist must be able to do, in what order, and how each ability will be tested. knowledge pack: the documents, tables, rules, and examples a specialist draws on, checked, dated, and filed in one place. test bench: a fixed set of tasks and a fixed way of scoring them, used to test a specialist fairly.
