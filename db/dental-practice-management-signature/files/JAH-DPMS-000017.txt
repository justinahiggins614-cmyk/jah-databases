A payment record is the written account of money received by a dental practice, naming the patient, the amount, and the date, so that what was paid can be counted against what was charged. The record is written on the day the money arrives, because a payment written down days later may be given the wrong date and the ledger would then be out of step. History
Dentists have kept records since the craft began, first in ledgers of teeth drawn and plates fitted, then in card indexes of patients and their treatments, and now in systems that hold the appointment book, the chart, the billing ledger, and the sterilization log side by side. A dental practice is a small world of appointments kept, treatments planned, materials ordered, and payments counted, and the management of it is the keeping of those records true. From the paper card filed by surname to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the practice down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every dental practice record true.
