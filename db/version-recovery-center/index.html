1. Descriptions

The Version and Recovery Center Database: version control, snapshots, backups, and rollback for the whole JAH Network — recorded as public practice and public standards.

Version and Recovery Center: Overview and Mission

JAH-VRC-000001 · Version and Recovery Center overview

This record is the Version and Recovery Center overview entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. The Version and Recovery Center is the network's memory and safety net: it keeps every version of every record and file, and it can bring back what was lost or broken. The center holds version records, snapshots, backups, and recovery reports as records; it is not itself a live backup service. Two promises sit at the front of the center: nothing is ever silently overwritten, and every recovery is rehearsed before it is needed.

Version and Recovery Center: Version Control

JAH-VRC-000002 · Keeping every change with its author and reason

This record covers Keeping every change with its author and reason in the Version and Recovery Center Database. Version control records every change as a version: what changed, who changed it, when, and why, so any earlier state can be found again. A version is a saved snapshot of a file at one moment; the history of versions is the story of how the file grew. The center records versions for databases and files alike, and never rewrites history to hide a mistake.

Version and Recovery Center: Snapshots and Checkpoints

JAH-VRC-000003 · Frozen moments you can return to

This record is the Frozen moments you can return to entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A snapshot is a frozen copy of a whole database or file at one moment, taken so the work can return to exactly that moment later. A checkpoint is a snapshot taken at a known-good moment, for example right after a verified merge, so recovery starts from solid ground. Snapshots are labelled with the date, the contents, and the reason they were taken, and the label is part of the record.

Version and Recovery Center: Backups, Full, Incremental, Differential

JAH-VRC-000004 · Copies that survive the original being lost

This record covers Copies that survive the original being lost in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A full backup copies everything; an incremental backup copies only what changed since the last backup; a differential backup copies what changed since the last full backup. The center records which kind each backup is, when it was taken, and what it covers, so a restore can be planned instead of guessed. A backup that has never been tested by restoring from it is treated as unproven, not as protection.

Version and Recovery Center: Restore and Rollback

JAH-VRC-000005 · Bringing back the earlier version

This record is the Bringing back the earlier version entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A restore brings back a saved copy; a rollback moves a live database back to an earlier version after a bad change. Every restore in the center is logged with what was restored, from which backup, by whom, and whether the result was verified afterwards. Rollback is a last resort with a written reason, because it also undoes the good changes made after the bad one.

Version and Recovery Center: Disaster Recovery Planning

JAH-VRC-000006 · A written plan for the worst day

This record covers A written plan for the worst day in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A disaster recovery plan is a written plan for the worst day: what is lost, what is restored first, who decides, and how long each step takes. The plan names the order of recovery, because restoring the least important thing first wastes the time the important things need. The plan is rehearsed, timed, and rewritten after every rehearsal, because an unrehearsed plan is a guess.

Version and Recovery Center: Change History and Audit Trail

JAH-VRC-000007 · Who did what, when, in order

This record is the Who did what, when, in order entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. An audit trail is the ordered list of every change, read, and administrative action, kept so that any event can be traced back. The trail records the actor, the action, the target, and the time, and it cannot be edited after the fact without leaving a mark. Audit trails in this center cover changes, executions, failures, and administrative actions, as the review asked for.

Version and Recovery Center: Branching and Merging

JAH-VRC-000008 · Trying a change without breaking the main line

This record covers Trying a change without breaking the main line in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A branch is a separate line of work that leaves the main line untouched, so a change can be tried, tested, and only then merged back. Merging brings a branch back into the main line; a merge conflict is when both lines changed the same part, and a human decides the outcome. The center records every branch, its purpose, and its merge result, so no experiment is lost and no merge is a mystery.

Version and Recovery Center: Release Tagging

JAH-VRC-000009 · Naming the version that went out

This record is the Naming the version that went out entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A release tag is a name pinned to one exact version, so everyone means the same thing when they say a version number. Tags are written once and never moved, because a moved tag rewrites what a version number means. Every tagged release in the center lists its contents and its verification result.

Version and Recovery Center: Data Integrity Checks

JAH-VRC-000010 · Proving the copy matches the original

This record covers Proving the copy matches the original in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A data integrity check compares a stored copy against a recorded digest, a short fingerprint of the original, to prove nothing changed. A SHA-256 digest (Secure Hash Algorithm, 256-bit) is the fingerprint used here: change one letter and the fingerprint changes completely. The center re-checks digests on a schedule and reports any mismatch the same day it is found.

Version and Recovery Center: Offsite and Redundant Copies

JAH-VRC-000011 · Copies kept where one accident cannot reach them

This record is the Copies kept where one accident cannot reach them entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A redundant copy is a second copy kept apart from the first, so one accident, one fire, or one failed disk cannot take both. Offsite means kept in a different place from the original; the center records where each copy lives and how far apart the copies are. Redundancy is counted, not assumed: the record says how many copies exist and where.

Version and Recovery Center: Recovery Time and Recovery Point Objectives

JAH-VRC-000012 · How fast, and how much may be lost

This record covers How fast, and how much may be lost in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A recovery time objective is the longest acceptable time to bring a database back after a failure; a recovery point objective is the most recent data that may acceptably be lost. Both objectives are written as numbers with reasons, not as wishes, because a number can be tested and a wish cannot. The center tests whether each database can meet its objectives, and records the test result.

Version and Recovery Center: Corruption Detection and Repair

JAH-VRC-000013 · Finding damage and fixing it from a good copy

This record is the Finding damage and fixing it from a good copy entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. Corruption is damage to stored data that makes it wrong or unreadable, and detection means finding it before anyone trusts the damaged copy. The center detects corruption with digests and with format checks, then repairs by replacing the damaged part from a verified good copy. A repair is never silent: the record says what was damaged, what replaced it, and how the replacement was verified.

Version and Recovery Center: Archive and Retention Policy

JAH-VRC-000014 · How long each version is kept, and why

This record covers How long each version is kept, and why in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A retention policy is the written rule for how long each kind of version, snapshot, and backup is kept, and when it may be removed. The center keeps the policy public and follows it exactly, because a policy followed sometimes is not a policy. Removal under the policy is recorded with the rule that allowed it, so nothing disappears without a trace.

Version and Recovery Center: Version Diffs

JAH-VRC-000015 · Exactly what changed between two versions

This record is the Exactly what changed between two versions entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A diff is the exact list of differences between two versions, line by line, so a reviewer can see the change without re-reading the whole file. The center stores diffs alongside versions, because a version without its diff is a locked box. Large diffs are summarized by section, but the full diff is always kept and reachable.

Version and Recovery Center: Rollback Testing, Rehearsed Restores

JAH-VRC-000016 · Practising the recovery before the emergency

This record covers Practising the recovery before the emergency in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A rehearsed restore is a restore done as practice, on a copy, to prove the backup works and the team knows the steps. The rehearsal is timed and its result recorded, including every step that was slow, confusing, or missing from the plan. A backup whose restore has never been rehearsed is labelled unproven in this center, however new it is.

Version and Recovery Center: Backup Encryption and Access Control

JAH-VRC-000017 · Copies that stay secret and stay in the right hands

This record is the Copies that stay secret and stay in the right hands entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. Backup encryption scrambles a backup so it can only be read with the right key, and access control decides who holds that key. The center records which backups are encrypted, which key protects them, and who may restore them, without ever writing a key into a public file. An unencrypted backup holding private data is recorded as a defect, not as a finished job.

Version and Recovery Center: Migration Between Versions

JAH-VRC-000018 · Moving data safely from the old shape to the new

This record covers Moving data safely from the old shape to the new in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A migration moves data from an old version's shape to a new version's shape, changing the format without losing the meaning. The center migrates on a copy first, checks the copy against the original record by record, and only then replaces the original. Every migration records its mapping: which old field became which new field, and what was done with fields that no longer exist.

Version and Recovery Center: Deleted-Record Recovery

JAH-VRC-000019 · Bringing back what was removed by mistake

This record is the Bringing back what was removed by mistake entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. Deleted-record recovery brings back a record that was removed by mistake, from a version, snapshot, or backup taken before the deletion. The center treats deletion as reversible until the retention policy says otherwise, and records every recovery with its source version. A deletion that cannot be recovered is recorded as a loss, with the reason, not hidden.

Version and Recovery Center: Multi-Generation Retention

JAH-VRC-000020 · Keeping several generations, not just the last one

This record covers Keeping several generations, not just the last one in the Version and Recovery Center Database. Multi-generation retention keeps several past generations of a file, not only the latest, so a slow-spreading error can be traced back past the last good copy. The center keeps at least three generations for important files, and records how many generations each file has. When a generation is dropped, the record says which one and under which rule.

Version and Recovery Center: Recovery Drills

JAH-VRC-000021 · Timed practice for the whole team

This record is the Timed practice for the whole team entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A recovery drill is a timed practice run of the whole recovery plan, with the team, the tools, and the backups all used for real. The drill measures time to first restored record, time to full service, and every step where someone had to guess. Drill results are filed as records, and the plan is changed where the drill found a gap.

Version and Recovery Center: Version Provenance

JAH-VRC-000022 · Where each version came from

This record covers Where each version came from in the Version and Recovery Center Database. Version provenance is the recorded origin of each version: who made it, from which source, on which date, and under which order. Provenance lets a reader trust a version or question it, because the chain from source to record is visible. A version without provenance is labelled unknown-origin in this center, and unknown-origin versions are never presented as verified.

Version and Recovery Center: Snapshot Scheduling

JAH-VRC-000023 · When snapshots are taken, decided in advance

This record is the When snapshots are taken, decided in advance entry of the Version and Recovery Center Database. A snapshot schedule is the written timetable for taking snapshots: how often, at what times, and after which events. The center schedules snapshots after every verified merge and on a regular clock, so a good moment is never missed by forgetting. Missed snapshots are logged as misses, with the reason, so the schedule can be fixed instead of quietly slipping.

Version and Recovery Center: Recovery Evidence Reports

JAH-VRC-000024 · Proof that the recovery worked

This record covers Proof that the recovery worked in the Version and Recovery Center Database. A recovery evidence report is the proof that a restore or rollback worked: what was restored, what was checked, and what the checks found. The report includes the digests before and after, the count of records restored, and any record that could not be restored. No recovery is called finished until its evidence report is filed.

4. History

The need for this center grew as the JAH Network grew: with 63,047 databases live, the work could no longer be checked, versioned, resourced, linked, or watched by hand. The system was proposed in another AI's review pasted 2026-11-18 (source unconfirmed, NOT Manon's words) and is built here as a database pair: public practice and Signature design studies.
