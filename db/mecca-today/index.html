Modern City Overview Rank 1 · simple

Mecca, also written Makkah and officially Makkah al-Mukarramah, is the capital of Makkah Province in western Saudi Arabia, about 70 kilometres inland from the Red Sea in the Hejaz region. It sits in a valley among mountains at roughly 277 metres above sea level, and its urban area is published at about 760 square kilometres for the city proper. In Islamic tradition Mecca is the holiest city of Islam, and the Grand Mosque at its centre is the focus of pilgrimage; that religious significance is a matter of tradition and belief, while the geography, administration and infrastructure in this record are public facts. Entry to Mecca is restricted to Muslims, as a matter of published Saudi regulation. The city combines an old core of dense neighbourhoods and markets with wide modern roads, high-rise hotel districts near the centre, and large pilgrimage infrastructure built for seasonal peaks far above the resident population. The demo below estimates resident density from a published population estimate and the published city area, so a reader can see why a city of this size needs transport planned for peaks, not only for residents.

Population: Published Estimates and As-Of Dates Rank 2 · simple

Published estimates for the resident population of Mecca vary with the boundary and the year, so this record states a range rather than a single false-precise figure. Older published city listings give about 1.68 million residents, while 2024 estimates cited in city guides give about 2.2 to 2.4 million, and some 2026 listings give up to about 2.8 million; the wider Makkah Region is far larger, at about 7.77 million in published regional tables. The honest reading is therefore a city-proper range of roughly 1.7 to 2.8 million depending on source and date, with the most recent city estimates clustering around 2.2 to 2.4 million as of 2024. Pilgrimage seasons add millions of temporary visitors on top of residents, so any single headcount that mixes residents and visitors is misleading, and this record keeps the two counts apart. Districts and Neighbourhoods Rank 3 · simple

Mecca's districts grew outward from the central mosque area along valley roads, and the modern city is organised into neighbourhoods that serve very different daily lives. Central districts near the Grand Mosque are dominated by hotels, pilgrim services and shops, while outer districts such as Al-Aziziyah, Al-Rusayfah (home of the Haramain station), Al-Shawqiyah, Al-Nuzha and the districts toward Jeddah and Taif are largely residential, with schools, clinics, markets and mosques of their own. Older quarters keep narrow streets and low-rise buildings, while newer districts are laid out for cars, with wider roads and larger plots. Because the city sits in a valley, districts also differ by slope and flood exposure, a fact that matters for drainage planning and is treated in the environment pair. District names and boundaries change as the city grows, so this record names well-known districts without claiming a fixed official ward list, which is not in the public record consulted. Haramain High Speed Railway Rank 4 · simple

The Haramain High Speed Railway is an electrified passenger railway linking Mecca and Medina via Jeddah and King Abdullah Economic City, operated by Saudi Arabia Railways. Its main line is published at 449.2 kilometres, with a 3.75-kilometre branch to King Abdulaziz International Airport, and it opened to the public on 11 October 2018 after inauguration on 25 September 2018. Trains run at up to 300 kilometres per hour, and the five stations are Mecca, Jeddah Al-Sulaymaniyah, King Abdulaziz International Airport, King Abdullah Economic City, and Medina. Each train carries 417 seats across economy and business classes, and published ridership was 6.97 million passengers in 2023. For Mecca the railway matters because it moves pilgrims and residents between the airport, Jeddah and Medina without adding cars to the Mecca-Jeddah road, and its Mecca station sits in Al-Rusayfah, a few kilometres from the Grand Mosque. King Abdulaziz International Airport (Jeddah) Link Rank 5 · simple

King Abdulaziz International Airport in Jeddah is the main air gateway for most international visitors to Mecca, and the link between airport and city is therefore part of Mecca's transport system even though the airport itself sits in Jeddah. The Haramain railway has a station integrated into the airport terminal, with published Mecca-to-airport journey times of roughly 35 to 50 minutes depending on stops, and road travel runs along the Mecca-Jeddah expressway. Pilgrims also arrive by road coaches and licensed transport, and Hajj and Umrah seasons produce sharp arrival peaks that the airport, railway and road network must absorb together. This record gives no booking, visa or entry advice beyond the factual link described here, and the entry restriction stated in the overview record applies to Mecca itself. Timetables, fares and terminal arrangements change, so a traveller must check the operator's current timetable rather than treating this record as a live schedule. Roads and Buses Rank 6 · simple

Mecca's road network is built around expressways to Jeddah, Medina and Taif, ring roads around the centre, and valley roads that funnel traffic toward the Grand Mosque area, where private cars are restricted at peak times and much movement is by bus, taxi and on foot. Buses are the workhorse of ordinary city movement and of pilgrim movement between accommodation, the centre and the holy sites, with shuttle systems expanded sharply during Hajj. The road system must cope with two very different loads: ordinary resident traffic all year, and pilgrimage peaks in which the number of people in the city multiplies. Road works, diversions and seasonal traffic management therefore change frequently, and this record describes the structure of the network rather than a live traffic state, which is not in the public record consulted. Safety and capacity planning for the largest movements is treated as crowd science in the companion pair. Hotels and Hospitality (General, Public Fact) Rank 7 · compounded

Hospitality in Mecca is organised around pilgrimage: thousands of hotels, furnished apartments and hostels cluster near the Grand Mosque, with larger hotel towers in the central area and more ordinary accommodation in residential districts. Published project reporting describes tens of thousands of hotel rooms in the city, with large new developments such as the Masar project reported as adding more than 24,000 rooms in its plans, though planned rooms are not the same as rooms open today, and this record keeps plans and openings apart. Room prices and availability swing sharply with season, rising at Hajj and Ramadan and falling in quiet months, and many visitors book through group pilgrimage arrangements rather than as independent travellers. Hospitality employment is a major part of the city's economy, covering housekeeping, catering, transport and guiding services. This record describes the sector in general public terms and gives no booking advice, prices or endorsements. Hospitals and Services Rank 8 · compounded

Mecca's health services combine general hospitals, specialist centres, primary-care clinics and seasonal pilgrimage health facilities that expand during Hajj. Major public hospitals serve residents all year, while Hajj seasons add temporary clinics, field facilities and emergency capacity near the holy sites, staffed for heat illness, crowd-related injuries and the ordinary medical needs of a very large, often elderly, visiting population. Published health planning for Hajj emphasises heat, dehydration, respiratory illness and chronic-disease care, and pilgrims are advised by health authorities to carry medication and proof of required vaccinations, though this record gives no personal medical advice. Pharmacies, ambulance services and civil-defence coordination sit alongside hospitals as part of the same system. Exact bed counts change as facilities open and close, so this record describes the structure and the seasonal surge rather than a fixed bed total, which is not in the public record consulted. Schools and Universities Rank 9 · compounded

Mecca's education system includes state schools for boys and girls, private and international schools, Qur'an memorisation schools, technical institutes, and Umm Al-Qura University, the city's major university, founded in its modern form in 1981 from earlier colleges, with faculties spanning Islamic studies, medicine, engineering, science and education. Schools follow the Saudi national curriculum, and universities in the city also serve students from across the Muslim world, particularly in Islamic and Arabic studies. As in other Saudi cities, schooling is organised separately for boys and girls in state provision, and higher education enrolment has grown with the city's population. School places, like hospital beds, must be planned against a resident population that is itself uncertain within the range given in the population record, and against seasonal visitor numbers that do not use local schools. Markets and Commerce Rank 10 · compounded

Commerce in Mecca is centred on pilgrimage trade and on ordinary resident shopping, and the two overlap in the same streets. Central souqs and shopping streets sell prayer goods, textiles, dates, perfumes, gold and souvenirs alongside everyday goods, while modern malls and supermarkets serve residential districts, and wholesale markets supply hotels and caterers. Pilgrim spending supports a large service economy of shops, restaurants, transport and accommodation, and seasonal peaks in trade mirror seasonal peaks in visitors. The city's markets are also social places where residents from many countries meet, reflecting a resident population drawn from across the Muslim world. City Administration (General, Factual) Rank 12 · complex

Mecca is the capital of Makkah Province and is administered by the Makkah Municipality (Amanat Al-Asimah Al-Muqaddasah) for city services, with provincial government above it and national ministries responsible for pilgrimage affairs, transport, health and security. Municipal services cover roads, drainage, waste collection, parks, building permits and markets, while pilgrimage operations are coordinated across many agencies, including transport operators, health authorities and civil defence, because no single department can run a gathering of millions alone. The Royal Commission for Makkah City and Holy Sites was established to oversee development of the city and holy sites, and large projects such as Masar are delivered through dedicated development vehicles. This record is deliberately general: it describes the structure of administration without attributing specific decisions, budgets or internal deliberations, none of which are in the public record consulted, and it contains no political commentary. Mecca Metro (Al Mashaaer Al Mugaddasah Metro, Factual) Rank 14 · complex

The Al Mashaaer Al Mugaddasah Metro is an electric shuttle railway serving the holy sites of Mina, Muzdalifah and Arafat during Hajj, operated by Saudi Arabia Railways. It entered service in November 2010, runs about 18 kilometres with nine stations (three in each of Arafat, Muzdalifah and Mina, plus the Jamarat station in published counts), and is reported at a capacity of about 72,000 passengers per hour, with 17 trains each carrying up to 3,000 passengers at up to 80 kilometres per hour, covering Arafat to Mina in about 20 minutes. Published reporting for Hajj 2025 put its ridership at about 1.87 million passengers over 2,154 trips, and preparations for Hajj 1447 AH were reported as targeting more than two million passengers. The metro runs only during the Hajj season, not as an all-year city metro, and its purpose is to replace tens of thousands of bus trips inside the holy sites. A separate all-year Mecca metro has been proposed in published reporting but is not the same system, and this record does not conflate the two. Utilities and Services Rank 15 · complex

Mecca's utilities must serve a resident city and, at peak, a pilgrimage city several times larger, which makes water, electricity, waste and drainage planning a seasonal problem, not only a resident one. Water supply, treated in the environment pair, combines desalination from the Red Sea coast, groundwater and storage, while electricity supply follows the Saudi national grid with local distribution, and waste collection scales up sharply during Hajj. Drainage matters in a valley city where rare heavy rain can produce flash flooding, and published flood-management works include dams, channels and drainage upgrades. Telecommunications and emergency communications are coordinated across pilgrimage agencies during Hajj. Living in Mecca and Sources Index Rank 16 · complex

Living in Mecca means living in a city whose daily rhythm is shaped by prayer times, by pilgrimage seasons, and by a resident community drawn from many countries, all of whom are Muslim residents under the entry rule stated in the overview record. Ordinary life centres on neighbourhood mosques, schools, markets, clinics and family networks, while work is concentrated in pilgrimage services, hospitality, trade, government and education. Housing ranges from older low-rise quarters near the centre to newer apartment districts further out, and commuting follows the valley road network and bus services described in the transport records. The cost of living, housing availability and job markets change with development projects and pilgrimage demand, so this record gives no prices, rents or employment advice, and no personal recommendation. It describes resident life in general factual terms, as a complement to the visitor-focused pilgrimage records elsewhere in the suite.
