Jasper Stone Data Rank 1 · feasibility: demonstrated

Text-faithful view (faith interpretation): Revelation 21:18 describes the wall as built of jasper, and Revelation 21:11 compares the city's radiance to a jasper stone, clear as crystal. The passage gives the material name, not a laboratory specification.

Engineering view: Jasper in modern gemology is an opaque, cryptocrystalline variety of quartz, typically red, brown, yellow, or green from iron-oxide inclusions. Its hardness is 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale and its specific gravity is about 2.58 to 2.91, so it is a workable ornamental stone for cladding and carving, though a fully transparent crystal-clear jasper is not an established material.

Jasper is a microcrystalline quartz aggregate coloured by mineral inclusions, most often iron oxides. It takes a good polish, cuts with diamond tooling, and has been used for seals, veneer, and ornamental masonry for thousands of years. A wall described as jasper therefore has a real, workable material family behind it, even though the passage's crystal-clear quality is a visionary description rather than a measured property.

Wall Optical Properties Rank 4 · feasibility: demonstrated

Text-faithful view (faith interpretation): The city's light is compared to jasper, clear as crystal, in Revelation 21:11. The passage uses light as a visionary image, not as an optical measurement.

Engineering view: Polished quartz-group stone has a refractive index near 1.54, so light entering a polished face bends measurably and reflects in part from the surface. Jasper itself is opaque, so transmission through a thick jasper block is negligible; the visible brilliance of a jasper wall comes from surface polish and reflected light, not from light passing through the stone.

Signature-original design: this record approximates the corresponding public record with Signature-original materials and detailing. Where any part of it is speculative it is labelled Signature-original, and it is never presented as an authentic public record. Optical behaviour is therefore specified by surface finish rather than transmission: a high polish gives sharp specular highlights, a honed finish gives soft diffuse reflection, and a rough face gives matte scattering. The demo computes refraction at a polished quartz face from the refractive index, which is the honest, measurable part of the wall's optics.
