A Model of a Coral Reef Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

An ocean twin website: a visitor picks a generic part of the sea, such as a reef or a tide, and the site shows the model in plain words, what it holds, how it moves, and what a reader can learn from it. Every model is a generic study, and the site never claims a live reading of a real sea. This record covers A Model of a Coral Reef.

A model of a generic coral reef, showing the shapes that shelter small fish. The design for this record must state the idea in plain sentences, name what a visitor does first, and say what good looks like when it works, so a reader can judge the design without building anything. Without that plain statement, the idea stays vague, a visitor cannot tell what the design promises, and a promising idea is lost because nobody could explain it in words a newcomer can follow. A reader who can follow the design in words can also check it, and a design that can be checked is a design worth building.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a model of a coral reef runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
