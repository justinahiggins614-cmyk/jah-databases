A Riverside Walk With Written Directions Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A local discovery website: a visitor types a place or a kind of outing, and the site lists nearby experiences with the distance, the cost, the time needed, and what to bring, each in plain words. Listings are written by local people and checked before they are published, and every listing says when it was last checked, so a visitor is never sent to a place that has closed. This record covers A Riverside Walk With Written Directions.

A riverside walk needs written directions a visitor can follow without a guide: where to start, how far each stretch runs, and where the path turns. Without written directions, a visitor must guess at every junction, and a wrong turn can add an hour to a walk that was meant to take one. The directions must name landmarks a walker can actually see, such as a stone bridge or a wooden gate, so the walk is easy to follow and hard to lose.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a riverside walk with written directions runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by place and by kind of outing, and a check date on every listing. Written walking directions are as old as guidebooks, which have always told a walker where to start and where to turn.
