The Signature College Mathematics design specification. This is a design-study specification: it specifies Signature-made designs for teaching and using College Mathematics. Every design in it is a proposal, labelled design-study, and no design here is presented as established fact or as an authentic public record.

1. Purpose and scope. College mathematics is the undergraduate core of the subject: numbers, sets, functions, proof, and the language in which every other college subject states its results. The database covers the subject at college level: its core ideas, its standard methods, its worked examples, and its honest limits. It does not claim to cover graduate or doctoral material, and it does not present generated content as authentic public record. The public edition records established college-level knowledge, fact-checked against cited sources; the Signature edition records Signature-made designs, labelled design-study throughout.

2. Prerequisites. A reader needs school-level algebra and arithmetic, comfort with symbols and with reading a definition carefully, and a willingness to work examples by hand before trusting a program. No prior college course in the subject is assumed. Where a unit needs an earlier unit, the record says so in plain words, and the earlier unit is linked. A reader who can follow a worked example step by step can follow this database.

3. Learning outcomes. After working through the database, a learner will be able to: 1. State a mathematical claim exactly, using definitions rather than loose words. 2. Read and write a proof, naming each step and its justification. 3. Work with sets, functions, relations, and numbers as objects with rules. 4. Translate a word problem into symbols, solve it, and translate the answer back. 5. Check a result by a second method, by a special case, or by estimation. 6. Use mathematical language correctly, including quantifiers such as for all and there exists. 7. Explain a result in plain words to a reader who does not share the jargon. 8. Judge whether a claimed result is proved, partly proved, or unproved.

4. Syllabus. The subject is taught in eight units. Unit 1, Numbers and sets: Natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and sets with unions, intersections, and complements. Unit 2, Functions and relations: Functions as rules, domain and range, composition, inverses, and relations including equivalence. Unit 3, Logic and proof: Statements, truth, implication, quantifiers, direct proof, contradiction, and induction. Unit 4, Counting: Permutations, combinations, and the counting arguments used throughout discrete mathematics. Unit 5, Sequences and series: Convergence, divergence, and the sums that appear in calculus and analysis. Unit 6, Geometry and measurement: Distance, angle, area, volume, and the coordinate description of shapes. Unit 7, Probability beginnings: Sample spaces, events, and the counting that gives probabilities their values. Unit 8, Putting it together: A capstone set of problems that use several units at once, with full worked solutions.

5. Content and record requirements. The database holds 24 records in the public edition and 24 records in the Signature edition, 48 records in the pair. Every record is a full record, never a stub: at least 800 words of prose plus this full specification, a worked example with numbers, a demo that is executed at build time, a working program, at least two cited sources with their URLs and retrieval date, a version-1 JCC code (JAHDB/slug#id) that round-trips, and a stated evidence label. Data is added, never reduced: a record that is corrected keeps its history, and a correction is recorded as a new version, never as a silent edit. Every technical term is defined in plain words on first use, and no acronym (a shortened form made from initial letters) is used before its full expansion. Records render as human-readable sentences and paragraphs, never garbled text.

6. Interfaces. 1. Search by title, subject, and keyword across all 24 records of the edition. 2. Retrieval by record identifier, for example JAH-COLMATH-000001, returning the full record. 3. A demo runner that executes the record's program and returns its printed output. 4. A Master AI connection: the subject specialist AI answers questions using only this database's records. Search returns full records, not bare names. Retrieval by identifier returns the whole record, including its demo, program, sources, and specification. The Master AI connection is read-only: the specialist AI answers from this database's records only, and says plainly when a question goes beyond them. There is no login, no payment, and no key for the reader to enter.

7. Worked examples and demos. Every record carries a worked example with real numbers, and a demo program that recomputes the checkable part of that example. The demo sums and averages a named figure table, prints the results, and prints a SHA-256 digest (a 256-bit fingerprint of the result text) of two separate runs; the runs must match, which proves the computation is deterministic (the same input always gives the same output). The expected output is captured from an actual execution at build time, never written by hand. A record whose demo does not run, or whose output does not match, is not shipped.

8. Testing and validation. Acceptance tests for this database: 1. Every record carries at least two cited sources that return HTTP 200. 2. Every record is at least 800 words of prose plus its full specification. 3. Every demo executes and its printed output matches the stored expected output byte for byte. 4. Every demo is deterministic: two runs give the same SHA-256 digest. 5. Every record carries a JCC version 1 code that round-trips. 6. Every record's identifiers are sequential and unique within the edition. 7. Every technical term is defined in plain words on first use. 8. Every record states its evidence label: documented for public, design-study for Signature. 9. Every record's figures are recomputed by its demo, never asserted. 10. The generator for the edition passes node --check, runTests, and seed determinism.

9. Version history and provenance. Version 1.0 of this database was built 2026-11-17 as Wave 1 of the 100 College-Level Resources program, package-only, from the cross-check of 2026-11-17 (manifest HEAD 36cff9863, 63,047 entries). Public records carry source public-harvest and evidence label documented; Signature records carry source signature-generated and evidence label design-study. Every record carries its sources with URLs and the retrieval date 2026-11-17, and its JCC version 1 code, so any record can be traced back to its sources and its build. Future versions add records and corrections; they never rewrite history silently.

10. Specialist AI. Each edition has its own specialist AI, named in the record: the public specialist AI answers questions about the established subject, and the Signature specialist AI answers about the Signature designs. Both explain their own duties in plain words, show their reasoning, run the record's demo and program on request, and say when a question goes beyond the database. Both connect to the Master AI (the central planning, coordination, and retrieval AI of the network) for questions the subject database cannot answer.

11. Expansion mechanism. The database grows by a seeded boundless generator: a deterministic generator program per edition that, given a seed number, emits a new record of the same shape, with the same checks. The generator has a hard stop at 1,000,000,000 outputs per file and is throttled to 100 outputs per call, so it never overloads the host. Generated outputs are generated, not stored files, and generated content is labelled as generated, never presented as authentic public record. New records are added in the same full-record shape, with sources, demo, and JCC code, and the database marches toward a million records or is marked complete with a reason.

12. Boundaries. Health and medical content, where it appears, is defensive and educational only: no cure claims and no specific dosing. Chemistry content gives no synthesis-enabling instructions for hazardous compounds and no pathogen or toxin content. Cybersecurity content is defensive only, with no working exploit code. Speculative material is labelled as design-study or as a forecast, never as fact.
