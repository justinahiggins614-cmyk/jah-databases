A River Level Model Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A freshwater twin website: a visitor picks a generic source of water, such as a river or a reservoir, and the site shows the model in plain words, what it holds, how it moves, and what a reader can learn from it. Every model is a generic study, and the site never claims a live reading of a real source of water. This record covers A River Level Model.

This record is about a river level model, one model in a shared list of freshwater models that any reader can follow in plain words, with the source, the place, and the limits of the model written out. The design for this record must state the idea in plain sentences, name what a visitor does first, and say what good looks like when it works, so a reader can judge the design without building anything. Without that plain statement, the idea stays vague, a visitor cannot tell what the design promises, and a promising idea is lost because nobody could explain it in words a newcomer can follow. A reader who can follow the design in words can also check it, and a design that can be checked is a design worth building.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a river level model runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

A River Level Model grows out of an older habit: keeping one shared tally of water, so a town never promises more than its river and wells can give, and the website form adds a plain list, a search, and a written record for it.
