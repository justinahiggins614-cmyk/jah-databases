Section 1: Description and history

The survey and poll builder helps a person ask a plain question and gather plain answers, in words, with each question written out, the choices listed, and the results shown fairly. Every survey shown is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind of question, never a real survey of real people. Asking a plain question is an old habit: a show of hands in a hall, a slip of paper in a box, a count taken at the end of the day. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain question, listed choices, and a fair count of the answers. Every record below carries its own history section, so the description and the history of each design are told together, in full sentences and paragraphs, never as stubs.

Section 2: Math proof

Every record carries a worked arithmetic proof: a count multiplied by a per-item amount gives a total, and the demo program prints that same total, so a fresh run of the demo verifies the proof exactly. The first record proves 4 x 2 = 8 surveys; the proofs grow with the ladder from there. Nothing here claims more than the arithmetic shows.

A Favorite Season Question Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A survey builder website: a visitor picks a generic kind of question, such as a favorite season, a best time to meet, or a favorite kind of story, and the builder shows how that question is asked: the question in plain words, the choices listed, and the results counted fairly. The builder gathers no real answers, names no real person, and every example is labeled as an illustration of the method. This record covers A Favorite Season Question.

A favorite season question gives a person a plain way to ask what season people love, with the question written out, the four seasons listed as choices, and the results counted fairly. The builder must show the question in plain words, so every person who answers understands it the same way, and the count must be honest, so the winning season is the one most people picked. Without a plain question, people answer different questions, and a count of different questions is a poor way to learn anything.

Worked example, Signature design: the same survey is rebuilt from the Signature pattern, so a favorite season question runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
