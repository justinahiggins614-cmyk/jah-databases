# Dictionary entries — The Provenance Registry Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## provenance

The history of where a thing came from: its source, its retrieval date, its edition, and every hand it passed through.

## registry

One ordered list that holds the provenance of many things, so any one of them can be looked up by name.

## source

Where a record's facts came from: the document, the site, or the person, named in full with a retrieval date.

## retrieval date

The date a source was read, written beside the facts taken from it, so later readers know how fresh they are.

## edition

One numbered version of a record, kept beside the earlier versions, so that no version is ever lost.

## chain of custody

The ordered list of hands a thing passed through, each named, so that nothing in its history is hidden.

## attribution

Naming who made or wrote a thing, as part of its provenance, so credit and blame both land in the right place.

## withdrawal

Marking a record as withdrawn when it is proven wrong, keeping it readable with the reason, never erasing it.

## verification

Checking a record's provenance by re-reading its source and comparing what the source says with what the record says.

## gap

A missing link in a chain, marked as a gap, never filled in by guessing.

## claim

One statement in a record, which stands or falls with the source that supports it.

## ledger

The registry's own written record of every entry and every change, kept in order.
