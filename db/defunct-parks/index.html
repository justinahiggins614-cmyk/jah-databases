Astroworld Rank 1 · simple · defunct park · beginner

Astroworld was a large amusement park in Houston, Texas, that operated from 1968 until 2005. It opened as part of the Astrodomain complex beside the Astrodome, and its skyline was long marked by tall rides and a looping steel coaster that regulars remembered by sight. The park mixed thrill rides, family rides, water rides, and live shows across themed sections, and it drew visitors from across Texas for school trips, summer days, and evening outings. Its final season came in 2005, and the park closed at the end of that year; the site was later cleared, so nothing of the park itself remains for visitors to walk through today. What survives is memory: photographs, home movies, ride names passed between fans, and the stories of people who spent childhood summers there.

Geauga Lake Rank 2 · simple · defunct park · beginner

Geauga Lake was an amusement park on a lake in Aurora, Ohio, with roots reaching back to 1888, when the lake itself was already a picnic and swimming destination. Over more than a century it grew from a lakeside resort into a full amusement park with wooden coasters, a boardwalk, and a water park beside the lake. Generations of Ohio families visited for the same reasons: the lake breeze, the big wooden coaster on the hill, and the water slides on hot days. The amusement park side closed after the 2007 season, while the water park continued a few seasons longer before it too closed. The lake remains, but the rides are gone, and the grounds have since been redeveloped, leaving the park to live on in local memory and archives.

Opryland USA Rank 3 · simple · defunct park · beginner

Opryland USA was a music-themed amusement park in Nashville, Tennessee, that operated from 1972 until 1997. It grew out of Nashville's country music industry and built its identity around live performance: stages and show venues sat alongside rides, and a day at the park mixed coasters and flumes with concerts and musical revues. Themed areas carried musical names and styles, and the park's shows were as much a draw as its rides. After the 1997 season the park closed, and the site was redeveloped, so the rides and stages no longer stand. Its place in Nashville's story remains, though, as the park that tried to put a whole music city inside a single day out.

Riverview Park Chicago Rank 6 · compounded · defunct park · intermediate

Riverview Park was a huge amusement park on Chicago's North Side, operating from 1904 until 1967, and at its peak it was one of the largest amusement parks in the world, with dozens of rides spread over a vast site. River Country was a water park at Walt Disney World in Florida, operating from 1976 until 2001, and it was Disney's first water park. Its theme was an old-fashioned swimming hole: a lagoon with a sandy shore, a lazy river, water slides built into a hillside, and a rope swing over the water, all designed to feel like a rustic summer swimming spot rather than a modern water park. It closed in 2001 and sat abandoned for years before being demolished, and the site was later redeveloped. River Country is remembered fondly by Disney fans as a gentler, more naturalistic water park than the larger parks that followed it, and its swimming-hole idea still shapes how fans talk about water park design.
