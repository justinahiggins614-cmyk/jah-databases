History
When a maker promises that a product will work, the promise needs a written form: what is covered, for how long, and what happens if it fails. From the craftsman's spoken word to the printed warranty card in the box, the promise has always rested on the same plain practice — write down the product, the period, and the cover, and keep the record so that the promise can be proved when the product fails. Managing warranties means keeping every such promise true, watching its dates, and answering each claim by the words that were written. This database is built on that public practice of recording every warranty whole, with its claim beside it.
