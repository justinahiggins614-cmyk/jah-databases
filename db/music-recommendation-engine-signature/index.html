Section 1: Description and history

The music recommendation engine suggests a kind of music a listener may like, from plain answers about mood, tempo, and instruments, with every suggestion labeled as an illustrative sample of a generic kind, never a real song by a real artist. Recommending music is an old habit: a shopkeeper who knew your taste, a friend who lent you a record, a radio host who played the next song for you. The website form grows out of that habit, adding plain questions and a written reason for each suggestion. Every record below carries its own history section, so the description and the history of each design are told together, in full sentences and paragraphs, never as stubs.

A Recommendation by Mood Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A recommendation website: a visitor answers plain questions about mood, tempo, and instruments, and the engine suggests a generic kind of music that fits those answers, with a plain reason for each suggestion. The engine names no real song, no real artist, and every suggestion is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind. This record covers A Recommendation by Mood.

A recommendation by mood gives a listener music that fits how they feel, from a calm tune for a quiet evening to a bright tune for a fresh morning. The engine must ask the mood in plain words, match it to a kind of music, and give a written reason for the match, so the listener can see why this tune and not another. Without a written reason, the listener must trust the engine blind, and blind trust is a poor way to find music.
