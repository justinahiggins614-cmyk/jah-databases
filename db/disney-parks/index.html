Disneyland California Rank 1 · simple · california · beginner

Disneyland in Anaheim, California, opened on July 17, 1955, and is the original Disney theme park, the only one built under Walt Disney's direct supervision. Its lands ring a central hub overlooked by Sleeping Beauty Castle: Main Street, U.S.A. at the entrance, then Adventureland, Frontierland, Fantasyland, Tomorrowland, New Orleans Square, Critter Country, Mickey's Toontown, and Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge, with the Rivers of America and Tom Sawyer Island at its heart. The park's identity rests on its scale, smaller and more walkable than its later siblings, and on its layered history, where rides from the 1950s stand beside lands opened in 2019. Its full land-by-land treatment, including past and changed areas, lives in the companion disneyland-park database, and this record serves as its catalog entry and summary.

Theming Ecosystems

Magic Kingdom Rank 2 · simple · florida · beginner

Magic Kingdom at Walt Disney World in Florida opened on October 1, 1971, as the resort's first park, and it remains its most visited. Its layout follows the Disneyland pattern at a larger scale: Main Street, U.S.A. leads to a central hub overlooked by Cinderella Castle, with Adventureland, Frontierland, Liberty Square, Fantasyland, and Tomorrowland around it. The park's character comes from its completeness: classic dark rides, a frontier river and island, a liberty-themed colonial district found nowhere else in the Disney parks, and a fantasy land expanded in 2012 with new storybook areas. Magic Kingdom also carries the resort's nightly fireworks tradition, with the castle as the show's centerpiece, and its parades run the length of Main Street to the hub, closing the park's main artery twice on busy days.

Lands Areas

Main Street, U.S.A.; Adventureland; Frontierland; Liberty Square; Fantasyland; Tomorrowland

Rides New

Cinderella Castle stage shows (NEW); Pirates of the Caribbean (NEW); Haunted Mansion (NEW); Space Mountain (NEW); Seven Dwarfs Mine Train (NEW)

Rides Past

Mr. Toad's Wild Ride (PAST, closed 1998); Snow White's Scary Adventures (PAST, replaced 2014); 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea (PAST, closed 1994)

Theming Ecosystems

Colonial, frontier, storybook, and future ecosystems around a castle hub, with parade-scale main street architecture

Hidden Spots Caves

Guest-visible castle-area gardens; frontier island trails; fantasyland storybook courts

Planning Note

Arrive early for the headliners, watch fireworks from the hub lawn, and use Liberty Square as the quiet crossing between frontier and fantasy.

EPCOT Rank 3 · simple · florida · beginner

EPCOT at Walt Disney World opened on October 1, 1982, as a park built around two ideas: future technology and world cultures. Its front half holds pavilions devoted to science, imagination, oceans, and land, grouped in recent years into the neighborhoods of World Celebration, World Discovery, and World Nature, while its back half, World Showcase, rings a lagoon with pavilions for eleven nations, each with architecture, food, and entertainment from the country it represents. EPCOT's pace differs from its siblings: it rewards walking, tasting, and lingering, with festivals through the year that fill the Showcase with seasonal food and art. Its geodesic sphere, Spaceship Earth, is the park's icon and its first ride, carrying guests through a history of communication from cave painting to computers.

Lands Areas

World Celebration; World Discovery; World Nature; World Showcase lagoon and eleven national pavilions

Rides New

Spaceship Earth (NEW); Test Track (NEW); Soarin' (NEW); Frozen Ever After (NEW); Remy's Ratatouille Adventure (NEW)

Rides Past

Horizons (PAST, closed 1999); World of Motion (PAST, closed 1996); Maelstrom (PAST, replaced 2014)

Theming Ecosystems

Science, nature, and imagination ecosystems in front; national architecture, food, and music ecosystems around the lagoon

Hidden Spots Caves

Guest-visible lagoon promenades, national gardens, and pavilion courtyards; all are public guest areas

Planning Note

Tour the front neighborhoods in the morning, circle the lagoon in the afternoon, and stay for the lagoon fireworks.

Hollywood Studios Rank 4 · simple · florida · beginner

Disney's Hollywood Studios at Walt Disney World opened on May 1, 1989, as a park devoted to film, television, and stagecraft, and it has been reworked more thoroughly than any of its siblings. Its entrance recreates a golden-age Hollywood boulevard leading to a replica of Grauman's Chinese Theatre, while its newer lands carry guests into a toy's world and into a galaxy far away, alongside surviving streets themed to old Hollywood and New York. The park's thrill identity rests on its tower drop ride and its launched coaster, and its show identity on its stunt spectacular and its stage musicals. Hollywood Studios is the smallest of the Florida parks by walkable area, which makes its headliner queues the longest in the resort and its planning the most important.

Hidden Spots Caves

Animal Kingdom Rank 5 · simple · florida · beginner

Disney's Animal Kingdom at Walt Disney World opened on April 22, 1998, as a park built around animals, nature, and conservation, and its icon is the Tree of Life, a sculpted baobab whose trunk carries hundreds of carved animals. Its lands are Africa, Asia, Discovery Island, DinoLand U.S.A., and Pandora, the world of Avatar, each blending real animal habitats, walking trails, and rides into one landscape. The park's live-animal trails are its quiet heart: gorilla, tiger, and bird habitats viewed from shaded paths, with keepers and guides nearby. Its thrill identity rests on its white-water rapids, its mountain coaster through a Himalayan landscape, and its flight simulator over Pandora's floating mountains. Animal Kingdom closes earlier than its siblings, because its animals keep their own hours, and its evenings belong to the Tree of Life's light displays.

Disney California Adventure Rank 6 · compounded · california · intermediate

Disney California Adventure, beside Disneyland in Anaheim, opened on February 8, 2001, as a park celebrating California, and it was rebuilt on a large scale in 2012 into the park guests know today. Its lands now run from Buena Vista Street, a 1920s Los Angeles entrance, through Hollywood Land and Grizzly Peak's wilderness, to Pixar Pier, Paradise Gardens, and Avengers Campus, with Cars Land recreating a desert highway town in full. The park's identity is its variety: a boardwalk pier of family and thrill rides, a mountain rapids ride, a desert town built to film-set completeness, and a superhero campus of stunt shows and interactive rides. Its nighttime water show on Paradise Bay remains its closing tradition, and its food festival calendar makes it the resort's tasting park.

Disneyland Paris Rank 7 · compounded · paris · intermediate

Disneyland Park at Disneyland Paris opened on April 12, 1992, as the Disney park built for Europe, and it follows the Disneyland pattern with a distinctly European castle and a frontier land built around a mining mountain. Its lands are Main Street, U.S.A., Frontierland, Adventureland, Fantasyland, and Discoveryland, the last a Jules Verne-inspired future land of brass, iron, and rivets rather than the sleek white future of its American siblings. Sleeping Beauty Castle here is taller and more ornate, with a dragon sleeping in a cave beneath it that guests can visit, and the park's gardens and covered arcades reflect a climate where rain and cold shape the guest experience. Disneyland Paris draws visitors from across Europe, and its shows and parades carry a multilingual character its siblings do not need.

Theming Ecosystems

European storybook, mining-frontier, adventure, and Verne-future ecosystems, with covered arcades for wet weather

Hidden Spots Caves

Guest-visible dragon cave beneath the castle; covered arcades and garden courts; all are public guest areas

Planning Note

Walt Disney Studios Park Rank 8 · compounded · paris · intermediate

Walt Disney Studios Park at Disneyland Paris opened on March 16, 2002, as a second gate devoted to film production, and it has spent two decades growing from a studio-backlot park into a fuller destination. Its original identity was the working studio: soundstages, backlot streets, and shows about how films are made, and its newer lands add animation courtyards, a toy-scale world, a rat's-eye Parisian kitchen district, and a superhero campus. The park's thrill anchors are its tower drop ride and its launched coaster, and its family identity rests on its animation and toy lands. Its ongoing expansion toward a lake and a frozen kingdom continues the same direction: from a park about making films to a park about living inside them.

Tokyo Disneyland Rank 9 · compounded · tokyo · intermediate

Tokyo Disneyland in Urayasu, Japan, opened on April 15, 1983, as the first Disney park built outside the United States, and it follows the Disneyland pattern at a grander scale with a Japanese guest culture of its own. Its lands are World Bazaar, a covered main street built for rainy weather, Adventureland, Westernland, Fantasyland, Tomorrowland, Critter Country, and Toontown, with Cinderella Castle at its hub. The park is famous for its hospitality standards, its seasonal events, and its parades, which draw dedicated audiences who arrive hours early for viewing spots. Its version of classic rides often differs in detail from the American originals, and its newer fantasy expansion added a storybook forest and a bay-area district that extended the park's eastern edge.

Tokyo DisneySea Rank 10 · compounded · tokyo · intermediate

Tokyo DisneySea, beside Tokyo Disneyland, opened on September 4, 2001, and it is widely regarded as the most richly themed Disney park, a park built around water and the romance of sea voyages. Its ports are Mediterranean Harbor, American Waterfront, Port Discovery, Lost River Delta, Arabian Coast, Mermaid Lagoon, and Mysterious Island, the last a volcanic island hollowed into the world of Jules Verne, with its volcano as the park's icon. Its thrill anchors are its journey to the center of the earth ride inside the volcano and its tower drop on the waterfront, and its evening lagoon show closes the park over the harbor.

Hong Kong Disneyland Rank 11 · compounded · hong-kong · intermediate

Hong Kong Disneyland on Lantau Island opened on September 12, 2005, as the smallest of the castle parks, built on reclaimed land with room planned for growth. Shanghai Disneyland Rank 12 · compounded · shanghai · intermediate

Shanghai Disneyland in Pudong opened on June 16, 2016, as the newest castle park and the largest by area, built with a distinctly Chinese character alongside its Disney identity. Its lands are Mickey Avenue, Gardens of Imagination, Fantasyland, Treasure Cove, Adventure Isle, Tomorrowland, and Toy Story Land, plus the Disneytown shopping district outside the gate, and its castle, the Enchanted Storybook Castle, is the largest of any Disney park, holding a restaurant, a walkthrough, and a boat ride beneath it. Blizzard Beach Rank 13 · complex · water · advanced

Disney's Blizzard Beach at Walt Disney World opened on April 1, 1995, as a water park built on a joke told straight: a ski resort that melted in the Florida sun, leaving its slopes, lifts, and lodge to become slides, rivers, and pools. Its icon is Mount Gushmore, a snow-capped mountain in central Florida, with slides running down its faces, a chairlift carrying guests to the top, and a lazy river circling its base. The park's slides range from family raft slides to Summit Plummet, a near-vertical speed slide that remains one of the tallest and fastest body slides in the world, and its children's areas carry the ski-lodge story at a small scale. Blizzard Beach operates seasonally in rotation with its sister water park, and its theming is the fullest joke in the Disney parks: snow in a swamp, taken seriously in every sign, costume, and building.

Typhoon Lagoon Rank 14 · complex · water · advanced

Disney's Typhoon Lagoon at Walt Disney World opened on June 1, 1989, as a water park built around a storm story: a typhoon struck a tropical resort, beaching a shrimp boat named Miss Tilly on top of a mountain, and the park's slides, rivers, and pools are the storm's aftermath made playful. Its centerpiece is the Surf Pool, one of the largest wave pools in the world, producing waves large enough to surf, and its lazy river, Castaway Creek, circles the whole park past waterfalls, grottos, and rainforest planting. The park's slides include family raft rides, body slides, and the Crush 'n' Gusher water coaster, which uses jets to push rafts uphill as well as down. Typhoon Lagoon operates seasonally in rotation with Blizzard Beach, and its storm story is carried in every detail, from tilted buildings to scattered cargo.

Discovery Island (past) Rank 15 · complex · past · advanced

Discovery Island at Walt Disney World was a zoological island park in Bay Lake, opened in 1974 and closed on April 8, 1999, when its animals and its conservation mission moved to the new Animal Kingdom. Guests reached it by boat and walked trails past birds, reptiles, and small mammals, with a flamingo lagoon, a tortoise beach, and an aviary among its quiet attractions, and its character was closer to a nature preserve than to a thrill park. The island's closure was a transfer, not an abandonment of its purpose: its breeding and conservation programs continued on the mainland, and the island itself has remained closed to guests ever since, its buildings slowly reclaimed by vegetation. Discovery Island is remembered as the resort's first attempt at a living-animal park, and its lessons shaped Animal Kingdom's larger, more durable design.

River Country (past) Rank 16 · complex · past · advanced

River Country at Walt Disney World opened on June 20, 1976, as the resort's first water park, a swimming-hole park themed to an old-fashioned American riverbank, with a sandy beach, a rope swing, a barrel bridge, and slides built to look like wooden waterworks. Its water came from Bay Lake, filtered and treated for its pools, and its character was deliberately rustic: no towering slides, no concrete mountain, just a riverbank where families swam, slid, and picnicked. River Country closed on November 2, 2001, after years of declining attendance beside its larger, newer sister water parks, and its site stood unused for years before redevelopment. It is remembered as the water park that proved the idea: guests would travel for a themed place to swim, and the resort's later water parks, Blizzard Beach and Typhoon Lagoon, built on its success at a far larger scale.
