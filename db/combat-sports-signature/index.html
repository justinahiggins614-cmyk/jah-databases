Judo Rank 1 · simple · grappling · beginner

Signature judo is our own school of the gentle way, built on the same truth that balance decides everything. A Signature judoka trains falling as a daily habit, not a warm-up, and treats every grip as a question: where is the partner's weight, and where is it going next? Our sessions open with slow, cooperative throwing drills in which the thrower must land the partner softly, and only then move to free practice where both players may attack. We keep the contest scores as a training language rather than a goal, so that a student learns to read a match the way a reader reads a page, one shift of weight at a time.

History

The Signature school traces its line to the Kodokan idea that maximum efficiency with mutual welfare beats brute strength, and restates it for a modern training hall.

Training

A week pairs two throwing sessions with one groundwork session and one falling-and-mobility session, so no student throws hard before they can fall well.

Techniques

Signature emphasis falls on foot sweeps and hip throws that need timing more than size, plus pins that control without pain.

Safety

Tapping ends every hold instantly, and any throw that cannot be landed softly is not practised at speed.

Working demo program

DAYS = [('Monday throws', 60), ('Wednesday groundwork', 60), ('Friday falling and mobility', 45), ('Saturday free practice', 75)]
print('Signature judo weekly plan')
total = sum(m for _, m in DAYS)
best = max(DAYS, key=lambda d: d[1])
for day, m in DAYS:
    print('%s: %d minutes' % (day, m))
print('Weekly total: %d minutes' % total)
print('Daily average: %.1f minutes' % (total / len(DAYS)))
print('Longest session: %s (%d minutes)' % (best[0], best[1]))

Taekwondo Rank 2 · simple · striking · beginner

Signature taekwondo keeps the high kick but rebuilds the lesson around it: a kick is only good if the kicker is still standing well after it lands. Our students drill the chamber, the hip turn, and the return to guard as one movement, and they practise kicking to a pad held at three heights so the body learns control before power. Sparring in our hall uses the same scoring language as the sport, but a round is won in training by clean technique and calm footwork, not by chasing points. Patterns are treated as moving lessons in balance, and breaking is practised only as a test of focus on a board that gives way cleanly.

Training

Each session opens with hip mobility and balance work, then pad rounds at low, middle, and high targets, then controlled sparring.

Techniques

Signature drills stress the roundhouse and side kick first, because both teach chamber, hip rotation, and recovery in one action.

Safety

Head contact in training is light and controlled, pads and guards are always worn, and any kick that loses balance is repeated slowly.

Competitions

Students may enter sport competition, but the hall's own test is a pattern and pad round judged on control, not on points.

Karate Rank 3 · simple · striking · beginner

Signature karate begins from stillness. Before any punch is thrown, our students learn to stand, breathe, and bow properly, because a technique thrown from a poor stance is a wasted movement. The hall teaches one kata deeply before adding another, and each kata is walked slowly, then at speed, then against an imagined opponent, so the student understands why each block turns the way it does. Sparring stays light and controlled, and the highest praise in our hall goes to the student who stops a technique exactly on target without touching hard. Power matters, but it is always the servant of control.

Training

A session divides into stance and breathing work, basics in lines, one kata studied in depth, and light controlled sparring.

Techniques

Signature basics stress the straight punch from a settled hip, the front kick, and three blocks practised until they need no thought.

Safety

All sparring contact is controlled and stopped on target; protective gear is worn, and hard contact ends the round.

History

The Signature line honours the Okinawan roots of the art and the Japanese styles that carried it worldwide, while teaching it as character training first.

Aikido Rank 4 · simple · grappling · beginner

Signature aikido is taught as a study of entering and turning. Our first lesson is not a throw at all but a walk: stepping off the line of an attack while staying upright and calm. From there, students learn one wrist control deeply, then a throw that uses the same entry, so the art grows as a small family of movements rather than a long list. There is no contest in our hall, and progress is measured by how softly a partner can be brought down and how safely they can fall. The wooden sword appears early, not as a weapon to master but as a teacher of distance: it shows at once when a student is standing too close or too far.

Training

Sessions pair entry footwork with one throw or pin, and every technique is practised on both sides of the body.

Techniques

Signature emphasis falls on the entering throw and the wrist turn, both taught from the same off-line step.

Safety

Falling skill is practised every session, locks are applied slowly, and tapping stops everything immediately.

History

The Signature school follows Ueshiba's principle of blending with force rather than opposing it, restated for a modern cooperative hall.

Muay Thai Rank 5 · simple · striking · beginner

Signature muay Thai keeps the eight limbs but teaches them as a rhythm first and a fight second. A new student spends weeks on the teep and the round kick to the pad before any sparring, because those two tools teach distance, hip turn, and recovery. Clinch work in our hall is practised slowly, with partners learning to balance and off-balance each other before knees are added at all. The wai kru is taught as a mark of respect for teachers and training partners, and fitness rounds are built so that a student of any age can finish them. Sparring stays technical and light, and the coach stops any round that turns into a contest of toughness.

Training

A Signature week mixes pad rounds, bag rounds, clinch balance drills, skipping, and one technical sparring session.

Techniques

The teep and the round kick come first, then elbows and knees from the clinch, always from a balanced stance.

Safety

Sparring is technical and light, shin guards and gloves are always worn, and coaches stop rounds that escalate.

History

The Signature school honours the Thai tradition and the wai kru ritual while teaching the art as a fitness and self-defence discipline.

Capoeira Rank 6 · compounded · striking · intermediate

Signature capoeira is taught as a conversation before it is taught as a contest. A new student learns the ginga until it feels like walking, then one dodge and one kick, and then enters the roda to play a slow game in which the aim is to keep moving, not to hit. Music is not background in our hall: every student learns to play the berimbau and to sing a chorus, because the music sets the speed of the game and tells players when to enter and leave. Acrobatics come late, only when the base steps are strong, and the highest praise goes to the player whose game makes their partner look good.

Training

Classes divide into ginga and dodge drills, music practice, slow roda games, and only later acrobatics.

Techniques

Signature basics are the ginga, one esquiva dodge, the queixada kick, and the au cartwheel, in that order.

Safety

Roda games are controlled, kicks are placed rather than driven through, and the music leader may stop a game that turns rough.

History

The Signature school honours capoeira's Afro-Brazilian roots and teaches its history plainly, including the era when it was banned.

Sambo Rank 7 · compounded · grappling · intermediate

Signature sambo is taught as practical grappling for people who need to stay on their feet. Our students begin with jacket grips and foot sweeps, because control of the jacket decides most exchanges, and they learn leg locks only after they can fall, pin, and escape a pin reliably. Training alternates between throw rounds, ground rounds, and wrestling-style leg-attack rounds, so no student becomes a specialist in only one phase. Combat elements are taught as self-defence principles, such as keeping distance and escaping a grip, rather than as a fighting sport. The hall rule is simple: a technique that only works on a partner who is not resisting is not yet a technique.

Training

A Signature week pairs throw rounds, ground rounds, and leg-attack rounds, with breakfalls in every session.

Techniques

Jacket throws and foot sweeps come first, then pins and escapes, then leg locks, which are applied slowly.

Safety

Leg locks are taught late, applied gradually, and stopped by tapping; no lock is ever forced through resistance.

History

The Signature school follows the Soviet systematisation of folk wrestling and judo into a practical grappling art.

Krav Maga Rank 8 · compounded · striking · intermediate

Signature krav maga is built on one sentence: the best fight is the one you never have. Our classes open with awareness drills, such as spotting exits and reading a street, before any physical technique is taught, and de-escalation is practised as a skill with words, not as a lecture. Physical training keeps only a small set of tools: a palm strike, a knee, an elbow, and escapes from the most common grabs and chokes, drilled until they work when the student is tired and surprised. Weapon material is taught late, slowly, and honestly, with the plain warning that no empty-hand defence against a weapon is reliable. The hall never sells confidence it cannot back with practice.

Training

Sessions divide into awareness work, de-escalation practice, pad drills under fatigue, and scenario practice.

Techniques

Signature tools are few by design: palm strike, knee, elbow, and escapes from grabs and chokes.

Safety

All contact uses pads or cooperative partners, weapon drills use training weapons, and no technique is promised as reliable against a real weapon.

History

The Signature school follows Lichtenfeld's practical method while teaching it as civilian self-defence with avoidance first.

Sumo Rank 9 · compounded · grappling · intermediate

Signature sumo is taught as a study of the first step. In our hall, two students face each other in a marked circle and the whole lesson is the charge: low hips, driving legs, and hands that meet the opponent before the opponent meets you. Weight is never the lesson, and our classes use weight classes so that smaller students learn technique instead of being pushed aside. Ritual is taught with respect: salt, bowing, and the leg stomp are explained as part of a living Japanese tradition, not copied as costume. Bouts are short, refereed, and followed by a bow, and the coach's praise goes to the wrestler who wins by balance and timing rather than by bulk.

Training

Sessions pair charge drills, pushing drills, belt-grip practice, and short refereed bouts in a marked circle.

Techniques

Signature emphasis falls on the low charge, the thrust, and the sidestep, all of which reward timing over size.

Safety

Weight classes are used in training, falls are practised on soft ground, and bouts stop at once on a clean ring-out.

History

The Signature school honours sumo's ancient Japanese roots and teaches its rituals as a living tradition.

Kendo Rank 10 · compounded · weapon art · intermediate

Signature kendo begins with the bow and the foot. Students learn to advance and retreat without crossing their feet, to hold the shinai correctly, and to shout the target as they cut, because a silent cut is treated as an unfinished one. Our hall teaches kata with wooden swords early, since kata shows distance and timing more plainly than free practice does. Free matches use the same two-point rule as the wider art, and referees judge posture and spirit beside the strike itself. The highest praise in our hall goes to the student whose cut is straight, loud, and balanced, whether or not it scores.

Training

Sessions divide into footwork, suburi cutting practice, kata with wooden swords, and free practice in armour.

Techniques

Signature basics are the head cut, wrist cut, and torso cut, each shouted and driven by footwork.

Safety

Armour is always worn for free practice, shinai are inspected for splinters, and thrusts to the throat are practised only under supervision.

History

The Signature school follows the samurai sword tradition into its modern sport form, taught as character training.

Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Rank 11 · complex · grappling · advanced

Signature jiu-jitsu is taught position before submission. A new student learns to survive underneath, then to escape, then to hold a dominant position, and only then to finish, because a finish attempted from a poor position teaches nothing. Our classes open with a positional game in which one student must hold a position for a minute while the other escapes, and submissions are introduced slowly, with tapping treated as a normal part of learning rather than a defeat. Sparring is the centre of the art, but it is graded: beginners roll lightly, and no round is worth an injury. The hall's rule is that the best student is the one everyone wants to train with.

Training

Sessions pair a positional game, a technique of the week, and graded live sparring, with beginners rolling lightly.

Techniques

Signature order is survival, escape, position, then submission, starting from guard, side control, mount, and back control.

Safety

Tapping ends every hold instantly, sparring intensity is graded by experience, and no submission is forced through resistance.

History

The Signature school follows the Brazilian development of judo groundwork into a live-tested ground art.

Mixed Martial Arts Rank 12 · complex · striking · advanced

Signature mixed martial arts is taught as three arts learned in turn, never as a single brawl. A student first builds a striking base, then learns wrestling takedowns and how to stop them, then ground grappling, and only then combines the phases in controlled drills. Our hall never runs hard sparring for beginners: striking sparring is technical and light, grappling sparring is graded, and full combination sparring comes only after months of single-phase practice. Weight classes and medical sense are taught as part of the sport, not as paperwork, and the coach's first lesson is that the cage rewards the athlete who stays calm across all three phases.

Training

A Signature week rotates striking, wrestling, and grappling days, with combination drills only after single-phase fundamentals are solid.

Techniques

The three phases are striking, wrestling takedowns and defence, and ground grappling, each trained separately before combining.

Safety

Beginner sparring is technical and light, grappling is graded by experience, and hard sparring is reserved for prepared students.

History

The Signature school follows the sport's path from early unregulated contests to a regulated multi-discipline sport.

Savate Rank 13 · complex · striking · advanced

Signature savate is taught as fencing with the feet. Our students learn to move on the balls of their feet, to kick long and return to guard, and to treat the shoe as a precise tool rather than a heavy one. The first kicks taught are the low fouette to the leg and the chasse to the body, because both teach distance without exposing the kicker. Boxing hands are drilled beside the kicks from the start, so the art never becomes legs alone. Cane work appears as a companion study for advanced students, and the hall's praise goes to the fighter whose footwork makes their kicks look easy.

Training

Sessions pair footwork drills, kicking on pads, boxing hands, and controlled sparring in shoes.

Techniques

Signature basics are the low fouette, the chasse side kick, and the revers turning kick, all returned to guard.

Safety

Sparring is controlled, shoes are inspected, and protective gear is always worn.

History

The Signature school follows the French formalisation of street kicking and boxing into an elegant sport.

Music and artistic forms are taught beside the sport form, so students understand that the dance-like movements carry real entries and exits. Weapons appear late and only as a study of distance, with training versions. The hall's rule is that a silat player who stops moving has already lost the exchange.

Training

Escrima and Kali Rank 16 · complex · weapon art · advanced

Signature escrima begins with one stick and one angle. A student learns the first downward angle until it is clean, then its mirror, then the two woven together, because the art is built from pairs, not from lists. Partner flow drills come early but slowly: partners exchange strikes at a speed where both can keep the pattern going, and speed rises only when the pattern survives it. Knife work uses training blades and is taught as a study of respect for the blade's danger, never as a trick. The hall's praise goes to the student whose flow never breaks, even when the pattern changes without warning.

Training

Sessions pair solo angle drills, sinawali weaving, slow partner flow, and footwork triangles.

Techniques

Signature basics are the first two angles, their weave, and the triangle step that moves the body off the line.

Safety

Sparring uses padded sticks and protective gear, blades are training versions only, and flow speed rises only when the pattern holds.

History

The Signature school follows the Filipino family schools that systematised stick, blade, and empty-hand work.
