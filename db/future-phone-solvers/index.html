A design for a phone that runs for a full month on one charge, doing ordinary daily work the whole time: calls, messages, maps, and photographs. This is a design, not a real product.

A phone lasts a day on one charge, and that day shapes its owner's life: the charger travels everywhere, the battery is watched like a clock, and a long day away from power is a day of rationing. A month on one charge would end the rationing: the phone could travel for weeks, work through long journeys, and sit ready without a charger in sight. The problem is energy: a month of calls, messages, maps, and photographs needs far more energy than any battery a phone can hold today, and the design must cut the phone's hunger, store more energy in less space, and prove the month with real daily work, not with the phone asleep. History

A neighborhood can lose its network while one phone still has a connection: a tower fails, a cable is cut, or a storm takes the service down, and every phone nearby goes dark except the one that still reaches. A phone that generates its own network for neighbors would share its connection with the phones around it, so a whole street can be online from one phone, until the service returns. The problem is the sharing: the sharing phone must divide its connection fairly, protect each neighbor's traffic from the others, and keep working itself while it serves them all. Without fairness and protection, the shared network is a gift that costs its giver their own connection.

Worked example: after a storm takes a street's service down, one phone with a connection shares it with its neighbors, each neighbor gets a fair share, and the street stays online until the service returns.

History

Generating a network for neighbors descends from community networks and from hotspots, where one connection served many, and from the old habit of neighbors sharing what they had; the phone version needs that sharing to be fair and protected, not only generous.

Proposed solution

Share the phone's connection with the phones around it; Divide the connection fairly, so no neighbor takes it all; Protect each neighbor's traffic from the others; Keep the sharing phone working while it serves its neighbors; Stop sharing when the service returns, and say so plainly.

Dependencies

Published research on mesh networking and hotspot sharing described in general terms; a radio that can serve many neighbors at once; a way to divide a connection fairly; a standard for protecting shared traffic.

Feasibility

research-stage — Public research on mesh networking, hotspots, and community networks exists and is published, but no complete phone that generates its own fair, protected network for neighbors exists at the needed scale yet.

Verification path

A simulation of the sharing budget shows the connection divided fairly among a stated number of neighbors; a prototype shares with two phones and protects each one's traffic; a full-scale test shares with a real street and logs every share.
