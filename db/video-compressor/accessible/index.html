The Video Compressor Database (Public)

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. A video compressor shrinks a video file by using fewer bits: a lower bitrate, a smaller resolution, or both. The demo on this page compresses a seeded clip locally: a clip of 30 seconds, 900 frames and 30 megabytes. A full transcode needs a backend, and that is BLOCKED; this is a local partial demo, and it says so. You can also upload your own clip, which is read in the browser and never leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced.

Full record index: https://justinahiggins614-cmyk.github.io/jah-databases-data-2/data/video-compressor/index.jsonl.gz

  1. What a Video Compressor Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. A video compressor shrinks a video file by using fewer bits. A video compressor shrinks 30 megabytes to 15 megabytes

    Math proof: Halving 8 megabits a second gives 4 megabits a second, and 30 megabytes becomes 15 megabytes. 30 megabytes over 15 megabytes is a ratio of 2 to 1. A sample of 8 frames totalling 80 kilobytes compresses to 40 kilobytes. A target size of 15 megabytes needs 4 megabits a second for 30 seconds. Compressing 8 megabits a second to 6 megabits a second shrinks 30 megabytes to 22.5 megabytes.

    Science proof: A video compressor works because a file's size is its bitrate times its length divided by 8: halving the bitrate from 8 to 4 halves the file from 30 megabytes to 15 megabytes, while the 900 frames and the 30 seconds stay the same. That is why the local demo compresses the seeded clip's numbers and shows the ratio, and says plainly that a full transcode needs a backend and is BLOCKED.
