History
Authors have sought protection for their writings since the first printing presses, and the law answered with a register: a public record of who made a work and when. Copyright registration grew beside the register: clerks and advisers who read the requirements aloud, named the right form, and wrote down what was filed and when. From the paper certificate kept in a drawer to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the guidance down whole.
