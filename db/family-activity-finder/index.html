Three Plain Questions to Start Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A family activity website: a family states how many people are coming, how much time they have, and whether they want to stay inside or go out, and the finder shows activities the whole family can do together, each with steps in plain sentences, a time, and a list of what is needed. Every activity is written for all ages, with a clear age note before it starts, and every activity uses ordinary things a family already has at home or can find nearby. This record covers Three Plain Questions to Start.

A finder that asks twenty questions before showing anything is a finder that loses the family before the first activity appears. Three plain questions to start, how many people, how much time, and inside or outside, are enough to narrow the field without tiring anyone. Without the three questions, the finder shows everything at once, and a family facing a hundred activities chooses none of them. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, three plain questions to start runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.
