  1. Three Plain Questions to Start

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The family activity finder is a plain website where a family states how many people are coming, how much time they have, and whether they want to stay inside or go out, and finds an activity the whole family can do together, with steps in plain sentences. A finder that asks twenty questions before showing anything is a finder that loses the family before the first activity appears. Three plain questions to start, how many people, how much time, and inside or outside, are enough to narrow the field without tiring anyone. Without the three questions, the finder shows everything at once, and a family facing a hundred activities chooses none of them. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. Worked example: in a website built this way, three plain questions to start runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Families have always looked for something to do together, from a walk after lunch to a game on a rainy evening. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a finder that asks three plain questions and shows activities that fit the answers, with steps any family member can follow. Three plain questions are an old habit of good hosts, who have always asked how many, how long, and where before planning a day.

    Proposed solution: Name the family activity and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every family activity the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one family activity without signing in; Check the family activity works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: a three-question finder, an activity library for all ages, steps in plain sentences, and a clear age note on every activity. Nothing else is needed to open the first family activity.
