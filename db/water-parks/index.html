Blizzard Beach Rank 1 · simple · water park · beginner

Blizzard Beach is a water park at Walt Disney World in Florida, opened in 1995, themed as a ski resort that melted into a water park: snow-capped peaks, ski lifts, and alpine lodges surround slides, a lazy river, and a wave pool. The joke of the theme is that a freak snowstorm hit Florida, a ski resort was built, and then the snow melted, leaving the ski infrastructure to become water slides, so chair lifts carry swimmers instead of skiers and the slopes became flumes. Its signature slide, Summit Plummet, drops riders down a near-vertical chute from the top of the mountain. NEW and operating, it remains one of Disney's two water parks and a favorite for families who want big slides with a playful story.

Wet 'n Wild Orlando Rank 15 · complex · water park · advanced

Wet 'n Wild Orlando was a water park in Orlando, Florida, operating from 1977 until 2016, and it was one of the first modern water parks in the United States, founded by SeaWorld's founder as a purpose-built water park rather than a beach or pool with slides added. It pioneered many water park ride types, including early versions of the multi-person raft rides and high-speed slides that later became standard, and it drew Orlando visitors for nearly four decades. The park closed in 2016 and was demolished, and the site was redeveloped. Its place in water park history is secure: it helped invent the modern water park.
