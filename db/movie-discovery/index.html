A Mystery Film About a Small Town Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A movie discovery website: a visitor picks a generic kind of film, such as a mystery film or a film about a long journey, and the site shows illustrative sample films of that kind, each with a plain description of the story shape, the mood, and the length. The site names no real film, copies no real listing, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real film. This record covers A Mystery Film About a Small Town.

A mystery film about a small town gives a viewer a patient puzzle: a missing letter, a quiet street, and a truth found slowly by watching closely. The listing must say what kind of puzzle it is, how long the film runs, and what mood it carries, so a viewer who wants a gentle mystery can find it. Without those plain facts, a viewer who wanted a gentle puzzle may find a loud chase instead, and a loud chase is a poor gift for a quiet wish.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a mystery film about a small town runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Finding a film worth watching is an old habit: a poster outside a cinema, a list in a newspaper, a tip from a friend. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by kind of story and a plain description for every listing. Mystery films about small towns are an old habit, from a village hall screening to a quiet evening film at home.

Proposed solution

Name the film listing and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every film listing the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one film listing without signing in; Check the film listing works the same way on a phone and on a computer.
