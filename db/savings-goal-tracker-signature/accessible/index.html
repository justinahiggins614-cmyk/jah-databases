    The savings goal tracker helps a visitor set a savings target, record what is saved each month, and see how many months remain at the current pace. All figures are educational estimates, never financial advice, and no real account data is ever used. A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Savings Goal With a Target and a Date, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, The Savings Goal Championship Format, in the Signature edition.

    History: Savers have always kept a jar or a notebook for a goal, marking each coin saved toward a holiday or a new bicycle. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a target anyone can see, a monthly record, and a pace that shows how many months remain. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
