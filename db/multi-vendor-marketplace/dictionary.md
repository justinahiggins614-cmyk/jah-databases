# Dictionary entries — The Multi-Vendor Marketplace Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## marketplace

One market that holds many sellers, each with their own stall and listings, under one set of rules, so that buyers can find many sellers in one place.

## vendor

One seller in a marketplace, named with their stall and their listings, so that every good in the market can be traced to the seller who offers it.

## multi-vendor

A market that holds more than one seller, where each seller keeps their own stall, so that the market is many shops under one roof and not one shop alone.

## listing

One good as a vendor offers it in the market, with its description, its price, and its seller named, so that the market's goods can be counted one by one.

## stall

One vendor's place in the marketplace, holding their listings and their name, so that a buyer can find everything one vendor sells in one place.

## commission

The share a marketplace keeper takes from a vendor's sale, stated in the rules before any sale, so that every vendor knows the cost of selling in the market.

## seller rating

The recorded judgement of a vendor's past dealings, counted from buyers' reports by a stated rule, so that a vendor's standing can be read at a glance.

## buyer

A person who seeks goods in a marketplace, named only in their own orders, so that the market can serve them without keeping more about them than the order needs.

## dispute

A disagreement between a buyer and a vendor, recorded with both sides' words, and followed until it is settled, so that no disagreement is left unanswered.

## settlement

The written ending of a dispute, saying what was decided and what each side will do, kept with the dispute it closes, so that the ending can be shown.

## catalogue

The ordered list of everything a marketplace holds, from every vendor, kept current, so that the market shows only what its vendors truly have to sell.

## market keeper

The party that keeps a marketplace, writing its rules and holding its records, so that every vendor and buyer in the market answers to the same rules.
