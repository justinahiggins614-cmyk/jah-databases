JAH-R20-HARV-000001 — Harvest dimension 1 — by energy type

This database harvests collider knowledge "every way"; this record is the energy-type extraction. Every collision in the lane's databases is classified by which energy forms dominate. Kinetic energy: the LHC's 6.8 TeV protons, RHIC's ions, Rutherford's alphas — the collider's native currency. Rest-mass energy: E = mc^2 — the Higgs's 125 GeV, annihilation's 511 keV photons, the top quark's 173 GeV. Thermal energy: the quark-gluon plasma's trillions of kelvin, the Sun's core, supernova shock heating. Electromagnetic energy: synchrotron radiation losses (LEP's 3.5 percent per turn), undulator light sources, the photons of every detector. Nuclear binding energy: fission/fusion energetics, the mass defect behind decay heat. Gravitational potential energy: infall onto the Sun at 617 km/s, accretion onto black holes, tidal heating of Io.

The harvest rule: for each energy type the record lists the lane's collisions where it dominates, the conversion pathways between types (kinetic to thermal in shocks, rest-mass to photons in annihilation, gravitational to kinetic in infall), and the public figures. Cross-links point to the scenario or machine records. Nothing here is new data — it is the same public data, re-indexed so that a reader asking "show me every thermal-energy collision" gets a complete answer.

JAH-R20-HARV-000002 — Harvest dimension 2 — by mass type

The mass-type extraction classifies collisions by what the masses involved are and how mass behaves. Rest mass (invariant mass): the fixed public values — electron 0.511 MeV, proton 938.272 MeV, Higgs 125.1 GeV — from the PDG tables. System invariant mass: the mass of a multi-particle system, which is not the sum of rest masses — a proton's 938 MeV is mostly gluon-field energy, not its quarks' few MeV; this is the public explanation of where mass comes from. Relativistic energy: the historical "relativistic mass" language is retired in this database in favor of total energy E = gamma m c^2, with the note that modern physics uses invariant mass only.

Massless participants: photons and gluons (massless, but carrying energy and momentum — annihilation photons, synchrotron light). Missing mass/energy: neutrinos carrying away undetected energy (the classic beta-decay inference, the LHC's missing-transverse-energy searches). Effective mass in media: quasiparticles in detectors, plasma frequency effects — noted where relevant.

For each mass type the record lists the lane's collisions where it matters: invariant-mass reconstruction is how the Higgs was found (the two-photon invariant mass peak); missing energy is how dark-matter searches proceed; massless-particle kinematics governs every photon in every detector. Same public data, re-indexed by mass behavior.

JAH-R20-HARV-000003 — Harvest dimension 3 — moving vs stationary: frames of reference

The moving/stationary extraction: every collision looks different in different frames, and the database harvests each collision in all its useful frames. Laboratory frame: the target sits still (Rutherford's gold foil, fixed-target experiments, DART's Dimorphos) — simple to build, but much of the energy goes into moving the centre of mass rather than into new physics. Centre-of-mass frame: both beams move (every collider ring) — all the energy is available, which is why colliders beat fixed targets at the same beam energy by orders of magnitude. The public formula: a fixed-target machine needs beam energy scaling as E_cm^2 to match a collider.

Cosmic frames: the CMB rest frame (the universe's own "stationary"), the galactic rest frame, the solar-system barycentre. Relativistic beaming: particles moving near light speed throw their decay products forward (why LHCb is a forward spectrometer, why cosmic-ray air showers are pancake-thin). Time dilation: muons from cosmic rays reach the ground because their 2.2 µs lifetime is stretched ~50x; B mesons at LHCb fly millimetres for the same reason.

For each collision in the lane, this record states the natural frame, the transformation to the lab, and what the frame choice costs or buys. Same public physics, indexed by motion.

JAH-R20-HARV-000004 — Harvest dimension 4 — by environment factor

The environment-factor extraction: no collision happens in a vacuum (sometimes literally not). This record harvests every collision in the lane against the environmental conditions that shaped it, cross-linked to the Environmental Factors Database (item 117) for the full factor listings with ranges.

Vacuum: the LHC beam pipe's ultrahigh vacuum (~10^-10 mbar) versus interstellar space (~10^-16 mbar in the sparsest regions) versus the QGP's perfect-fluid density. Magnetic fields: LHC dipoles at 8.33 T, Earth's 25-65 µT, magnetars at 10^11 T — the field the beams live in determines everything about a ring collider. Temperature: 1.9 K magnet cryogenics, 2.725 K space, trillions of K in heavy-ion collisions. Radiation: the LHC detectors' radiation-hard design doses, Van Allen belt fluxes, cosmic-ray backgrounds in every underground lab. Gravity: free-fall beamlines versus the Sun's 274 m/s^2 surface gravity versus neutron-star 10^12 m/s^2 in merger scenarios.

For each factor the record states the collision's operating point, the factor's range across the lane (pointing to item 117's full tables), and the design consequence: why the LHC needs cryogenics, why underground labs need shielding, why space instruments need radiation hardness. Same public data, indexed by environment.

JAH-R20-HARV-000005 — Harvest dimension 5 — by particle species and interaction

The species-and-interaction extraction: every collision re-indexed by who collides and how. By species: proton-proton (LHC, RHIC polarized), proton-antiproton (Tevatron, SPS), electron-positron (LEP, SLC, B-factories), electron-proton (HERA), electron-ion (EIC), heavy ion (RHIC Au-Au, LHC Pb-Pb), alpha-nucleus (Rutherford), photon-involved (pair production, annihilation), cosmic-ray primaries (protons and nuclei on atmospheric nitrogen/oxygen). By interaction: electromagnetic (scattering, annihilation, synchrotron), strong (hadron collisions, QGP formation), weak (beta decay, neutrino scattering), gravitational (all the cosmic scenarios).

The harvest's value is comparative: electron-positron gives clean events with known initial energy (precision); proton-proton gives the highest energies but messy composite initial states (discovery); heavy ions give extreme energy density (matter phases); electron-ion gives structured probes of composite targets. The record states for each species pair which lane machines use it, at what energies, and what physics it accesses — the complete who-collides-what matrix of the lane.

JAH-R20-HARV-000006 — Harvest dimension 6 — by era, detector, and outcome

The final harvest dimensions slice the lane three more ways. By era: the electrostatic age (1930s), the cyclotron/synchrotron age (1940s-70s), the collider discovery age (1980s-90s: W, Z, top), the precision age (1990s-2000s: LEP, B-factories, HERA), the LHC age (2010s-2020s: Higgs and beyond), and the proposed future (2030s+: HL-LHC, EIC, FCC/ILC/CLIC/CEPC/muon). Each era's headline collisions are listed with public dates.

By detector: tracking (silicon vertices, TPCs), calorimetry (electromagnetic, hadronic), muon systems, Cherenkov/TOF particle ID, and the trigger/DAQ chain — every lane experiment's detector stack decomposed into these functions, so a reader can compare ATLAS's toroids with CMS's solenoid or ALICE's TPC with STAR's.

By outcome: discoveries (W, Z, top, Higgs, QGP liquid, CP violation), precision confirmations (Z width, W mass, parton maps), null results that mattered (no 115 GeV Higgs at LEP, no superpartners yet — each redirecting the field), and open questions (dark matter, matter-antimatter asymmetry, hierarchy). The harvest's promise — "every way" — is kept by these six dimensions jointly: pick any angle, the database answers.
