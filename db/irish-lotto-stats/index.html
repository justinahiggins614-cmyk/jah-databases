Irish Lotto Game Overview and Current Matrix

Irish Lotto is the national lottery draw game of Ireland, operated by the National Lottery in Ireland. A player selects six numbers from 1 to 45 on a line, and a draw produces six main numbers plus a bonus number drawn from the balls that remain. Matching all six main numbers wins the jackpot. A line costs 2 euro, and a minimum of two lines is required, so the minimum entry is 4 euro.

The current matrix is 6 from 45 with a bonus number. The jackpot starts at 2 million euro and is capped at 16 million euro. Draws take place on Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday evenings. The bonus number matters only for prize tiers that include it, and it is drawn from the same pool after the six main numbers, not chosen separately by the player.

Irish Lotto Exact Jackpot Odds

The exact jackpot odds for the current Irish Lotto matrix follow directly from combinations. Choosing 6 numbers from 45 gives 8,145,060 possible lines, so one line has a 1 in 8,145,060 chance of matching all six main numbers. That figure is exact arithmetic, not an estimate, and it applies to a single line in a single draw.

The former 6 from 47 matrix gave 10,737,573 possible lines, so the September 2026 reduction from 47 balls to 45 balls shortened the jackpot odds from 1 in 10,737,573 to 1 in 8,145,060 per line. Buying extra lines adds chances in proportion to the number of distinct lines bought, and the cost rises by the same proportion. No selection of numbers is more likely than any other selection.

Irish Lotto All Prize Tier Odds

Irish Lotto has eight prize tiers. The jackpot tier is six main numbers. Below it, tiers reward five main numbers with the bonus, five main numbers without the bonus, four with the bonus, four without, three with the bonus, three without, and two main numbers with the bonus. Lower tiers pay fixed amounts, while higher tiers are shared or variable under the published prize structure, so the demo below lists odds rather than prize amounts for the shared tiers.

The tier odds are computed by counting, for a fixed ticket line, how many possible draws produce each match pattern, divided into the total number of possible draws including the bonus ball. The overall chance of any prize on one line is about 1 in 25. That overall figure is the sum of the eight tier chances, and it does not mean a prize is expected within any particular number of plays.

Irish Lotto Draw Mechanics and Schedule

Irish Lotto draws take place on Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday evenings at about 7:55 pm Irish time, with entries closing at 7:45 pm on draw nights. Six main balls are drawn first, then one bonus ball is drawn from the balls that remain. The bonus ball can only upgrade a tier that already matches five, four, three, or two main numbers, depending on the tier table; it cannot create a jackpot on its own.

Draws are broadcast on television in Ireland. Results are published through the official lottery channels after each draw. A ticket must be checked against the official result for the draw it entered, because numbers, prize amounts, and rollover states change from draw to draw. Advance entry for several draws is offered through official channels, and each draw on an advance ticket is a separate draw with its own result.

Irish Lotto Matrix History

Irish Lotto launched in 1988 as a 6 from 36 game. The pool was widened over the following years, and the game became a 6 from 47 game in September 2015. On Saturday 5 September 2026 the pool was reduced from 47 balls to 45 balls, retiring numbers 46 and 47, and a third weekly draw was added on Monday 7 September 2026, alongside the existing Wednesday and Saturday draws.

The September 2026 change also lowered the jackpot cap from 19.06 million euro to a fixed cap of 16 million euro, raised the Lotto Plus 2 top prize from 250,000 euro to 1 million euro, and removed the Lotto Plus Raffle from the game. Ticket prices did not change in that refresh. Older results, odds tables, and prize lists from before 5 September 2026 describe the 6 from 47 game and must not be mixed with results from the current 6 from 45 game.

Irish Lotto Record Jackpots and the Jackpot Cap

This record does not present a complete ranked list of record jackpots, because a complete verified list was not assembled at build time. It records the cap mechanics that govern large jackpots and one verified capped-jackpot event. When the jackpot reaches its cap and is not won, a Will-Be-Won draw is required within five draws after the cap is reached, so that the jackpot is paid out rather than rolling indefinitely.

In January 2022, after a long capped rollover sequence, a Will-Be-Won draw was held on Saturday 15 January 2022, and a family syndicate from County Mayo matched all six numbers to win a 19,060,800 euro jackpot outright. That amount was the then all-time high figure cited for the capped jackpot before the September 2026 cap was fixed at 16 million euro. Any larger claim about record jackpots should be checked against the official result archive before it is added to this database.

Irish Lotto Plus 1 as a Related Game

Lotto Plus 1 is an optional companion draw played with the same numbers as the main Irish Lotto line, for an extra 1 euro per line. It takes place after the main draw, with its own draw of six numbers from the same 1 to 45 pool plus a bonus number. A player cannot enter Lotto Plus 1 without entering the main Lotto draw, and the same line is entered into both Plus draws when the Plus option is added.

The Lotto Plus 1 top prize is 1 million euro for matching all six numbers. There is no rollover in Lotto Plus 1, so its top prize stays fixed from draw to draw. Its tier odds under the current 6 from 45 matrix match the main-game tier odds, because the matrix and the bonus mechanism are the same, even though its prize amounts and rollover rules differ from the main game.

Irish Lotto Plus 2 as a Related Game

Lotto Plus 2 is the second optional companion draw, entered together with Lotto Plus 1 through the single Plus option on a Lotto ticket. It also uses the player's main Lotto numbers in a separate draw of six numbers from 1 to 45 plus a bonus number, giving the same line a third independent chance at a top prize in the same draw night.

From September 2026 the Lotto Plus 2 top prize is 1 million euro, raised from 250,000 euro, so a Lotto with Plus entry has three separate 1 million euro top-prize chances per draw night: the main jackpot, which starts at 2 million euro and can grow, plus the two fixed 1 million euro Plus top prizes. Prize amounts for lower Plus 2 tiers are fixed under the published Plus tables and should be confirmed against the current official table before any amount is quoted.

Irish Lotto Plus Raffle as a Historical Record

The Lotto Plus Raffle was a historical companion element, not a current game. When a player added the Plus option, each line received an automatically generated raffle code of four numbers. One code was drawn as the winner on draw nights, and a matching code won 500 euro in a regular draw. Codes were not unique, so a drawn code typically produced many winners, with contemporary reports describing around 80 to 120 winners in most draws depending on ticket sales.

The raffle was removed from the game in the September 2026 refresh, and current Lotto with Plus entries no longer include a raffle code. This record is kept for historical completeness: raffle codes, raffle prize amounts, and raffle winner counts from before September 2026 must not be presented as current rules, and no current ticket should be described as carrying a raffle entry.

Irish Lotto Frequency Method and Simulated Set Demo

The frequency method counts how often each number has appeared in past draws and ranks numbers from most frequent to least frequent. It is a descriptive summary of history: it answers the question of what has been drawn before, and it says nothing about what will be drawn next, because each draw is an independent random selection from the same pool.

Irish Lotto Hot Numbers Method

The hot numbers method selects numbers that have appeared most often in a chosen window of recent draws. Supporters of the method describe hot numbers as numbers on a run, but the draw mechanism has no memory: the balls are not weighted by past appearances, and a number that appeared often in the last fifty draws has exactly the same chance as any other number in the next draw.

A hot list is therefore a report about a chosen window, and different windows give different hot lists. Changing the window, adding or removing a few draws, or starting the window one draw later can move a number on or off the list. The demo below illustrates that sensitivity on simulated data: it ranks simulated numbers over two different windows and shows that the rankings disagree. Irish Lotto Cold and Overdue Numbers Method

The cold and overdue method selects numbers that have appeared least often, or that have gone longest without appearing, on the theory that such numbers are due to appear. That theory is the gambler's fallacy applied to a lottery: a random draw does not compensate for past absences, and a number that has been absent for many draws is no more likely in the next draw than a number drawn in the previous draw.

Overdue counts are still useful as descriptive statistics. They show gaps in a chosen history, and long gaps are expected in random data: with 45 numbers and six drawn per draw, a typical number is absent from most draws, so gaps of many draws occur routinely without any mechanism behind them. Irish Lotto Rollover Patterns

A rollover happens when no ticket matches all six main numbers, so the jackpot amount is carried into the next draw and the jackpot grows. Rollover length is therefore a record of how many consecutive draws produced no jackpot winner. Because each draw has the same jackpot chance per line, a long rollover is not evidence that a jackpot is due; the next draw has the same per-line chance as the draw that started the sequence.

Two structural facts shape Irish rollovers. First, the number of lines sold affects the chance that at least one line wins: more distinct lines sold means a higher chance that some line matches, even though each individual line keeps the same 1 in 8,145,060 chance. Second, the cap rule bounds the sequence: once the jackpot reaches its 16 million euro cap, a Will-Be-Won draw must follow within five draws. Irish Lotto Expected Value, General Educational Only

Expected value is the average prize value of a line over a very large number of identical plays, computed as the sum over prize tiers of tier chance multiplied by tier prize, minus the price of the line. It is a teaching tool for comparing games and for showing why a lottery line, on average, returns less than its price: the prize fund is a fraction of total sales, and the jackpot is shared when more than one line matches.

Irish Lotto Verification Guide

A ticket should be verified against the official result for its own draw, not against a summary, a prediction list, or a result from another draw night. The official channels are the National Lottery in Ireland and the television broadcast of the draw. The draw night, the six main numbers, the bonus number, and the Plus draws each need to be checked separately, because a line can win in a Plus draw while winning nothing in the main draw, and vice versa.

Three checks prevent the most common errors. First, confirm the matrix: results from before 5 September 2026 belong to the 6 from 47 game, and results from that date onward belong to the 6 from 45 game. Second, confirm the draw date and the draw night, since Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday draws are separate. Third, keep the ticket until any prize claim is settled, and confirm claim deadlines and claim routes through official channels before acting on any prize claim.

Irish Lotto Fallacies and Comparison with UK Lotto

Common lottery fallacies all share one error: treating independent random draws as if they had memory, balance, or intent. Numbers are not due after an absence, hot numbers are not on a run, a pattern of past draws does not make a combination more or less likely, and buying a line that looks random does not change its chance relative to a line that looks patterned. Every line in a given draw has the same chance, and the only factor that changes a player's total chance is the number of distinct lines held.

For comparison, UK Lotto uses a 6 from 59 matrix, giving 45,057,474 possible lines and jackpot odds of 1 in 45,057,474 per line, which is much longer than Irish Lotto at 1 in 8,145,060 per line under the current 6 from 45 matrix. The comparison is about matrix size only: a shorter jackpot odds figure does not make any particular line likely, and it does not change the fact that each draw is random.
