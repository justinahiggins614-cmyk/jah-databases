1. Overview

The bonobo (Pan paniscus) has its own mathematics, its own science, and its own court, the "Peep" court. This website is the per-animal website for the bonobo, built first, before the networks, under humanity, under God. It holds 24 public records and 24 Signature records, in three ladders of eight: mathematics and measurement; science, evolution, and court; Signature, Light, and human translation.

Archive records: 48 (24 public + 24 Signature).

The bonobo lives in the rainforests of the Congo Basin, south of the Congo River, eats fruit, leaves, seeds, and small animals, and lives in a peaceful community led by females, held together by close social bonds. It is about 30 to 45 kilograms and stands about 1.0 to 1.2 metres tall when standing. Its characteristic sound is the soft peep call, a high short sound used in close social contact.

5. Math Proof

The bonobo is measurable in its own system. Proof by calculation: one mouse lemur unit is 60 grams. An adult male bonobo of about 39 kilograms is about 650 mouse lemur units. An adult female of about 33 kilograms is about 550 mouse lemur units. A human of 70 kilograms is about 1166 mouse lemur units. The translation is checked both ways: a number translated into human units and back returns to the number it started from. The probabilistic behaviour model gives chances with counted ranges, and the evolutionary cascade measures the ancestor in the same units.
