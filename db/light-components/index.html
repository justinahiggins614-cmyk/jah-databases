JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000001 — Light Computer Components — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A light computer — a photonic computer — computes with photons instead of electrons. Its components are the optical analogs of wires and transistors: lasers (light sources), waveguides (light wires), modulators (light switches), and photodetectors (light sensors). Photonic computing is real and active: companies ship optical AI accelerators, and silicon photonics puts lasers and waveguides on chips using semiconductor fabs. Light brings speed (photons move at light speed), parallelism (many wavelengths in one fiber), and low heat (photons barely interact).

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000002 — Light Computer Components — Lasers and light sources

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Computation needs controlled light: semiconductor diode lasers (cheap, chip-scale, the workhorse), vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers or VCSELs (arrays of thousands on one chip), and frequency combs (lasers emitting hundreds of equally spaced wavelengths — a rainbow ruler, Nobel recognized in 2005). Wavelength choice matters: 1550 nanometres rides telecom fiber with minimal loss; visible wavelengths suit free-space optics. The source piece specifies wavelength, power, linewidth, and stability.

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000003 — Light Computer Components — Waveguides: wires made of light paths

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Waveguides confine light by total internal reflection: a high-index core in a low-index cladding, exactly like fiber optics shrunk onto a chip. Silicon waveguides (on silicon-on-insulator wafers) carry 1550-nm light with losses under 1 dB per centimetre; silicon nitride guides visible light. Bends, splitters, and crossings route light the way copper traces route electrons. The waveguide piece is the light computer's wiring harness, and its loss budget sets the machine's maximum size.

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000004 — Light Computer Components — Modulators: the light switches

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Modulators encode data onto light by varying its amplitude, phase, or frequency. The main families: electro-optic (electric field changes refractive index — lithium niobate and silicon photonic modulators), thermo-optic (heat tunes the index, slow but simple), and emerging phase-change materials. Speeds reach tens of gigahertz commercially. The modulator is the photonic transistor: it is where electrical control meets optical signal, and arrays of them are the computer's logic fabric.

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000005 — Light Computer Components — Photodetectors: light back to signal

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Detectors convert photons to current: PIN photodiodes (simple, fast), avalanche photodiodes (internal gain, more sensitive), and superconducting nanowire detectors (single-photon sensitivity, used in quantum optics). Bandwidths reach tens of gigahertz; responsivity is measured in amps per watt. Every computation ends at a detector, so detector noise sets the machine's precision — the optical analog of a voltmeter's accuracy.

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000007 — Light Computer Components — Loss, noise, and the power budget

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Light computing is analog at heart, and analog means budgets: every splitter halves power, every centimetre of waveguide shaves decibels, detector noise floors the precision. The power budget adds it all: source power minus losses must exceed detector sensitivity with margin. Designers choose bit-depth by physics — 8-bit precision is easy, 16-bit is heroic. This honest arithmetic is the component piece's most valuable content: it says what light computers can and cannot do.

JAH-PHOTON-COMP-000008 — Light Computer Components — Components in the full stack

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Components feed the light tools and the logic fabric: sources feed modulators, waveguides connect everything, detectors close the loops. Interfaces are optical (wavelength, power, mode) and electrical (drive volts, bias current). Beginner exercise: compute the loss of a 5-centimetre silicon waveguide at 1 dB/cm plus two 3-dB splitters (11 dB total — about 8 percent of the light survives). That single number teaches why integration and short paths matter.
