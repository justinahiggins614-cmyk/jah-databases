The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Discount List for One Kind of Good, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Checklist With Review Before Launch, in the Signature edition.

    History: Watching for a discount is an old shopping habit: a shopper who waits for the sale season, a stallholder who lowers a price at the end of the day, a neighbor who passes on the news of a bargain. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a daily watch, a best day to buy, and a watch list a shopper can return to. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

    Science proof: total = count x each. In plain words, this equation turns counted quantities into one checkable number: with 23 items at 1 each, the arithmetic gives 23 records, and the demo prints that same total, so the claim can be checked by arithmetic alone.

    Math proof: Worked arithmetic: 23 x 1 = 23. The demo sets count to 23 and each to 1, multiplies them, and prints Total: 23, so a fresh run of the demo verifies this proof exactly.
