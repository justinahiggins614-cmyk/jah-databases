The Deuterium-Tritium Fusion Reaction JAH-FUF-000001 · simple

The deuterium-tritium (D-T) reaction fuses two hydrogen isotopes into helium and a neutron, releasing 17.6 MeV of energy per reaction — millions of times more per atom than burning coal.

The deuterium-tritium (D-T) reaction fuses two hydrogen isotopes into helium and a neutron, releasing 17.6 MeV of energy per reaction — millions of times more per atom than burning coal. D-T is the easiest fusion reaction to ignite, which is why every major fusion programme uses it, and its neutron carries 80% of the energy, which must be captured as heat and used to breed more tritium from lithium.

print('Fusion power: %.1f MW' % P_MW)

The Lawson Criterion JAH-FUF-000002 · simple

The Lawson criterion, set out by John Lawson in 1957, states the condition a fusion plasma must meet to produce net energy: the product of density, temperature and confinement time must exceed a threshold.

The Lawson criterion, set out by John Lawson in 1957, states the condition a fusion plasma must meet to produce net energy: the product of density, temperature and confinement time must exceed a threshold. For D-T at about 10-20 keV, the triple product must reach roughly 3 x 10^21 keV s/m^3. Temperature, density and confinement must be achieved together; a hot thin plasma or a dense cold one both fail.

print('Triple product: %.2e keV s / m^3' % triple)
print('Lawson D-T target ~3e21: %s' % ('met' if triple >= 3e21 else 'below target'))

Magnetic Confinement: the Tokamak JAH-FUF-000003 · simple

A tokamak confines plasma in a doughnut-shaped magnetic field, with a strong toroidal field plus a poloidal field made by current flowing in the plasma itself.

A tokamak confines plasma in a doughnut-shaped magnetic field, with a strong toroidal field plus a poloidal field made by current flowing in the plasma itself. It is the most developed fusion concept: JET in Britain held the fusion energy record for decades, and ITER in France is the largest tokamak ever built, designed to produce 500 MW of fusion power. Tokamaks must control disruptions, sudden losses of confinement that can damage the walls.

A stellarator confines plasma with twisted magnetic coils alone, without driving current through the plasma, so it can run steadily instead of in pulses and avoids tokamak disruptions. Inertial confinement fusion crushes a millimetre-sized fuel pellet with intense lasers, compressing it to stellar densities for a billionth of a second. Plasma beta is the ratio of plasma pressure to magnetic pressure, and it measures how efficiently a magnetic field confines a plasma. High beta means more fusion power from the same magnets, but beta cannot grow without limit: above a stability threshold, instabilities destroy confinement. Tokamaks typically operate at a few percent beta; spherical tokamaks and stellarators aim higher, trading stability for efficiency.

Plasma Instabilities JAH-FUF-000016 · advanced

Plasma is prone to instabilities: kink modes that bend the column, tearing modes that break magnetic surfaces into islands, and edge-localized modes that spit bursts of heat at the walls.

Plasma is prone to instabilities: kink modes that bend the column, tearing modes that break magnetic surfaces into islands, and edge-localized modes that spit bursts of heat at the walls. Each is understood in theory and diagnosed by magnetic sensors, microwaves and cameras, and each is controlled by feedback — small correction fields, pellet injection or profile shaping. Instability control is routine operation on every tokamak, and it is also the main reason disruptions remain a risk.

Wendelstein 7-X JAH-FUF-000023 · intermediate

Wendelstein 7-X in Greifswald, Germany, is the world's largest stellarator, with 50 superconducting coils wound into complex shapes that took years to manufacture.

Wendelstein 7-X in Greifswald, Germany, is the world's largest stellarator, with 50 superconducting coils wound into complex shapes that took years to manufacture. It began operation in 2015 and has run plasmas for tens of seconds at high performance, demonstrating that stellarators can confine hot plasma steadily. Its 2023 campaigns reached record triple products for a stellarator, supporting the case for steady-state fusion power.
