## 1. Description and history — what the mix is

The Complete Grinder System is a mix of two records about the same supply: the public Manual Herb Grinder (JAH-MJS-000001), the real, fact-checked record of a common 63 mm four-piece aluminium grinder, and the Signature Helix Grinder (JAH-MJSS-000001), a Signature-made design study that reworks the same form with one deliberate change — a helical tooth ring. The mix keeps the public record's four-piece layout (grind, store, sieve, collect, with a 100-micron kief screen) and takes the Signature design's helical tooth ring and larger body size, so the mixed grinder grinds with a spiral tooth advance instead of a flat tooth pass, and collects through the public form's kief screen.

**How the two editions combine:** the public edition contributes the proven layout and the numbers that anchor it (63 mm diameter, 28 mm chamber, 49 teeth, 56 mm screen, 12 turns in the worked example); the Signature edition contributes the helical ring (teeth set on a shallow spiral so one twist advances herb steadily downward through the grinding plate instead of trapping it at the rim), the ceramic-coated grinding plate, and the larger body (70 mm diameter, 30 mm chamber, 56 teeth, 62 mm screen, 8 turns). The mix does not average the two records: at each scale it takes the public layout and the Signature mechanism, and the dimensions follow the Signature body at standard scale because the helical ring needs the extra chamber depth (30 mm vs 28 mm) to advance herb downward.

**History of the mix:** the public form's history runs from the 1905 Wingfield & Balding grinding device through the 1960s popularisation to the modern four-piece aluminium grinder (see the file website). The Signature Helix Grinder has no manufacturing history — it is a design study made in 2026 for this database — and the mix itself was made in this pilot (2026-11-08) as the website-per-artifact program's first mix website. Its history is therefore short and fully documented: two records, one deliberate change, three scales.

## 3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

**Standard math — the mix, worked at all three scales.** The same cylinder formula both source records use:

V = π · (d / 2)² · h ;  A_screen = π · (d_screen / 2)² ;  cutting passes = teeth × turns

| Scale | d (mm) | h (mm) | V (mm³) | V (cm³) | Screen (mm²) | Teeth/cm² | Passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 Basic | 40 | 14 | 17,592.92 | 17.59 | 1,017.88 | 1.91 | 240 |
| A2 Improved | 70 | 30 | 115,453.53 | 115.45 | 3,019.07 | 1.46 | 448 |
| A3 Best | 100 | 40 | 314,159.27 | 314.16 | 6,361.73 | 1.15 | 540 |

**Worked example (A2, step by step):** r = 70 / 2 = 35.0 mm; V = π · 35.0² · 30.0 = 115,453.53 mm³ = 115.45 cm³; A_screen = π · 31.0² = 3,019.07 mm²; teeth density = 56 ÷ (π · 35.0² / 100) = 1.46 per cm²; cutting passes = 56 × 8 = 448.

**The mix's gain, computed against the two source records' own chambers:**

- Public A2 chamber: 87,282.87 mm³. Mixed A2 chamber: 115,453.53 mm³. **Gain over the public record: +32.28%.**
- Signature A2 chamber: 115,453.53 mm³. Mixed A2 vs Signature: **0.00%** — the mix takes the Signature body size at standard scale, so the volume gain over the Signature design is zero by construction; the mix's improvement over the Signature design is not volume but the public four-piece layout (a separate kief chamber and the magnet lid), which the Signature record's own mechanism does not describe.
- Total cutting passes across the ladder: 240 + 448 + 540 = **1,228**.

The mix demo (`demos/grinder_mix_demo.py`) computes all of these numbers, runs twice, and prints matching SHA-256 digests (see Section 6). Every number above is reproducible with a pocket calculator from the table in Section 1.

## 4. Science proof

The mix's physics is the sum of its two parents' physics, and the combination changes one thing: the tooth path.

- **From the public record — shear and sieving:** opposing teeth cut dried flower along its natural fracture planes by shear, not crushing. Ground particles fall through sized holes by gravity once smaller than the hole diameter. The 100-micron mesh is a sieve: particles finer than 100 microns pass through, larger ground herb stays above. Aluminium 6061 is light, machines cleanly, and its anodised surface resists corrosion from plant resins. The magnet lid keeps the halves aligned so the teeth never clash metal on metal.
- **From the Signature design — helical advance:** teeth set on a shallow spiral advance herb steadily downward through the grinding plate with each twist, instead of trapping it at the rim. That is why the mixed standard needs only 8 turns where the public worked example uses 12: each turn does more of the work, because the spiral moves material as it cuts. The ceramic-coated grinding plate lowers friction further, so the twist stays smooth as the chamber fills.
- **Why the combination works (pilot reasoning, stated as reasoning, not as a tested claim):** the helical ring changes the direction of travel, not the cutting action — the teeth still shear, the holes still size the particles, the mesh still sieves. The mix therefore inherits both parents' checks: measure the parts, apply the formulas in Section 3, run the demo, and the results should agree within the program's rounding. The mixed design has not been built or tested; the science proof here is a design argument from the two records' stated physics, and it is labeled as such. It makes no claim about health, safety, or benefit, and gives no advice about how much of anything to use.

## 5. Spec / patent + 3D-print version

**Spec:** the mixed specification is the table in Section 2 (materials, dimensions, mechanism, use, care), synthesized from the two records. **Patent:** the public form's documented origin is the 1905 Wingfield & Balding grinding device; the Signature helical ring is a design study with no patent filing, and this page claims none. **Build it yourself (pilot extension, honestly labeled):** a metal mixed grinder cannot be made at home — machining a helical tooth ring in anodised aluminium is a machine-shop job. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought kit in the mixed layout: buy a four-piece grinder body in the 70 mm size and a helical-ring grinding plate in the matching diameter, check the fit dry before any load goes in, confirm the ring's teeth clear the plate holes and the magnet faces meet flush, and make a wooden stand (circular recess the diameter of the foot, cork-lined) as on the file website. Check the fit dry, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage.

**3D-print directions (pilot extension — actual directions a printer can use):** the metal mixed grinder is not home-3D-printable — no home filament prints anodised aluminium, a stainless mesh, or a ceramic-coated plate, and printed plastic teeth wear flat quickly and cannot hold the 100-micron sieve tolerance. The printable items are the protective stand/cradle, the lid grip, and a helical-ring display model (a non-working model of the ring, for study only). Print them in PETG, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 perimeters, and 25 percent gyroid infill. Print the cradle flat on its bottom face with no supports; print the lid grip on its side with supports under the overhang; print the ring model flat with supports under the spiral overhang. Print time (ESTIMATE): 2 hours 40 minutes for the cradle, 35 minutes for the lid grip, and 1 hour 50 minutes for the ring display model, at the settings given. Unmodelled factors that change real print time and strength are wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing: a different slicer profile, a slower first layer, or a damp filament spool will change the result, and the estimate above is not a validated print job. A printed grinder body or ring is a display model only, not a working grinder, and must not be presented as equivalent to the metal supply. Check each finished accessory against the piece it serves before use, and reprint it if the fit is loose.
