Zamzam Well Overview Rank 1 · simple

The Zamzam Well is a well within the Grand Mosque complex in Mecca, about 20 metres east of the Kaaba according to published references, and it is the source of Zamzam water, which pilgrims drink and carry home. The well is described in published technical summaries as about 30 metres deep and 1.08 to 2.66 metres in diameter, tapping groundwater from the Wadi Ibrahim alluvium and from bedrock below. Water was formerly drawn with ropes and buckets, and since 1964 the well opening has been in a basement room inaccessible to the public, where it can be seen behind glass panels. This record is a fact record about a physical well and its documented dimensions and arrangements; the traditional account of the well's origin is tradition, and it is told as tradition in the history record of this pair, not blended into the measurements here.

Location Within the Grand Mosque Rank 2 · simple

The Zamzam Well stands inside the precinct of the Grand Mosque in Mecca, about 20 metres east of the Kaaba, and its opening was moved to a basement about 2.5 metres deep so that space above ground could be freed for pilgrims, according to published references. Zamzam water is distributed at points throughout the holy mosques, in cooled and uncooled dispensing containers and fountains for visitors, so that a pilgrim need not go to the well itself to obtain it. The well's position, close to the Kaaba yet below ground and out of the main circulation, reflects a long history of fitting a working well into a mosque that must also hold very large crowds. This record is a fact record about location and arrangement, and it gives distances as published approximations, since the mosque has been rebuilt and extended many times and any single measurement is tied to a particular layout.

FROM_LON = 39.8262

MECCA_LON = 39.8262
R = 6371.0
p1 = math.radians(FROM_LAT); p2 = math.radians(MECCA_LAT)
dp = math.radians(MECCA_LAT - FROM_LAT); dl = math.radians(MECCA_LON - FROM_LON)
a = math.sin(dp/2)**2 + math.cos(p1)*math.cos(p2)*math.sin(dl/2)**2
dist = 2*R*math.asin(math.sqrt(a))
y = math.sin(dl)*math.cos(p2); x = math.cos(p1)*math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1)*math.cos(p2)*math.cos(dl)
bearing = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360) % 360
print('Place: ' + TITLE)
print('From: ' + FROM_CITY)
print('Great-circle distance to Mecca: %.1f km' % dist)
print('Initial bearing to Mecca: %.1f degrees' % bearing)
print('Method: haversine great-circle arithmetic on published coordinates; road distance differs.')

History: Tradition and Documented History Rank 3 · simple

This record keeps two kinds of history apart, because the Zamzam pair must never blend them. In Islamic tradition, the well is associated with Hagar and her infant son Ismail, who in the traditional account searched for water in the valley of Mecca, and the well's name is traditionally explained from words spoken to stop its flowing; this is tradition, told here as tradition, and it is not presented as a measured historical fact. The documented history, by contrast, is that the well has been in use for centuries, was expanded by hand over the years, was at times dry or buried in sand, and in the modern period was fitted with electric pumps, a basement well house, and a treatment and distribution plant. Both histories belong in this pair, but they are labelled differently, and the reader is told which is which in every sentence that could be misread.

Geology and Aquifer Rank 4 · simple

The Zamzam aquifer is fed by rainfall on the hills surrounding Mecca, which drains into the Wadi Ibrahim valley floor and infiltrates through layers of sand and gravel that act as a natural preliminary filter before the water reaches deeper storage. The upper part of the well, about 13.5 metres according to published hydrogeological summaries, is excavated in the Wadi Ibrahim alluvium, unconsolidated sandy gravels and silts, and below that the well taps bedrock, so the water comes from two connected stores, the alluvium and the fractured rock. The Saudi Geological Survey has a Zamzam studies programme, reflecting the well's importance and the need to monitor a heavily used urban aquifer. This record is a fact record about published hydrogeology; where a thickness or depth is approximate, the record says so, because the well has been hand-expanded and reworked over a long period.

Well Depth and Structure Rank 5 · simple

Published technical summaries describe the Zamzam Well as about 30 metres, or about 100 feet, deep, and 1.08 to 2.66 metres in diameter, with some published studies reporting 30.5 metres and others 35 metres, so this record gives the range rather than a single exact depth. The variation is understandable: the well was expanded by hand over many years, its upper section is in alluvium and its lower section in bedrock, and different surveys measured different points and different layouts. The well's structure is therefore best described as a hand-dug shaft, widened and deepened over time, with a modern basement well house and pumping arrangements at the top, rather than as a machine-drilled borehole of uniform diameter. This record is a fact record, and it states the range and the reason for the range, which is more honest than a false precision.

Water Extraction and Distribution Rank 6 · simple

Zamzam water is moved by two electric pumps operating alternately, at a published pace of between 11 and 18.5 litres per second, about 5 kilometres southwards to the King Abdullah bin Abdulaziz Zamzam Water Project in Kudai, which opened in September 2010 and cost a published 700 million Saudi riyals to construct. At Kudai the water is treated, using filters and ultraviolet light, stored, and distributed, and it is operated by the National Water Company of Saudi Arabia according to published references. From there it reaches dispensing points in the holy mosques and a bottling and public distribution centre outside the mosque for pilgrims who wish to carry it home. This record is a fact record about a documented engineering system; pumping rates are given as a published range because the pumps operate alternately and rates vary with demand and management.

Published Water-Quality Studies Rank 7 · compounded

Published water-quality studies of Zamzam water report its mineral composition and compare it with drinking-water guidelines, and this record reports what those studies found without adding any medical claim. A study published in 2011 examined 30 samples for metals, cations, anions, and other parameters, and reported average concentrations within World Health Organization permissible limits for all parameters except four, arsenic, nitrate, calcium, and potassium, whose averages were higher, at 27 micrograms per litre for arsenic and 120, 75, and 45 milligrams per litre for nitrate, calcium, and potassium respectively. Other published comparative studies report an alkaline pH of about 7.9 to 8.0 and total dissolved solids around 669 milligrams per litre, below the WHO maximum of 1,000 but above a stricter 500 milligram guideline used by some agencies. This record makes no claim that Zamzam water cures, treats, or prevents any condition, and it reports study results as dated published findings, not as this database's own testing.

Monitoring Rank 11 · compounded

The Zamzam aquifer is monitored by the Saudi Geological Survey through its Zamzam studies programme, which reflects the need to watch water levels, quality, and pumping in a heavily used urban aquifer that also carries religious significance. Monitoring matters because the well's history includes periods when it became dry or was buried in sand, and because heavy pumping can lower water levels, a process described in published hydrogeological studies as overdrafting. Published monitoring therefore tracks the balance between recharge from rainfall on the surrounding hills and withdrawal by pumping, so that extraction can be managed rather than left to run unchecked. This record is a fact record about the existence and purpose of monitoring; it does not publish monitoring data of its own, and where such data is not in the public record consulted, the record says so.

Glossary: Zamzam Terms Rank 13 · complex

This glossary defines the words used across the Zamzam pair, in plain language. An aquifer is a body of rock or sediment that stores and transmits groundwater. Alluvium is sand, gravel, and silt deposited by flowing water, and in Wadi Ibrahim it forms the upper store of the Zamzam aquifer. Bedrock is the solid rock beneath the alluvium, here the fractured basement rock that forms the lower store. Total dissolved solids means the combined dissolved mineral content of water, reported in milligrams per litre. Ultraviolet treatment means disinfecting water with ultraviolet light, used at Kudai alongside filtration. This record is a fact record of standard technical usage, and where a term also has a traditional use, the glossary says so and points to the tradition record for that meaning.
