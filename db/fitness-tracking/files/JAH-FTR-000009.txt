History
People have counted their exercise since the first athletes kept tallies of laps run and weights lifted, first in notebooks of miles walked and hours swum, then in charts of times and distances, and now in trackers that hold the step count, the workout record, the route, and the weekly summary side by side. Fitness tracking is the writing down of what the body did, when, and for how long, so that the exercise of a life can be read as a line. From the paper diary kept by the door to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the exercise down whole. This database holds records and general guidance only, and gives no medical advice. It is built on that public practice of recording every fitness tracking record true.
