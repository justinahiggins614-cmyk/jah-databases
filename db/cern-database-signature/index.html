This Signature-layer record mirrors the public record "CERN — the organization". The public facts — founded 1954, twelve founding states, Franco-Swiss site, 23 member states, the Web's invention — belong to the public record. This layer adds original Signature analysis.

Signature analysis: CERN's institutional design is itself a technology worth studying. Its founding convention solved a coordination problem — pooling the cost of instruments no single nation could afford — with a governance trick: open publication as the default, so every member's scientists benefit whether or not their nation built the machine. That design choice is why a public CERN database is even possible. The Signature completion here maps each CERN governance feature (member-state council, open data, rotating leadership) to a reusable pattern for any large scientific collaboration. Signature analysis: the WLCG is the largest working example of federated computing — 170+ centres, 40+ countries, no single owner of the whole — and it works because the workload (independent collision events) is embarrassingly parallel and the trust model (X.509 certificates, virtual organizations) was designed before "zero trust" was a buzzword.
