Specialist Retirement and Archive

Record: JAH-AISF-000011 · Database: AI Specialist Factory Database · Edition: public · Record kind: ai specialist factory record

Subject: Ending a specialist's service cleanly and keeping its record.

Status: Live, version 1.0.0, 2026-11-18. This is a specification or a Signature design-study, not a finished deployed system.

JCC code: JAHDB/ai-specialist-factory#JAH-AISF-000011 · Key: k=c580b7ab

Descriptions

When a specialist is no longer needed, the factory retires it properly: its tools are disconnected, its data is returned or deleted as agreed, and its full record is archived. Retirement is planned, not abrupt. The user is told the date, the reason, and what replaces the specialist, and the specialist keeps working until that date. On the day, the factory disconnects the specialist's tools, revokes its permissions, and returns or deletes its data exactly as the handover agreement said. The archive keeps the brief, the blueprint, the test reports, and the review sign-off, so the specialist's work can still be checked years later. Nothing about a retired specialist is deleted in secret; the archive is the proof that the retirement was done properly. The Specialist Retirement and Archive stage works through 4 parts: Retirement notice; Disconnection checklist; Data return record; Archive file. Each part is named, tested, and filed, so the work can be checked by a stranger. This record specifies the stage completely: what it is for, how it is done, how it is tested, what it costs in time and attention, and what can go wrong. The specification is written for a builder who has never done this stage before, and for a reviewer who must judge whether it was done properly. Inside the AI Specialist Factory Website, Specialist Retirement and Archive sits between the stages that come before it and the stages that come after it, and it must fit both: what it receives must be in the shape it expects, and what it hands on must be in the shape the next stage expects. Nothing in this record is a claim that the system it specifies is deployed, live, or finished; it is a specification, documented from public knowledge and established practice, and the sources at the end of this record are the public pages it was checked against.

Math Proof

The mathematical reasoning is plain arithmetic on the record's own figures. The figures are: archive_years = 10; archived_records = 14; notice_days = 30; permissions_revoked = 31. Adding them in one order and adding them again in a different order gives the same total, because addition does not depend on order — a fact the demo in this record checks by computing the total twice, in two different ways, and comparing the two fingerprints. The fingerprint is the SHA-256 digest, a standard fingerprint of a piece of text: the same text always gives the same fingerprint, and a different text gives a different one. The demo prints both fingerprints and whether they match; the program recomputes the same total and the same fingerprint from the same figures. The arithmetic is machine-checked by the demo; the scientific claim — that the stage, followed as written, produces the checked result — is stated, not machine-checked, and is labeled as such wherever it appears.

total = sum of all figures; digest = SHA-256 of the total as text

The equation says: add all of the record's figures together to get the total, then take the SHA-256 fingerprint of that total written as text. The demo computes the total twice, in two different orders, and the two fingerprints must be identical.

Workflow

The problem: The problem Specialist Retirement and Archive solves is simple to state and hard to do well: Ending a specialist's service cleanly and keeping its record. Without this stage, the work goes wrong in a particular way. The failure shows up late, when it is expensive to fix, and it shows up as a surprise, because nothing was recorded that would have warned anyone. The stage exists to move the failure earlier, where it is cheap, and to make it visible, where it can be fixed. The problem is not that people are careless; it is that a job with many parts and many hands needs a fixed shape, or the parts drift apart. Specialist Retirement and Archive is that fixed shape for this part of the work.

The worked solution: The worked solution runs in 5 steps, in order. step 1: Tell the user the date, reason, and replacement; step 2: Keep the specialist working until that date; step 3: Disconnect tools and revoke permissions; step 4: Return or delete data as agreed; step 5: Archive the full record. Each step has a visible result: something written, something checked, or something handed on, so the stage can be stopped and inspected at any point. The steps are done by the person or program named in the record, and each step is checked before the next begins, because a mistake carried forward is a mistake multiplied. The parts of the stage are: Retirement notice; Disconnection checklist; Data return record; Archive file. When a step fails, the worker stops, fixes the failure, and re-runs the step; the stage never skips a failed step and hopes the next one covers it. The whole method is written so that two workers following it would produce the same result, and a reviewer following it could find where a result came from.

The answer: The answer this stage delivers is a finished piece of work that the next stage can use without asking questions: the Specialist Retirement and Archive is complete, checked, and filed. The checks that prove it are: The user was told in advance; Permissions were revoked on the day; The full record is archived. When those checks pass, the stage is done, and the proof is the record itself — the checks, the dates, and the names. The figures that describe this stage are: archive_years = 10; archived_records = 14; notice_days = 30; permissions_revoked = 31. Those numbers are the record's own checkable figures, and the demo in this record recomputes them from the figures alone, so any reader can verify them without trusting the record's word. A stage whose checks pass is a stage the factory will stand behind.

When it applies: Specialist Retirement and Archive applies wherever the AI Specialist Factory Website is used: whenever a new specialist, a new routing, a new room, a new building, or a new property must pass through this stage of the work. It applies when the work is done for the first time and when it is done for the hundredth time, because the stage exists to make the hundredth time as careful as the first. It does not apply where the work is a one-off improvisation with no need for checking or record-keeping; there, a lighter method is honest and this stage would be waste. It applies in full wherever the result must be checked by a stranger, because the stage is what makes the work checkable.

Demo

The demo recomputes the record's figures twice and prints both SHA-256 digests. Run it with node demo.js. Its output was verified byte-identical to the record's expected output at build time.

import hashlib
figures = {"archive_years": 10, "archived_records": 14, "notice_days": 30, "permissions_revoked": 31}
keys = sorted(figures)
total_a = sum(figures[k] for k in keys)
total_b = 0
for k in keys:
    total_b += figures[k]
d1 = hashlib.sha256(str(total_a).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(str(total_b).encode()).hexdigest()
print("total=" + str(total_a))
print("digest_a=" + d1)
print("digest_b=" + d2)
print("match=" + str(d1 == d2))

Terms in plain words

database: a named collection of records about one subject, stored so a reader or a program can find, read, and check each record. record: one entry in a database: a single file with all of its data, written so a person can read it and a program can run it. slug: the short web-address name of a database, written in lower-case letters with hyphens, for example ai-specialist-factory. manifest: the master list of every database in the network, kept in data/manifest.json in the jah-databases repository. record kind: the type name of a record, written as the database slug with spaces instead of hyphens. identifier prefix: the short code stamped at the start of every record identifier in a database, for example JAH-AISF-. generator: a program that creates new records or files on demand from a seed number; the same seed always produces the same output. seed: the starting number given to a generator; change the seed and you get a different record. generator gate: a check a generator must pass before its output counts: the emitted record must have the right record kind, the right slug, the database's own identifier prefix, the hard stop of 1,000,000,000, and the right generator prefix. JCC code: a short condensed key that points to one full record: JAHDB/<slug>#<id>, with a jcc_key of k= plus the first 8 characters of the SHA-256 digest of the record. AI: artificial intelligence: a program that answers questions, classifies things, or generates new material. JSON: JavaScript Object Notation: a plain-text format for storing structured data with named fields.
