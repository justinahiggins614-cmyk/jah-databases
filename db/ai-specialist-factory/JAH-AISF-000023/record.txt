DESCRIPTIONS
The audit trail is the factory's memory: every brief, every build, every test, every review, every release, and every retirement, filed in order with dates and names. The trail exists so that any specialist can be checked backwards: from a wrong answer to the test that should have caught it, from a test to the blueprint that ordered it, from a blueprint to the brief that asked for it. The trail is kept in a form that cannot be quietly rewritten — entries are dated and signed, and changes are new entries, not edits to old ones. The trail is reviewed every quarter by someone outside the factory, because a trail nobody reads is a trail nobody trusts. When the trail shows a pattern — the same failure twice, the same delay every month — the factory changes how it works. The Factory Audit Trail stage works through 4 parts: Dated entries; Signatures; Quarterly review; Pattern notes. Each part is named, tested, and filed, so the work can be checked by a stranger. This record specifies the stage completely: what it is for, how it is done, how it is tested, what it costs in time and attention, and what can go wrong. The specification is written for a builder who has never done this stage before, and for a reviewer who must judge whether it was done properly. Inside the AI Specialist Factory Website, Factory Audit Trail sits between the stages that come before it and the stages that come after it, and it must fit both: what it receives must be in the shape it expects, and what it hands on must be in the shape the next stage expects. Nothing in this record is a claim that the system it specifies is deployed, live, or finished; it is a specification, documented from public knowledge and established practice, and the sources at the end of this record are the public pages it was checked against.

WORKFLOW
Problem: The problem Factory Audit Trail solves is simple to state and hard to do well: Records of everything the factory did, in order. Without this stage, the work goes wrong in a particular way. The failure shows up late, when it is expensive to fix, and it shows up as a surprise, because nothing was recorded that would have warned anyone. The stage exists to move the failure earlier, where it is cheap, and to make it visible, where it can be fixed. The problem is not that people are careless; it is that a job with many parts and many hands needs a fixed shape, or the parts drift apart. Factory Audit Trail is that fixed shape for this part of the work.
Solution: The worked solution runs in 5 steps, in order. step 1: Record every step with date and name; step 2: Never rewrite an entry; add a new one; step 3: Review the trail quarterly from outside; step 4: Note patterns and change the work; step 5: Keep the trail readable by a stranger. Each step has a visible result: something written, something checked, or something handed on, so the stage can be stopped and inspected at any point. The steps are done by the person or program named in the record, and each step is checked before the next begins, because a mistake carried forward is a mistake multiplied. The parts of the stage are: Dated entries; Signatures; Quarterly review; Pattern notes. When a step fails, the worker stops, fixes the failure, and re-runs the step; the stage never skips a failed step and hopes the next one covers it. The whole method is written so that two workers following it would produce the same result, and a reviewer following it could find where a result came from.
Answer: The answer this stage delivers is a finished piece of work that the next stage can use without asking questions: the Factory Audit Trail is complete, checked, and filed. The checks that prove it are: Entries are dated and signed; Changes are new entries; An outsider reviews quarterly. When those checks pass, the stage is done, and the proof is the record itself — the checks, the dates, and the names. The figures that describe this stage are: patterns_found = 5; quarterly_reviews = 4; trail_entries = 1840; trail_years = 3. Those numbers are the record's own checkable figures, and the demo in this record recomputes them from the figures alone, so any reader can verify them without trusting the record's word. A stage whose checks pass is a stage the factory will stand behind.
Applies: Factory Audit Trail applies wherever the AI Specialist Factory Website is used: whenever a new specialist, a new routing, a new room, a new building, or a new property must pass through this stage of the work. It applies when the work is done for the first time and when it is done for the hundredth time, because the stage exists to make the hundredth time as careful as the first. It does not apply where the work is a one-off improvisation with no need for checking or record-keeping; there, a lighter method is honest and this stage would be waste. It applies in full wherever the result must be checked by a stranger, because the stage is what makes the work checkable.
