History
Examinations have been watched over for as long as they have been set, because a mark that cannot be trusted cannot show what a learner knows. Proctoring grew beside the examination hall: a watcher who checks the candidate, reads the rules aloud, and writes down what happened in the sitting, so that the result can be trusted. From the invigilator walking the rows of a hall to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the sitting down whole. This database is built on that public practice of recording every act of online examination proctoring true. It is a record of published practice only, and it gives no advice about any examination.
