  1. A Shared Shelf Anyone Can Browse

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    Shared collections are shelves that a community fills together: books, recipes, maps, or photos, each item credited to the person who added it, and the whole shelf open to read. A shared shelf anyone can browse is a shelf that belongs to the whole community, because a shelf that only a few can read is a shelf that only a few can love. The shelf should show every item, with its description and its credit, in plain words. Without a shelf anyone can browse, the collection is only a list kept by one person, and the community never sees what it owns together. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a shelf anyone can browse, a credit on every item, and a history of every addition.
