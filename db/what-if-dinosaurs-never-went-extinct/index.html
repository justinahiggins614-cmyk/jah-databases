This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A What-If Timeline of Dinosaurs in the Forest, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Timelines.

History

Wondering what the world would look like if the great lizards had stayed is an old habit: a story told at a museum, a drawing of a dinosaur in a garden, a question asked on a walk. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain list, a search, and a written record for it. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

Dependencies

What must exist first: a list of places, a plain what-if timeline for each place, a clear imagining label on every page, and a review step before publishing. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex what-if timelines open.
