3. Science proof

A time calculator works because time units are fixed by definition and by the motion of the Earth: a day is one turn of the Earth, an hour is one twenty-fourth of that turn, a minute is one sixtieth of an hour, and a second is one sixtieth of a minute. Because the units nest by fixed factors, any duration has one exact count in seconds, and every question about time becomes arithmetic on that count: divide to convert, add to combine, subtract to find a gap. Time zones and daylight saving are the only parts that need a table instead of pure arithmetic, because they are set by law rather than by the sky, and the records state their offsets plainly so the arithmetic stays checkable.

Math proof: 7,265 divided by 3,600 is 2 with a remainder of 65, so there are 2 whole hours. 65 divided by 60 is 1 with a remainder of 5, so the remainder is 1 minute and 5 seconds. Together: 7,265 seconds is 2 hours, 1 minute and 5 seconds. As a check, 2 times 3,600 plus 1 times 60 plus 5 is 7,200 plus 60 plus 5, which is 7,265.

Science proof: Time is measured in fixed units: 60 seconds make a minute and 60 minutes make an hour, because the counting system divides each larger unit into 60 smaller ones. Converting Between 12-Hour and 24-Hour Clock

JAH-UTMC-000009 — In the 24-hour clock, morning hours keep their number, afternoon hours add 12, midnight is 00 and noon is 12. 3:45 PM is 15:45, and 12:30 AM is 00:30, because 3 plus 12 is 15, and 12 AM is the midnight hour, numbered 0.

Math proof: 3:45 PM: 3 plus 12 is 15, so the time is 15:45. 12:30 AM: 12 AM is hour 0, so the time is 00:30. As a check, 15:45 minus 12 is 3:45, and 00:30 plus 12 hours is 12:30 PM.

Science proof: The 12-hour clock repeats the numbers 1 to 12 twice a day, using AM for the first cycle and PM for the second. The 24-hour clock counts 0 to 23 without repeating, so each moment of the day has one number, which is why timetables and computers prefer it.

Records: Seconds to Minutes and Hours; Minutes to Hours and Days; Hours to Days and Weeks; Days to Weeks, Months and Years; Adding Times Together; Subtracting Times; Elapsed Time Between Two Clock Times; Duration Across Midnight; Converting Between 12-Hour and 24-Hour Clock; Time Zones: Converting Between Zones; Daylight Saving Time: Spring Forward; Daylight Saving Time: Fall Back; Countdown to a Future Date and Time; Calendar Days Between Two Dates; Business Days Between Two Dates; Weeks and Weekdays: Finding a Weekday; Speed, Distance and Time; Pace and Time per Distance; Adding Durations in Hours, Minutes and Seconds; Time Remaining in a Day; Leap Years and February Days; Months: Days in Each Month and Adding Months; Average Time and Total Time.

Math proof: The database holds 24 records: 23 content records plus this index. 8 are simple, 8 are working and 7 are advanced, and 8 plus 8 plus 7 is 23 content records. The first content record is Seconds to Minutes and Hours and the last is Average Time and Total Time.

Science proof: An index works because it lists every part of a subject in the order a learner should meet it, from the smallest unit to the whole system. A reader who follows the ladder from seconds to averages meets each new idea only after the idea it depends on.
