An Entry Written in Your Own Words Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A mood journal website: a visitor writes a short entry about how the day felt, in their own words, picks a simple word for the day such as calm, bright, tired, or restless, and saves the entry in a private list only they can read. The journal shows past entries on a simple calendar so the writer can notice patterns for themselves. It is personal reflection only: no medical claims, no treatment suggestions, no labels for anyone, and a plain note on every page to seek professional help if the reader feels they need support. This record covers An Entry Written in Your Own Words.

An entry written in someone else's words is not a journal entry at all, because it says what a form wanted instead of what the writer felt. An entry in the writer's own words keeps the day honest: the words are theirs, the feelings are theirs, and the page holds what actually happened. Without the writer's own words, the journal becomes a checklist, and a checklist cannot hold a day. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, An Entry Written in Your Own Words, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, The Mood Journal Yearly Review.

History

People have always kept journals to write down how a day felt, from diaries kept in a drawer to notebooks carried in a pocket. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a calendar view of past entries, a word for each day, and a lock that keeps every entry private to its writer. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
