    Concert discovery helps a listener find a live music evening worth attending, in plain words, with the kind of music, the length, and the cost written out before the listener sets out. Every listing is a clearly labeled illustrative sample of a generic kind of concert, never a real concert by a real performer. A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need. Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, An Evening of String Music, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Concerts, in the Signature edition.
