JAH-R20-PUSIM-000001 — Public data: the cosmic inventory — Lambda-CDM parameters

This public record holds the measured parameters every universal-collider scenario uses as input. From the Planck satellite's cosmic microwave background measurements and corroborating surveys: the universe is 13.8 billion years old; its content is ~5 percent ordinary matter, ~27 percent dark matter, ~68 percent dark energy; the Hubble constant is ~67-73 km/s/Mpc (the public "Hubble tension" between early- and late-universe measurements is noted honestly); the CMB temperature is 2.725 K; the observable universe spans ~93 billion light-years across.

Structure: galaxies group into clusters (hundreds to thousands of galaxies, 10^14-10^15 solar masses) and superclusters; the cosmic web's filaments were mapped by surveys like SDSS and DESI. The Milky Way is a barred spiral of ~1.5 x 10^12 solar masses including its dark halo; Andromeda approaches at ~110 km/s; the merger estimate of ~4.5 billion years comes from Hubble Space Telescope proper-motion measurements.

These are the fixed inputs. The simulation edition may explore what-ifs, but every scenario's starting point must be traceable to this table. Where public measurements disagree (Hubble tension), the database records both values and the disagreement rather than picking a winner.

JAH-R20-PUSIM-000002 — Public data: the gravitational-wave catalog

The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration's public catalog is the empirical foundation for all compact-merger scenarios. Headline public entries: GW150914 (2015, first detection; 36+29 solar masses merging, ~3 solar masses radiated); GW170817 (2017, first neutron-star merger; seen in gravitational waves, gamma rays, and the kilonova across the spectrum — the merger that proved heavy elements like gold and platinum are forged in such collisions); GW190521 (2020, ~150-solar-mass remnant — an intermediate-mass black hole, a new class); and by 2026, ~90+ confident detections, nearly all stellar-mass black-hole mergers.

Public measured patterns: black-hole masses cluster with a pileup near ~35 solar masses and a debated gap between ~50-120 solar masses (the pair-instability gap); spins are mostly small and aligned; merger rates are roughly tens per cubic gigaparsec per year for black holes. The detectors: LIGO's 4-km interferometers in Hanford and Livingston, Virgo's 3-km near Pisa, KAGRA in Japan — measuring spacetime ripples smaller than a proton's width.

Future public program: LIGO A+ upgrades, the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer proposals, and LISA (Laser Interferometer Space Antenna, ESA/NASA, 2030s) for supermassive black-hole mergers. The simulation edition draws variant masses only from near these measured ranges.

JAH-R20-PUSIM-000003 — Public data: interacting and merging galaxies observed

Astronomers don't need to simulate mergers — the public sky is full of them. The Antennae Galaxies (NGC 4038/39): two spirals mid-collision, tidal tails spanning 360,000 light-years, a starburst factory imaged by Hubble. The Mice Galaxies (NGC 4676): long tidal tails from a grazing encounter. Arp 147: a ring galaxy from a head-on plunge. Stephan's Quintet: a compact group with a giant intergalactic shock wave, imaged in detail by JWST. The public Arp Atlas of Peculiar Galaxies (1966) catalogued 338 interacting systems — the observational basis for the whole merger sequence.

Our neighborhood: the Milky Way is currently devouring the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy (tidal streams wrap the sky) and the Canis Major overdensity; Andromeda shows the Giant Stellar Stream from a past merger ~2 billion years ago. The future Milky Way-Andromeda merger estimate (~4.5 Gyr) comes from Hubble proper motions plus orbital modeling.

Cluster scale: the Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-558) with its separated dark matter and gas; MACS J0025.4-1222, a second "bullet"; Abell 520's puzzling dark core. These are the observed templates every cluster-merger scenario must resemble. This edition records what is seen; the simulation edition computes what-ifs.
