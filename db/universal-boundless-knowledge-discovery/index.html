Known Knowns and Unknowns JAH-FBD-000001 · simple

Donald Rumsfeld's 2002 framing — known knowns, known unknowns, and unknown unknowns — is a useful map of ignorance: things we know, things we know we do not know, and things we do not know we do not know.

Donald Rumsfeld's 2002 framing — known knowns, known unknowns, and unknown unknowns — is a useful map of ignorance: things we know, things we know we do not know, and things we do not know we do not know. Science advances by moving items from the third category to the second, and from the second to the first. A discovery database must track all three, because the unknown unknowns are where the biggest discoveries hide.

Literature-Based Discovery JAH-FBD-000004 · advanced

Literature-based discovery, pioneered by Don Swanson in 1986, finds hidden links in published research: if A causes B in one literature and B causes C in another, then A may cause C — a link no paper states.

Literature-based discovery, pioneered by Don Swanson in 1986, finds hidden links in published research: if A causes B in one literature and B causes C in another, then A may cause C — a link no paper states. Swanson's classic case linked fish oil to Raynaud's syndrome through blood viscosity, and later trials confirmed it. The method works only when the links are checked against evidence, not merely asserted.
