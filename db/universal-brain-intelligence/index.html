Neurons and Synapses JAH-FUB-000001 · simple

A neuron is a cell that signals with electrical spikes and chemical messages.

A neuron is a cell that signals with electrical spikes and chemical messages. It receives input through dendrites, fires an action potential down its axon, and passes the signal across a synapse to the next cell. The human brain holds about 86 billion neurons and on the order of 100 trillion synapses, and learning changes the strength of those connections.

Cortical Columns JAH-FUB-000002 · intermediate

The cerebral cortex is organized into columns of neurons that share a response preference — for example, a column in visual cortex responds best to lines of one orientation.

The cerebral cortex is organized into columns of neurons that share a response preference — for example, a column in visual cortex responds best to lines of one orientation. Columns were first mapped by Vernon Mountcastle in the 1950s and by Hubel and Wiesel in visual cortex, work that won the 1981 Nobel Prize. Columns are a real organizing pattern, though how strictly the cortex is columnar is still debated.

Functional MRI JAH-FUB-000005 · intermediate

Functional MRI (fMRI) measures brain activity indirectly, through changes in blood oxygenation: active neurons demand more oxygenated blood, and the scanner detects the difference.

Functional MRI (fMRI) measures brain activity indirectly, through changes in blood oxygenation: active neurons demand more oxygenated blood, and the scanner detects the difference. A typical scan divides the brain into voxels of 2-3 millimetres, each averaging hundreds of thousands of neurons, and records a volume every 1-2 seconds. fMRI is the workhorse of human cognitive neuroscience, and its limits — slowness and indirectness — are as important as its results.

print('Covered volume: %.1f ml' % vol_ml)

EEG and MEG JAH-FUB-000006 · intermediate

Electroencephalography (EEG) records the brain's electrical activity from the scalp, and magnetoencephalography (MEG) records its magnetic fields.

Electroencephalography (EEG) records the brain's electrical activity from the scalp, and magnetoencephalography (MEG) records its magnetic fields. Both measure neural activity in milliseconds — far faster than fMRI — but with coarser spatial precision, and EEG is cheap and portable enough for clinics and brain-computer interfaces. The brain's rhythms — alpha, beta, gamma — were first seen with EEG by Hans Berger in 1924.

Two-Photon Imaging JAH-FUB-000007 · advanced

Two-photon microscopy images living neurons deep in the brain by exciting fluorescent indicators with infrared light, which scatters less than visible light.

Two-photon microscopy images living neurons deep in the brain by exciting fluorescent indicators with infrared light, which scatters less than visible light. With calcium indicators, it records the firing of hundreds of neurons at once in a behaving animal, and with optogenetics it can also control them. The Visual Cortex JAH-FUB-000012 · intermediate

The visual cortex at the back of the brain turns retinal signals into sight through a hierarchy: early areas detect edges and orientations, later areas detect shapes, faces and places.

The visual cortex at the back of the brain turns retinal signals into sight through a hierarchy: early areas detect edges and orientations, later areas detect shapes, faces and places. The ventral stream answers 'what' and the dorsal stream answers 'where', a division shown by Ungerleider and Mishkin in 1982. Damage to face areas causes prosopagnosia, the inability to recognize faces, while vision otherwise remains intact.

The Basal Ganglia JAH-FUB-000014 · advanced

The basal ganglia are a cluster of nuclei deep in the brain that select and gate actions: they release the brake on wanted movements and hold back unwanted ones.

The basal ganglia are a cluster of nuclei deep in the brain that select and gate actions: they release the brake on wanted movements and hold back unwanted ones. Dopamine from the substantia nigra tunes the gate, and its loss in Parkinson's disease locks the brake on, causing slowness and tremor. The Cerebellum JAH-FUB-000015 · intermediate

The cerebellum, the 'little brain' at the back of the skull, holds about 80% of the brain's neurons and coordinates movement, timing and balance.

The cerebellum, the 'little brain' at the back of the skull, holds about 80% of the brain's neurons and coordinates movement, timing and balance. It compares intended movement with actual movement and corrects errors, which is why cerebellar damage causes clumsiness rather than weakness. Recent work shows it also contributes to cognition, language and emotion.

The Thalamus JAH-FUB-000016 · intermediate

The thalamus is the brain's relay station: nearly all sensory information passes through it on the way to the cortex, and it also carries signals back.

The thalamus is the brain's relay station: nearly all sensory information passes through it on the way to the cortex, and it also carries signals back. Francis Crick called it the seat of attention, and its nuclei map onto every cortical area.

Neural Decoding JAH-FUB-000018 · advanced

Neural decoding reads information out of brain activity: which image was seen, which word was heard, which movement was intended.

Neural decoding reads information out of brain activity: which image was seen, which word was heard, which movement was intended. Decoders are trained on examples and tested on new data, and their accuracy is measured, not assumed. Neural Plasticity JAH-FUB-000021 · intermediate

Neural plasticity is the brain's ability to rewire itself: synapses strengthen and weaken with use, new connections form, and maps reorganize after injury or practice.

Neural plasticity is the brain's ability to rewire itself: synapses strengthen and weaken with use, new connections form, and maps reorganize after injury or practice. Long-term potentiation, discovered by Bliss and Lomo in 1973, is the best-studied mechanism. Plasticity is strongest in youth but continues throughout life, which is why rehabilitation after stroke can recover lost function.
