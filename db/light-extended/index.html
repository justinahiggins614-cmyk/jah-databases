JAH-PHOTON-EXT-000003 — Light Computer Extended Features — Self-calibration against drift

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A photonic chip's paths detune as temperature shifts — a fraction of a degree moves a resonator off its wavelength. Self-calibration runs continuously: pilot tones probe each path, heaters or carrier injection re-tune it, and the control loop holds everything on-wavelength. Telecom transceivers do this today; the computer scales it to thousands of paths. Calibration is the light computer's immune system: invisible, constant, essential.

JAH-PHOTON-EXT-000004 — Light Computer Extended Features — Fault tolerance in photonics

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Emitters age, detectors go blind, waveguides crack — and the machine must not care. Tolerance comes from redundancy (spare modulators per array), rerouting (tunable splitters steer around dead paths), and graceful degradation (lose 5 percent of channels, lose 5 percent of speed, not the machine). Built-in self-test sweeps every component at boot and maps the damage, like memory testers mapping bad RAM. The contract: components fail; computations do not.
