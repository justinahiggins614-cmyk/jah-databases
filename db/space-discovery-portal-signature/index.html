The portal teaches only public sky knowledge: the names of stars and constellations, the phases of the moon, and the movements of the planets as seen from Earth. This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A First Look at the Night Sky, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Portal That Predicts the Next Great Sky Event, in the Signature edition.

History

People have watched the sky for as long as people have lived under it: star charts drawn on stone, calendars built from the moon, sailors steering by the stars. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a tour a beginner can follow, a map that can be printed, and a calendar that says what is overhead tonight. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
