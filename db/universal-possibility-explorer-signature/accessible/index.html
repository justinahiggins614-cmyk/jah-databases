The Universal Possibility Explorer Database (Signature)

Accessible version: plain text, no scripts, no sign-in, free forever. A universal possibility explorer maps the branches a choice can take, with chances and outcomes for each branch, so the best path can be seen and compared. Classification: FRONTIER — a simulation and design-study tool. It never claims to explore all possibilities or to predict the future.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced.

Full record index: https://justinahiggins614-cmyk.github.io/jah-databases-data-2/data/universal-possibility-explorer-signature/index.jsonl.gz

  1. One Possibility, Written Down

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: frontier. Classification: FRONTIER. A possibility is a sentence that says what could happen, written plainly enough to test. Possibility 'It rains tomorrow' can be checked against tomorrow's weather.

    Math proof: A possibility space grows by multiplication: with b choices at each of n steps, the number of complete paths is b to the power n. With 2 choices at each of 3 steps there are 2 times 2 times 2, which is 8 complete paths. Probabilities add to one: if the branches from one choice have chances 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2, they sum to 1.0, so every outcome is counted once and none is missed. The chance of one complete path is the product of the chances along it: 0.5 then 0.5 then 0.5 gives 0.125, or 1 in 8. An expected value is the sum of each outcome times its chance: outcomes worth 10, 4 and 0 with chances 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 give 5 plus 1.2 plus 0, which is 6.2.

    Science proof: Exploring possibilities works because a human mind can hold only a few branches at once, while a written tree can hold hundreds. Writing each branch down, with its chance and its outcome, turns guessing into comparison: the best path can be seen, not just felt. Probabilities here are estimates built from stated assumptions, not measurements of the future, so every result must be read with its assumptions beside it. That is why a possibility explorer is a simulation and design-study tool: it shows what follows from the assumptions, and it is honest about what it does not know.
