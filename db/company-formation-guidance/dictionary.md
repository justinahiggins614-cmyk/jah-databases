# Dictionary entries — The Company Formation Guidance Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## formation

The whole process of bringing a company into being, from choosing its form to filing its papers and opening its books, done in order so no step is skipped.

## incorporation

The filing that creates a corporation as its own legal person, recorded by the authority that keeps the register, with a date that becomes the company's birthday.

## articles of incorporation

The founding document filed to create a corporation, naming the company, its purpose, its shares, and its first directors, kept as the company's birth record.

## bylaws

The written rules a company adopts for running itself, saying how directors are chosen, how meetings are held, and how decisions are made, kept with the founding papers.

## shareholder

A person who owns a part of a company through shares, with the rights the company's rules give, including a vote on the matters the rules name.

## director

A person chosen to run a company on behalf of its owners, named in the founding papers, and answerable for the company's decisions under its rules.

## officer

A person given a named job in a company, such as keeping the books or signing papers, with the duties the company's rules attach to that job.

## share

One equal part of a company's ownership, counted and recorded, so that who owns how much of the company is always known from the share register.

## registered office

The official address of a company, where its papers are kept and where official notices are sent, recorded in the register and kept up to date.

## founding meeting

The first meeting of a company's owners and directors, where the rules are adopted, the officers are named, and the first decisions are written down.

## share register

The company's own book of who owns its shares, kept current with every sale and gift, so ownership can be proved from the book alone.

## formation checklist

The ordered list of formation steps, each ticked when done, so a founder can see at a glance which steps remain before the company is complete.
