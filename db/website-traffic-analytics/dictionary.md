# Dictionary entries — The Website Traffic Analytics Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## visit

One person's arrival at a site and the pages they read before they leave, counted as one visit however many pages they read.

## page view

One reading of one page, counted each time the page is shown, so a visit that reads five pages makes five page views.

## visitor

One person counted once over a chosen period, however many visits they make, so that the count says how many people came, not only how many times.

## bounce

A visit that reads one page and leaves, counted so that pages which lose their readers at once are known, not guessed.

## referrer

The place a visitor came from, such as another page or a search, recorded beside the visit so the site knows which roads bring people.

## path

The order of pages one visit reads, from arrival to departure, which shows what readers do after they arrive.

## conversion

A visit that does the thing the page was made for, such as reading to the end or sending a message, counted beside the visits that did not.

## period

The span of time a count covers, such as a day or a month, named beside every number, because a number without its period means nothing.

## count

A number made by counting, never guessed, with the rule that made it written beside it, so that anyone can recount it.

## report

A summary of counts over a period, showing visits, views, and the pages that matter, counted from the ledger, never written by hand.

## privacy

The rule that analytics counts what happened, never who did it: no names, no addresses, and nothing that follows a person from site to site.

## ledger

The analytics' own written record of every visit and every count, kept in order.
