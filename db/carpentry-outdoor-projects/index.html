Birdhouse Rank 1 · simple · wildlife · beginner

Source: public-harvest

A birdhouse is a small cedar house with a sloped roof, an entrance hole sized for the intended bird, and no perch, because a perch helps predators reach the nest. The front panel is hinged or screwed for yearly cleaning, the floor has drainage gaps at the corners, and ventilation slots sit under the roof so summer heat escapes. Cedar is left unfinished outside so it weathers naturally, and the house is mounted on a post with a predator guard rather than nailed to a tree that predators climb easily.

Project diagram

Original dimensioned project sketch for Birdhouse: a front-view diagram showing the overall 8 x 12 x 8 inch (W x H x D) form, the main cut pieces, and the overall dimensions labeled on the drawing, drawn for this record.

Specifications

Dimensions: 8 x 12 x 8 in (W x H x D)
Tolerances: Cut to within 1/16 in; check square by measuring both diagonals and matching them.
Load notes: A birdhouse carries no human load; it hangs from a post or tree at 5 to 10 feet.
Arithmetic: Cut list adds up: Total pieces: 7 (1 x Front, 1 x Back, 2 x Side, 1 x Roof, left, 1 x Roof, right, 1 x Floor). Longest piece 12 in fits within the 14.4222 in face diagonal of the overall form. Cut the longest pieces first so a mistake on a short piece does not waste a long board.

Materials

  • House boards: cedar, 3/4 in

Cut list

PieceDimensionsQty
Front8 x 10 x 0.75 in1
Back8 x 12 x 0.75 in1
Side6.5 x 10 x 0.75 in2
Roof, left9 x 7 x 0.75 in1
Roof, right9 x 7 x 0.75 in1
Floor6.5 x 6.5 x 0.75 in1

Fasteners

  • Wood glue and 1-1/2 in exterior screws, 16 total; no perch, which helps predators

How to build

  1. Read the cut list and the arithmetic note in full, and check every piece against the plan; measure twice before cutting.
  2. Cut the pieces in order, longest first: Front (8 x 10 x 0.75 in), Back (8 x 12 x 0.75 in), Side (6.5 x 10 x 0.75 in).
  3. Dry-fit the frame without glue and check both diagonals match so the assembly is square.
  4. Do not add a perch: a perch helps predators reach the nest, not the nesting bird.
  5. Glue and fasten as listed in the specs, keeping the assembly square while fasteners go in; wipe glue squeeze-out before it dries.
  6. Sand through the grits, ease the edges, and finish as described in the record; check the finished piece for square and for its rated load before use.

Tools required

  • Tape measure
  • Combination square
  • Sharp pencil
  • Handsaw or circular saw
  • Drill driver
  • Clamps (2)
  • Sandpaper, 80/120/180 grit
  • Workbench or sawhorses

Safety gear

  • Safety glasses
  • Hearing protection
  • Dust mask
  • Work gloves for handling rough lumber (never near rotating blades)

Wear eye protection for every cut and sanding pass, hearing protection for power tools, and a dust mask for sanding. Unplug power tools or remove batteries before changing blades or bits, keep guards in place, and keep hands clear of blades. Clamp work before cutting, never hold a small piece by hand near a blade, and clear offcuts before the next cut.
