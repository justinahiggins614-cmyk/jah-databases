1. Description

A summarizer keeps the main points of a text and drops the detail. The demo on this page summarizes in this browser by scoring sentences on the words they share with the whole text, with no sign-in and no hosted call.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A sentence scores by word frequency: each word in the sentence earns its count in the whole text, and the sentence score is the sum. The top sentences are the highest scorers, kept in their original order. A quarter summary keeps one sentence in four: 20 sentences become 5.

3. Science proof

Extractive summarizing works because the words that matter in a text repeat, while passing detail does not. A sentence holding many repeated words is likely a main sentence. Picking top sentences in original order keeps the summary readable, because the text's own order is also its argument's order.
