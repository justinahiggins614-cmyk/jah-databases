Badminton Rank 1 · simple · racquet sport · beginner

Badminton is a racquet sport played with a shuttlecock, in which players hit the shuttlecock over a net, and it is the fastest racquet sport, with smashes recorded above 400 kilometres per hour. Singles and doubles are played on a court with a net 1.55 metres high at the posts, and matches are best of three games to 21 points, with a two-point lead needed at 20-all and a cap at 30. The sport demands footwork, deception, and control of clears, drops, smashes, and net shots, and it is played indoors, since wind disturbs the light shuttlecock. Badminton has been an Olympic sport since 1992, and its leading events include the Olympic programme, the World Championships, and the Thomas and Uber Cups for teams.

History

Badminton grew from battledore games in Britain and India in the nineteenth century and became an Olympic sport at Barcelona in 1992.

Rules

Matches are best of three games to 21 points, with a two-point lead needed and a cap at 30, and the shuttlecock must pass over the net into the opponent's court.

Techniques

Clears, drops, smashes, drives, and net shots, linked by fast footwork and deception, are the core skills.

Training

Training pairs footwork drills, multi-shuttle feeding, and match play, with strength and agility work.

Squash Rank 2 · simple · racquet sport · beginner

Squash is a racquet sport played in a four-walled court, in which two players take turns hitting a small rubber ball against the front wall, and the ball may bounce once on the floor between hits. Rallies are fast and physical, since the ball stays in play off the walls and players must move quickly in a small space, and matches are best of five games to 11 points, with a two-point lead needed. The sport rewards length, width, and deception: deep rails to the back, cross-courts, drops to the front, and boasts off the side wall. Squash has a professional tour and world championships, and it will join the Olympic programme at Los Angeles 2028.

History

Squash grew from rackets at Harrow School in nineteenth-century England and became a worldwide professional sport with a world tour.

Rules

Matches are best of five games to 11 points, the ball may bounce once, and players must avoid interfering with each other, with lets and strokes called when they do.

Techniques

Rails, cross-courts, drops, boasts, and volleys, linked by movement to the T position at the court's centre, are the core skills.

Safety

Eye protection is standard, courts are kept clear, and players call lets rather than playing through interference.

Table Tennis Rank 3 · simple · racquet sport · beginner

Table tennis is a racquet sport played on a table divided by a net, in which players hit a light celluloid or plastic ball with small paddles, and it is one of the fastest ball sports, with rallies decided in fractions of a second. Games run to 11 points with a two-point lead, matches are usually best of five or seven games, and serves alternate every two points, or every point after deuce. Spin is the sport's central skill: topspin, backspin, sidespin, and no-spin serves and returns change the ball's bounce and flight, and players read spin from the opponent's paddle angle and contact. Table tennis has been an Olympic sport since 1988, and its leading events include the Olympic programme and the World Championships.

History

Table tennis grew from parlour games in late nineteenth-century Britain and became an Olympic sport at Seoul in 1988.

Rules

Games run to 11 points with a two-point lead, serves alternate every two points, and the ball must bounce on the server's side and then the receiver's side.

Techniques

Forehand and backhand drives, pushes, loops, blocks, and flicks, all with spin variation, are the core skills.

Training

Training pairs multi-ball feeding, footwork, serve practice, and match play.

Padel Rank 4 · simple · racquet sport · beginner

Padel is a racquet sport played in an enclosed court with walls, in which pairs hit a ball over a net using solid paddles, and the ball may bounce off the walls, which stay in play. Courts are smaller than tennis courts, about twenty metres by ten, and the walls, usually glass, let players play rebounds, which makes rallies long and tactical. Scoring follows tennis, with games, sets, and matches, and serves are underarm, which keeps rallies accessible to beginners. Padel grew rapidly in Spain and Latin America and then worldwide, and its leading events include the World Padel Championship and professional tour events.

History

Padel was created in Mexico in the 1960s by Enrique Corcuera and grew rapidly in Spain from the 1980s onward.

Rules

Scoring follows tennis, serves are underarm, and the ball may rebound off the walls, which stay in play after bouncing on the court.

Techniques

Wall rebounds, lobs, bandejas, viboras, and net volleys are the core skills, with doubles positioning central.

Training

Training pairs wall-rebound drills, doubles positioning, and match play.

Pickleball Rank 5 · simple · racquet sport · beginner

Pickleball is a paddle sport played on a court smaller than a tennis court, with a perforated plastic ball and solid paddles, and it is one of the fastest-growing sports in the world. Players serve underarm diagonally, the ball must bounce once on each side before volleys are allowed, and volleys are forbidden inside the non-volley zone, called the kitchen, near the net. Games usually run to 11 points with a two-point lead, and doubles is the most popular form, with dinking, soft shots into the kitchen, as a central tactic. Pickleball was created in the United States in 1965 and grew rapidly from the 2010s onward, with professional tours and national championships.

History

Pickleball was created on Bainbridge Island, Washington, in 1965 by Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell, and Barney McCallum.

Rules

The serve is underarm and diagonal, the ball must bounce once per side before volleys, and volleys are forbidden in the kitchen.

Techniques

Dinks, drops, drives, lobs, and volleys are the core skills, with kitchen play central to doubles.

Training

Training pairs dinking drills, third-shot drops, and match play, and the sport is accessible to beginners.

Racquetball Rank 6 · compounded · racquet sport · intermediate

Racquetball is a racquet sport played in a four-walled court, in which players hit a hollow rubber ball against the front wall, and the ball may hit side walls and the ceiling, which stay in play. Unlike squash, the ball may bounce more than once in some forms and the ceiling is in play, which makes rallies fast and angles wide, and the sport uses a short racquet and a bouncier ball than squash. Games usually run to 15 points with a two-point lead, and matches are best of three games, with the third, if needed, to 11. Racquetball grew in the United States in the mid-twentieth century and has professional tours and world championships.

History

Racquetball grew in the United States in the 1940s and 1950s from paddleball and became an organised sport with a professional tour.

Rules

Games run to 15 points with a two-point lead, the ball may hit the side walls and ceiling, and the serve must reach the front wall and land beyond the short line.

Techniques

Ceiling balls, passing shots, pinch shots into corners, and kill shots low on the front wall are the core skills.

Safety

Eye protection is mandatory in organised play, and players avoid swinging at a ball when an opponent is in the way.

Basque Pelota Rank 7 · compounded · racquet sport · intermediate

Basque pelota is a family of ball games from the Basque Country, played against a wall called a fronton, in which players hit a ball with a hand, a wooden bat, a basket-like cesta, or a racquet, depending on the modality. The best-known form outside the region is jai alai, played with a cesta, but the family also includes hand pelota, played bare-handed, and pala, played with a wooden bat. Games are usually played to a set score, and the ball, made with a core and wound wool, is hard and fast, so players wear helmets in some modalities. Pelota is a central part of Basque culture, with frontons in villages and towns across the region, and its leading events include world championships.

History

Basque pelota grew from medieval handball games in the Basque Country and became a defining cultural sport with many modalities.

Rules

Players hit the ball against the fronton, and the ball must land within the court markings; modalities differ in equipment and scoring.

Techniques

Hand strikes, bat strikes, cesta catches and throws, and reading rebounds off the wall are the core skills.

Safety

Helmets are worn in fast modalities, and beginners start with softer balls and shorter courts.

Jai Alai Rank 8 · compounded · racquet sport · intermediate

Jai alai is the fastest ball game in the world, played with a cesta, a long curved basket strapped to the hand, in which players catch a hard ball and hurl it against a fronton wall at speeds above 300 kilometres per hour. The ball, called a pelota, is made with a hard core and wound wool, and players must catch and throw in one continuous motion, without holding or juggling. Jai alai is played in a large three-walled court. The sport's leading events include professional seasons in the Basque Country and exhibition play elsewhere.

History

Jai alai developed in the Basque Country as cesta punta and spread to Florida and Latin America in the twentieth century.

Rules

Players catch and throw in one continuous motion, the ball must hit the fronton within bounds, and holding or juggling is a fault.

Techniques

Catching in the cesta, throwing with a full swing, and reading high rebounds are the core skills.

Safety

Helmets are mandatory, the ball is hard and very fast, and spectators sit behind protective screening.

Real Tennis Rank 9 · compounded · racquet sport · intermediate

Real tennis, also called court tennis, is the original racquet sport, played in an indoor court with walls, galleries, and sloped roofs, in which players hit a ball with a wooden racquet and may use the walls and openings as part of play. The court is complex, with a net, a tambour, a grille, and galleries with openings, and balls that enter certain openings score, which makes strategy unlike any other racquet sport. Scoring follows the old tennis system, with points, games, and sets, and the sport keeps traditions such as the chase, in which a point is replayed from the position where a ball stopped. Real tennis is played at a small number of historic courts worldwide, and its leading event is the world championship.

History

Real tennis developed from medieval French jeu de paume and became the court sport of European royalty, with Henry VIII among its famous players.

Rules

Scoring follows the old tennis system, balls may use walls and galleries, and the chase replays points from where a ball stopped.

Techniques

Reading wall rebounds, placing balls into openings, and serving with cut are the core skills.

Training

Training pairs wall drills, serve practice, and match play on the complex court.

Soft Tennis Rank 10 · compounded · racquet sport · intermediate

Soft tennis is a racquet sport played with a soft rubber ball and a light racquet, and it is widely played in Asia, particularly in Japan, Korea, and Taiwan, as well as in schools worldwide. The game resembles tennis but uses a softer, lighter ball that flies more slowly and bounces differently, and matches are played as singles and doubles with scoring to four games in a set format. Soft tennis rewards soft hands, spin, and placement over power, and its equipment is lighter and cheaper than tennis equipment, which helped it spread in schools. The sport's leading events include the Asian Games, where it has appeared since 1994, and the World Soft Tennis Championships.

History

Soft tennis developed in Japan in the late nineteenth century as a school adaptation of tennis and spread across Asia.

Rules

Matches are played in sets to four games, with singles and doubles, and the soft ball's flight rewards placement and spin.

Techniques

Soft volleys, sliced groundstrokes, lobs, and drop shots are the core skills.

Training

Training pairs soft-ball rallies, placement drills, and match play, and the sport suits school programmes.

Beach Tennis Rank 11 · complex · racquet sport · advanced

Beach tennis is a racquet sport played on sand, in which players hit a depressurised ball over a net with solid paddles, and it blends tennis, beach volleyball, and badminton. The game is played as doubles on a sand court with a net 1.7 metres high, volleys are central since the ball must not bounce, and scoring follows tennis with no-ad games in most events. Beach tennis rewards quick feet in sand, soft hands at the net, and lobs over opponents, and it is played at beaches and beach clubs worldwide. The sport's leading events include the World Championships run by the international tennis federation, and it grew rapidly in Brazil and Italy.

History

Beach tennis grew in Italy and Brazil in the 1970s and 1980s and became an organised international sport in the 2000s.

Rules

Platform Tennis Rank 12 · complex · racquet sport · advanced

Platform tennis is a racquet sport played on a raised, fenced platform court with a wire mesh, in which players hit a sponge rubber ball with solid paddles, and the ball may rebound off the wire screens, which stay in play. Courts are smaller than tennis courts and are heated in winter, so the sport is played year-round outdoors in cold climates, particularly in the northeastern United States. The game is played as doubles, scoring follows tennis, and the screens make rallies long and tactical, since players retrieve balls off the wire. Platform tennis grew in the United States in the 1920s and has national championships and a professional tour.

History

Rules

Techniques

Screen rebounds, lobs, overheads, and doubles positioning are the core skills.

Safety

Courts are checked for screen and surface, warm clothing is worn in winter, and warm-ups precede play.

Speedminton Rank 13 · complex · racquet sport · advanced

Speedminton, also called crossminton, is a racquet sport played without a net, in which players hit a shuttlecock-like speeder across a distance, indoors or outdoors, day or night with glowing speeders. Players stand in squares marked on the ground, hit the speeder back and forth, and score when the opponent fails to return it into the square, and the game is played as singles and doubles. The sport was created in Germany in 2001 and grew as a recreational and competitive game that needs little equipment and no net, which makes it playable in parks, gyms, and beaches. Its leading events include world championships, and night play with fluorescent equipment is a distinctive feature.

History

Speedminton was created in Berlin in 2001 by Bill Brandes and grew as a net-free racquet game playable almost anywhere.

Rules

Players hit the speeder into the opponent's square, and points are scored when a return fails or lands outside.

Techniques

Overhead clears, drives, and drop shots, with footwork between squares, are the core skills.

Training

Training pairs rally drills, footwork, and match play, and the sport suits schools and parks.

Matkot is a beach paddle game from Israel, played on sand with wooden or carbon paddles and a small rubber ball, in which pairs rally the ball back and forth without a net, keeping the rally alive as long as possible. Unlike most racquet sports, matkot is traditionally non-competitive: there is no scoring, and the aim is a long, enjoyable rally rather than winning a point, though competitive forms have since developed. The game is a fixture of Israeli beaches, particularly in Tel Aviv, and it rewards soft hands, control, and rhythm. Matkot's leading events include beach tournaments, and its culture values long rallies and sportsmanship over results.

History

Rules

Traditional matkot has no scoring and no net: pairs rally to keep the ball alive as long as possible.
