This record covers A Listing for One Kind of Used Item.

Without a single list, listings live in scattered places, the buyer compares from memory, and the best listing is often the one forgotten.

A used item marketplace website: each listing describes a generic kind of used item, such as a wooden chair, a cotton coat, or a steel kettle, with its condition in plain words, its price as a plain example number, and the neighborhood where it can be collected. Listings are sorted from lowest to highest price, a buyer can save a listing to return to, and a plain safety guide says to meet in a public place, bring a friend, and check the item before agreeing anything. No real seller is named, no living person's private data appears anywhere, and no money changes hands on this site: it is a design study with example listings only. This record covers A Marketplace That Combines Listings and Safety Checks.

A buyer who must check listings in one place and safety rules in another visits two lists, remembers two answers, and chooses from memory. A marketplace that combines listings and safety checks puts the safety guide beside every listing in one view, so the buyer can see the condition, the price, and the safety rules together at a glance. Without a combined view, the buyer reads the listing but never finds the safety guide, and the meeting arranged without safety is a meeting that puts the buyer at risk.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a marketplace that combines listings and safety checks runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Selling used items is an old habit: a market stall of second hand goods, a notice pinned to a board, a neighbor who passes a good chair to another neighbor. Combined marketplaces grew out of market squares, where the goods and the rules of fair trading stood side by side.

A used item marketplace website: each listing describes a generic kind of used item, such as a wooden chair, a cotton coat, or a steel kettle, with its condition in plain words, its price as a plain example number, and the neighborhood where it can be collected. Listings are sorted from lowest to highest price, a buyer can save a listing to return to, and a plain safety guide says to meet in a public place, bring a friend, and check the item before agreeing anything. No real seller is named, no living person's private data appears anywhere, and no money changes hands on this site: it is a design study with example listings only. This record covers A Checklist With Review Before Launch.

A marketplace launched without a checklist launches with its mistakes still in it, because nobody checked the kinds of items, the honest conditions, the prices, the safety guide, and the review step before the first buyer arrived. A checklist with review before launch walks through every part of the marketplace in order, and a second reader signs each part before the list opens. Without a checklist, the list opens with a wrong price or a missing safety guide, and the first buyers find the mistakes instead of the bargains.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a checklist with review before launch runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Selling used items is an old habit: a market stall of second hand goods, a notice pinned to a board, a neighbor who passes a good chair to another neighbor. The website form grows out of that habit, adding honest condition in plain words, a clear price, and a safety guide that says to meet in a public place. Checklists before launch grew out of workshops and kitchens, where a list checked twice kept a job from going wrong.

Proposed solution

Add a tournament bracket with rounds, divisions, and a season; Add a creator studio so visitors can build a new used-item listing; Add a review step so every new used-item listing is checked before it is published; Add the used-item listing in many languages, with the same rules in each; Add an offline pack so the used-item listing works with no connection.

Dependencies

What must exist first: a listing for each generic kind of used item, honest condition in plain words, a clear price, and a plain safety guide. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex used-item listings open.

Feasibility
