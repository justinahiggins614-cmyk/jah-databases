  1. A Daily Movement Estimate

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The fitness calculator turns steps, minutes of movement, and rest days into plain estimates of weekly movement, with every figure labeled an estimate. It gives general information only, never personal health advice. A daily movement estimate turns steps and minutes into one plain number a person can read at a glance, with the word estimate printed beside it. The estimate must use round numbers and show them, because a figure that hides its inputs cannot be checked. Without the label, a reader may treat the estimate as a measured fact, and a rough number will be mistaken for a precise one. This record shows its inputs, its total, and its label together. Worked example: in a website built this way, a daily movement estimate runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Counting movement is an old habit: steps tallied on a walk, minutes timed with a watch, a week of effort written in a notebook. The calculator form grew out of printed tables, then pocket counters, then web pages that show the working. Daily movement estimates grew out of step tallies kept in notebooks, where a walker wrote down each day's steps beside the date.
