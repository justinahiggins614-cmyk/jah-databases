History
An audit is a second pair of eyes on the books: someone who did not write the entries reads them against their proof, and says in writing whether the books tell the truth. The practice grew with trade and with public money, where those who trusted others with funds wanted an honest witness. Managing an audit means planning what will be checked, gathering the proof, recording each finding with its evidence, and following each finding until it is answered. This database is built on that public practice of checking the books independently and writing down what was found.
