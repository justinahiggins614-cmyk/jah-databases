A History Talk Show About a Small Town Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A podcast discovery website: a visitor picks a generic kind of spoken show, such as a history talk show or a science talk show, and the site shows illustrative sample shows of that kind, each with a plain description of the subject, the length, and the style. The site names no real show, copies no real listing, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real show. This record covers A History Talk Show About a Small Town.

A history talk show about a small town gives a listener a plain tale of how the town grew around its river, with dates, names, and the reasons things changed. The listing must say how long the show runs, what subject it covers, and whether it suits a curious evening, so a listener can learn without being lost. Without those plain facts, a listener who wanted a clear tale may find a tangle of dates instead, and a tangle of dates is a poor way to learn history.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a history talk show about a small town runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Finding a spoken show worth hearing is an old habit: a talk on the radio, a lecture in a hall, a story told by a friend. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by subject and a plain description for every listing. History talk shows are an old habit, from a lecture in a village hall to a tale told by a teacher after supper.

Proposed solution

Name the podcast listing and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every podcast listing the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one podcast listing without signing in; Check the podcast listing works the same way on a phone and on a computer.
