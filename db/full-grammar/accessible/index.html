Sentence

A sentence is the largest unit of grammar: a group of words that expresses a complete thought and can stand alone. The cat sat is a sentence; cat sat, without the, is not a complete English sentence. Sentences are built from clauses, and clauses from phrases, in a hierarchy that grammar describes from the top down. Clause

Main clauses stand alone, subordinate clauses depend on a main clause, and a sentence is one or more clauses joined together. Case

Case is the marking of a noun's role in the sentence: subject, object, possessor, and more. Latin marks the subject with the nominative, the object with the accusative, and the possessor with the genitive, so puella, puellam, and puellae are one noun in three roles. English keeps case only in pronouns, as in I, me, and my, and in the possessive 's.
