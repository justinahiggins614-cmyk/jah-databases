Three Plain Questions After Every Show Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A feedback website: after a show, a reading, or a lesson, the audience answers a few plain questions, rates each part on a simple scale, and writes one sentence about what worked and one about what did not. The maker sees the answers grouped by question, the highest and lowest marks side by side, and the sentences sorted by topic, with every sample rating labeled illustrative and no real review presented as fact. This record covers Three Plain Questions After Every Show.

Three plain questions after every show ask what the audience liked, what confused them, and what they would change, so the maker hears honest reactions in words anyone can understand. The design is stated in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits: what a visitor sees first, what they can do next, and what happens after they finish. A feedback round built this way stays honest, with plain questions, a simple scale, and every sample rating labeled illustrative.
