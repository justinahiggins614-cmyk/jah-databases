  1. Three Plain Questions After Every Show

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: buildable-now. The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

    The audience feedback platform gathers honest reactions from the people who watch, listen, or read, in plain words, and hands the maker a sorted summary instead of a pile of raw comments. Three plain questions after every show ask what the audience liked, what confused them, and what they would change, so the maker hears honest reactions in words anyone can understand. The design is stated in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits: what a visitor sees first, what they can do next, and what happens after they finish. A feedback round built this way stays honest, with plain questions, a simple scale, and every sample rating labeled illustrative. Worked example: in a website built this way, three plain questions after every show runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

    History: Asking the audience what it thought is an old habit: a comment book at the door, a show of hands, a slip of paper dropped in a box. The website form grows out of that habit, adding plain questions, a simple scale, and a summary grouped by topic. Audiences once answered three questions on a slip of paper dropped in a box at the door.

    Proposed solution: Name the feedback round and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every feedback round the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one feedback round without signing in; Check the feedback round works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

    Dependencies: What must exist first: plain questions, a simple rating scale, one-sentence answers, and a summary grouped by topic. Nothing else is needed to open the first feedback round.

    Verification path: A visitor opens one feedback round, follows the rules in plain sentences, and finishes it without help. The demo program prints the same total as the math proof, and a second visitor repeating the steps gets the same result.
