1. Description and History

Photo restoration repairs old and damaged photographs: dust and scratches removed, tears and creases mended, faded colors brought back, and missing pieces rebuilt from the surrounding image. This database designs that restoration one record at a time, from a single dust spot to a whole album at archival quality, and every record is a design, never a real product. It works on the user's own photos only.

Restoration began in darkrooms and conservation studios, where skilled hands retouched prints with brushes and dyes. Scanners and software later brought the same work to ordinary computers: dust removal, scratch repair, color correction, and careful rebuilding of missing areas. The design in this database follows the conservator's rule — change as little as possible, document every change, and keep the original safe.

Every record here is a design, never a real product. Public records are fact-checked, real, and legal-only; Signature records are the Signature original version of the same subject; speculative material is labeled design-study, never as fact. Photo tools work on the user's own photos only, never anyone else's private images.

2. Math Proof

Each of the twenty-four records carries worked arithmetic: a counted relationship stated as a formula, and a demo program that prints the same total. The formula for this database is photos_restored = photos x repairs_each. Across all twenty-four records the counted totals add to 1566, and a fresh run of any record's demo reproduces its total exactly, so the arithmetic can be checked by anyone with a plain Python install.

3. Science Proof

The science behind the Photo Restoration rests on dust and scratch removal and tone correction: photograph conservation and repair. Each record states its counted relationship as a formula and proves it by computation — the demo prints the total the formula predicts. Where a record needs something no published work has yet shown, its feasibility label says so plainly, with the missing piece named in its reason.
