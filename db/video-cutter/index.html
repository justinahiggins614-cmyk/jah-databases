1. Description

A video cutter keeps part of a video and drops the rest: pick a start time and an end time, and the kept part is the cut. The demo on this page cuts a seeded frame list locally: a seeded clip of 30 seconds, 900 frames and 30 megabytes. A full transcode needs a backend, and that is BLOCKED; this is a local partial demo, and it says so. You can also upload your own clip, which is read in the browser and never leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A clip of 30 seconds has 900 frames at 30 frames a second. Cutting from 5 seconds to 15 seconds keeps 10 seconds. A cut of 10 seconds at 30 frames a second has 300 frames. A clip of 10 seconds at 8 megabits a second is 10 megabytes. Cutting frames 151 to 450 keeps 300 frames.

3. Science proof

A video cutter works because a video is frames in order, and a cut keeps the frames between two times: from 5 seconds to 15 seconds keeps 10 seconds and 300 frames at 30 frames a second. A shorter cut is a smaller file: 10 seconds at 8 megabits a second is 10 megabytes. That is why the local demo cuts the seeded frame list and shows the count, and says plainly that a full transcode needs a backend and is BLOCKED.
