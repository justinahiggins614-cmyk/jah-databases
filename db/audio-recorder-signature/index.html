1. Description

An audio recorder captures sound and keeps it as a clip, in the browser: press Start, allow the microphone, and press Stop to save. The demo on this page records for real through the browser media API, and it is local-demo-only: a real microphone is needed, and no recording is ever faked. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A clip of 10 seconds at 44100 samples a second has 441000 samples. A clip of 441000 samples at 16 bits each is 882000 bytes. Two clips: 10 seconds and 5 seconds, 15 seconds in all. Stereo to mono halves 882000 bytes to 441000 bytes.

3. Science proof

An audio recorder works because sound is samples in order, and each sample is a number of bits: 441000 samples at 16 bits each is 882000 bytes, and halving the sample rate halves the samples to 220500. A microphone is needed to record: without one, 0 recordings are made. That is why the page asks for a microphone and says plainly that no recording is faked.
