The Transformer JAH-FAA-000001 · simple

The transformer, introduced by Vaswani and colleagues in 2017, is the architecture behind nearly all modern AI: it processes sequences with self-attention, letting every token weigh every other token, and it trains in parallel, unlike recurrent networks.

The transformer, introduced by Vaswani and colleagues in 2017, is the architecture behind nearly all modern AI: it processes sequences with self-attention, letting every token weigh every other token, and it trains in parallel, unlike recurrent networks. GPT, BERT, Claude and Gemini are all transformers at their core. Its cost is attention's quadratic scaling with sequence length, which is why long contexts are expensive.

The Attention Mechanism JAH-FAA-000002 · intermediate

Attention computes, for each token, a weighted average of all tokens, where the weights come from the similarity of queries and keys.

Attention computes, for each token, a weighted average of all tokens, where the weights come from the similarity of queries and keys. Scaled dot-product attention divides by the square root of the dimension to keep gradients stable. Multi-head attention runs several attentions in parallel, letting different heads track different relationships — syntax in one head, long-range reference in another.

Mixture of Experts JAH-FAA-000003 · intermediate

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models contain many expert sub-networks and a router that sends each token to only a few of them.

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models contain many expert sub-networks and a router that sends each token to only a few of them. Total parameters can reach trillions while active parameters per token stay modest — for example, a model with 8 experts activating 2 per token uses a quarter of its capacity per token. MoE is how the largest models scale without proportional compute, and routing quality is measured, not assumed.

Working demo program

total_B = 671
experts = 8
active = 2

State-Space Models (Mamba) JAH-FAA-000005 · advanced

State-space models such as Mamba (2023) replace attention with a selective state space that compresses the past into a fixed-size state, giving linear scaling with sequence length instead of quadratic.

State-space models such as Mamba (2023) replace attention with a selective state space that compresses the past into a fixed-size state, giving linear scaling with sequence length instead of quadratic. They match transformers on many language tasks and handle very long contexts efficiently, though transformers still lead on tasks needing precise recall of distant details. Mamba is the leading challenger architecture, not yet the standard.

Diffusion Models JAH-FAA-000006 · advanced

Diffusion models generate images, audio and video by learning to reverse a process that adds noise: starting from pure noise and denoising step by step until a coherent output appears.

Diffusion models generate images, audio and video by learning to reverse a process that adds noise: starting from pure noise and denoising step by step until a coherent output appears. They power modern image generators and video models, and they have largely replaced GANs for high-quality generation. Their cost is the many denoising steps, which distillation and faster samplers are reducing.

Neuro-symbolic AI combines neural networks, which learn from data, with symbolic reasoning, which manipulates rules and logic. Spiking Neural Networks JAH-FAA-000008 · advanced

Spiking neural networks communicate with discrete spikes like real neurons, processing events only when they occur, which promises far lower energy use than conventional networks.

Spiking neural networks communicate with discrete spikes like real neurons, processing events only when they occur, which promises far lower energy use than conventional networks. They are the natural software for neuromorphic hardware such as Intel's Loihi. Their difficulty is training: spikes are not differentiable, so training uses surrogate gradients or conversion from conventional networks, and accuracy still lags on most benchmarks.

Neuromorphic Hardware JAH-FAA-000009 · advanced

Neuromorphic hardware mimics the brain's structure: many simple units, local memory, and event-driven communication, instead of a central processor fetching data from distant memory.

Neuromorphic hardware mimics the brain's structure: many simple units, local memory, and event-driven communication, instead of a central processor fetching data from distant memory. Intel's Loihi and IBM's TrueNorth demonstrated the approach, and BrainChip's Akida brings it to the edge. Sparse Activation JAH-FAA-000012 · intermediate

Sparse activation means only a fraction of a network fires for any input — as in mixture-of-experts, or in networks with sparse gating.

Sparse activation means only a fraction of a network fires for any input — as in mixture-of-experts, or in networks with sparse gating. Sparsity promises large compute savings, but unstructured sparsity is hard to accelerate on dense hardware, which is why structured forms such as MoE dominate in practice.

Scaling Laws JAH-FAA-000013 · advanced

Neural scaling laws, fitted by Kaplan and colleagues in 2020 and refined by Chinchilla in 2022, describe how loss falls as a power law in parameters, data and compute.

Neural scaling laws, fitted by Kaplan and colleagues in 2020 and refined by Chinchilla in 2022, describe how loss falls as a power law in parameters, data and compute. Chinchilla showed that many large models were undertrained: a smaller model trained on more data beats a larger one trained on less, for the same compute. Scaling laws are empirical fits to measured runs, and they guide how training budgets are spent — but they do not guarantee that scaling alone reaches new capabilities.

World models are AI systems that learn a predictive model of an environment — how actions change states — and then plan inside the model instead of the real world.

World models are AI systems that learn a predictive model of an environment — how actions change states — and then plan inside the model instead of the real world. They are central to model-based reinforcement learning and to video generation models that predict future frames. Continual Learning JAH-FAA-000019 · advanced

Continual learning means learning new tasks without forgetting old ones — the opposite of catastrophic forgetting, where training on a new task erases earlier knowledge.

Continual learning means learning new tasks without forgetting old ones — the opposite of catastrophic forgetting, where training on a new task erases earlier knowledge. Methods include replaying old examples, regularizing important weights, and growing the network. Humans learn continually; AI systems mostly do not, and closing that gap is essential for agents that live and work over months and years.

Evaluation and Benchmarks JAH-FAA-000021 · intermediate

Evaluation measures what AI systems can actually do, with benchmarks such as MMLU (general knowledge), HumanEval (coding), GSM8K (mathematics) and SWE-bench (real software tasks).

Evaluation measures what AI systems can actually do, with benchmarks such as MMLU (general knowledge), HumanEval (coding), GSM8K (mathematics) and SWE-bench (real software tasks). Benchmarks saturate as models improve, and they can be gamed by training on test data, which is why evaluation now includes held-out tests, human judgement, and task-based measurement. A benchmark score is a measurement under stated conditions, not a verdict on intelligence.
