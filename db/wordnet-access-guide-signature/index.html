Science proof

WordNet is lexicography as a graph: a synset is a concept, a word is a lexicalisation of one or more concepts, and relations (hypernym, hyponym, meronym, antonym) are edges between concepts. Lexicography gives the working rules: a gloss defines the concept, the example sentences show use, and the hypernym chain gives the concept's genus (a dalmatian is a dog, a dog is a canine, a canine is a carnivore, and so on up to entity). Word-sense disambiguation is the task of choosing the right synset for a word in context, and the hypernym chain is the main evidence for that choice. For this aspect (What It Is and Its Scope), the same lexicography rules decide what counts as a correct answer: the pattern must match the model, and the result must be checkable against the source.

It grew into the standard English lexical resource for computational linguistics, used for word-sense disambiguation, information retrieval, and machine translation. Princeton's latest release was in 2011; the Open English WordNet project now publishes annual editions.

Science proof

WordNet is lexicography as a graph: a synset is a concept, a word is a lexicalisation of one or more concepts, and relations (hypernym, hyponym, meronym, antonym) are edges between concepts. Lexicography gives the working rules: a gloss defines the concept, the example sentences show use, and the hypernym chain gives the concept's genus (a dalmatian is a dog, a dog is a canine, a canine is a carnivore, and so on up to entity). Word-sense disambiguation is the task of choosing the right synset for a word in context, and the hypernym chain is the main evidence for that choice.
