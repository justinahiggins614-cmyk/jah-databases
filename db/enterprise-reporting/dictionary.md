# Dictionary entries — The Enterprise Reporting Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## report

A written summary of an enterprise's state over a stated period, with every figure traceable to the record it came from, kept so the period can be checked later.

## reporting period

The span of time a report covers, named with its first and last day, so that two reports can only be compared when their periods match.

## key figure

One number a report watches closely, such as sales or costs, chosen because it shows the health of the enterprise at a glance, and counted the same way every period.

## dashboard

A single page that shows the enterprise's key figures together, kept current, so that anyone who looks can see how the enterprise stands without reading the whole report.

## data warehouse

The enterprise's store of past records, kept in order, from which reports are drawn, so that every figure in a report can be traced back to its source.

## source record

The original record a reported figure comes from, named in the report, so that any figure can be checked by going back to where it was first written.

## variance

The difference between a figure and the figure it was measured against, such as a plan or the same period last year, shown with its size and its direction.

## trend

The direction of a figure over several periods, read from the figures in order, so that a slow rise or fall can be seen before it becomes a crisis.

## consolidation

Bringing the figures of several parts of an enterprise into one report, with the parts named and their figures added by the same rules, so the whole is the sum of its parts.

## narrative

The written words of a report, saying what the figures mean, what changed, and why, so the numbers are read with their story and not alone.

## distribution

The act of sending a finished report to the people who must read it, with the list of readers kept, so that no reader who needs the report goes without it.

## archive

The kept set of past reports, filed in order, so that any past period can be re-read and any figure re-checked against the report that first carried it.
