# Dictionary entries — The Online Storefront Creation Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## storefront

The public face of an online shop: its pages, its catalogue, and its name, kept together, so that a visitor can see what the shop sells and who keeps it.

## catalogue

The ordered list of everything a shop sells, each with its description and its price, kept current, so that the shop shows only what it truly has to sell.

## product page

One page for one product, holding its description, its price, and its images, so that a visitor can judge that product alone, without the rest of the shop.

## template

A ready-made pattern for a storefront's pages, chosen by the keeper, so that the shop can be built from a sound pattern instead of from nothing.

## theme

The look of a storefront — its colours, its type, and its layout — chosen as one, so that every page of the shop looks like it belongs to the same shop.

## domain

The name by which a storefront is found on the network, recorded exactly, so that visitors who know the name can reach the shop without wandering.

## listing

One product as it appears in the catalogue, with its description and price, so that the shop's goods can be counted, checked, and found one by one.

## inventory

The count of what a shop holds for sale, kept beside the catalogue, so that the shop never offers what it does not have and never hides what it does.

## checkout

The pages where a visitor's chosen goods are gathered and the order is placed, kept apart from the catalogue, so that browsing and buying are never confused.

## order

One visitor's request for goods, recorded with what was asked, when, and by whom, so that the shop can answer it and the record can show it was answered.

## store owner

The person or business that keeps a storefront, named in the record, so that every shop can be traced to the keeper who answers for it.

## launch

The act of opening a finished storefront to visitors, on a named date, after every part has been checked, so that the shop opens whole and not half-built.
