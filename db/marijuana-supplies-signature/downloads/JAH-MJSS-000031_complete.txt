SCIENCE PROOF
The glass bowl works by two simple ideas: a cup and a taper. The cup is a frustum, wide at the rim and narrow at the bottom, so the material sits in a cone that funnels smoke toward the hole. The hole at the bottom is small, 3 millimetres in the common example, so ground material cannot fall straight through, and a screen can be fitted over it to stop finer particles. The taper is the ground-glass joint: two tapered surfaces pressed together seal by friction, because the taper angle makes the contact area large and the glass faces grip each other. The joint size sets the airflow ceiling: an 18 mm joint has about 1.65 times the cross-sectional area of a 14 mm joint, so the same draw meets less restriction through the larger joint, and 18 mm bowls usually have bigger cups. Borosilicate glass is used because the cup sits where the material burns, and a glass that tolerates heat without cracking is what survives that spot. The stem below the cup carries the smoke into the downstem, and its bore sets how easily the smoke flows onward. HISTORY
The glass bowl is the small detachable cup that holds the material on a water pipe, and its history is the history of the ground-glass joint, borrowed from laboratory glassware. Two tapered glass surfaces, one male and one female, meet and seal by friction alone, with no threading, gasket, or sealant, and the joint size names the nominal diameter where the two pieces meet. Three sizes cover almost the whole market, 10 mm, 14 mm, and 18 mm, with 14 mm the everyday standard on most beakers and straight tubes and 18 mm common on larger bongs. The sizes are not interchangeable: a 14 mm bowl drops loosely into an 18 mm joint and leaks air, and an 18 mm bowl will not fit a 14 mm joint at all. Adapters can bridge a mismatch, but the joint itself sets which bowls, downstems, and ash catchers fit a piece. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape.
