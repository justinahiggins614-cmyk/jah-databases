SCIENCE PROOF
An airtight glass jar protects its contents by controlling four enemies: air, light, heat and moisture. Oxygen in air slowly oxidises cannabinoids, so limiting air exchange slows that loss; the lid gasket is the seal that does it, compressing against the glass rim so that air can only enter when the jar is opened. Glass itself is a nearly perfect barrier: it is non-porous, so water vapour and oxygen do not diffuse through the walls the way they slowly do through plastic, and it is chemically inert, so it neither absorbs terpenes nor gives off flavours. Light is the worst enemy of all: a 2019 study in the Journal of Pharmacy and Pharmacology found that light, particularly ultraviolet, was the single greatest factor in cannabinoid degradation across the storage conditions tested, because ultraviolet photons carry enough energy to break chemical bonds. Amber glass absorbs most ultraviolet light before it reaches the contents, which is why it protects better than clear glass. Static electricity matters too: plastic builds up charge and pulls fine trichomes off flower, while glass does not. The jar does not stop time, but by holding the air still, dark and at a steady humidity, it slows every decay process at once.

MATH PROOF
The quart jar holds 946 millilitres, which is 946 cubic centimetres. Filled to the recommended 75 percent, the contents occupy 946 x 0.75 = 709.5 cubic centimetres and the headspace air is 946 - 709.5 = 236.5 cubic centimetres. Treating the jar as a cylinder 9.5 centimetres in diameter, its radius is 4.75 centimetres and its cross-sectional area is pi times radius squared: 3.1416 x 4.75 x 4.75 = 70.9 square centimetres. Dividing volume by area gives the height: 946 / 70.9 = 13.3 centimetres, close to the measured 14 centimetres once the shoulder and base thickness are allowed for. At 20 degrees Celsius, air at 62 percent relative humidity holds about 10.7 grams of water vapour per cubic metre; the headspace is 236.5 cubic centimetres, which is 0.0002365 cubic metres, so it holds 0.0002365 x 10.7 = 0.00253 grams of water vapour, about two and a half milligrams. The pint jar at 473 millilitres is exactly half the quart, and the half-gallon at 1893 millilitres is 1893 / 946 = 2.0 times the quart.

HISTORY
Glass jars for preserving food are an old technology: the mason jar, with its two-piece lid of a flat disc and a screw band, was patented in the nineteenth century and became the standard home canning jar because glass does not react with food and the lid seals tightly. Cannabis storage adopted the same jar, because the problems are the same: air, light, heat and moisture all degrade the contents over time. Guides now call the mason jar the gold standard for cannabis storage, and makers have added amber and violet glass, rubber gaskets and silicone sleeves to block ultraviolet light. The jar itself has not changed much in a century; what changed is the understanding of what to put inside it, including humidity packs and the rule to fill the jar no more than three quarters full so air can circulate. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.
