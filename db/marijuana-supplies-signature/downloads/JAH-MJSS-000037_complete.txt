SCIENCE PROOF
A ceramic ashtray works by heat resistance, mass, and a sealed surface. Fired stoneware has a density near 2.4 grams per cubic centimetre and a specific heat near 0.85 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish of about 469 grams stores roughly 79,800 joules when heated by 200 kelvin, which is why the dish feels cool for a long time after an ember lands in it. Ceramic is a poor thermal conductor, so heat from an ember stays local instead of racing through the dish to the table, and the fired glaze seals the porous clay body so tar and smell sit on the surface instead of soaking in. The glaze is a thin glass skin, and because it is fired onto the clay it does not peel or fade with washing, which is why a glazed ashtray can be washed in a dishwasher. The bowl is a round cylinder, so its capacity is pi times radius squared times depth, and the rim notches are U-shaped cuts that hold a cigarette by gravity. HISTORY
The ceramic ashtray is as old as the ashtray itself, because pottery was one of the first fire-retardant materials a household could make, and the earliest ashtrays were simple clay dishes. The word ashtray came into use around 1920, and by the start of the twentieth century ceramic ashtrays were already common, from plain stoneware dishes to porcelain trays with floral decoration. Ceramic won the table for the same reasons it won the kitchen: it tolerates heat, it takes glaze in any colour, and a glazed surface wipes clean. Over the twentieth century the form split into round, square, and slab shapes, into earthenware, stoneware, and porcelain bodies, and into hand-built and wheel-thrown making, while the job stayed the same. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. """Demo: ceramic ashtray capacity and heat storage — Signature Ceramic Ashtray."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 140.0; h = 37.0; depth = 30.0; wall = 7.0; rests = 4; rho = 2.4; cp = 0.85; dT = 200.0
    outer_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    cap_mm3 = math.pi * ((d - 2.0 * wall) / 2.0) ** 2 * depth
    cap_cm3 = cap_mm3 / 1000.0
    shell_mm3 = outer_mm3 - cap_mm3
    shell_cm3 = shell_mm3 / 1000.0
    mass_g = shell_cm3 * rho
    heat_J = mass_g * cp * dT
    return {"outer_volume_mm3": round(outer_mm3, 2), "capacity_mm3": round(cap_mm3, 2), "capacity_cm3": round(cap_cm3, 2), "shell_volume_cm3": round(shell_cm3, 2), "ceramic_mass_g": round(mass_g, 2), "heat_to_200C_J": round(heat_J, 2), "rim_notches": rests}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['outer_volume_mm3', 'capacity_mm3', 'capacity_cm3', 'shell_volume_cm3', 'ceramic_mass_g', 'heat_to_200C_J', 'rim_notches']:
