SCIENCE PROOF
A torch lighter is a small premixed combustion engine. Butane is stored as a liquid under its own vapour pressure, and when the valve opens it boils into gas; the gas expands through a narrow jet, and the fast-moving stream drags surrounding air with it through the venturi, so fuel and air arrive at the nozzle already mixed in roughly the proportion that burns completely. Complete combustion of butane follows the equation 2 C4H10 + 13 O2 giving 8 CO2 + 10 H2O, and the energy released is about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram of butane. Because the mixture is premixed, the flame is short and blue: the blue colour is the light of excited molecules in a clean, nearly soot-free burn, while a yellow soft flame is coloured by glowing soot particles from incomplete combustion. The flame is hottest at its outer envelope, where mixing with outside air is complete, and cooler in the inner cone, where fresh mixture is still heating up. Wind resistance comes from speed: the jet leaves the nozzle fast enough that a side wind cannot easily blow the burning zone away from the nozzle. The piezoelectric igniter works because striking certain crystals produces a voltage high enough to spark across a small gap, with no battery and no flint to wear out.

MATH PROOF
The reference lighter holds 8 grams of butane, which is 0.008 kilograms. Butane releases about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram, so the full tank holds 0.008 x 49.5 = 0.396 megajoules, or 396 kilojoules of chemical energy. If the lighter burns butane at 0.05 grams per second, the tank lasts 8 / 0.05 = 160 seconds of continuous flame, and the power output is 0.00005 kilograms per second x 49.5 megajoules per kilogram = 0.002475 megajoules per second, or 2475 watts while it is lit. HISTORY
The pocket lighter descends from the flint and steel fire kit, but the modern butane lighter arrived in the twentieth century when liquefied gas could be stored safely in a small sealed tank. A standard soft-flame lighter lets butane diffuse into the surrounding air and burns it as a tall yellow flame. The torch lighter is a later refinement: a small jet nozzle and a venturi draw air into the gas stream before ignition, so the fuel and air are already mixed when they burn. Piezoelectric ignition, a crystal that produces a spark when struck, replaced the flint wheel on most torch lighters. The result is a short, blue, hissing flame that keeps burning in wind that would blow a soft flame out, and it is used for lighting in wind, for craft work, and anywhere a small, precise, very hot flame is needed. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.
