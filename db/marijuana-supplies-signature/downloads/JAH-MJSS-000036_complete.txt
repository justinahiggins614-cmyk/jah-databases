SCIENCE PROOF
A glass ashtray works by heat resistance, mass, and surface chemistry. Borosilicate glass has a coefficient of linear thermal expansion of 3.3 times ten to the minus six per kelvin, about a third of ordinary soda-lime glass, so a hot ember on one side and a cool table on the other does not set up enough expansion difference to crack it. Its density is 2.23 grams per cubic centimetre and its specific heat is 0.83 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish of about 279 grams absorbs heat from embers without its own temperature rising fast, and its thermal conductivity near 1.2 watts per metre kelvin spreads that heat slowly through the wall. Glass is non-porous, so ash and tar sit on the surface instead of soaking in, which is why a glass ashtray washes clean while a wooden one keeps its smell. HISTORY
The glass ashtray is one of the oldest ashtray forms, because glass has been the default fire-retardant table material since ashtrays became common household objects in the early twentieth century. The word ashtray came into use around 1920, and the most common design from the start was a shallow cylinder with a flat base, made of glass, pottery, metal, or stone, with notches in the rim for resting a cigarette. Glass won the table because it is non-porous, easy to clean, and does not hold odour, and because a heavy glass dish stays put where a light one slides. Over the twentieth century the form split into round, square, and geometric shapes, into clear, amber, and frosted glass, and into plain and cut-glass decoration, while the job stayed the same. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. The sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage, and the history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 130.0; h = 30.0; wall = 5.0; rests = 4; rho = 2.23; alpha = 3.3e-06; dT = 200.0
    cap_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    cap_cm3 = cap_mm3 / 1000.0
    outer_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * (h + wall)
    inner_mm3 = math.pi * ((d - 2.0 * wall) / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    shell_cm3 = (outer_mm3 - inner_mm3) / 1000.0
    mass_g = shell_cm3 * rho
    expand_mm = alpha * d * dT
    return {"capacity_mm3": round(cap_mm3, 2), "capacity_cm3": round(cap_cm3, 2), "shell_volume_cm3": round(shell_cm3, 2), "glass_mass_g": round(mass_g, 2), "thermal_expansion_mm": round(expand_mm, 2), "rim_notches": rests}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['capacity_mm3', 'capacity_cm3', 'shell_volume_cm3', 'glass_mass_g', 'thermal_expansion_mm', 'rim_notches']:
