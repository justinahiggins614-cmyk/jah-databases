SCIENCE PROOF
A smell-proof bag works by adsorption, which is different from absorption: the odour molecules do not soak into the carbon, they stick to its surface. Activated carbon is made by heating carbon-rich material so that it becomes riddled with microscopic pores, giving a single gram an internal surface area of hundreds of square metres. Volatile organic compounds, the small molecules that carry aroma, are attracted to that surface by weak van der Waals forces, and once they stick they stay stuck until heat or sunlight gives them enough energy to leave. That is why the lining can be reactivated in direct sunlight: the heat drives the trapped molecules off and frees the pores again. The zip and the outer shell matter too, but in a supporting role: they slow the bulk flow of air so that the air that does move must pass through the carbon layer, where the odour is stripped out. No fabric bag is perfectly airtight, so the claim is not that no air escapes, but that the air which escapes has had its odour molecules removed. The carbon eventually saturates, because the pore surface is finite, and that is why the bag has a working life of about one to two years.

MATH PROOF
The reference pouch measures 20 centimetres by 14 centimetres, so one face has an area of 20 x 14 = 280 square centimetres, and the two faces together give 560 square centimetres of carbon lining. If the pouch is about 2 centimetres thick when filled, its volume is roughly 20 x 14 x 2 = 560 cubic centimetres, or 0.56 litres, close to the published 0.5-litre volume of the similar Revelry Confidant pouch. A duffle at 20 by 11 by 11 inches converts to centimetres at 2.54 centimetres per inch: 50.8 x 27.9 x 27.9 centimetres, giving a volume of 50.8 x 27.9 x 27.9 = 39547 cubic centimetres, or 39.5 litres, which is 39.5 / 0.56 = 70.5 times the volume of the reference pouch. If a carbon lining holds about 0.2 grams of odour compounds per gram of carbon and the pouch lining contains 10 grams of carbon, it can trap 10 x 0.2 = 2.0 grams before it saturates. At a working life of 1 to 2 years, the midpoint is 1.5 years, or 547 days, so the average loading is 2.0 / 547 = 0.0037 grams per day.

HISTORY
Smell-proof storage grew out of two older technologies: the activated carbon filter, used for a century in gas masks and water filters, and the zip-sealed pouch. Early pouches relied on thick fabric and tight zips alone, which slow smells but cannot stop them, because fabric and zips cannot form a truly airtight seal. The carbon lining changed that: activated carbon has an enormous internal surface area, and odour molecules stick to it as air passes through. Brands such as Revelry, with a three-layer carbon filter system, and ONGROK, with activated bamboo carbon linings, turned the idea into everyday carry, from a 4 by 6 inch wallet to a 20 by 11 by 11 inch duffle. Combination locks were added so the bag is also a small secure container, and mylar pouches offered a different approach, blocking odour as a barrier film rather than adsorbing it. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.
