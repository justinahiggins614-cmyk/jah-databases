SCIENCE PROOF
A mini bong filters smoke through water in a small chamber, and its physics is the same as a full-size bong with less room for error. Smoke from the bowl travels down the downstem and bubbles through water, which cools it and traps some particles before the smoke rises through the neck. The water depth matters: the sources say just enough to submerge the downstem slits by about half an inch, roughly an inch of water total in most mini bongs, because too much water floods the stem and too little leaves the slits exposed. Borosilicate glass is the standard material because its low thermal expansion resists cracking under the repeated heating of the bowl joint and the cooling of the water chamber, while thin soft glass cracks under the same stress. The beaker base matters for stability: a wide foot lowers the centre of gravity and holds more water than a straight tube of the same height, which is why the demo computes the water volume in the wide base separately from the narrow tube above it. The chamber volume sets the size of a single draw: the smoke path is short, the chamber clears quickly, and the demo checks that the water volume is a sensible fraction of the total chamber, not more than the chamber can hold. Small water pipes are not new — bubblers and pocket pipes have been sold for decades — but the mini bong as a named type grew with borosilicate glassmaking, which made a thin, heat-resistant tube cheap enough to shrink without cracking.
