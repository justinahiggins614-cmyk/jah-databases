1. Description and history

The Signature the butane torch lighter is a Signature-made design study of a refillable pocket lighter that premixes butane gas with air and burns it as a narrow, hot, wind-resistant blue jet flame, drawn as a design for the Signature edition of the marijuana supplies database. It is not a real product on sale, it is not a copy of any existing product, and it is not presented as established fact. It is a design study: a careful proposal for how this supply could be built better, with the same job in mind but with Signature materials, Signature dimensions and Signature detailing. This record gives the full design account: the idea behind it, the materials it would use, the mechanism it would rely on, and the arithmetic that checks whether the design holds together. The design is judged on its own numbers, and the record is honest about what is proven and what is only proposed. Nothing here is a medical claim and nothing here gives a dose. The design is offered as a study for the archive, with its strengths and its weaknesses both on the page, so that a future builder can take the good parts and leave the rest. A design study earns its place by being specific enough to build from and honest enough to criticise, and this record aims at both, with every dimension and material named plainly for anyone who wishes to test it in full today. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

The pocket lighter descends from the flint and steel fire kit, but the modern butane lighter arrived in the twentieth century when liquefied gas could be stored safely in a small sealed tank. A standard soft-flame lighter lets butane diffuse into the surrounding air and burns it as a tall yellow flame. The torch lighter is a later refinement: a small jet nozzle and a venturi draw air into the gas stream before ignition, so the fuel and air are already mixed when they burn. Piezoelectric ignition, a crystal that produces a spark when struck, replaced the flint wheel on most torch lighters. The result is a short, blue, hissing flame that keeps burning in wind that would blow a soft flame out, and it is used for lighting in wind, for craft work, and anywhere a small, precise, very hot flame is needed. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.

The problem it solves

The design problem for the Signature the butane torch lighter is the same problem the public version faces, stated as a design brief. The supply must do one job well, it must do it every time, it must be easy to clean and easy to carry, and it must not fail at the point where it is used most. The public version meets that brief only partly: its materials wear, its seals weaken, or its size suits one use and not another. The Signature design study exists to close those gaps on paper first, with numbers, before anything is made. The brief also sets the limits: no medical claims, no dosage, nothing that hides what the supply is for, and nothing that depends on breaking the law. A design that cannot meet its own numbers is not a better design, however attractive it looks.

Method

The Signature method is to start from the public supply, keep the mechanism that works, and redesign the parts that wear out or waste material. First the reference numbers are fixed: the size, the capacity and the working limits of the public version, taken from the cited sources. Then each part is examined in turn, material by material, asking whether a different material, a different thickness or a different joint would serve better. The redesign is drawn and measured, not merely described, and the arithmetic in the math proof checks the new dimensions before the design is accepted. Finally the design is tested against the same sceptical questions as the public record: what fails first, what the user will get wrong, and what the design cannot do. Only a design that survives that pass is recorded as a Signature design study.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference lighter holds 8 grams of butane, which is 0.008 kilograms. Butane releases about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram, so the full tank holds 0.008 x 49.5 = 0.396 megajoules, or 396 kilojoules of chemical energy. If the lighter burns butane at 0.05 grams per second, the tank lasts 8 / 0.05 = 160 seconds of continuous flame, and the power output is 0.00005 kilograms per second x 49.5 megajoules per kilogram = 0.002475 megajoules per second, or 2475 watts while it is lit. The torch flame temperature of 1430 degrees Celsius converts to Fahrenheit as 1430 x 9 / 5 + 32 = 2606 degrees Fahrenheit. A soft butane flame at 1000 degrees Celsius is 1000 x 9 / 5 + 32 = 1832 degrees Fahrenheit, so the torch flame is 1430 - 1000 = 430 degrees Celsius hotter. If a task needs 10 kilojoules of heat, the butane required is 10 / 49500 = 0.000202 kilograms, or 0.202 grams, which is 0.202 / 8 = 0.025 of a full tank, about two and a half percent.

4. Science proof

A torch lighter is a small premixed combustion engine. Butane is stored as a liquid under its own vapour pressure, and when the valve opens it boils into gas; the gas expands through a narrow jet, and the fast-moving stream drags surrounding air with it through the venturi, so fuel and air arrive at the nozzle already mixed in roughly the proportion that burns completely. Complete combustion of butane follows the equation 2 C4H10 + 13 O2 giving 8 CO2 + 10 H2O, and the energy released is about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram of butane. Because the mixture is premixed, the flame is short and blue: the blue colour is the light of excited molecules in a clean, nearly soot-free burn, while a yellow soft flame is coloured by glowing soot particles from incomplete combustion. The flame is hottest at its outer envelope, where mixing with outside air is complete, and cooler in the inner cone, where fresh mixture is still heating up. Wind resistance comes from speed: the jet leaves the nozzle fast enough that a side wind cannot easily blow the burning zone away from the nozzle. The piezoelectric igniter works because striking certain crystals produces a voltage high enough to spark across a small gap, with no battery and no flint to wear out.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view of this design study is that a drawing is not a product, and a design that has never been built has never failed in the ways real products fail. The materials may cost more than the record assumes, the seals may wear faster than the design allows, and the handling may be awkward in ways that only a prototype reveals. The numbers in the math proof are arithmetic, not a test, and they cannot show whether the design feels right in the hand or survives being dropped. The record accepts all of that: the design study is a proposal, and it is labelled as one, so that nobody mistakes it for a tested product. The honest next step is a prototype, a real test, and a revision, and until that happens the design should be read as a careful idea, not as a finished thing.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
fuel_mass_g = 8.0
energy_mj_per_kg = 49.5
burn_rate_g_per_s = 0.05
flame_temp_c = 1430.0
soft_temp_c = 1000.0
energy_kj = fuel_mass_g / 1000.0 * energy_mj_per_kg * 1000.0
burn_seconds = fuel_mass_g / burn_rate_g_per_s
power_w = burn_rate_g_per_s / 1000.0 * energy_mj_per_kg * 1000000.0
flame_temp_f = flame_temp_c * 9.0 / 5.0 + 32.0
soft_temp_f = soft_temp_c * 9.0 / 5.0 + 32.0
butane_for_10kj_g = 10.0 / (energy_mj_per_kg * 1000.0) * 1000.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.5, 0.5)
    return {"tank_energy_kj": round(energy_kj, 2), "burn_seconds": round(burn_seconds, 2), "power_w": round(power_w, 2), "flame_temp_f": round(flame_temp_f + jitter, 2), "soft_flame_temp_f": round(soft_temp_f, 2), "butane_for_10kj_g": round(butane_for_10kj_g, 3)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Tank energy (kJ): %.2f" % r1["tank_energy_kj"])
print("Burn time (s): %.2f" % r1["burn_seconds"])
print("Power while lit (W): %.2f" % r1["power_w"])
print("Flame temperature (F): %.2f" % r1["flame_temp_f"])
print("Soft flame temperature (F): %.2f" % r1["soft_flame_temp_f"])
print("Butane for 10 kJ (g): %.3f" % r1["butane_for_10kj_g"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
