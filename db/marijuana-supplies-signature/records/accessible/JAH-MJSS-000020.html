1. Description and history

The Glass Dab Rig — Signature edition — is a Signature-made design study of the same supply, drawn up as a new product rather than a report on an existing one. This design keeps the same job, glass dab rig water pipe for concentrates, but reworks the parts as a single matched set: a borosilicate glass body with a water chamber, a fixed or removable downstem, a percolator, a quartz banger, a carb cap, and a bent neck mouthpiece. The Signature design specifies borosilicate glass body, fused quartz banger, stainless steel or titanium carb cap option, silicone-free glass joints throughout, with each part sized to the others so that no tool in the case is a loose fit for its task. The design dimensions are overall height 200 mm; chamber diameter 70 mm; water fill 60 ml covering the percolator slits by 12 mm; banger 25 mm across with a 20 mm deep bucket; joint size 14 mm. In the Signature design, concentrate placed on the heated banger vaporises, the vapour is drawn down the stem into the water chamber, and the percolator breaks the vapour into many small bubbles so it cools against the water before rising up the neck. Fill with clean water just above the percolator slits, heat the banger, let it cool to the working range, place the concentrate in the banger, cap it, and draw slowly so the water bubbles without splashback. Maintenance of the design follows the same rules as the public version. Change the water after every session, clean the banger with a cotton swab while it is still warm, soak the glass in isopropyl alcohol and salt when residue builds, and never shake a rig hard around a percolator, since a hard shake can snap thin glass. Planned variants of the Signature design are the mini rig 150 mm tall for travel; recycler rig that loops water through a second chamber; showerhead-perc rig; honeycomb-perc rig. This is a design study, not a real product and not an established fact: no Signature edition unit has been manufactured, tested, or sold, and the dimensions and figures in this record are design targets, not measured results. The problem it solves

The Signature design starts from the same problem as the public record, residue that narrows airways and thickens bowl cake, but it treats the problem as a design brief rather than a maintenance chore. The brief asks for a kit in which every tool is sized to the space it works in, so that no step of the clean needs a borrowed or improvised tool. The design problem has three parts. First, reach: the longest tool must follow the full length of the airway it serves, with enough length left in the hand to control it. Second, force: the scrubbing parts must be firm enough to lift hardened residue and soft enough not to scratch glass, wood, or glaze. Third, order: the tools must be stored in the sequence they are used, so the kit teaches its own routine. As a design study, this record does not claim the design has been built or tested. It claims only that the geometry works on paper, that the materials are compatible, and that the routine is complete, and it invites a builder to test it before calling it a product.

Method

The Signature method is a design method, not a cleaning routine: it works out the kit on paper before any part is made. It starts from the dimension sheet, overall height 200 mm; chamber diameter 70 mm; water fill 60 ml covering the percolator slits by 12 mm; banger 25 mm across with a 20 mm deep bucket; joint size 14 mm, and checks that each tool reaches the space it must clean, that the case holds every tool in its pocket, and that the materials, borosilicate glass body, fused quartz banger, stainless steel or titanium carb cap option, silicone-free glass joints, are compatible with each other and with the pipes they will touch. Next it assigns each tool one job and one job only, so that the cleaner cleans, the brush brushes, and the reamer reams, and no tool is asked to do work it is not shaped for. The routine is then written as a sequence, left to right across the case, so that a user who follows the pockets follows the method. A physical prototype would still be needed before any claim about how the design feels in the hand or how long it lasts, and this record makes no such claim.

A reach is checked against the airway it must follow, a volume against the bowl or chamber it must fill, an aperture against the particle sizes on either side of it, and a combination count against the number of dials on the lock. The demo performs those checks in code: it takes the design figures from the key figures list, computes the derived quantities, prints each with its name, and then repeats the whole computation with the same fixed seed and prints a SHA-256 digest of both runs. Because the seed is fixed, the two digests match, which shows the computation is reproducible. That is the whole claim of the math proof: the design is arithmetically consistent, and any reader can repeat the check. It does not show that a prototype would pass a physical test, and the record says so plainly, because a design study that pretended its arithmetic was a test would be misleading.

4. Science proof

The Signature design uses the same science as the public version, because the physics does not change when a product is redesigned, and the design study checks that the chosen materials and dimensions respect it. The solvent chemistry is unchanged: isopropyl alcohol dissolves resin because its polar hydroxyl group and its carbon chain can both interact with the tar-like film, and coarse salt stays undissolved in alcohol, so it keeps its edges and scours. The mechanical science is also unchanged: bristles clean by friction, a mesh separates by size, a seal contains odour by compression, and a lock resists opening by counting combinations. What the design adds is a check that the numbers fit the science. The reach must exceed the airway, the aperture must sit between the particle sizes it should pass and the ones it should hold back, the water depth must cover the percolator without reaching the mouthpiece, and the heat capacity of a tool must be small enough that it does not steal the heat of the surface it touches. Where the public record reports what guides have observed, the Signature record states what the design intends, and it labels the difference plainly. The science proof therefore proves the design is consistent with known physics, not that it has been tested, and the demo computes the numbers that show that consistency.

Skeptical analysis

A design study deserves the same scepticism as a product claim, and more, because nothing has been built yet. First, paper geometry is not proof of comfort: a handle that measures well can still feel wrong, and a case that packs neatly on a drawing can be awkward to open with one hand. Second, material compatibility on paper is not durability in use, and a mesh that is rated for a given aperture can still stretch, a seal can still take a set, and a lock can still jam. Third, the arithmetic in this record checks dimensions and counts, not performance: it shows that a cleaner reaches, that a screen passes a particle, or that a lock has a thousand combinations, and it does not show that the clean is faster or the sift is purer. Fourth, the design inherits the limits of the public version it is based on, including the material warnings, and it adds a new one, which is that an unbuilt design has no failure history to learn from. The honest use of this record is as a starting drawing for a prototype, to be tested, corrected, and only then called a product.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, math, random

CHAMBER_DIA_MM = 70
WATER_DEPTH_MM = 16
PERC_SLITS = 12
BUBBLES_PER_SLIT = 8
VAPOUR_TEMP_C = 220
WATER_TEMP_C = 20

def compute(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    radius = CHAMBER_DIA_MM / 2.0
    water_ml = round(math.pi * radius ** 2 * WATER_DEPTH_MM / 1000.0, 1)
    bubble_dia_mm = 3.0
    bubble_area = math.pi * bubble_dia_mm ** 2
    bubbles = PERC_SLITS * BUBBLES_PER_SLIT
    total_bubble_area = round(bubbles * bubble_area, 1)
    water_kg = water_ml / 1000.0
    vapour_drop_c = 85.0
    heat_j = round(water_kg * 4186.0 * vapour_drop_c, 1)
    out_temp = VAPOUR_TEMP_C - vapour_drop_c
    splash_margin = round(rng.uniform(4.0, 6.0), 1)
    out = {
        "chamber_diameter_mm": CHAMBER_DIA_MM,
        "water_depth_mm": WATER_DEPTH_MM,
        "water_volume_ml": water_ml,
        "percolator_slits": PERC_SLITS,
        "bubbles_per_draw": bubbles,
        "total_bubble_area_mm2": total_bubble_area,
        "vapour_in_temp_c": VAPOUR_TEMP_C,
        "vapour_out_temp_c": out_temp,
        "heat_absorbed_by_water_j": heat_j,
        "splash_margin_mm": splash_margin,
    }
    return out

def main():
    first = compute(7)
    second = compute(7)
    for key, value in first.items():
        print(f"{key}: {value}")
    d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(first, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
    d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(second, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
    print(f"digest_run_1: {d1}")
    print(f"digest_run_2: {d2}")
    print("Deterministic: MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "Deterministic: MISMATCH")

main()
