1. Description and history

The Signature Cascade Beaker Bong is a Signature-made design study for beaker bong, a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — smoke from the bowl travels down a stem below the water line in a wide flared base, bubbles up through the water, collects in the tall neck, and is drawn to the mouthpiece — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature borosilicate glass with a thickened 5 mm base wall; stepped ice pinches; glass downstem and bowl. The design is sized at 315 mm tall; flared base 110 mm diameter at the foot; neck 54 mm diameter; water depth 40 mm in the base, which sets every number in this record, from the volumes and areas in the demo to the scale variants at the end. The beaker bong takes its shape from the laboratory beaker, the wide-based glass vessel chemists have used since the nineteenth century, and from the water pipe itself, whose word comes from the Thai baung, a wooden cylinder or pipe cut from bamboo. Glass water pipes became common in the twentieth century as borosilicate glass made a heat-resistant, clean-tasting water chamber practical, and a 1981 United States patent (4,253,475) describes the classic form in detail: a single vertical pipe sealed at the bottom, a bowl on a tube leading in near the bottom, and water just above the junction. The beaker base was one of the first variations makers tried, because the flared foot spreads the weight low and wide. Trade guides still call the beaker the most stable shape and the usual first bong, and the wide base holds more water than a straight tube of the same height. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. Trade guides and manufacturer manuals preserve the steps of that change. The history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

Materials and design

This is a Signature design study, not a real product. Borosilicate glass is chosen because it tolerates the hot bowl joint without cracking: its thermal expansion is low, so a hot spot does not pull the cold glass apart. The base wall is made thicker than the neck wall, because the base carries the weight of the water and takes the knocks of being set down, while the neck only carries smoke. Water is the filtration medium because it is cheap, clean, and easy to replace, and because it absorbs heat well: a millilitre of water takes far more heat to warm by one degree than a millilitre of glass. The joints are ground-glass tapers, 14 mm or 18 mm, and the taper itself is the seal: two ground surfaces pressed together by friction, with no gasket and no thread. The downstem seats into the base joint at an angle of about 45 degrees, and its length is set so its tip ends below the water line by about 25 mm. The tolerance that matters is the taper angle: if the downstem taper is too steep it will rock, and if it is too shallow it will wedge and stress the base joint. The outside is left smooth and clear so the water level can be read at a glance, and the rim of the mouthpiece is fire-polished so it has no sharp edge. Ice pinches are three small glass dents pressed into the neck while it is hot, and they must be even, or ice will sit crooked and block the draw. Variants change the wall thickness, the base diameter, and the pinch pattern, but the frustum base and the straight neck stay the same in every version.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = pi * h / 3 * (R^2 + R*r + r^2)

V is the volume of a frustum in cubic millimetres; h is the frustum height in millimetres; R is the wide-end radius in millimetres; r is the narrow-end radius in millimetres.

The demo treats the base as a frustum. With a wide-end radius of 55.0 mm, a narrow-end radius of 27.0 mm, and a base height of 75.0 mm, the base volume is pi times the height divided by 3, times the sum of the wide-end radius squared, the wide-end radius times the narrow-end radius, and the narrow-end radius squared. The water fills the frustum only up to the water depth of 40.0 mm, so the radius at the water line is smaller than the base radius, and the same frustum formula with that radius gives the water volume. The neck is a cylinder with a diameter of 54.0 mm over a height of 240.0 mm, so its volume is pi times the neck radius squared times the neck height. Adding the air above the water in the base to the neck volume gives the chamber air volume, and the program prints each volume in millilitres so the reader can repeat the arithmetic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation. The computation is run twice and the SHA-256 digests of both runs are printed, confirming the result is deterministic.

4. Science proof

The beaker works by geometry. Its base is a frustum, a cone with its tip cut off, so its volume grows faster than its height: the wide bottom holds most of the water while the narrow top keeps the neck slim. Water volume is the frustum volume up to the water line, and the water depth matters because the downstem must end below the surface or smoke will not bubble at all. Bubbles rising through water cool the smoke by contact: each bubble is a sphere of hot gas, and the smaller the bubble the larger its surface area for its volume, so more of the heat passes into the water. The wide base also lowers the centre of gravity, which is why the beaker resists tipping: the mass of water sits low and wide, so the tipping moment needed to lift one edge is larger than for a straight tube of the same height. Borosilicate glass is used because it expands very little when heated, so the hot bowl joint does not crack the cold base, and because it does not give flavour to the smoke. The neck above the water is a reservoir: its volume sets how much smoke one draw can hold before the chamber must be cleared. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Build it yourself

As a design study, the Signature Cascade Beaker Bong has not been built; the steps below describe the closest genuine maker activity for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. A beaker bong cannot be made at home: blowing a borosilicate frustum base with a fused neck and a ground joint is a skilled glassblowing trade, and no home method produces it safely. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought kit and making a stand for it. Buy a beaker base, a downstem, and a bowl in matching joint sizes, and check the fit dry before any water goes in: the downstem should seat firmly without wobble, and its tip should end below the intended water line. Then make a stand from a block of hardwood or a sheet of plywood: cut a circular recess the diameter of the base foot, sand it smooth, and line it with cork or felt so the glass does not rest on bare wood. Measure the water line with a ruler and mark it on the stand, not on the glass, so the fill is the same every time. Test the stand by setting the filled bong in it and pressing the rim gently from the side; it should not rock. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

Home 3D printing cannot make every supply in this database, and this section says plainly where the limit falls. A glass beaker bong is not 3D-printable at home: no home filament prints glass, and a printed plastic base would soften near the hot bowl joint and would not seal to a glass downstem. The printable accessory is a protective stand and base cradle for the bong. Print it in PETG, which tolerates warm water and does not crack as easily as PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 4 perimeters, and 25 percent gyroid infill. Print it flat on its bottom face with no supports, so the circular recess prints cleanly, and add a thin TPU pad insert printed separately for the glass to rest on. 6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: beaker bong water and chamber volumes — Signature Cascade Beaker Bong."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    R = 55.0; r = 27.0; base_h = 75.0; water_h = 40.0; neck_d = 54.0; neck_h = 240.0
    r_water = R - (R - r) * (water_h / base_h)
    water_mm3 = math.pi * water_h / 3.0 * (R * R + R * r_water + r_water * r_water)
    base_mm3 = math.pi * base_h / 3.0 * (R * R + R * r + r * r)
    neck_mm3 = math.pi * (neck_d / 2.0) ** 2 * neck_h
    air_mm3 = (base_mm3 - water_mm3) + neck_mm3
    return {"water_volume_mL": round(water_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "base_volume_mL": round(base_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "neck_volume_mL": round(neck_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "chamber_air_mL": round(air_mm3 / 1000.0, 2), "water_depth_mm": water_h, "base_diameter_mm": 2 * R}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["water_volume_mL", "base_volume_mL", "neck_volume_mL", "chamber_air_mL", "water_depth_mm", "base_diameter_mm"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
