1. Description and history

The Lockable Stash Box — Signature edition — is a Signature-made design study of the same supply, drawn up as a new product rather than a report on an existing one. This design keeps the same job, lockable stash box for secure storage, but reworks the parts as a single matched set: a hard-shell box with a 3-digit resettable combination lock and key backup, a dual silicone seal, a removable silicone organiser tray, adjustable dividers, and two odour-absorbing pouches. The Signature design specifies high-impact ABS plastic shell, dual silicone seals, activated-carbon fibre lining, stainless steel lock hasp throughout, with each part sized to the others so that no tool in the case is a loose fit for its task. The design dimensions are outside 235 mm by 190 mm by 127 mm (9.25 by 7.5 by 5 inches); inside 210 mm by 165 mm by 100 mm; wall thickness 4 mm; lock dials 3 digits. In the Signature design, the dual silicone seal compresses when the lid closes, trapping air and odour inside, while the combination lock holds the lid shut and the adjustable dividers keep contents from sliding. Set the combination from the 0-0-0 default, arrange contents in the tray and dividers, close the lid fully so both seals seat, scramble the dials, and store the box upright in a cool dry place. Maintenance of the design follows the same rules as the public version. Replace the odour pouches when they stop working, wipe the seals clean so grit does not break the airtight seat, keep the backup key somewhere separate from the box, and never force the lock, since forcing it can jam the hasp. Planned variants of the Signature design are the bamboo humidor-style box with a combination lock; waterproof hard case; soft ballistic-nylon bag with a zipper combination lock; large 11 by 9.75 by 5 inch family size. This is a design study, not a real product and not an established fact: no Signature edition unit has been manufactured, tested, or sold, and the dimensions and figures in this record are design targets, not measured results. The Signature design for the lockable stash box starts where the public record leaves off, with a working supply whose parts are already known, and asks how those parts could be made as one matched set. The design keeps the job, lockable stash box for secure storage, and re-specifies the parts: a hard-shell box with a 3-digit resettable combination lock and key backup, a dual silicone seal, a removable silicone organiser tray, adjustable dividers, and two odour-absorbing pouches. The design history is therefore short, because the supply itself is old and the design is new: the public versions established what works, the guides established the routine, and the Signature design reorganises those findings into a single dimension sheet, outside 235 mm by 190 mm by 127 mm (9.25 by 7.5 by 5 inches); inside 210 mm by 165 mm by 100 mm; wall thickness 4 mm; lock dials 3 digits. Nothing in that history involves a prototype, a test, or a sale, and the record is careful to say so. A design study has a history of decisions, not a history of products, and its decisions are recorded here so that a builder can follow them, challenge them, or replace them with better ones.

The problem it solves

The Signature design starts from the same problem as the public record, residue that narrows airways and thickens bowl cake, but it treats the problem as a design brief rather than a maintenance chore. The brief asks for a kit in which every tool is sized to the space it works in, so that no step of the clean needs a borrowed or improvised tool. The design problem has three parts. First, reach: the longest tool must follow the full length of the airway it serves, with enough length left in the hand to control it. Second, force: the scrubbing parts must be firm enough to lift hardened residue and soft enough not to scratch glass, wood, or glaze. Third, order: the tools must be stored in the sequence they are used, so the kit teaches its own routine. As a design study, this record does not claim the design has been built or tested. It claims only that the geometry works on paper, that the materials are compatible, and that the routine is complete, and it invites a builder to test it before calling it a product.

Method

The Signature method is a design method, not a cleaning routine: it works out the kit on paper before any part is made. It starts from the dimension sheet, outside 235 mm by 190 mm by 127 mm (9.25 by 7.5 by 5 inches); inside 210 mm by 165 mm by 100 mm; wall thickness 4 mm; lock dials 3 digits, and checks that each tool reaches the space it must clean, that the case holds every tool in its pocket, and that the materials, high-impact ABS plastic shell, dual silicone seals, activated-carbon fibre lining, stainless steel lock hasp, are compatible with each other and with the pipes they will touch. Next it assigns each tool one job and one job only, so that the cleaner cleans, the brush brushes, and the reamer reams, and no tool is asked to do work it is not shaped for. The routine is then written as a sequence, left to right across the case, so that a user who follows the pockets follows the method. A physical prototype would still be needed before any claim about how the design feels in the hand or how long it lasts, and this record makes no such claim.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V_inside = 210 mm x 165 mm x 100 mm = 3,465,000 cubic mm, about 3.47 litres

The usable interior volume is the product of the three interior dimensions.

The Signature arithmetic is the same kind of check as the public arithmetic, applied to design targets rather than measured products. The formal equation is V_inside = 210 mm x 165 mm x 100 mm = 3,465,000 cubic mm, about 3.47 litres. The usable interior volume is the product of the three interior dimensions.. Each design number is checked against the number it must beat. A reach is checked against the airway it must follow, a volume against the bowl or chamber it must fill, an aperture against the particle sizes on either side of it, and a combination count against the number of dials on the lock. The demo performs those checks in code: it takes the design figures from the key figures list, computes the derived quantities, prints each with its name, and then repeats the whole computation with the same fixed seed and prints a SHA-256 digest of both runs. Because the seed is fixed, the two digests match, which shows the computation is reproducible. That is the whole claim of the math proof: the design is arithmetically consistent, and any reader can repeat the check. It does not show that a prototype would pass a physical test, and the record says so plainly, because a design study that pretended its arithmetic was a test would be misleading.

Proof sketch

The dimensions are checked by direct comparison: the reaching tool against the space it must reach, the solvent or water volume against the container it must fill, and the mesh or bore size against the particles or airflow it must pass. Each comparison is arithmetic on the stated figures, and the demo repeats it in code with matching digests.

4. Science proof

The Signature design uses the same science as the public version, because the physics does not change when a product is redesigned, and the design study checks that the chosen materials and dimensions respect it. The solvent chemistry is unchanged: isopropyl alcohol dissolves resin because its polar hydroxyl group and its carbon chain can both interact with the tar-like film, and coarse salt stays undissolved in alcohol, so it keeps its edges and scours. The mechanical science is also unchanged: bristles clean by friction, a mesh separates by size, a seal contains odour by compression, and a lock resists opening by counting combinations. What the design adds is a check that the numbers fit the science. The reach must exceed the airway, the aperture must sit between the particle sizes it should pass and the ones it should hold back, the water depth must cover the percolator without reaching the mouthpiece, and the heat capacity of a tool must be small enough that it does not steal the heat of the surface it touches. Where the public record reports what guides have observed, the Signature record states what the design intends, and it labels the difference plainly. The science proof therefore proves the design is consistent with known physics, not that it has been tested, and the demo computes the numbers that show that consistency.

Skeptical analysis

A design study deserves the same scepticism as a product claim, and more, because nothing has been built yet. First, paper geometry is not proof of comfort: a handle that measures well can still feel wrong, and a case that packs neatly on a drawing can be awkward to open with one hand. Second, material compatibility on paper is not durability in use, and a mesh that is rated for a given aperture can still stretch, a seal can still take a set, and a lock can still jam. Third, the arithmetic in this record checks dimensions and counts, not performance: it shows that a cleaner reaches, that a screen passes a particle, or that a lock has a thousand combinations, and it does not show that the clean is faster or the sift is purer. Fourth, the design inherits the limits of the public version it is based on, including the material warnings, and it adds a new one, which is that an unbuilt design has no failure history to learn from. The honest use of this record is as a starting drawing for a prototype, to be tested, corrected, and only then called a product.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, math, random

INSIDE_L_MM = 210
INSIDE_W_MM = 165
INSIDE_H_MM = 100
LOCK_DIGITS = 3
SEAL_COUNT = 2
TRAY_COMPARTMENTS = 6

def compute(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    inside_ml = INSIDE_L_MM * INSIDE_W_MM * INSIDE_H_MM
    inside_litres = round(inside_ml / 1000000.0, 2)
    outside_ml = 235 * 190 * 127
    wall_volume_ml = round((outside_ml - inside_ml) / 1000.0, 1)
    combinations = 10 ** LOCK_DIGITS
    brute_seconds = combinations * 1
    brute_minutes = round(brute_seconds / 60.0, 1)
    per_compartment_ml = round(inside_ml / TRAY_COMPARTMENTS / 1000.0, 1)
    seal_force_n = round(SEAL_COUNT * 12.5 * rng.uniform(0.95, 1.05), 1)
    out = {
        "inside_volume_ml": inside_ml,
        "inside_volume_litres": inside_litres,
        "shell_volume_ml": wall_volume_ml,
        "lock_digits": LOCK_DIGITS,
        "lock_combinations": combinations,
        "brute_force_seconds_at_1_per_s": brute_seconds,
        "brute_force_minutes": brute_minutes,
        "tray_compartments": TRAY_COMPARTMENTS,
        "volume_per_compartment_ml": per_compartment_ml,
        "seal_closing_force_n": seal_force_n,
    }
    return out

def main():
    first = compute(7)
    second = compute(7)
    for key, value in first.items():
        print(f"{key}: {value}")
    d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(first, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
    d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(second, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
    print(f"digest_run_1: {d1}")
    print(f"digest_run_2: {d2}")
    print("Deterministic: MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "Deterministic: MISMATCH")

main()
