1. Description and history

The Signature Vortex Recycler is a Signature-made design study for recycler bong, a new design in the Signature Marijuana Supplies Database that reworks the familiar public form with one deliberate change. It keeps the job the same — smoke and water circulate together in a loop: the percolator diffuses smoke in the main chamber, an uptake tube carries water and smoke up to a second chamber where they separate, and a drain tube returns the water to the main chamber — but changes how the parts are arranged, using signature borosilicate glass with a widened uptake funnel; showerhead percolator; glass downstem and bowl. The design is sized at 360 mm tall; main chamber 380 ml; secondary chamber 200 ml; uptake tube 12 mm diameter; drain tube 10 mm diameter; water 230 ml in total, which sets every number in this record, from the volumes and areas in the demo to the scale variants at the end. Design-study label: this is a Signature-made product design, presented as a design study only. It is not a real manufactured product, not an established fact, and not a claim that such a product exists or has been tested. What it does offer is a complete, checkable design: a full specification, the physics of how it would work, worked arithmetic tied to its own dimensions, a runnable program that computes those numbers, and three boundless scale variants. Readers can follow the design from first principles, repeat the arithmetic by hand, run the program, and see exactly where each number comes from. The Signature edition exists alongside the public edition so the archive holds both what exists in the world and what the Signature system designs next, clearly labelled, so the two are never confused. Like every Signature record, it is filed as a design study, with its own generator and its own file AI, and it marches with the rest of the database toward a million records.

The recycler bong is a recent design, developed in the 2010s as glassblowers working on concentrate rigs looked for a way to keep water moving instead of letting it sit still. The idea borrows from the Klein bottle, a mathematical shape with no inside or outside, and some recycler designs are still called Klein recyclers for the sealed loop their glass forms. Early recyclers were small dab rigs; the loop layout then spread to flower bongs, with two chambers joined by an uptake tube and a drain tube so water circulates while smoke travels to the mouthpiece. Trade guides describe the loop as doing three jobs at once: it keeps the percolator covered, it keeps water off the user's lips, and it keeps smoke bubbles surrounded by water longer. The design is now a recognised type in its own right, sold alongside beakers and straight tubes. Over time the same basic form was copied, adapted, and refined by many makers, so the record today covers a family of closely related objects rather than a single patented shape. Trade guides and manufacturer manuals preserve the steps of that change. The history matters because it explains why the parts are arranged the way they are: each feature survives because it solved a practical problem for the people who used the supply every day.

Materials and design

This is a Signature design study, not a real product. Borosilicate glass is chosen for the recycler because the loop is made of thin tubes fused at several joints, and only a glass that tolerates heat and holds a fine fused joint will keep those tubes from cracking where they meet the chambers. The uptake tube is wider than the drain tube because it carries a two-phase flow, water and smoke together, while the drain carries water alone, and a tube that is too narrow for its flow will choke and break the loop. Water is both the filtration medium and the working fluid of the loop: it must be deep enough to cover the percolator and shallow enough that the upper chamber stays dry when the piece is still. The joints are the same ground-glass tapers as on the beaker, 14 mm or 18 mm, sealing by friction with no gasket and no thread. The loop tubes themselves have no joints: they are fused to the chambers while the glass is hot, so the main chamber, the uptake tube, the secondary chamber, and the drain tube are one continuous piece of glass. The tolerance that matters is the height of the drain outlet in the secondary chamber: if it is too high, water pools in the upper chamber, and if it is too low, the loop drains before it starts. The downstem seats into the main chamber joint at about 45 degrees. The outside is left smooth and clear so the circulating water can be seen, which is part of the appeal of the design and the easiest way to check that the loop is running. The fused joints are annealed slowly so they do not hold stress. Variants change whether the loop tubes run outside the body where they can be seen or inside it where they are protected, and whether the loop is a plain circuit or a Klein-style sealed loop, but the two chambers and the two tubes stay the same in every version.

4. Science proof

The recycler works by keeping water in motion. In a plain bong the water sits still and the smoke passes through it once; in a recycler the draw creates a pressure difference that pulls water up the uptake tube into the secondary chamber, where the water and the smoke separate because water is far denser than smoke and falls back under gravity. The drain tube carries that water back to the main chamber, so the percolator never runs dry during a long pull, which is the failure that makes a plain percolator gurgle and splash. The loop time is the loop water volume divided by the draw flow rate: at a draw of 1.5 litres per minute the 230 millilitres of water in the loop takes about nine seconds to make one full circuit. The uptake tube must be wider than the drain tube, because it carries water and smoke together while the drain carries water alone, and a drain that is too narrow will back up and flood the upper chamber. The smoke is not filtered a second time by the loop: the loop's jobs are to keep the percolator covered, to keep water off the lips, and to keep each bubble surrounded by water longer, which is where the extra cooling comes from. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

Build it yourself

As a design study, the Signature Vortex Recycler has not been built; the steps below describe the closest genuine maker activity for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. This section gives the closest genuine maker activity for this supply, honestly labelled. A recycler bong cannot be made at home: fusing a loop of uptake and drain tubes between two glass chambers is a skilled glassblowing trade, and no home method produces it safely. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought kit and making a padded carry cradle for it. Buy a recycler body, a downstem, and a bowl in matching joint sizes, and check the fit dry before any water goes in: the downstem should seat firmly without wobble, and the water must cover the percolator while leaving the upper chamber dry when still. Then make a cradle from a block of dense foam: trace the recycler's outline on the foam, cut the recess with a sharp knife in shallow passes, and test the fit with the dry piece until it sits without rocking. The cradle protects the thin loop tubes in storage and in transport. Test it by tipping the cradle gently from side to side; the piece should stay seated. Check the fit dry before any water or load goes in, work slowly, and keep the finished piece clean and dry in storage, because a build that is checked at each step is the one that works the first time it is used.

3D-print version

As a design study, the Signature Vortex Recycler has not been printed or built; the directions below are for the printable accessory for the public form, adapted to the Signature dimensions. Home 3D printing cannot make every supply in this database, and this section says plainly where the limit falls. A glass recycler bong is not 3D-printable at home: no home filament prints glass, and a printed plastic loop could not hold the fused uptake and drain tubes or seal to a glass downstem. The printable accessory is a protective carry cradle and a loop-tube guard for the piece. Print the cradle in TPU, which stays flexible and grips the glass without scratching it, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer height, 3 perimeters, and 20 percent gyroid infill, and print the guard in PETG with the same nozzle and layer height at 25 percent infill. Print the cradle flat on its bottom face with no supports, and the guard upright with a brim. Print time (estimate): 2 hours 30 minutes for the cradle and 1 hour 15 minutes for the guard, at the settings given. Unmodelled factors that change real print time and strength are wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing: a different slicer profile, a slower first layer, or a damp filament spool will change the result, and the estimate above is not a validated print job. Check the finished accessory against the piece it serves before use, and reprint it if the fit is loose, because a holder that does not grip is worse than no holder at all.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: recycler bong loop volumes and cycle time — Signature Vortex Recycler."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    main_mL = 150.0; sec_mL = 80.0; up_d = 12.0; dr_d = 10.0; up_len = 200.0; dr_len = 170.0; draw = 1.5
    up_area = math.pi * (up_d / 2.0) ** 2
    dr_area = math.pi * (dr_d / 2.0) ** 2
    loop_mL = main_mL + sec_mL
    flow_mL_s = draw * 1000.0 / 60.0
    loop_time = loop_mL / flow_mL_s
    up_vol = up_area * up_len / 1000.0
    return {"main_water_mL": main_mL, "secondary_water_mL": sec_mL, "loop_water_mL": round(loop_mL, 2), "uptake_area_mm2": round(up_area, 2), "drain_area_mm2": round(dr_area, 2), "loop_time_s": round(loop_time, 2), "uptake_volume_mL": round(up_vol, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["main_water_mL", "secondary_water_mL", "loop_water_mL", "uptake_area_mm2", "drain_area_mm2", "loop_time_s", "uptake_volume_mL"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
