1. Description and history

The Signature the humidity control pack is a Signature-made design study of a sealed packet holding a saturated salt-and-water solution behind a semipermeable membrane that adds or removes water vapour to hold a closed container at a fixed relative humidity, drawn as a design for the Signature edition of the marijuana supplies database. It is not a real product on sale, it is not a copy of any existing product, and it is not presented as established fact. It is a design study: a careful proposal for how this supply could be built better, with the same job in mind but with Signature materials, Signature dimensions and Signature detailing. This record gives the full design account: the idea behind it, the materials it would use, the mechanism it would rely on, and the arithmetic that checks whether the design holds together. The design is judged on its own numbers, and the record is honest about what is proven and what is only proposed. Nothing here is a medical claim and nothing here gives a dose. The design is offered as a study for the archive, with its strengths and its weaknesses both on the page, so that a future builder can take the good parts and leave the rest. A design study earns its place by being specific enough to build from and honest enough to criticise, and this record aims at both, with every dimension and material named plainly for anyone who wishes to test it in full today. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

Two-way humidity control was first developed for cigars, where a dry cigar burns hot and harsh and a damp cigar will not draw, and the same problem appears in any stored plant material. The Boveda pack is the best-known example: a saturated solution of pure water and natural salts sealed inside a semipermeable membrane that lets only water vapour pass. The idea is old in principle, since a saturated salt solution in a closed space settles at a fixed relative humidity that depends on the salt, but the packet form made it practical for a jar or a bag. Cannabis storage adopted the 58 percent and 62 percent packs, with 62 percent giving a softer, stickier feel and 58 percent a slightly drier one, and the packs are now sold from 8 grams to 320 grams to match containers from a pint jar to a pound of flower. The Signature design takes that history as its starting point and asks what the next version should keep and what it should change, keeping the proven shape and reworking the weak points in materials, sealing and handling.

The problem it solves

The design problem for the Signature the humidity control pack is the same problem the public version faces, stated as a design brief. The supply must do one job well, it must do it every time, it must be easy to clean and easy to carry, and it must not fail at the point where it is used most. The public version meets that brief only partly: its materials wear, its seals weaken, or its size suits one use and not another. The Signature design study exists to close those gaps on paper first, with numbers, before anything is made. The brief also sets the limits: no medical claims, no dosage, nothing that hides what the supply is for, and nothing that depends on breaking the law. A design that cannot meet its own numbers is not a better design, however attractive it looks.

Method

The Signature method is to start from the public supply, keep the mechanism that works, and redesign the parts that wear out or waste material. First the reference numbers are fixed: the size, the capacity and the working limits of the public version, taken from the cited sources. Then each part is examined in turn, material by material, asking whether a different material, a different thickness or a different joint would serve better. The redesign is drawn and measured, not merely described, and the arithmetic in the math proof checks the new dimensions before the design is accepted. Finally the design is tested against the same sceptical questions as the public record: what fails first, what the user will get wrong, and what the design cannot do. Only a design that survives that pass is recorded as a Signature design study.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference pack targets 62 percent relative humidity with an accuracy of about plus or minus 2 percent, so in a truly airtight jar the humidity should sit between 62 - 2 = 60 percent and 62 + 2 = 64 percent. At 20 degrees Celsius, saturated air holds about 17.3 grams of water vapour per cubic metre, so air at 62 percent holds 17.3 x 0.62 = 10.73 grams per cubic metre. A quart jar has a headspace of about 236.5 cubic centimetres, which is 0.0002365 cubic metres, so the vapour in that headspace weighs 0.0002365 x 10.73 = 0.00254 grams. If the jar air starts at 40 percent relative humidity, it holds 17.3 x 0.40 = 6.92 grams per cubic metre, so the pack must add (10.73 - 6.92) x 0.0002365 = 0.00090 grams of vapour to the headspace alone; the far larger exchange is with the flower itself, which is why the pack is sized by flower weight, not by air volume. The size 67 pack covers 450 grams of flower, so the ratio is 450 / 67 = 6.7 grams of flower per gram of pack; the size 8 pack covers one ounce, 28.35 grams, giving 28.35 / 8 = 3.5 grams of flower per gram of pack, a more generous ratio for small jars.

4. Science proof

A humidity control pack is applied physical chemistry: a saturated salt solution has a fixed equilibrium relative humidity. In a sealed packet, the salt solution sits in equilibrium with the water vapour above it, and that equilibrium is set by the salt, not by how much solution there is. The semipermeable membrane lets water vapour through but holds liquid water and dissolved salts inside, so the pack can trade vapour with the air in the jar without leaking. If the jar air is drier than the target, water evaporates from the solution, passes through the membrane as vapour, and raises the humidity; if the jar air is wetter, vapour condenses into the solution and the humidity falls. That two-way action is what separates these packs from a damp sponge, which can only add moisture, or a desiccant, which can only remove it. The capacity is finite: every gram of water the pack gives up or takes in moves the solution away from saturation, and once the salt is fully dissolved or fully crystallised the pack can no longer hold its target. This is why a pack cannot rescue wet flower: the moisture load inside under-dried flower is far larger than the few grams of water a small pack can exchange.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view of this design study is that a drawing is not a product, and a design that has never been built has never failed in the ways real products fail. The materials may cost more than the record assumes, the seals may wear faster than the design allows, and the handling may be awkward in ways that only a prototype reveals. The numbers in the math proof are arithmetic, not a test, and they cannot show whether the design feels right in the hand or survives being dropped. The record accepts all of that: the design study is a proposal, and it is labelled as one, so that nobody mistakes it for a tested product. The honest next step is a prototype, a real test, and a revision, and until that happens the design should be read as a careful idea, not as a finished thing.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
target_rh = 62.0
accuracy = 2.0
saturation_g_per_m3 = 17.3
headspace_ml = 236.5
size67_mass_g = 67.0
size67_capacity_g = 450.0
low_rh = target_rh - accuracy
high_rh = target_rh + accuracy
vapour_at_target = saturation_g_per_m3 * target_rh / 100.0
headspace_m3 = headspace_ml / 1000000.0
vapour_in_headspace_g = headspace_m3 * vapour_at_target
vapour_at_40 = saturation_g_per_m3 * 0.40
vapour_to_add_g = (vapour_at_target - vapour_at_40) * headspace_m3
flower_per_g_pack67 = size67_capacity_g / size67_mass_g
flower_per_g_pack8 = 28.35 / 8.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.05, 0.05)
    return {"rh_low": round(low_rh, 2), "rh_high": round(high_rh, 2), "vapour_at_target_g_per_m3": round(vapour_at_target + jitter, 2), "vapour_in_headspace_g": round(vapour_in_headspace_g, 5), "vapour_to_add_from_40pct_g": round(vapour_to_add_g, 5), "flower_per_gram_pack67": round(flower_per_g_pack67, 2), "flower_per_gram_pack8": round(flower_per_g_pack8, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("RH low bound (percent): %.2f" % r1["rh_low"])
print("RH high bound (percent): %.2f" % r1["rh_high"])
print("Vapour at target (g per m3): %.2f" % r1["vapour_at_target_g_per_m3"])
print("Vapour in headspace (g): %.5f" % r1["vapour_in_headspace_g"])
print("Vapour to add from 40 percent (g): %.5f" % r1["vapour_to_add_from_40pct_g"])
print("Flower per gram, size 67 pack: %.2f" % r1["flower_per_gram_pack67"])
print("Flower per gram, size 8 pack: %.2f" % r1["flower_per_gram_pack8"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
