1. Description and history

The butane torch lighter is a refillable pocket lighter that premixes butane gas with air and burns it as a narrow, hot, wind-resistant blue jet flame. It belongs in any honest archive of marijuana supplies because it is one of the ordinary, practical tools that people actually use, and because it can be described, measured and tested without exaggeration. This record gives the full public account: what the supply is, where it came from, how it is made, how it works, and what its numbers are. The supply is not a medical device and it is not a treatment, and nothing in this record makes a health claim or gives a dose. What it does is keep a practical job simple, repeatable and tidy, and it does that job with plain materials and plain physics. The description below follows the record through its history, its construction, the science of how it works, and the arithmetic that checks its size and capacity. Every figure in this record comes from the cited public sources or from arithmetic on those figures, and where a figure is an estimate the record says so. The record also explains how to choose between the common sizes and materials, how to look after the supply so that it lasts, and what its limits are in ordinary use. A buyer who reads the whole record should be able to tell a well-made example from a poor one, to know which size suits a pocket and which suits a table, and to use the supply without wasting material or damaging it. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

The pocket lighter descends from the flint and steel fire kit, but the modern butane lighter arrived in the twentieth century when liquefied gas could be stored safely in a small sealed tank. A standard soft-flame lighter lets butane diffuse into the surrounding air and burns it as a tall yellow flame. The torch lighter is a later refinement: a small jet nozzle and a venturi draw air into the gas stream before ignition, so the fuel and air are already mixed when they burn. Piezoelectric ignition, a crystal that produces a spark when struck, replaced the flint wheel on most torch lighters. The result is a short, blue, hissing flame that keeps burning in wind that would blow a soft flame out, and it is used for lighting in wind, for craft work, and anywhere a small, precise, very hot flame is needed. That history matters because it shows which parts of the design are settled by long use and which parts are still open to improvement, and it keeps this record honest about what is established and what is merely new.

The problem it solves

The problem the the butane torch lighter solves is a practical one, and it is easy to understate. Without it, the job it does becomes slower, messier and less repeatable: material is lost, the result varies from one attempt to the next, and the user improvises with whatever is at hand. The supply exists because that improvisation has a cost in wasted material, in uneven results and in time. The problem is not dramatic, but it is real, and it is the reason this object is sold in the first place. The record states the problem plainly so that the solution can be judged against it, and so that a reader can tell whether the supply is worth having at all. A supply that solves a problem nobody has is decoration, and this record does not treat it as one.

Method

The method of this record is to describe the supply from public sources, measure it against its own numbers, and test it with arithmetic rather than with adjectives. First the history and the construction are taken from the cited guides, manufacturer pages and patent records, so that the description rests on evidence rather than on opinion. Then the mechanism is explained in terms of ordinary physics and materials science, so that a reader can see why the supply works rather than merely being told that it does. Then the demo computes the supply's size, capacity and limits from its own figures, twice, with a digest, so that the numbers can be checked by anyone who runs the program. The record does not rely on a single source for any important claim, and where sources disagree the record says so instead of picking the more convenient figure.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The reference lighter holds 8 grams of butane, which is 0.008 kilograms. Butane releases about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram, so the full tank holds 0.008 x 49.5 = 0.396 megajoules, or 396 kilojoules of chemical energy. If the lighter burns butane at 0.05 grams per second, the tank lasts 8 / 0.05 = 160 seconds of continuous flame, and the power output is 0.00005 kilograms per second x 49.5 megajoules per kilogram = 0.002475 megajoules per second, or 2475 watts while it is lit. The torch flame temperature of 1430 degrees Celsius converts to Fahrenheit as 1430 x 9 / 5 + 32 = 2606 degrees Fahrenheit. A soft butane flame at 1000 degrees Celsius is 1000 x 9 / 5 + 32 = 1832 degrees Fahrenheit, so the torch flame is 1430 - 1000 = 430 degrees Celsius hotter. If a task needs 10 kilojoules of heat, the butane required is 10 / 49500 = 0.000202 kilograms, or 0.202 grams, which is 0.202 / 8 = 0.025 of a full tank, about two and a half percent.

4. Science proof

A torch lighter is a small premixed combustion engine. Butane is stored as a liquid under its own vapour pressure, and when the valve opens it boils into gas; the gas expands through a narrow jet, and the fast-moving stream drags surrounding air with it through the venturi, so fuel and air arrive at the nozzle already mixed in roughly the proportion that burns completely. Complete combustion of butane follows the equation 2 C4H10 + 13 O2 giving 8 CO2 + 10 H2O, and the energy released is about 49.5 megajoules per kilogram of butane. Because the mixture is premixed, the flame is short and blue: the blue colour is the light of excited molecules in a clean, nearly soot-free burn, while a yellow soft flame is coloured by glowing soot particles from incomplete combustion. The flame is hottest at its outer envelope, where mixing with outside air is complete, and cooler in the inner cone, where fresh mixture is still heating up. Wind resistance comes from speed: the jet leaves the nozzle fast enough that a side wind cannot easily blow the burning zone away from the nozzle. The piezoelectric igniter works because striking certain crystals produces a voltage high enough to spark across a small gap, with no battery and no flint to wear out.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view is that the sources for this supply are mostly guides, retailers and manufacturers, not laboratory studies, and their figures should be read as practical guides rather than as exact measurements. Flame temperatures, burn rates and humidity figures vary with conditions, and different guides give different numbers for the same supply, sometimes by a wide margin. The record handles that by using midpoints where guides give a range, by naming the source for each figure, and by showing its arithmetic so that a reader can substitute a different figure and see what changes. The demo is a check on the arithmetic, not a laboratory test of the supply itself, and the record does not pretend otherwise. A reader who wants certainty should test the supply directly, on a flat surface, with a known weight or a known volume, and compare the result with the numbers here.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, random
fuel_mass_g = 8.0
energy_mj_per_kg = 49.5
burn_rate_g_per_s = 0.05
flame_temp_c = 1430.0
soft_temp_c = 1000.0
energy_kj = fuel_mass_g / 1000.0 * energy_mj_per_kg * 1000.0
burn_seconds = fuel_mass_g / burn_rate_g_per_s
power_w = burn_rate_g_per_s / 1000.0 * energy_mj_per_kg * 1000000.0
flame_temp_f = flame_temp_c * 9.0 / 5.0 + 32.0
soft_temp_f = soft_temp_c * 9.0 / 5.0 + 32.0
butane_for_10kj_g = 10.0 / (energy_mj_per_kg * 1000.0) * 1000.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.5, 0.5)
    return {"tank_energy_kj": round(energy_kj, 2), "burn_seconds": round(burn_seconds, 2), "power_w": round(power_w, 2), "flame_temp_f": round(flame_temp_f + jitter, 2), "soft_flame_temp_f": round(soft_temp_f, 2), "butane_for_10kj_g": round(butane_for_10kj_g, 3)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Tank energy (kJ): %.2f" % r1["tank_energy_kj"])
print("Burn time (s): %.2f" % r1["burn_seconds"])
print("Power while lit (W): %.2f" % r1["power_w"])
print("Flame temperature (F): %.2f" % r1["flame_temp_f"])
print("Soft flame temperature (F): %.2f" % r1["soft_flame_temp_f"])
print("Butane for 10 kJ (g): %.3f" % r1["butane_for_10kj_g"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
