1. Description and history

The rolling papers is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a rolling papers that works by a thin sheet of plant-fibre paper with a gum strip along one edge is wrapped around ground herb, sealed by moistening the gum, and the resulting cylinder is lit at one end and drawn from the other. It is made from hemp, rice, flax, or wood-pulp paper, 10 to 14 gsm, with a gum arabic adhesive strip, and a common example measures 1 1/4 size 78 mm x 44 mm; king size 110 mm x 44 mm; booklets of 32 leaves are common. Rolling papers began in Alcoy, Spain, in the 1500s, where smokers wrapped salvaged cigar tobacco in paper scraps; dedicated paper factories followed in the town in the mid-1700s, and a Spanish monk is credited with the first folded booklet around 1765. The trade spread through France and Spain in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries: Zig-Zag traces its origins to France in 1879, introduced interleaved booklets in 1894, and trademarked the name in 1906. RAW launched its first unbleached papers in 2005. Papers were first made for tobacco; cannabis smokers adopted the same sheets, and makers later offered hemp, rice, and flax papers aimed at them. In everyday use it is a simple, mechanical object with no electronics in the manual form and no hidden parts: what you see is what does the work, and its behaviour can be checked by measuring its parts and doing the arithmetic in this record. This record gives the full public account: its history, how it works in detail, a complete specification, the physics behind it, worked mathematics tied to its own dimensions, and a runnable program that computes its key volumes, areas, and energies. It makes no medical claim and gives no dosage advice; it describes a supply, its materials, and its mechanism, and it cites real public sources for every fact it states. Laws on cannabis and cannabis supplies vary by jurisdiction, so readers should check local law before buying, possessing, or using this supply. The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: rolling papers needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: A = L * W

A is the area of one paper in square millimetres; L is its length in millimetres; W is its width in millimetres.

The demo starts with one sheet: length times width, 78.0 mm times 44.0 mm, gives the sheet area in square millimetres, and dividing by 100 gives square centimetres. Multiplying the sheet area by 32 leaves gives the booklet area, and dividing by one million converts square millimetres to square metres. Multiplying that area by the basis weight of 12.0 grams per square metre gives the mass of paper in one booklet. The gum strip area is the paper length times the gum width of 4.0 mm. Every number comes from the specification in this record, the program prints each result with its unit, and it runs the computation twice with SHA-256 digests so the reader can confirm the result is deterministic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation.

4. Science proof

Rolling paper is a thin, porous sheet of plant fibre, and its behaviour is set by three material facts. First, basis weight, measured in grams per square metre, sets thickness and stiffness: at 10 to 14 grams per square metre the sheet is thin enough to roll tightly yet strong enough not to tear. Second, the fibres are cellulose, and cellulose burns by pyrolysis followed by oxidation, releasing heat that sustains a slow, even burn along the cylinder. Third, the gum strip is gum arabic, a plant polysaccharide that dissolves in water and re-forms a bond as it dries, which is why a licked seam seals without heat. Rice papers are very thin and burn slowly because their dense fibre mat limits airflow; hemp papers are slightly thicker and more structured, which many rollers find easier to handle. The area of one sheet is length times width, and the area of a whole booklet is that area times the leaf count, so a 32-leaf booklet of 1 1/4 papers carries a little over a tenth of a square metre of paper in total. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: rolling paper area — Rolling Papers."""
import hashlib
def compute():
    length = 78.0; width = 44.0; leaves = 32; gsm = 12.0; gum = 4.0
    sheet_mm2 = length * width
    sheet_cm2 = sheet_mm2 / 100.0
    booklet_mm2 = sheet_mm2 * leaves
    booklet_m2 = booklet_mm2 / 1000000.0
    booklet_g = booklet_m2 * gsm
    gum_mm2 = length * gum
    return {"sheet_area_mm2": round(sheet_mm2, 1), "sheet_area_cm2": round(sheet_cm2, 2), "booklet_area_m2": round(booklet_m2, 4), "booklet_mass_g": round(booklet_g, 3), "gum_area_mm2": round(gum_mm2, 1), "leaves": leaves}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["sheet_area_mm2", "sheet_area_cm2", "booklet_area_m2", "booklet_mass_g", "gum_area_mm2", "leaves"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
