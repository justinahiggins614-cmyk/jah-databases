The mini bong is a full-size water pipe reduced to pocket scale: the same beaker or straight tube, the same downstem and bowl, the same water filtration, in a piece under about eight inches tall. Small water pipes are not new — bubblers and pocket pipes have been sold for decades — but the mini bong as a named type grew with borosilicate glassmaking, which made a thin, heat-resistant tube cheap enough to shrink without cracking. The sources put most mini bongs between four and seven inches tall: four to five inches for the most portable pieces, six to seven inches for a longer smoke path and a larger chamber that is easier to clear. The beaker base became the common form because its wide foot holds more water and keeps a small piece planted on a table, while the straight tube stays the most compact. A six-inch beaker with a 10 or 14 millimetre joint, a removable downstem, and about an inch of water is the standard example in this record. The sources cited at the end of this record give the reader places to check each stage of that change.

Materials and design

A mini bong is a small glass vessel where the material choice is settled: borosilicate glass, 5 millimetres thick in the standard example, because it resists the thermal shock of a hot bowl joint next to cold water and because it does not change the taste of the smoke. Thin soft glass cracks under the same stress, which is why the sources warn against it at any scale. The beaker base is the defining design choice: its wide foot, 82 millimetres across, holds more water than a straight tube of the same height and lowers the centre of gravity, so a small piece stays planted on a table instead of tipping. The joint is 10 millimetres in the standard example, with 14 millimetres as the common alternative, and the downstem is removable so it can be cleaned and replaced; the fixed fission downstem on some six-inch beakers diffuses smoke through about 1.25 inches of water. Tolerances are glassblowing tolerances: the joint must be ground true so the bowl seals without wobbling, the downstem must reach the water without touching the base, and the wall thickness must stay even so the piece does not crack at a thin spot. Design varies by form: straight tube for the most compact piece, beaker for stability and water volume, percolator for extra diffusion, ice catcher for a longer cooled path, and silicone for a piece that cannot shatter. The finish is plain polished glass, sometimes with colour in the glass itself, never a coating inside the smoke path.

4. Science proof

A mini bong filters smoke through water in a small chamber, and its physics is the same as a full-size bong with less room for error. Smoke from the bowl travels down the downstem and bubbles through water, which cools it and traps some particles before the smoke rises through the neck. The water depth matters: the sources say just enough to submerge the downstem slits by about half an inch, roughly an inch of water total in most mini bongs, because too much water floods the stem and too little leaves the slits exposed. Borosilicate glass is the standard material because its low thermal expansion resists cracking under the repeated heating of the bowl joint and the cooling of the water chamber, while thin soft glass cracks under the same stress. The beaker base matters for stability: a wide foot lowers the centre of gravity and holds more water than a straight tube of the same height, which is why the demo computes the water volume in the wide base separately from the narrow tube above it. The chamber volume sets the size of a single draw: the smoke path is short, the chamber clears quickly, and the demo checks that the water volume is a sensible fraction of the total chamber, not more than the chamber can hold. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the piece, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

Build it yourself

There is no genuine do-it-yourself build for a glass mini bong: blowing borosilicate to a 5 millimetre wall, grinding a 10 millimetre joint true, and fusing a downstem needs a glass torch, an annealer, and training, so a home-made glass pipe would be unsafe and this record does not pretend otherwise. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling a bought mini bong kit and making the stand it sits on. Buy a six-inch beaker mini bong with a removable downstem and bowl, fit the downstem into the joint so it reaches the water without touching the base, and fit the bowl into the downstem joint, checking that both seal without wobbling. Then make a stand: cut a disc of hardwood 120 millimetres across and 15 millimetres thick, drill a shallow 85 millimetre recess in the centre so the beaker base sits in it, sand the disc to 220 grit, and finish it with hard oil. The stand keeps the small piece upright on a table and stops it sliding, which is the real risk with a six-inch piece. Fill the bong with water to about 31.75 millimetres so the downstem slits are submerged by about half an inch, check the level against the slits, and empty and rinse the piece after use. Never try to cut or drill glass at home to make a bong: scored glass breaks unpredictably and the edges stay sharp.

3D-print version

A mini bong itself is not 3D-printable: it is borosilicate glass, and no home printer prints glass, so a printed copy would be plastic, would melt at the bowl joint, and would be unsafe to use. This record says so plainly and gives directions for a printable accessory instead: a base stand and a bowl holder. Print the stand in PETG, which is tough and survives being knocked on a table: use a 0.4 millimetre nozzle, 0.2 millimetre layer height, 4 walls, and 40 percent gyroid infill, printed flat on the bed with no supports. The stand is a disc 120 millimetres across with a recess 85 millimetres across and 10 millimetres deep, so the beaker base of the standard six-inch mini bong sits in it and cannot slide. Print the bowl holder as a small ring in TPU at 0.2 millimetre layers and 20 percent infill, so it grips a spare bowl without scratching the glass. Print time (estimate): about 1 hour 50 minutes for the stand and 25 minutes for the holder on a standard desktop printer. After printing, check the recess with the real bong, sand the rim lightly, and wash both parts in warm soapy water. Unmodeled factors: wall thickness changes the recess diameter, nozzle settings change stringing in the recess, printer motion affects the roundness of the disc, and PETG shrinks slightly as it cools, so measure the printed recess with calipers before trusting the fit. The stand never goes near the flame.

Print times are estimates, not validated print jobs. Unmodelled real-world factors: wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, material-specific slicing.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: mini bong — The Mini Bong."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    height_mm = 152.4; tube_d = 25.0; base_d = 82.0; base_h = 50.0; water_depth = 31.75
    water_vol = math.pi * (base_d / 2.0) ** 2 * water_depth / 1000.0
    base_vol = math.pi * (base_d / 2.0) ** 2 * base_h / 1000.0
    tube_h = height_mm - base_h
    tube_vol = math.pi * (tube_d / 2.0) ** 2 * tube_h / 1000.0
    chamber_vol = base_vol + tube_vol
    water_frac = water_vol / chamber_vol
    return {"height_mm": height_mm, "water_depth_mm": water_depth, "water_volume_ml": round(water_vol, 1), "base_volume_ml": round(base_vol, 1), "tube_volume_ml": round(tube_vol, 1), "chamber_volume_ml": round(chamber_vol, 1), "water_fraction": round(water_frac, 3)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    print(f"height_mm: {a['height_mm']}")
    print(f"water_depth_mm: {a['water_depth_mm']}")
    print(f"water_volume_ml: {a['water_volume_ml']}")
    print(f"base_volume_ml: {a['base_volume_ml']}")
    print(f"tube_volume_ml: {a['tube_volume_ml']}")
    print(f"chamber_volume_ml: {a['chamber_volume_ml']}")
    print(f"water_fraction: {a['water_fraction']}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Use, as a program

A mini bong is used like a full-size water pipe, only smaller. Fill it with water to about 31.75 millimetres, just enough to submerge the downstem slits by about half an inch, and check the level against the slits: too much water floods the stem and too little leaves the slits exposed. Fit the bowl into the downstem joint, light the bowl, and draw slowly so the smoke bubbles through the water and rises through the neck; the short chamber clears quickly, so one steady draw is enough. Clear the chamber, empty the water after use, and rinse the piece with warm water. The program in this record computes the numbers behind that routine: the water volume in the beaker base at the stated depth, the volume of the base and of the tube above it, the total chamber volume, and the fraction of the chamber that the water occupies, so the reader can see that the water is a sensible share of the chamber and not more than it can hold. Those figures are what the demo prints, and the demo output is captured exactly as the expected output of this record, so the reader can run the same arithmetic and check it.
