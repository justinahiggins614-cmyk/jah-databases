1. Description and history

The manual rolling machine is a small hand-powered device with two parallel rollers and a looped belt that shapes loose material into a cylinder and wraps it in paper. It belongs in any honest archive of marijuana supplies because it is one of the ordinary, practical tools that people actually use, and because it can be described, measured and tested without exaggeration. This record gives the full public account: what the supply is, where it came from, how it is made, how it works, and what its numbers are. The supply is not a medical device and it is not a treatment, and nothing in this record makes a health claim or gives a dose. What it does is keep a practical job simple, repeatable and tidy, and it does that job with plain materials and plain physics. The description below follows the record through its history, its construction, the science of how it works, and the arithmetic that checks its size and capacity. Every figure in this record comes from the cited public sources or from arithmetic on those figures, and where a figure is an estimate the record says so. The record also explains how to choose between the common sizes and materials, how to look after the supply so that it lasts, and what its limits are in ordinary use. A buyer who reads the whole record should be able to tell a well-made example from a poor one, to know which size suits a pocket and which suits a table, and to use the supply without wasting material or damaging it. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

Hand rolling is the oldest way to make a cigarette, and the rolling machine was invented to make the result repeatable without practised fingers. Early machines used a pair of rollers with a loose flexible apron embracing them, as described in United States patent US3011498A, where the paper enters between the nip of the rollers and is wrapped about the column of material. Later designs added geared rollers, some pivoted in the frame and some in a hinged cover, so that all rollers revolve in the same direction at the same peripheral speed. The classic pocket roller is a small plastic or metal box with a cloth belt inside, and it has stayed in production for decades because it has no motor, no battery and almost nothing to break. Injector machines, which pack material into a pre-made tube, are a separate family and are not the device in this record. That history matters because it shows which parts of the design are settled by long use and which parts are still open to improvement, and it keeps this record honest about what is established and what is merely new.

The problem it solves

The problem the the manual rolling machine solves is a practical one, and it is easy to understate. Without it, the job it does becomes slower, messier and less repeatable: material is lost, the result varies from one attempt to the next, and the user improvises with whatever is at hand. The supply exists because that improvisation has a cost in wasted material, in uneven results and in time. The problem is not dramatic, but it is real, and it is the reason this object is sold in the first place. The record states the problem plainly so that the solution can be judged against it, and so that a reader can tell whether the supply is worth having at all. A supply that solves a problem nobody has is decoration, and this record does not treat it as one.

Method

The method of this record is to describe the supply from public sources, measure it against its own numbers, and test it with arithmetic rather than with adjectives. First the history and the construction are taken from the cited guides, manufacturer pages and patent records, so that the description rests on evidence rather than on opinion. Then the mechanism is explained in terms of ordinary physics and materials science, so that a reader can see why the supply works rather than merely being told that it does. Then the demo computes the supply's size, capacity and limits from its own figures, twice, with a digest, so that the numbers can be checked by anyone who runs the program. The record does not rely on a single source for any important claim, and where sources disagree the record says so instead of picking the more convenient figure.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

Each roller is 12 millimetres in diameter, so its circumference is pi times diameter: 3.1416 x 12 = 37.7 millimetres. One full turn of a roller therefore feeds 37.7 millimetres of paper past the nip. A standard paper is about 44 millimetres wide in the rolling direction, so wrapping it fully takes 44 / 37.7 = 1.17 turns of the roller. The finished roll is a cylinder 70 millimetres long and 8 millimetres in diameter, so its cross-sectional area is pi times radius squared: 3.1416 x 4 x 4 = 50.3 square millimetres, and its volume is area times length: 50.3 x 70 = 3518 cubic millimetres, or 3.52 cubic centimetres. If the loose material is packed to a density of 0.25 grams per cubic centimetre, the roll holds 3.52 x 0.25 = 0.88 grams. The king-size version at 110 millimetres long has a volume of 50.3 x 110 = 5533 cubic millimetres, or 5.53 cubic centimetres, holding 5.53 x 0.25 = 1.38 grams at the same density, which is 1.38 / 0.88 = 1.57 times as much.

4. Science proof

A belt rolling machine works by turning a flat belt into a cylinder-forming mould. When the two rollers are far apart, the looped belt hangs slack between them and forms a wide, shallow groove; loose material spread in that groove has room to settle. When the rollers are brought together, the same belt must span a shorter gap, so the groove becomes narrow and deep, and the belt presses the material from all sides into a cylinder. Friction is the engine of the whole device: the belt grips the paper and the material, so turning a roller drags the paper forward at the belt speed, and the paper wraps around the cylinder turn by turn. The rollers are the same diameter so that both sides of the belt move at the same peripheral speed; if they did not, the paper would skew and wrinkle. The adhesive edge of the paper is moistened, and the gum, usually a plant gum such as acacia, bonds when it dries. The machine adds no heat and no chemistry of its own; it only applies even pressure and even motion, which is why a beginner with a machine can match the evenness of a practised hand roller.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view is that the sources for this supply are mostly guides, retailers and manufacturers, not laboratory studies, and their figures should be read as practical guides rather than as exact measurements. Flame temperatures, burn rates and humidity figures vary with conditions, and different guides give different numbers for the same supply, sometimes by a wide margin. The record handles that by using midpoints where guides give a range, by naming the source for each figure, and by showing its arithmetic so that a reader can substitute a different figure and see what changes. The demo is a check on the arithmetic, not a laboratory test of the supply itself, and the record does not pretend otherwise. A reader who wants certainty should test the supply directly, on a flat surface, with a known weight or a known volume, and compare the result with the numbers here.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, math, random
roller_diameter_mm = 12.0
roller_length_mm = 70.0
paper_width_mm = 44.0
density_g_per_cm3 = 0.25
circumference_mm = math.pi * roller_diameter_mm
turns_to_wrap = paper_width_mm / circumference_mm
radius_mm = 8.0 / 2.0
cross_area_mm2 = math.pi * radius_mm * radius_mm
volume_cm3 = cross_area_mm2 * roller_length_mm / 1000.0
mass_g = volume_cm3 * density_g_per_cm3
king_volume_cm3 = cross_area_mm2 * 110.0 / 1000.0
king_mass_g = king_volume_cm3 * density_g_per_cm3
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.01, 0.01)
    return {"roller_circumference_mm": round(circumference_mm, 2), "turns_to_wrap_paper": round(turns_to_wrap, 2), "roll_volume_cm3": round(volume_cm3 + jitter, 2), "roll_mass_g": round(mass_g, 2), "king_size_mass_g": round(king_mass_g, 2), "king_to_standard_ratio": round(king_mass_g / mass_g, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Roller circumference (mm): %.2f" % r1["roller_circumference_mm"])
print("Turns to wrap paper: %.2f" % r1["turns_to_wrap_paper"])
print("Roll volume (cm3): %.2f" % r1["roll_volume_cm3"])
print("Roll mass (g): %.2f" % r1["roll_mass_g"])
print("King-size mass (g): %.2f" % r1["king_size_mass_g"])
print("King to standard ratio: %.2f" % r1["king_to_standard_ratio"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
