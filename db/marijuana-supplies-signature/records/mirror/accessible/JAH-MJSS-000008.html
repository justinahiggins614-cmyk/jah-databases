1. Description and history

The pre-rolled cones is one of the basic supplies of the marijuana supplies archive: a pre-rolled cones that works by a paper sheet cut as a trapezoid is rolled around a form into a tapered tube with a filter tip at the narrow end; the user fills the wide end, packs the contents, and twists the tip closed. It is made from hemp, rice, or wood-pulp paper, gum arabic seam, rolled paper filter tip 24 mm long, and a common example measures 1 1/4 cone 84 mm long, king cone 109 mm long, filter tip 24 mm. Pre-rolled cones are the factory answer to hand rolling: the paper is cut, shaped, and tipped before it reaches the user, so filling replaces rolling. Cone making grew with the pre-roll trade, where production runs in four steps described by trade guides: grind the flower to a consistent 1 to 2 mm, sift out stems and sharp material, fill pre-rolled cones by hand or by machine, then close and weigh each unit. Machines such as the Futurola Knockbox can fill many cones in a single cycle. Common cone sizes settled at Standard 84 mm, Classic 98 mm, and King Size 109 mm, with a rolled paper filter tip, called a crutch or roach, already fitted at the narrow end. In everyday use it is a simple, mechanical object with no electronics in the manual form and no hidden parts: what you see is what does the work, and its behaviour can be checked by measuring its parts and doing the arithmetic in this record. The problem it solves

The problem this supply solves is practical and narrow: pre-rolled cones needs to be prepared or used evenly, reliably, and with ordinary tools, without waste and without guesswork. Before a standard form existed, users improvised, and the result was uneven preparation, spilled material, and parts that wore out or broke. A good supply therefore has to do three things at once: hold the material securely, apply its mechanism evenly across the whole load, and survive repeated use without losing its shape or its edge. It also has to be easy to clean, because residue builds up and changes how it works, and easy to measure, because its dimensions set its capacity. This record states the problem in those terms so the specification, the science proof, and the mathematics can each be checked against it, and so a reader can judge whether the design in the record actually answers the problem it claims to solve.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: V = (pi * h / 3) * (R^2 + R*r + r^2)

V is the cone volume in cubic millimetres; h is the cone length in millimetres; R is the wide-end radius in millimetres; r is the narrow-end radius in millimetres.

The demo uses the frustum volume formula: pi times height divided by 3, times the sum of the wide-end radius squared, the wide-end radius times the narrow-end radius, and the narrow-end radius squared. With a length of 109.0 mm, a wide-end radius of 6.0 mm, and a narrow-end radius of 3.5 mm, that gives the cone volume in cubic millimetres, and dividing by 1000 gives cubic centimetres. Multiplying the volume by the packing density of 0.15 grams per cubic centimetre gives the estimated capacity in grams. A 5000-metre paper bobbin divided by the cone length gives the number of cones per bobbin. All numbers come from the specification in this record, the program prints each result with its unit, and it runs the computation twice with SHA-256 digests so the reader can confirm the result is deterministic. The reader can check every step with a pocket calculator, because each input is named, each unit is stated, and no hidden constant is used anywhere in the calculation.

4. Science proof

A cone is a frustum, a cone with its tip cut off, and its shape does real work. The wide end holds more material than the narrow end, so the contents pack more densely toward the filter and more loosely at the tip, which helps the roll burn evenly from the wide end down. The filter tip, or crutch, is a rolled cylinder of paper: it holds the cone open, stops loose material from reaching the mouth, and gives the user something cool to hold. The paper is a trapezoid before rolling, cut so the gum edge runs straight and the opposite edge runs at an angle, which is what makes the finished tube taper instead of running parallel. Gum arabic seals the seam as it dries. The volume of a frustum is one third of pi times height times the sum of the two end radii squared plus their product, and multiplying that volume by the packing density of ground herb gives an estimate of the mass the cone can hold. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports. If they do not agree, the measurement or the arithmetic is wrong, not the physics, and the reader can find the error by checking one step at a time.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: pre-rolled cone volume — Pre-Rolled Cones."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    h = 109.0; R = 6.0; r = 3.5; tip = 24.0; rho = 0.15
    vol_mm3 = (math.pi * h / 3.0) * (R * R + R * r + r * r)
    vol_cm3 = vol_mm3 / 1000.0
    mass_g = vol_cm3 * rho
    bobbin_m = 5000.0
    cones_per_bobbin = bobbin_m * 1000.0 / h
    return {"cone_volume_mm3": round(vol_mm3, 2), "cone_volume_cm3": round(vol_cm3, 2), "capacity_g": round(mass_g, 2), "filter_tip_mm": tip, "cones_per_5000m_bobbin": round(cones_per_bobbin, 0)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ["cone_volume_mm3", "cone_volume_cm3", "capacity_g", "filter_tip_mm", "cones_per_5000m_bobbin"]:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
