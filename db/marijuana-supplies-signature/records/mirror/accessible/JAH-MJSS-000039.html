1. Description and history

The silicone ashtray with lid is one of the eight supplies built in Wave 2 Lane B of the marijuana supplies pair, a smoke-shop supply with a full description, a specification, a science proof, a math proof, a working demo, and a boundless generator. The silicone ashtray with lid works by giving ash and spent material a soft rubber dish to land in, with a lid that closes over the bowl to keep ash in, keep wind out, and keep smell down. It is made from food-grade silicone rubber, and a common example measures about 100 mm across and 28 mm deep, with a 4 mm wall and a lid 104 mm across and 5 mm thick, holding about 186 cubic centimetres of ash and debris. The silicone tolerates heat up to about 230 degrees C, it cannot break if dropped, and it washes clean in a dishwasher. The lid seals by contact: a lip on the lid presses into the rim of the dish, and the soft rubber gives enough to seal without a gasket. The parts fit together in a fixed order, and each part has one job: to hold the material, to apply the mechanism, or to carry the result to the user. Because the parts are simple and the mechanism is mechanical rather than electronic in its basic form, the supply can be understood by inspection, measured with a ruler, and checked by the arithmetic in this record. This record also gives the materials and design, a build-it-yourself section, 3D-printing directions, and a use program, with scale variants at pocket, standard, and workshop sizes. The silicone ashtray with a lid is the newest ashtray form in this lane, because it only became possible when food-grade silicone rubber became cheap enough for everyday goods in the 2010s. Silicone ashtrays were first sold as unbreakable travel ashtrays: soft, light, and impossible to shatter, with a lid that closed over the bowl to keep ash in and smell down. Materials and design

The silicone ashtray is a rubber design, and the choice is food-grade silicone against cheaper general-purpose rubber. Food-grade silicone is chosen here because it tolerates heat to about 230 degrees C without giving off fumes, it does not hold smell, and it is safe to wash in a dishwasher, while cheaper rubber softens, smells, and can melt. The wall is 4 mm thick, which is the working compromise: thinner walls are lighter and fold flat but let heat through to the table, thicker walls insulate better and hold their shape but make the dish stiff and hard to empty. The lid is 5 mm thick with a lip that presses into the rim, and its tolerance is the lip diameter, sized to stretch over the rim and spring back to seal without a gasket. The rim rests are moulded into the dish wall, so they cannot come loose, and their tolerance is the width of the cut, sized to hold a cigarette without letting it roll. The finish is matte silicone in plain or glow colours, and the design varies by dish size, by lid type, hinged or loose, and by whether the dish folds flat for travel, while the working principle stays the same.

4. Science proof

A silicone ashtray works by heat tolerance, elasticity, and a contact seal. Silicone rubber tolerates continuous heat up to about 230 degrees C, the figure the cited supplier guides give, because its silicon-oxygen backbone does not break down the way a carbon backbone does, so a hot ember on the surface chars the ash but not the dish. Its density is near 1.15 grams per cubic centimetre and its specific heat near 1.46 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish and lid of about 124 grams store roughly 36,200 joules when heated by 200 kelvin, and its thermal conductivity is low, so the outside stays cool to touch while the inside takes the heat. Silicone is elastic, so the lid lip stretches over the rim and springs back to seal, and it is non-porous, so tar and smell sit on the surface instead of soaking in. The dish is a shallow cylinder, so its capacity is pi times radius squared times depth, and the lid is a flat disc whose volume is pi times radius squared times thickness. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

Build it yourself

A silicone ashtray with a lid is a genuine home build, and this record gives it in full, because casting silicone in a mould is a real do-it-yourself craft. Start with a bought two-part food-grade silicone rubber kit, the kind sold for mould making, and a mould: either a bought ashtray mould or a home mould made by pressing a bought glass ashtray, 100 mm across, into a box of modelling clay to form the cavity. Mix the two silicone parts in equal measure by weight, stir slowly for three minutes to avoid trapping air, and pour the mix into the mould from a low height so it flows into the rim notches. Tap the mould on the table to bring air bubbles to the surface, and let it cure for 24 hours at room temperature without moving it. For the lid, pour a second, thinner mix into a flat mould, a 104 mm circle cut from a plastic tub lid, with a 5 mm spacer ring to set the thickness, and let it cure the same way. When both parts are cured, peel them from their moulds, trim any flash with scissors, wash them in warm soapy water, and check the lid lip against the dish rim: it should press on with a light push and lift off with one hand. That cast dish and lid are the build, and they work because the silicone is the same food-grade rubber the bought form uses.

3D-print version

The silicone ashtray with lid is printable in TPU on a home printer, and this record gives the full directions, because TPU is the flexible filament that behaves most like silicone rubber. Print the dish and lid in 95A TPU, which is soft enough to seal and firm enough to hold its shape, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, a 0.2 mm layer height, 15 percent gyroid infill, and 4 perimeters, giving a wall about 1.6 mm thick. Use a direct-drive extruder if the printer has one, because TPU buckles in a long Bowden tube, and print at 25 to 30 mm per second, slower than rigid filament, with a first layer at 15 to 20 mm per second. Set the nozzle to 225 to 235 degrees C and the bed to 50 to 60 degrees C, with retraction at 0.8 mm and 25 mm per second, and the cooling fan at 50 percent. Print the dish upright on its base with no supports, and the lid flat on its face, also with no supports. Print time (estimate): about 2 hours 30 minutes for the dish and lid together at standard size. Post-processing is simple: remove any brim, check the 100 mm dish and the 104 mm lid with calipers, and wash the parts in warm soapy water before use. Unmodeled factors: wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing all change the real print time and strength, so the estimate is not a validated print job; measure the printed lip before trusting the seal. A printed TPU ashtray is a working copy, not a silicone original, and it should be checked for heat damage after its first use.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: silicone ashtray and lid volumes and heat storage — The Silicone Ashtray with Lid."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 100.0; h = 28.0; wall = 4.0; lid_d = 104.0; lid_t = 5.0; rho = 1.15; cp = 1.46; dT = 200.0
    cap_mm3 = math.pi * ((d - 2.0 * wall) / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    cap_cm3 = cap_mm3 / 1000.0
    outer_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * (h + wall)
    body_cm3 = (outer_mm3 - cap_mm3) / 1000.0
    body_g = body_cm3 * rho
    lid_mm3 = math.pi * (lid_d / 2.0) ** 2 * lid_t
    lid_cm3 = lid_mm3 / 1000.0
    lid_g = lid_cm3 * rho
    total_g = body_g + lid_g
    heat_J = total_g * cp * dT
    return {"capacity_mm3": round(cap_mm3, 2), "capacity_cm3": round(cap_cm3, 2), "body_volume_cm3": round(body_cm3, 2), "body_mass_g": round(body_g, 2), "lid_volume_cm3": round(lid_cm3, 2), "lid_mass_g": round(lid_g, 2), "total_mass_g": round(total_g, 2), "heat_to_200C_J": round(heat_J, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['capacity_mm3', 'capacity_cm3', 'body_volume_cm3', 'body_mass_g', 'lid_volume_cm3', 'lid_mass_g', 'total_mass_g', 'heat_to_200C_J']:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Use, as a program

To use the silicone ashtray with lid, set it on a flat surface, rest the cigarette or joint in a rim rest between draws, and tap ash into the dish. Close the lid when the session ends or when wind picks up, and the seal will keep ash in and smell down. Empty the dish when the ash reaches a third of the depth, while the embers are out, and wash it with warm soapy water or in a dishwasher, because silicone releases tar easily. Do not leave a lit ember pressed against one spot for a long time, even though silicone tolerates 230 degrees C, because a concentrated hot spot can scorch the surface. The program in this record computes what the dish holds and how it handles heat: the capacity from its diameter and depth, the body shell volume from the outer and inner cylinders, the lid volume from its diameter and thickness, the total mass from the silicone density, and the stored heat from the mass, the specific heat, and a 200 kelvin rise. Run the demo to see the numbers, then change one input at a time — a wider dish, a thicker lid, a deeper bowl — and watch the capacity and the total mass move. The program is the use check: if the lid volume it reports is far above the body volume, the lid is thicker than the record states, and the reader should re-measure the parts before trusting the seal.
