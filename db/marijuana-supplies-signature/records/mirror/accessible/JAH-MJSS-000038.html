1. Description and history

The metal ashtray with poker is one of the eight supplies built in Wave 2 Lane B of the marijuana supplies pair, a smoke-shop supply with a full description, a specification, a science proof, a math proof, a working demo, and a boundless generator. The metal ashtray with poker works by giving ash and spent material a steel dish to land in, with rim rests to hold a cigarette or joint and a steel poker to stir and clear. It is made from stainless steel, and a common example measures about 100 mm across and 30 mm deep, with a 2 mm wall and a poker rod 4 mm across and 120 mm long, holding about 217 cubic centimetres of ash and debris. The steel dish cannot crack from heat, cannot break if dropped, and can be used outdoors in wind and rain, while the poker reaches into a pipe bowl to clear ash that fingers cannot reach. The parts fit together in a fixed order, and each part has one job: to hold the material, to apply the mechanism, or to carry the result to the user. Because the parts are simple and the mechanism is mechanical rather than electronic in its basic form, the supply can be understood by inspection, measured with a ruler, and checked by the arithmetic in this record. The metal ashtray with a poker is the working ashtray of bars, casinos, and patios, because metal is the only ashtray material that cannot break, cannot crack from heat, and can be hosed down at the end of the night. Materials and design

The metal ashtray is a steel design, and the choice is between stainless steel, brass, and cast iron. Stainless steel is chosen here because it is rustproof, easy to clean, and strong at a 2 mm wall, while brass is heavier and prettier but tarnishes, and cast iron is heavier still and rusts unless it is oiled. The wall is 2 mm thick, which is the working compromise: thinner walls dent and ring when tapped, thicker walls survive any drop but make the dish heavy to carry. The poker is the same steel, 4 mm across, with a flattened tip for stirring and a loop or ball handle so it cannot slip through the clip. The joint between dish and poker is not a joint at all but a clip or socket on the rim, and its tolerance is the grip of the clip, tight enough to hold the poker in wind and loose enough to pull it free with one hand. The finish is brushed or polished steel, and the design varies by dish size, by the number of rests, and by whether the poker clips to the rim or sits in a socket, while the working principle stays the same.

4. Science proof

A metal ashtray works by conductivity, mass, and corrosion resistance. Stainless steel has a density near 7.9 grams per cubic centimetre and a specific heat near 0.50 joules per gram per kelvin, so a dish and poker of about 282 grams store roughly 28,200 joules when heated by 200 kelvin, and its thermal conductivity near 16 watts per metre kelvin spreads heat quickly through the wall, so the whole dish warms evenly instead of cracking at a hot spot. Steel cannot crack from thermal shock the way glass and ceramic can, because it yields instead of fracturing, and its chromium oxide skin keeps it from rusting outdoors, which is why the same dish works on a patio in rain. The poker is a simple lever: a 120 mm rod with a 4 mm diameter gives the hand enough reach and stiffness to stir ash and clear a bowl without bending. The dish is a shallow cylinder, so its capacity is pi times radius squared times depth, and the poker is a long thin cylinder whose volume is pi times radius squared times length. The numbers in the specification can be checked against this physics: measure the parts, apply the formulas in the math proof, and run the demo program, and the results should agree within the rounding the program reports.

Build it yourself

There is no genuine home build for a metal ashtray with poker, and this record says so plainly: spinning or pressing a steel dish and drawing a steel poker need a metal workshop, and sheet-metal work is a skilled trade, not a home craft. The closest genuine maker activity is assembling and fitting a bought dish and poker, and it works like this. Start with a bought stainless steel ashtray, 100 mm across, and a bought steel poker, 4 mm across and 120 mm long, and check that the poker fits the rim clip without rattling. If the dish has no clip, make one: bend a 20 mm strip of 1 mm stainless steel around the poker to form a loop, drill two 3 mm holes in the rim, and rivet the loop in place with 3 mm blind rivets, checking that the poker slides free with one hand. Then make the accessory a home workshop can do: a wooden base board, cut to a 140 mm square from 12 mm hardwood, sanded smooth and oiled, that sits under the dish and keeps it from sliding or scratching the table. Then make the accessory a home printer can do: a PETG poker holster that clips to the board and holds the poker upright. That clip, board, and holster are the build; the steel dish and poker themselves are bought, not made at home.

3D-print version

The metal ashtray itself is not 3D-printable in steel on a home printer, and this record says so plainly: home printers extrude plastic filament, and no plastic survives contact with hot embers or the weight of a steel dish, so a printed ashtray would melt and give off fumes. What is printable is the accessory that goes with it: a PETG poker holster that clips to the base board and holds the poker upright. Print it in PETG, which tolerates warm water and cleaning better than PLA, with a 0.4 mm nozzle, a 0.2 mm layer height, 20 percent gyroid infill, and 3 perimeters, giving a wall about 1.2 mm thick. Print the holster upright on its base with supports inside the poker socket only, at a nozzle temperature of 235 degrees C and a bed temperature of 80 degrees C, with the fan at 50 percent. Print time (estimate): about 45 minutes for the holster at standard size. Post-processing is simple: remove the supports from the socket, check the 4 mm bore with calipers, and sand the rim lightly if it is sharp. Unmodeled factors: wall thickness, supports, nozzle settings, printer motion, and material-specific slicing all change the real print time and strength, so the estimate is not a validated print job; measure the printed bore before trusting it with a real steel poker.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

"""Demo: metal ashtray and poker volumes and heat storage — The Metal Ashtray with Poker."""
import hashlib, math
def compute():
    d = 100.0; h = 30.0; wall = 2.0; pk_d = 4.0; pk_l = 120.0; rho = 7.9; cp = 0.50; dT = 200.0
    cap_mm3 = math.pi * ((d - 2.0 * wall) / 2.0) ** 2 * h
    cap_cm3 = cap_mm3 / 1000.0
    outer_mm3 = math.pi * (d / 2.0) ** 2 * (h + wall)
    dish_cm3 = (outer_mm3 - cap_mm3) / 1000.0
    dish_g = dish_cm3 * rho
    pk_mm3 = math.pi * (pk_d / 2.0) ** 2 * pk_l
    pk_cm3 = pk_mm3 / 1000.0
    pk_g = pk_cm3 * rho
    total_g = dish_g + pk_g
    heat_J = total_g * cp * dT
    return {"capacity_mm3": round(cap_mm3, 2), "capacity_cm3": round(cap_cm3, 2), "dish_volume_cm3": round(dish_cm3, 2), "dish_mass_g": round(dish_g, 2), "poker_volume_cm3": round(pk_cm3, 2), "poker_mass_g": round(pk_g, 2), "total_mass_g": round(total_g, 2), "heat_to_200C_J": round(heat_J, 2)}
def main():
    a = compute(); b = compute()
    sa = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(a.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    sb = hashlib.sha256(repr(sorted(b.items())).encode()).hexdigest()
    for key in ['capacity_mm3', 'capacity_cm3', 'dish_volume_cm3', 'dish_mass_g', 'poker_volume_cm3', 'poker_mass_g', 'total_mass_g', 'heat_to_200C_J']:
        print(f"{key}: {a[key]}")
    print(f"Run 1 SHA-256: {sa}")
    print(f"Run 2 SHA-256: {sb}")
    print(f"Deterministic: {'MATCH' if sa == sb else 'MISMATCH'}")
if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Use, as a program

To use the metal ashtray with poker, set it on a flat surface, rest the cigarette or joint in a rim rest between draws, and tap ash into the dish. Use the poker to stir ash, to clear a clogged pipe bowl, and to push spent material off the rests, then clip the poker back into its socket. Empty the dish when the ash reaches a third of the depth, while the embers are out, and wash it with warm soapy water, because steel releases tar easily and does not hold smell.
