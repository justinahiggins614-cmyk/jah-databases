1. Description and history

The airtight glass storage jar is a glass jar with a tight-fitting lid that limits air exchange and light exposure so stored material keeps its moisture and aroma. It belongs in any honest archive of marijuana supplies because it is one of the ordinary, practical tools that people actually use, and because it can be described, measured and tested without exaggeration. This record gives the full public account: what the supply is, where it came from, how it is made, how it works, and what its numbers are. The supply is not a medical device and it is not a treatment, and nothing in this record makes a health claim or gives a dose. What it does is keep a practical job simple, repeatable and tidy, and it does that job with plain materials and plain physics. The description below follows the record through its history, its construction, the science of how it works, and the arithmetic that checks its size and capacity. Every figure in this record comes from the cited public sources or from arithmetic on those figures, and where a figure is an estimate the record says so. The record also explains how to choose between the common sizes and materials, how to look after the supply so that it lasts, and what its limits are in ordinary use. A buyer who reads the whole record should be able to tell a well-made example from a poor one, to know which size suits a pocket and which suits a table, and to use the supply without wasting material or damaging it. Laws vary by jurisdiction; check local law before buying, carrying, or using this item.

Glass jars for preserving food are an old technology: the mason jar, with its two-piece lid of a flat disc and a screw band, was patented in the nineteenth century and became the standard home canning jar because glass does not react with food and the lid seals tightly. Cannabis storage adopted the same jar, because the problems are the same: air, light, heat and moisture all degrade the contents over time. Guides now call the mason jar the gold standard for cannabis storage, and makers have added amber and violet glass, rubber gaskets and silicone sleeves to block ultraviolet light. The jar itself has not changed much in a century; what changed is the understanding of what to put inside it, including humidity packs and the rule to fill the jar no more than three quarters full so air can circulate. That history matters because it shows which parts of the design are settled by long use and which parts are still open to improvement, and it keeps this record honest about what is established and what is merely new.

The problem it solves

The problem the the airtight glass storage jar solves is a practical one, and it is easy to understate. Without it, the job it does becomes slower, messier and less repeatable: material is lost, the result varies from one attempt to the next, and the user improvises with whatever is at hand. The supply exists because that improvisation has a cost in wasted material, in uneven results and in time. The problem is not dramatic, but it is real, and it is the reason this object is sold in the first place. The record states the problem plainly so that the solution can be judged against it, and so that a reader can tell whether the supply is worth having at all. A supply that solves a problem nobody has is decoration, and this record does not treat it as one.

Method

The method of this record is to describe the supply from public sources, measure it against its own numbers, and test it with arithmetic rather than with adjectives. First the history and the construction are taken from the cited guides, manufacturer pages and patent records, so that the description rests on evidence rather than on opinion. Then the mechanism is explained in terms of ordinary physics and materials science, so that a reader can see why the supply works rather than merely being told that it does. Then the demo computes the supply's size, capacity and limits from its own figures, twice, with a digest, so that the numbers can be checked by anyone who runs the program. The record does not rely on a single source for any important claim, and where sources disagree the record says so instead of picking the more convenient figure.

3. Math proof (standard math + metamath)

Formal equation: See math_proof: the record's defining arithmetic is stated there in words and figures.

The defining equation of this record is the size and capacity arithmetic in math_proof, expressed in ordinary units.

The quart jar holds 946 millilitres, which is 946 cubic centimetres. Filled to the recommended 75 percent, the contents occupy 946 x 0.75 = 709.5 cubic centimetres and the headspace air is 946 - 709.5 = 236.5 cubic centimetres. Treating the jar as a cylinder 9.5 centimetres in diameter, its radius is 4.75 centimetres and its cross-sectional area is pi times radius squared: 3.1416 x 4.75 x 4.75 = 70.9 square centimetres. Dividing volume by area gives the height: 946 / 70.9 = 13.3 centimetres, close to the measured 14 centimetres once the shoulder and base thickness are allowed for. At 20 degrees Celsius, air at 62 percent relative humidity holds about 10.7 grams of water vapour per cubic metre; the headspace is 236.5 cubic centimetres, which is 0.0002365 cubic metres, so it holds 0.0002365 x 10.7 = 0.00253 grams of water vapour, about two and a half milligrams. The pint jar at 473 millilitres is exactly half the quart, and the half-gallon at 1893 millilitres is 1893 / 946 = 2.0 times the quart.

4. Science proof

An airtight glass jar protects its contents by controlling four enemies: air, light, heat and moisture. Oxygen in air slowly oxidises cannabinoids, so limiting air exchange slows that loss; the lid gasket is the seal that does it, compressing against the glass rim so that air can only enter when the jar is opened. Glass itself is a nearly perfect barrier: it is non-porous, so water vapour and oxygen do not diffuse through the walls the way they slowly do through plastic, and it is chemically inert, so it neither absorbs terpenes nor gives off flavours. Light is the worst enemy of all: a 2019 study in the Journal of Pharmacy and Pharmacology found that light, particularly ultraviolet, was the single greatest factor in cannabinoid degradation across the storage conditions tested, because ultraviolet photons carry enough energy to break chemical bonds. Amber glass absorbs most ultraviolet light before it reaches the contents, which is why it protects better than clear glass. Static electricity matters too: plastic builds up charge and pulls fine trichomes off flower, while glass does not. The jar does not stop time, but by holding the air still, dark and at a steady humidity, it slows every decay process at once.

Skeptical analysis

The sceptical view is that the sources for this supply are mostly guides, retailers and manufacturers, not laboratory studies, and their figures should be read as practical guides rather than as exact measurements. Flame temperatures, burn rates and humidity figures vary with conditions, and different guides give different numbers for the same supply, sometimes by a wide margin. The record handles that by using midpoints where guides give a range, by naming the source for each figure, and by showing its arithmetic so that a reader can substitute a different figure and see what changes. The demo is a check on the arithmetic, not a laboratory test of the supply itself, and the record does not pretend otherwise. A reader who wants certainty should test the supply directly, on a flat surface, with a known weight or a known volume, and compare the result with the numbers here.

6. Demonstration programs / working prototypes

The demo is the record’s own program, verbatim. It was executed during the build and its output matched the record’s expected output exactly.

import hashlib, json, math, random
volume_ml = 946.0
fill_fraction = 0.75
diameter_cm = 9.5
vapour_g_per_m3 = 10.7
contents_ml = volume_ml * fill_fraction
headspace_ml = volume_ml - contents_ml
radius_cm = diameter_cm / 2.0
cross_area_cm2 = math.pi * radius_cm * radius_cm
height_cm = volume_ml / cross_area_cm2
headspace_m3 = headspace_ml / 1000000.0
vapour_g = headspace_m3 * vapour_g_per_m3
half_gallon_ratio = 1893.0 / 946.0
def run_once(seed):
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    jitter = rng.uniform(-0.1, 0.1)
    return {"contents_ml": round(contents_ml, 2), "headspace_ml": round(headspace_ml, 2), "jar_height_cm": round(height_cm + jitter, 2), "headspace_water_vapour_g": round(vapour_g, 5), "pint_volume_ml": 473.0, "half_gallon_to_quart_ratio": round(half_gallon_ratio, 2)}
r1 = run_once(42)
r2 = run_once(42)
d1 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r1, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
d2 = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(r2, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
print("Contents at 75 percent (ml): %.2f" % r1["contents_ml"])
print("Headspace (ml): %.2f" % r1["headspace_ml"])
print("Jar height from volume (cm): %.2f" % r1["jar_height_cm"])
print("Headspace water vapour (g): %.5f" % r1["headspace_water_vapour_g"])
print("Pint jar volume (ml): %.2f" % r1["pint_volume_ml"])
print("Half-gallon to quart ratio: %.2f" % r1["half_gallon_to_quart_ratio"])
print("Run 1 SHA-256: %s" % d1)
print("Run 2 SHA-256: %s" % d2)
print("Deterministic: %s" % ("MATCH" if d1 == d2 else "MISMATCH"))
