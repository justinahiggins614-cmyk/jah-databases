# Dictionary entries — The Property Records Lookup Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## property records lookup

The recorded search of public land records for a named parcel, carried out so that the deed, the owner of record, and the boundaries can be read from the record rather than remembered.

## parcel

A single piece of land with stated boundaries, recorded under one description so that every deed, lien, and tax entry for that land can be matched to the same piece of ground.

## deed

The signed written document that transfers ownership of land from one holder to another, kept in the record with its date and its parties, so that the transfer can be proved from the paper.

## land registry

The public office and its books in which deeds and other land records are entered, kept in order by parcel, so that the holding of any parcel can be looked up in one place.

## title

The lawful holding of land as shown by the chain of deeds and records, written in the record so that who holds a parcel and on what terms can be read plainly.

## owner of record

The person or body named in the public records as the holder of a parcel, written in the record with the deed that names them, so that the holding can be traced to its paper.

## boundary

The stated line that marks where one parcel ends and the next begins, written in the record with its survey and its markers, so that the extent of the land can be read plainly.

## plat map

The drawn map that shows parcels, streets, and lots within a district, kept beside the deeds so that the shape and place of each parcel can be seen at a glance.

## lien

The written claim against a parcel for money owed, kept in the record with its amount and its holder, so that the charge on the land can be counted.

## easement

The written right for one party to use part of another parcel for a stated purpose, kept in the record with its terms, so that the shared use can be read plainly.

## chain of title

The unbroken line of deeds from one holder of a parcel to the next, kept in order so that the holding can be followed back through every transfer.

## certified copy

A copy of a land record marked by the keeping office as true to the original, kept in the record so that the copy can stand in place of the original where the original cannot travel.
