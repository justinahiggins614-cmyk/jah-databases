This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Software Listing With Plain Words Only, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Checklist With Review Before Launch, in the Signature edition.

History

Directories of useful programs are an old habit: a list of tools pinned in a workshop, a catalogue of instruments in a library, a handbook that says which tool does which job. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search box that finds a program by task, a plain statement of what free means, and a guide written for beginners. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
