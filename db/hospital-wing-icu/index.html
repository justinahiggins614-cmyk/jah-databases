Mechanical Ventilation Basics

A ventilator breathes for patients whose lungs cannot. The team sets how much air, how fast, and how much pressure, then adjusts daily based on blood gases and chest X-rays. Too much pressure injures lungs, so modern care uses small, gentle breaths.

Vasopressor Therapy

When blood pressure collapses in shock, vasopressors like norepinephrine squeeze blood vessels to keep the brain and heart perfused. Nurses titrate the drip minute by minute against the arterial line reading. They run through central lines because they can damage small veins.

Sedation and Analgesia

ICU patients on ventilators need pain control first and sedation second — pain is treated before adding sleep drugs. Daily sedation vacations wake the patient briefly to test readiness for weaning. Less sedation means fewer days on the ventilator and less delirium.

Hemodynamic Monitoring

An arterial line gives beat-to-beat blood pressure and easy blood draws. Central lines measure pressures near the heart and deliver powerful drugs. Together they let the team see exactly how the heart and vessels are responding to treatment.

Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy

CRRT is slow, gentle dialysis for unstable ICU patients whose kidneys have failed. It runs 24 hours a day, removing fluid and toxins gradually so blood pressure never crashes. Standard dialysis would be too rough for these patients.

ECMO Life Support

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation pumps blood outside the body, oxygenates it, and returns it — doing the work of failed heart or lungs. It is the last resort for patients who cannot survive on a ventilator alone. ECMO requires a dedicated specialist at the bedside around the clock.

Sepsis Bundles in the ICU

ICU sepsis care continues what the ED started: antibiotics, fluids, vasopressors, and source control like draining an abscess. Lactate levels and urine output guide resuscitation hour by hour. The goal is restoring tissue perfusion before organs fail.

ARDS Ventilation Strategy

Acute respiratory distress syndrome fills lungs with fluid, making them stiff. The strategy is low tidal volumes, higher PEEP to keep air sacs open, and prone positioning — flipping patients onto their stomachs to improve oxygenation. It is uncomfortable but lifesaving.

Delirium Prevention (ABCDEF Bundle)

ICU delirium — sudden confusion — lengthens stays and worsens outcomes. The ABCDEF bundle fights it: assess pain, wake and breathe trials, choose light sedation, screen for delirium, get patients moving early, and involve family. Every letter is evidence-based.

Central and Arterial Lines

Central lines go into large neck, chest, or groin veins for powerful drugs and pressure readings. Arterial lines go into wrist arteries for continuous pressure. Both are placed with ultrasound guidance and full sterile technique to prevent bloodstream infections.

ICU Nutrition Support

Critically ill patients burn enormous energy, so feeding starts within 24 to 48 hours, usually through a tube into the stomach. Dietitians calculate protein and calorie targets daily. Underfeeding weakens healing; overfeeding causes complications.

Pressure Injury Prevention

Immobilized ICU patients develop bedsores fast. Specialty beds redistribute pressure, and nurses reposition patients every two hours. Heels are floated off the mattress and skin is inspected daily — a stage 4 ulcer can take months to heal.

Weaning from the Ventilator

Weaning trials reduce ventilator support daily to test whether the patient can breathe alone. When trials succeed, the breathing tube comes out — extubation. Failed trials mean more time and a search for the cause: weakness, fluid, or infection.

Code Blue in the ICU

Even in the ICU, hearts stop. Because monitors alarm instantly and staff are steps away, compressions and drugs start in seconds. ICU codes have better survival than floor codes, but prevention — catching decline early — saves more lives.

Infection Control in the ICU

ICUs fight device infections: ventilator pneumonia, line infections, and catheter UTIs. Prevention bundles cover hand hygiene, sterile insertion, daily line-necessity checks, and head-of-bed elevation. One prevented infection can save a life and weeks of stay.

ICU Staffing Ratios

ICU nurses care for one or two patients at a time — the sickest get 1:1 care. This ratio exists because a ventilated patient on three drips needs constant eyes. Studies link better ratios directly to lower mortality.

Transfer and Step-Down Criteria

Patients leave the ICU when they breathe on their own, hold their blood pressure without drips, and need only routine monitoring. Step-down units bridge the gap with telemetry. Premature transfer risks bouncing back to the ICU.

End-of-Life Care in the ICU

When treatment cannot succeed, the ICU shifts to comfort: stopping painful interventions, controlling symptoms, and giving families time and space. Palliative teams guide these conversations. A peaceful, dignified death is a core ICU duty, not a failure.
