A Wooden Chair Offered to Neighbors Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A community marketplace website: a visitor picks a generic kind of thing, such as a wooden chair, a set of garden tools, or a box of books, and the site shows illustrative sample listings of that kind, each with a plain description, the condition, and the price. The site names no real person, copies no real listing, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real offer. This record covers A Wooden Chair Offered to Neighbors.

A wooden chair offered to neighbors gives a person a plain way to pass on a chair that is still sound, with what it is, its condition, and its price written out before anyone sets out. The marketplace must show the chair in plain words, so a neighbor who reads the listing knows whether the chair is sound and what it costs. Without those plain facts, a neighbor cannot tell a sound chair from a broken one, and a listing without a condition is a poor way to offer a chair.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a wooden chair offered to neighbors runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Offering useful things to neighbors is an old habit: a notice on a board, a table at a fair, a word passed along a street. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by kind of thing and a plain description for every listing. Offering wooden chairs to neighbors is an old habit, from a chair left at a door to a table at a fair.

Proposed solution

Name the marketplace listing and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every marketplace listing the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one marketplace listing without signing in; Check the marketplace listing works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

Dependencies

What must exist first: a list of generic kinds of things, a clearly labeled sample listing for each kind, a search box, and a review step before publishing. Nothing else is needed to open the first marketplace listing.

Feasibility
