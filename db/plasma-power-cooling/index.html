JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000001 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A plasma computer is a power-hungry machine by nature: every cell needs drive power to stay ionized, run its fields, and launch its waves. Power pieces are RF amplifiers, microwave sources, magnet supplies, and vacuum pumps; cooling pieces are the heat paths that carry waste energy away. The design discipline is the power budget: watts per cell times cell count, plus overhead, must fit the facility. Data centers already live by this math; plasma computing just changes the loads.

const per=1+0.5+0.3+0.2,total=per*1e6*1.3;J({MW:+(total/1e6).toFixed(1)});assert(Math.abs(total/1e6-2.6)<0.01,'budget');

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000002 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — RF and microwave drive plants

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The workhorse power sources are solid-state RF amplifiers (efficient at 13.56 MHz and below), klystrons and magnetrons for high-power microwaves, and gyrotrons at the extreme end (megawatt microwaves for fusion heating). A computing module needs many small sources rather than one big one: hundreds of 100-watt RF channels, each feeding a cell group. Solid-state RF keeps improving in efficiency and cost, which is the economic tailwind behind the whole concept.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000003 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Magnet and field supplies

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Confinement fields need steady, precise current: superconducting magnets for large strong fields (zero resistive loss once cold), copper electromagnets for small fast fields. A millitesla cell field needs only modest current, but thousands of cells need thousands of supplies — or clever sharing. Persistent-mode superconducting loops, which hold current with no supply at all, are the elegant answer for static fields: charge once, confine for months.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000004 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — The power budget: watts per cell

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Every cell's power splits four ways: ionization sustain (replacing energy lost to radiation and conduction), field coils (resistive losses, or cryocooler power for superconductors), wave drive (launching logic signals), and control overhead (probes, processors). A plausible budget for a millimetre cell: 1 to 10 watts. A million cells then needs 1 to 10 megawatts — a data-center-scale machine. The budget is the first number any serious design computes, and every other piece must fit inside it.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000005 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Cooling without touching the plasma

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Waste heat appears in the solid parts — antennas, coils, amplifiers, vessel walls — never in the plasma itself, which must stay hot to remain ionized. Cooling is conventional: water loops, refrigerant, and for superconductors, cryocoolers reaching a few kelvin. The thermal design keeps solid structures near room temperature while the plasma floats hot in its field bottle. Heat exchangers sized from the power budget complete the piece; this is data-center thermal engineering with a vacuum vessel in the middle.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000006 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Power integrity and ride-through

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Plasma is unforgiving of power dips: lose the drive and the plasma recombines in microseconds, taking the computation with it. Ride-through design includes uninterruptible supplies, local energy storage (capacitor banks at each module), and graceful degradation — shedding load region by region instead of dying all at once. Checkpointing to the archive tier (spheromak banks) lets the machine resume after an outage. Power integrity is a reliability piece, not a performance one.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000007 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Energy per operation and efficiency

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The efficiency metric is joules per logic operation: wave energy per gate divided by operations per second. A 9-GHz gate launching a picojoule wave does about 9e9 operations per watt of wave power — competitive with silicon in principle, before overhead. The honest accounting includes ionization sustain and cooling, which dominate. Efficiency tuning is a system sport: raise density for speed where it pays, lower it where it does not, and never spend watts holding plasma nobody is computing with.

JAH-PLASMA-PWR-000008 — Plasma Computer Power and Cooling — Power and cooling in the full stack

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Power and cooling underpin everything: no watts, no plasma; no cooling, no machine. Their interfaces are the power contract (volts, amps, and stability each subsystem gets) and the thermal contract (maximum temperatures at each interface). Beginner exercise: take a 1-watt cell budget and a million cells, add 30 percent overhead, and size the facility feed (about 1.3 megawatts) and the cooling plant to remove it. One multiplication, and the machine's scale is visible.
