# Dictionary entries — The Classified Advertisements Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## classified ad

A short paid notice, sorted by kind, saying what is offered or sought, kept with its advertiser and its dates, so that readers can find it under its true heading.

## advertisement

A public notice that offers something or seeks something, written to be read by strangers, and kept as a record with the name of the one who placed it.

## classified section

The part of a paper or a board where classified notices are gathered, sorted by kind, so that a reader can find one kind of notice without reading all the rest.

## advertiser

The person or business that places a classified notice, named in the record, so that every notice can be traced to the one who stands behind it.

## notice

One short announcement in the classified columns, written in few words, so that its meaning is plain and its kind is clear from its heading.

## listing

One notice as it appears in the classified section, with its heading, its words, and its dates, so that the notices can be counted one by one.

## category

One kind of notice, such as goods or services, named and kept apart from other kinds, so that a reader seeking one kind need not read the others.

## placement

The act of putting a notice into the classified section, under its true heading and for its stated run, so that the notice appears where its readers will look.

## expiry

The date a notice stops running, written with the notice when it is placed, so that the columns never show a notice whose time has passed.

## renewal

The act of running a notice for a further period, on terms written down again, so that a notice never runs on by silence and every period has its own record.

## response

A reader's answer to a notice, recorded with the notice it answers, so that the advertiser can see who answered and the record can show it was answered.

## reader

A person who reads the classified columns seeking a notice, named only in their own responses, so that the columns can serve them without keeping more than the response needs.
