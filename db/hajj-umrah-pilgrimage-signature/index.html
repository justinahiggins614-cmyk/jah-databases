Hajj Overview: The Rites in Order Rank 1 · simple

This Signature edition record is an original Signature companion study for the same subject, written for this database and never presented as an authentic public record. It follows the public record's facts where they are stated, and it adds a Signature framing: how a reader can study, map, and compare the subject using only this database's own tools, demos, and cross-links. Hajj is the annual pilgrimage to Mecca, performed in the Islamic month of Dhul-Hijjah, and its rites follow a fixed order that this record outlines factually and generally, without inventing doctrine. Pilgrims enter the state of Ihram, then perform Tawaf, the circling of the Kaaba, and Sa'i, the walking between Safa and Marwa, and then move to Mina, where they spend time before proceeding to Arafat for the Day of Arafat, then to Muzdalifah overnight, then back to Mina for the stoning at Jamarat, sacrifice, and the cutting of hair, followed by a further Tawaf, and further days at Mina. The exact sequence and the details of each rite vary in emphasis between schools of thought, and traditions differ on some points, so this outline is deliberately general: it gives the order that published factual outlines agree on, and it does not present one school's ruling as the ruling for all. This record is a fact record about a publicly documented sequence of events, not a religious ruling.

Umrah Overview Rank 2 · simple

This Signature edition record is an original Signature companion study for the same subject, written for this database and never presented as an authentic public record. Umrah is the lesser pilgrimage to Mecca, which, unlike Hajj, can be performed at any time of year, and its outline is shorter than Hajj. In published factual outlines, Umrah comprises entering Ihram, performing Tawaf around the Kaaba, performing Sa'i between Safa and Marwa, and then cutting or shaving the hair, which completes the Umrah. Umrah does not include the Mina, Arafat, and Muzdalifah sequence that defines Hajj, and during the Hajj season Umrah is suspended as the whole infrastructure shifts to Hajj, according to published pilgrimage management descriptions. Because Umrah can be performed year-round, its pilgrim numbers are spread across the year rather than concentrated in a few days, which is why published sources describe Umrah as drawing many times the annual Hajj number across a full year. This record is a fact record giving the general outline; schools of thought differ on some details, and traditions differ, so the outline stays general.

Ihram: A Factual Description Rank 3 · simple

Ihram is the sacred state a pilgrim enters before performing Hajj or Umrah, marked by an intention, by prescribed dress, and by a set of restrictions that last until the pilgrimage rites are completed. For men the dress is commonly described as two unsewn white cloths, and for women it is ordinary modest dress with the face and hands uncovered according to published general descriptions, though details vary and traditions differ on some points. The restrictions of Ihram are described in published sources as including refraining from cutting hair and nails, from using perfume, and from hunting, among others, and the state begins at designated boundary points, the Miqat, before entering Mecca. This record describes Ihram factually and generally: it states what published sources agree on, it notes where traditions differ rather than settling the difference, and it gives no religious ruling of its own.

Tawaf is the act of circling the Kaaba, the cube-shaped building at the centre of the Grand Mosque in Mecca, seven times in a counter-clockwise direction, beginning at the corner bearing the Black Stone. Published factual descriptions agree on the seven circuits and the counter-clockwise direction, and they describe pilgrims moving on the ground floor, on upper levels, and on the roof level of the mosque, which spreads the crowd across several floors. Tawaf is performed as part of both Hajj and Umrah, and it is also performed by visitors to the mosque at other times. The religious meaning of Tawaf belongs to Islamic tradition, and this record keeps the distinction clear: the circuits, direction, and levels are observable facts about what happens, while the spiritual significance is tradition, described as tradition in the tradition pair. Traditions differ on some details of Tawaf, and this general description does not settle those differences.

Mina is a valley east of Mecca where pilgrims stay in a vast temporary tent city during Hajj, and it is the site of the Jamarat, the three pillars at which pilgrims perform the stoning rite. Published descriptions describe Mina as holding hundreds of thousands of pilgrims in fire-resistant tents arranged in districts, with roads, services, and the multi-level Jamarat Bridge built to manage the crowds moving to and from the stoning. Arafat is an open plain east of Mecca, and the Day of Arafat, on the 9th day of Dhul-Hijjah, is the day pilgrims gather there, standing from midday until sunset in what published outlines describe as the central rite of Hajj, without which, in Islamic tradition, the Hajj is not complete. Hajj places are allocated by quota, described in published sources as roughly one place per 1,000 Muslims in each country, so that most Muslims who wish to perform Hajj cannot do so in any given year and waiting lists are long in many countries. Published logistics descriptions describe a system that begins before pilgrims arrive, with visas, flight coordination, airport movement, dedicated buses, and accommodation organised around fixed schedules, and continues on the ground with guided routes, crowd management, and health services. Since 2023 the Nusuk platform has been described in published sources as the online route by which pilgrims apply for Hajj, and the Makkah Route Initiative allows pilgrims from selected countries to clear passport and customs checks at their departure airport. This record is a fact record about a publicly described allocation and logistics system; country quotas change yearly, so no single year's quota table is given as permanent.

Pilgrim transport and accommodation form a seasonal system: the Haramain High Speed Railway links Mecca with Jeddah and Medina, fleets of buses move pilgrims between Mecca, Mina, Arafat, and Muzdalifah on fixed schedules, and accommodation ranges from hotels in Mecca to the tent city at Mina and open-air overnight stays at Muzdalifah. Published descriptions emphasise that the system is planned around the Hajj timetable rather than ordinary demand, with capacity, routes, and staffing rising sharply for a few days and then falling back. Outside Hajj, Umrah pilgrims use the same Mecca hotels and the same Jeddah airport and rail links year-round, but without the Mina and Arafat movements. This record is a fact record about publicly documented infrastructure and its seasonal pattern; prices, timetables, and operators change, so none are given as fixed facts here.

y = math.sin(dl)*math.cos(p2); x = math.cos(p1)*math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1)*math.cos(p2)*math.cos(dl)
bearing = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360) % 360
print('Place: ' + TITLE)
print('From: ' + FROM_CITY)
print('Great-circle distance to Mecca: %.1f km' % dist)
print('Initial bearing to Mecca: %.1f degrees' % bearing)
