A Lesson List for One Subject Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

An educational resources website: each resource is a free lesson on a plain subject, such as counting, spelling, map reading, or basic science, with its level shown clearly, its steps in plain words, and a practice task a learner can try at once. Lessons are sorted by subject and length, a learner can save a lesson to return to, and a weekly study plan gives every learner a path to follow. Nothing is sold on this site and no personal data is asked for: it is a design study with free lessons only. This record covers A Lesson List for One Subject.

A learner who cannot see the lessons for one subject in one place must hunt through many pages, and the lessons found are forgotten before the study begins. A lesson list for one subject writes every free lesson for that subject in one list, in plain words, so the learner can see them all at once. Without a single list, lessons live in scattered places, the learner compares from memory, and the best lesson is often the one forgotten.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a lesson list for one subject runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

Nothing is sold on this site and no personal data is asked for: it is a design study with free lessons only. This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Lesson List for One Subject, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Checklist With Review Before Launch.

History

Free lessons are an old habit: a public library shelf, an evening class in a hall, a teacher who writes a lesson on a board for anyone passing by. The website form grows out of that habit, adding lessons sorted by subject and level, a practice task with every lesson, and a weekly study plan a learner can follow. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.

Dependencies

What must exist first: a lesson list by subject, a level shown clearly, steps in plain words, and a practice task with every lesson. A tournament system, a creator studio, a review step, translations, and an offline pack are also needed before the complex learning resources open.
