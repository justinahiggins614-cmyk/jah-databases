History

Morphemes were identified as a unit of analysis in the nineteenth century, when linguists began to split words into meaningful parts instead of treating the word as indivisible. Jan Baudouin de Courtenay and later American structuralists, above all Leonard Bloomfield, made the morpheme the centre of word analysis, and generative morphology later treated morphemes as the pieces that word-formation rules combine. The topic is still taught and researched today, and the sources cited at the end of this record give the authoritative accounts on which this explanation rests.
