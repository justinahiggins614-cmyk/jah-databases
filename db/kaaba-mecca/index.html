Kaaba Overview Rank 1 · simple

The Kaaba is a near-cubic stone building at the centre of the Grand Mosque in Mecca, and it is the holiest site in Islam and the direction of prayer for Muslims worldwide. Published dimensions are about 12.86 metres long, 11.03 metres wide, and 13.1 metres high, built of stone with marble and limestone, at published coordinates 21.4225 degrees north and 39.82617 degrees east. Its exterior is draped in the Kiswah, a black covering replaced annually, and its gilded door stands above ground level on one side. Pilgrims circle the building seven times counterclockwise in tawaf, walking in the Mataf around it, and the building's role as qibla means that prayer direction everywhere is measured toward this point. The origin and early rebuilding of the Kaaba are reported in Islamic tradition, and this pair keeps those accounts in their own labeled records, so that measured facts and tradition accounts are never blended in one sentence. This pair contains no image of any person and no depiction of any kind beyond words about a building.

Kiswah (Covering) Rank 3 · simple

The Kiswah is the black cloth covering draped over the Kaaba, made of silk with a gold-embroidered band carrying calligraphy around the upper walls, and it is replaced annually in a published ceremony. A Kiswah factory was established in Mecca in 1928 according to published expansion history, so that the covering could be made locally rather than brought from elsewhere. The covering is fitted in panels around the four walls, with a separate section for the door, and its black colour against the white marble of the Mataf makes the Kaaba visible from every part of the mosque. The annual replacement is a fact about practice and manufacture, and the calligraphy on the band is described in published summaries in general terms only, because this record does not reproduce sacred text. Black Stone Rank 4 · simple

The Black Stone, Hajar al-Aswad, is set in the eastern corner of the Kaaba in a silver frame, and it marks the starting point for tawaf, the circling of the Kaaba. What a visitor sees today is a set of dark fragments held in that frame, because published history reports that the stone was broken and its fragments rejoined with a silver ligament, and that it was removed from Mecca for a period in the 10th century CE after a raid, returning later. Those historical events are reported in published histories and are stated here as published history. The origin of the stone, and its meaning for pilgrims, are reported in Islamic tradition, and this record labels those accounts as tradition: traditions differ in detail about the stone's origin, and this record says that traditions differ rather than settling the difference. No physical description beyond the published setting is offered here, and no claim is made about the stone's composition beyond what published summaries state, because composition claims without a cited survey would be speculation.

Construction and Repairs Through History Rank 8 · compounded

The present Kaaba was built after the original building was damaged by fire during the siege of Mecca in 683 CE, according to published histories, and it has been repaired and rebuilt in stages ever since. The most disruptive single episode in published history is the Qarmatian raid of 930 CE, when the Black Stone was carried away, returning in 952 CE after a ransom arrangement reported in published histories. Later centuries brought Ottoman repairs and re-cladding, and Saudi-era works reshaped the surroundings, moved the Maqam Ibrahim enclosure for circling room, and renewed the Kiswah system with a local factory from 1928. The building's dimensions have therefore been measured at different times with slightly different results, which is why this pair labels all dimensions approximate. The origin of the first building is reported in Islamic tradition, and that account is labeled tradition wherever it appears in this pair, never blended with the dated repair sequence given here.

Working demo program

import math
MECCA_LAT = 21.3891
MECCA_LON = 39.8579
def qibla(lat, lon):
    p1 = math.radians(lat); p2 = math.radians(MECCA_LAT)
    d = math.radians(MECCA_LON - lon)
    y = math.sin(d) * math.cos(p2)
    x = math.cos(p1) * math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.cos(d)
    b = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360.0) % 360.0
    a = math.sin((p2 - p1) / 2.0) ** 2 + math.cos(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.sin(d / 2.0) ** 2
    dist = 6371.0 * 2.0 * math.asin(math.sqrt(a))
    return b, dist
CITIES = [('London', 51.5074, -0.1278), ('Cairo', 30.0444, 31.2357), ('Jakarta', -6.2088, 106.8456), ('New York', 40.7128, -74.006), ('Jeddah', 21.4858, 39.1925)]

for name, lat, lon in CITIES:

    print('%s: qibla bearing %.2f degrees, distance %.1f km' % (name, b, d))

Qibla Role Rank 11 · compounded

That statement is a fact about religious practice: mosques are built with a prayer wall facing Mecca, prayer rows align toward it, and a compass or a computed bearing can verify the direction from any place. The meaning believers attach to facing one common point, unity in worship, is reported as tradition and belief, and this record labels it as such, because a shared meaning is not the same kind of statement as a measured bearing. Where a community has historically used a different local method for finding the direction, published histories report that variation, and this record notes that methods have varied while the point itself has remained the Kaaba. No depiction of any person appears in this record, and no doctrine is invented here.

Misconceptions Corrected Rank 13 · complex

Several common misconceptions about the Kaaba can be corrected from the published facts in this pair, and this record lists them plainly. First, the Kaaba is not a perfect cube: published dimensions of 12.86 by 11.03 by 13.1 metres make it a near-cube, longer in one direction and taller than it is wide. Second, the Black Stone as seen today is not a single unbroken stone: published history reports fragments held in a silver frame after breakage and repair. Third, the Grand Mosque's capacity is not one settled number: published figures from 820,000 to 3.0 million count different areas and eras, and the range is the honest answer. Fourth, tradition accounts and measured facts are not the same kind of statement: an origin reported in tradition remains tradition even when it is widely believed, and a dimension remains a measurement even when it is approximate. Fifth, the Kaaba's interior is not open to general visitors: published summaries state it is opened only rarely. Each correction names its published source, and no correction invents a new fact.

The Kaaba sits at the centre of the Grand Mosque, the Grand Mosque sits at the centre of Mecca, and Mecca sits at the centre of a worldwide direction of prayer, and those three nestings are the simplest way to hold this pair together. A reader of a city map sees the second: a mosque complex at the heart of a valley city, with roads, hotels, and services arranged around it for pilgrims. import math
MECCA_LAT = 21.3891
MECCA_LON = 39.8579
def qibla(lat, lon):
    p1 = math.radians(lat); p2 = math.radians(MECCA_LAT)
    d = math.radians(MECCA_LON - lon)
    y = math.sin(d) * math.cos(p2)
    x = math.cos(p1) * math.sin(p2) - math.sin(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.cos(d)
    b = (math.degrees(math.atan2(y, x)) + 360.0) % 360.0
    a = math.sin((p2 - p1) / 2.0) ** 2 + math.cos(p1) * math.cos(p2) * math.sin(d / 2.0) ** 2
    dist = 6371.0 * 2.0 * math.asin(math.sqrt(a))
    return b, dist
CITIES = [('Riyadh', 24.7136, 46.6753), ('Medina', 24.5247, 39.5692), ('Dubai', 25.2048, 55.2708), ('Istanbul', 41.0082, 28.9784)]
print('Mecca reference point: %.4fN, %.4fE' % (MECCA_LAT, MECCA_LON))
for name, lat, lon in CITIES:
    b, d = qibla(lat, lon)
    print('%s: qibla bearing %.2f degrees, distance %.1f km' % (name, b, d))
