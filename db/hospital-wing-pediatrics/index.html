Weight-Based Dosing

Every pediatric drug dose is calculated from the child's weight in kilograms, then double-checked by two nurses. A decimal slip can mean a tenfold overdose, so independent verification is mandatory. The Broselow tape gives emergency doses by length when there is no time to weigh.

def pediatric_dose(weight_kg, mg_per_kg, max_dose=None):
    """Weight-based pediatric dose calculator with safety cap."""
    dose = weight_kg * mg_per_kg
    if max_dose and dose > max_dose:
        return f"{max_dose} mg (capped at max)"
    return f"{round(dose,1)} mg for {weight_kg} kg child"

Broselow Tape

The Broselow tape measures a child's length and reads out the matching weight zone with pre-calculated drug doses and equipment sizes. In a code, the team reads doses straight off the tape instead of calculating. It turns chaos into a lookup table.

PALS Resuscitation

Common Admissions: Bronchiolitis

Bronchiolitis, usually from RSV, inflames tiny airways in infants, causing wheezing and poor feeding. Treatment is supportive: oxygen, suctioning, and fluids — antibiotics do not help viruses. Most babies recover fully with monitoring.

FLACC Pain Scale

Babies and young children cannot rate pain from 1 to 10, so nurses use FLACC: Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability. Each gets 0 to 2 points. It gives preverbal children a voice in their own pain care.

Fever Workups in Infants

Fever in babies under 28 days is treated as an emergency: full sepsis workup and admission, because their immune systems cannot localize infection. Older infants get risk-stratified testing. The rule is simple — young fever means thorough evaluation.

Croup and Airway Emergencies

Croup's barky cough and stridor mean a swollen airway; nebulized racemic epinephrine and steroids open it. True emergencies like epiglottitis are rare but need instant airway expertise. Calm, upright positioning helps every noisy breather.
