A Quiet Piano Piece for a Calm Evening Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A music discovery website: a visitor picks a generic kind of music, such as a quiet piano piece or a song with a strong chorus, and the site shows illustrative sample pieces of that kind, each with a plain description of the instruments, the tempo, and the mood. The site names no real song, no real artist, and makes no claim that any sample entry is a real recording. This record covers A Quiet Piano Piece for a Calm Evening.

A quiet piano piece gives a listener one piano, slow notes, and silence between them, with a calm that settles over the whole room. The listing must say how long the piece runs, how slow the tempo is, and what mood it carries, so a listener who wants calm can find it. Without those plain facts, a listener who wanted quiet may find a loud tune instead, and a loud tune is a poor gift for a calm wish. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a quiet piano piece for a calm evening runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

Finding music worth hearing is an old habit: a record played in a shop, a song heard on the radio, a tip from a friend. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a search by kind of music and a plain description for every listing. Quiet piano pieces are an old habit, from a player in a drawing room to a slow tune on a winter evening.

Proposed solution

Name the music listing and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every music listing the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one music listing without signing in; Check the music listing works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

Balancing a month by hand is slow work, and automatic balancing needs a system that understands variety, cost, and taste together, without choosing for the listener. That joint understanding is still research-stage: small tests balance a few days well, but a full month with real budgets still defeats the planner.
