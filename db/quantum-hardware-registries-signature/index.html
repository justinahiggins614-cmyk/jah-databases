A Josephson junction is two superconductors separated by a thin insulating barrier, through which Cooper pairs tunnel without resistance. Its two defining numbers are the critical current Ic and the normal-state resistance Rn; their product IcRn sets the characteristic voltage, and the Josephson energy EJ = Ic * Phi0 / (2 * pi) sets the qubit frequency scale.

Designers of superconducting qubits need the real parameter ranges of Josephson junctions before they can choose a junction for a transmon, flux, or phase qubit. Niobium junctions reach IcRn products near 1 millivolt. This record banks those published ranges with the formulas that connect them, so a reader can check any junction against the published scale instead of guessing.
