A fictional internet website: a visitor picks a kind of invented world, such as an internet of floating islands or an internet of endless libraries, and the site shows the invented sites of that world in plain words, what each site holds, who visits it, and what a visitor can do there. Every site is invented and labeled as fiction, and no real website is named or copied. This record is the field guide index for the whole database: it lists all twenty-three content records in ladder order, simple to compounded to complex, and tells a reader where to start.

A database of twenty-three records needs a map, or a reader will not know where to start, which records are simple and which are complex, and how the records fit together. Without an index, the simple records that teach the design are buried beside the complex records that assume it, and a visitor who wants one idea must read the whole database to find it. The index solves this by listing every record in ladder order, with its place in the ladder, so a reader can start at the beginning and climb, or jump straight to the record they need.

Worked example, Signature design: a reader who wants the simplest starting point opens record 1, A Fictional Internet of Floating Islands, and a reader who wants the hardest problem opens record 23, A Yearly Showcase of Fictional internets, in the Signature edition.

History

Inventing whole imaginary worlds is an old habit: a map drawn on the back of a schoolbook, a story about a town that never existed, a game where every shop has an invented name. The website form grows out of that habit, adding a plain guide for each invented world and a list a visitor can wander. Field guides and indexes are as old as books themselves: a table of contents that tells a reader where everything lives. This index follows that habit, listing the twenty-three content records of this database in the order a reader should meet them.
