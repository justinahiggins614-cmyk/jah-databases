1. Description

A workflow manager moves tasks through steps, from start to done. Add tasks, move them forward a step at a time, count them by step, and watch the report. Everything runs in this browser and saves locally; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

A workflow is a list of steps, and a task sits at one step at a time. With 5 tasks split 2, 2 and 1 across three steps, the counts add to 5. A report is a count: 5 tasks, 3 done, 2 still moving. Adding a step makes a workflow of 4 steps into 5, and removing one makes 5 into 4.

3. Science proof

A workflow works because work is easier in small named stages: each step has one job, and a task moves only when its step is finished. Counting tasks by step shows where work piles up, which is why a manager watches the counts rather than guessing. That is why the same steps, used for every task, keep the work steady.
