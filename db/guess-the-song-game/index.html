A Song Guessing Round From a Short Clip Record 1 · simple

Feasibility: buildable-now The design uses only web pages, forms, and counters that exist today; only the design work stands in the way.

A song guessing website: a short clip of a tune is played, the player types the song title in plain words, more of the tune is played as extra clues, and points are given for early correct guesses. The game is fair play only, with no real-money play of any kind, and every tune used is either a public-domain melody, an original tune written for the game, or a tune the player supplied themselves. This record covers A Song Guessing Round From a Short Clip.

A song guessing round plays only a short clip of a tune, because the whole song would give the answer away and no clip at all would leave nothing to guess. The clip must be short enough to challenge and long enough to recognize, which is a balance the game has to strike for every song. Without the short clip, the round is either a test of memory with no music at all or a concert with no question in it. This record states the design in plain sentences so a reader can judge it on its own merits, and its demo checks the arithmetic behind the design.

Worked example: in a website built this way, a song guessing round from a short clip runs as a small, complete feature a visitor can try in one sitting, with the fair-play and moderation rules of this database applied throughout.

History

The website form grows out of that pastime, adding a shared daily song, a score that can be compared fairly, and rounds sorted by era and style. Short clips became the heart of song guessing when radio quizzes learned that a few bars were enough for a good listener.

Proposed solution

Name the song round and what a visitor does in it; Write the rules in plain sentences a new visitor can follow; Give every song round the same fair start: the same clues, the same time, the same scoring; Let a visitor try one song round without signing in; Check the song round works the same way on a phone and on a computer.

This record covers A Streak System for Returning Players.

A streak gives a returning player a reason to come back tomorrow: one song guessed each day in a row builds a chain, and breaking the chain costs something the player has earned. The streak must be forgiving, because a player who misses one day through no fault of their own should not lose a month of play. Without a streak, every visit is a first visit, and the game gives a loyal player nothing that a stranger does not also get.
