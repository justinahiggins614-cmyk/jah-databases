Definition: Journalism is the gathering, checking, and publishing of news: the reporting of what has happened, to the people who need to know. The journalist finds the facts, checks them, and tells the story fairly, so the reader, the listener, and the viewer can understand the world and decide for themselves. Journalism is the work of the newspaper, the radio, the television, and the internet, and it is the work that holds power to account.

History: Journalism began with the printed news. In the 17th century, the first newspapers appeared in Europe, and the news became a regular, printed thing. In the 20th century, the radio and the television made the news instant, and the journalist became the witness of the world. In the 21st century, the internet made everyone a publisher, and the journalist became the checker, the verifier, and the explainer.
