# Dictionary entries — The Newsletter Creation and Distribution Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## newsletter

A letter sent on a schedule to people who asked for it, carrying news, written once and read by every subscriber.

## issue

One sending of a newsletter, with its own date and its own contents, kept in the archive so that any issue can be read again.

## subscriber

A person who asked to receive a newsletter, by giving their address, and who may stop receiving it at any time with one click.

## subscription

The agreement between a newsletter and a subscriber: the newsletter sends on its schedule, and the subscriber may leave at any time.

## unsubscribe

A subscriber's choice to stop receiving a newsletter, honored at once, with no questions and no delay, and the address removed from the list.

## archive

The kept record of every issue a newsletter has sent, in order, so that any reader can find what was said and when.

## template

The fixed shape of a newsletter's issues: the heading, the sections, and the ending, kept the same so that readers know where to look.

## delivery

The carrying of an issue to a subscriber's inbox, marked as delivered, bounced, or opened, so that the sender knows how far it reached.

## bounce

An issue that could not be delivered, because the address is wrong or the inbox is full, counted so that dead addresses are known, not guessed.

## open rate

The share of delivered issues that were opened, counted so that the sender knows how many readers actually read, not only how many received.

## schedule

The promised rhythm of a newsletter: weekly, monthly, or as news happens, written where every subscriber can see it and kept.

## ledger

The newsletter's own written record of every issue, every subscriber, and every delivery, kept in order.
