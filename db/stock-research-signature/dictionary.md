# Dictionary entries — The Stock Research Database

Key terms of this database, defined in plain words.

## stock

One part of a company's ownership, bought and sold in the market, with its price counted by the day and its owner recorded, so that who owns the company is always known.

## share

One equal part of a company's ownership, counted and recorded, so that the company's owners and their holdings can be read from the share record.

## stock market

The public place where shares are bought and sold, with prices counted and published, so that any reader can see what a share last sold for and when.

## price

The amount last paid for one share, counted at a stated time, so that the price is always a fact about a moment, never a fact about all time.

## dividend

Money a company pays its owners from its gains, counted per share and dated, so that the payment can be traced to the company's own published account.

## earnings

The gains a company reports for a stated period, counted by the company's own rules and published, so that any reader can see what the company earned and when.

## fundamental analysis

The study of a company by its published accounts — its gains, its debts, and its business — so that the company is judged by what it does, not by what its price did yesterday.

## technical analysis

The study of a share by its past prices, read in order, so that the pattern of the price can be seen, with the warning that past prices do not promise future ones.

## market value

The price of one share multiplied by the number of shares, counted at a stated time, so that the whole company's market size can be stated as one number.

## volume

The number of shares that changed hands in a stated period, counted and published, so that the market's activity can be seen beside its prices.

## sector

A named group of companies that do the same kind of work, so that a company can be compared with its peers and not with companies that do something else entirely.

## research note

A written account of what the research found about a company, with every figure traced to its published source, kept so the study can be checked and repeated.
