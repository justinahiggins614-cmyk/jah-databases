1. Description

A project manager tracks a project: its tasks, its people, its budget and its deadline. Add tasks, mark them done, and watch the progress percent and the money left. Everything runs in this browser and saves locally; no data leaves the page.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Progress is tasks done divided by all tasks: 4 of 8 tasks done is 50 percent, and 8 of 8 is 100 percent. Money left is the budget minus what is spent: a budget of 1000 with 400 spent leaves 600. Hours add across people: 3 people working 4 hours each is 12 hours. The longest chain of 3 tasks of 2 days is 6 days.

3. Science proof

A project works because a big piece of work is easier as small tasks with names, dates and owners: each task can be finished, counted and checked. Progress as a percent and money left are both counts, so the state of the project can be read at a glance instead of guessed. That is why the manager lists every task and counts it.
