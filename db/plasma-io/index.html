JAH-PLASMA-IO-000001 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Overview and definition

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Input-output is the plasma computer's skin: every bit that enters or leaves must cross from solid electronics into ionized gas and back, without letting the plasma touch anything solid or letting air poison the vacuum. The classic tools are antennas (to launch waves), probes (to sense them), windows (to pass light and microwaves), and differential pumping (to keep vacuum at the boundary). I/O is where the 'no solid state' rule bends: the computer's core is solid-free, but its ports necessarily meet the solid world.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000002 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Launch antennas and wave injection

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Waves enter plasma through antennas: small electrodes, loops, or waveguides that impose an oscillating field at the boundary. Capacitive probes launch electrostatic waves; loop antennas launch electromagnetic ones; phased arrays steer the launch direction. Antenna design is mature — radio engineers and fusion labs launch megawatts into plasma routinely. For computing, the antennas shrink to micro-scale and multiply: every cell edge gets its own launcher, and launch timing is the machine's write strobe.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000003 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Probes and sensing

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Sensing starts with the Langmuir probe: a small wire whose current-versus-voltage curve reveals electron density and temperature — the standard plasma diagnostic since the 1920s. Magnetic pickup coils sense vortices and field changes; interferometers measure line-averaged density by the phase shift of a beam passing through; spectroscopy reads temperature and impurities from emitted light. A computing cell's read port is a miniaturized diagnostic suite: density, field, and wave phase, all sampled every few plasma periods.

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Signals cross the vacuum wall through windows and feedthroughs: quartz and ceramic windows pass microwaves and light; metal-ceramic feedthroughs carry high voltage and RF; differential pumping stages let gas in while keeping vacuum. These are solved engineering — every vacuum tube, accelerator, and fusion device uses them. The computing requirement is density of ports: thousands of feedthroughs per module, each leak-tight to ultra-high vacuum, each rated for the drive power.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000005 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Analog-to-plasma and plasma-to-digital conversion

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. The solid world speaks in voltages on wires; the plasma speaks in waves. Conversion is modulation and demodulation: a digital-to-analog converter drives an antenna whose amplitude, phase, or frequency encodes the input bits; the returning wave is digitized by a fast ADC after a detector. This is exactly how radios and fiber links work — modulation theory is a century old. The plasma computer's 'modem' runs at gigahertz with microsecond packets, and its constellation diagrams are drawn in plasma density instead of voltage.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000006 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Bandwidth and latency of plasma ports

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. A port's bandwidth is set by the plasma frequency and the antenna coupling: at 9 gigahertz of plasma frequency, a well-coupled port moves gigabits per second. Latency is the wave transit time across the cell plus conversion delay — nanoseconds for millimetre cells. The I/O budget of the machine is a real engineering number: ports times bandwidth must exceed the program's data appetite, or the computer starves. Sizing ports is the same exercise as sizing memory buses in silicon.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000007 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — Contamination control at the boundary

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. Every port is a contamination risk: feedthroughs outgas, windows sputter, and pump oil creeps. The defenses are material choice (low-outgassing ceramics and metals), bakeout (heating the vessel to drive off adsorbed gas), getter pumps that chemically trap impurities, and gas curtains that sweep the boundary. Purity specs are written as partial pressures: water below 10^-7 torr, hydrocarbons below 10^-8. The I/O piece owns these numbers.

JAH-PLASMA-IO-000008 — Plasma Computer Input-Output — I/O in the full stack

Public edition. Public physics and engineering knowledge, fact-checked reference. Speculative material is absent from this edition; anything not yet established science lives in the Signature twin. I/O wraps the whole machine: it feeds programs and data in, streams results out, and carries diagnostics to the operator. Its interfaces are a port map (which physical port reaches which cell), a bandwidth contract, and a purity contract. Beginner exercise: compute the transit time of a 9-GHz wave across a 1-millimetre cell (about 0.3 nanoseconds at near light speed for electromagnetic waves; slower for electrostatic — look up the group velocity). That number is the machine's heartbeat at the boundary.
