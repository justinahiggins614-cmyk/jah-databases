  1. What an Invention Licensing Marketplace Is

    Record 1 of 24. simple. Feasibility: proven. An invention licensing marketplace is a catalog where fictional invention examples are listed with their licensing terms, so that a licensor who owns an idea and a licensee who can make and sell a product can find each other. This catalog lists twenty-four records: what the marketplace is, the roles on each side, the licence types, the money, the valuation methods, the agreement terms and the mechanics of listing, searching and negotiating. Every invention here is fictional, every figure is a worked example, and nothing here is a real invention for license or a real agreement. This is general information about licensing, not legal advice.

    Math proof: A royalty is a percentage of sales: royalty equals sales times rate divided by 100. In this worked example, 200,000 dollars of sales at 5 percent gives 10,000 dollars, because 200,000 times 5 divided by 100 is 10,000. Change the sales or the rate and the same steps give the new royalty, which is why every figure in this catalog can be checked with plain arithmetic.

    Science proof: A marketplace works because it lowers the cost of finding a partner: an inventor with an idea and a company that can make it would otherwise never meet. Listing many inventions in one place lets buyers compare, and comparison keeps prices honest. That is why a catalog with clear terms, categories and worked royalty examples helps more licences happen than scattered private introductions.
