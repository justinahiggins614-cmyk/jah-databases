History
People have planned their food since the first households kept stores for the winter, first in tallies of grain and salt, then in written menus and household books, and now in plans that hold the weekly menu, the shopping list, the pantry record, and the food diary side by side. Nutrition planning is the writing down of what is eaten, when, and from what, so that the food of a household can be read as a whole. From the paper menu pinned to the kitchen wall to the record kept in a database, the honest practice has been the same — write the food down whole. This database holds records and general guidance only, and gives no medical advice. It is built on that public practice of recording every nutrition planning record true.
