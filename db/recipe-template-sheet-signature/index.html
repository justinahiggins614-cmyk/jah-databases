Signature design: a bread-loaf template sheet designed for the Signature network, with the field order chosen so a new baker can follow it without re-reading.

This Signature design rethinks the plain loaf sheet for a cook who has never baked bread. The design decision is the field order: servings first, so the cook knows the scale before weighing anything; then the four weights in one line, so nothing is missed; then the two rises as separate numbered steps, so the cook can see where the waiting happens. The public edition records the settled method; this Signature design adds a design rule of its own: every quantity is given by weight, never by volume, and every time is a range, never a single number, so the sheet teaches judgment instead of pretending the kitchen is a laboratory. This is a design, not an established convention.

History

The Signature version keeps the same four ingredients and the same two rises, and changes the sheet itself: the fields are ordered for a first-time baker, the weights are grouped, and the example loaf is an invented Signature loaf with honey and oats. The design is forward-projecting and has not been put into practice as a published standard.

Method

Read the servings first, then weigh every ingredient before starting.; Mix and knead until the dough is smooth and springs back when pressed.; Let the dough rise until doubled, then shape it into the tin.; Let it rise again until it crowns the tin, then bake until deep gold.; Cool the loaf on a rack before slicing so the crumb sets..

Template

Servings: 8; prep time: 20 minutes; cook time: 35 minutes.

Ingredients

  • plain flour: 450 g
  • rolled oats: 50 g
  • water: 325 g
  • dried yeast: 7 g
  • salt: 10 g
  • honey: 20 g

Steps

  1. Weigh the flour, oats, water, yeast, salt, and honey.
  2. Mix into a rough dough and knead until smooth and elastic.
  3. Cover and leave to rise until doubled in size.
  4. Shape the dough and place it seam-down in a greased loaf tin.
  5. Leave to rise again until the dough crowns the rim of the tin.
  6. Bake until deep gold, then turn out and cool on a rack.

Equipment

  • large mixing bowl
  • loaf tin
  • clean work surface
  • tea towel
  • cooling rack
  • oven

Worked example

Knead until smooth, rise until doubled, shape into a tin, rise again until the dough crowns the rim, and bake until deep gold. Cool fully before slicing. The example is an invented Signature design, not a real or famous recipe.

Variations

Swap the honey for malt extract; add seeds on top before baking; shape the same dough into rolls instead of a tin loaf and shorten the bake; raise the oat share and add a little more water for a heartier crumb.

Storage

Cool fully, then keep in a bread bag or wrapped in a clean cloth at room temperature for a day or two; slice and freeze any loaf that will not be eaten promptly, and thaw slices as needed.

Scaling rules

Scale every ingredient by the serving ratio, including the yeast, the salt, and the honey. Do not scale the rise time: a doubled batch rises in about the same time, while the bake lengthens a little. Split a large batch into two tins rather than one oversized loaf.

import hashlib
import json

def scale(ingredients, factor):
    return [[name, round(qty * factor, 2), unit] for name, qty, unit in ingredients]

target_servings = 16
ingredients = [['plain flour', 450.0, 'g'], ['rolled oats', 50.0, 'g'], ['water', 325.0, 'g'], ['dried yeast', 7.0, 'g'], ['salt', 10.0, 'g'], ['honey', 20.0, 'g']]

scaled_b = scale(ingredients, factor)

print("SHA-256 run 1: " + digest_a)
print("SHA-256 run 2: " + digest_b)
