Canada Lotto 6/49 Overview and Matrix

Lotto 6/49 is Canada's long-running national lottery game. A play costs 3 Canadian dollars and includes one Classic selection of six numbers from 1 to 49, plus a 10-digit Gold Ball Draw number printed on the ticket. A draw produces six main numbers plus a bonus number drawn from the balls that remain, and matching all six main numbers wins the Classic jackpot.

The current matrix is 6 from 49 with a bonus number. The Classic jackpot is fixed at 5 million Canadian dollars for every draw: it does not roll over and does not grow. The growing jackpot in the game lives in the separate Gold Ball Draw, described in the matrix history and rollover records. Draws take place on Wednesday and Saturday evenings at about 10:30 pm Eastern Time. The exact jackpot odds for one Classic selection are 1 in 13,983,816.

Canada Lotto 6/49 Prize Tiers and Odds

Lotto 6/49 Classic has seven prize tiers: six main numbers, five plus the bonus, five without the bonus, four, three, two plus the bonus, and two without the bonus. The two-number tier pays a free play rather than cash, and the three-number and two-plus-bonus tiers pay fixed small amounts under the published table, while the top tiers are variable or fixed as published for the draw. Canada Lotto Max Overview and Current Matrix

Lotto Max is Canada's high-jackpot national game. The current format, in effect from 14 April 2026, is four selections of seven numbers from 1 to 52 per 6 Canadian dollar play: the player chooses one selection, and the terminal adds three further selections. A draw produces seven main numbers plus a bonus number used in several lower prize tiers. Matching all seven main numbers on one selection wins a share of the jackpot.

The jackpot starts at 10 million Canadian dollars and can grow to a cap of 90 million Canadian dollars. One selection has 133,784,560 possible combinations, so the jackpot odds per selection are 1 in 133,784,560, and the jackpot odds per 6 dollar play, which holds four selections, are 1 in 33,446,140. Draws take place on Tuesday and Friday evenings. The former format, before 14 April 2026, was three selections of seven numbers from 1 to 50 per 5 dollar play, with per-play jackpot odds of 1 in 33,294,800, and older tables describing that format must not be mixed with the current format.

Canada Lotto Max Prize Tiers and MaxMillions Mechanics

Lotto Max prize tiers, per selection, are seven main numbers, six plus the bonus, six without the bonus, five plus the bonus, five without, four plus the bonus, four without, three plus the bonus, and three without. Higher tiers are variable and move with ticket sales and winner counts, while lower tiers pay fixed amounts under the published table. Two additional prize layers run with the main draw. MaxMillions prizes of 1 million Canadian dollars each are drawn when the main jackpot reaches or exceeds 50 million Canadian dollars, using the player's original Lotto Max numbers, at no extra cost. MaxPlus prizes of 100,000 Canadian dollars each are drawn at every draw, with the count tied to the jackpot size in millions: a 10 million dollar jackpot gives 10 such prizes, a 20 million dollar jackpot gives 20, and so on up to 90 at the cap. MaxMillions prizes not won are redrawn in the following week until the main jackpot is won.

Canada Daily Grand as a Related Game

Daily Grand is a related Canadian national game with a lifetime-style top prize. A player selects five numbers from 1 to 49 plus one Grand Number from 1 to 7. The grand prize is 1,000 Canadian dollars per day for life, and the grand-prize odds are 1 in 13,348,188, which is the product of 1,906,884 combinations for five numbers from 49 and seven choices for the Grand Number.

Daily Grand draws take place on Monday and Thursday evenings. The game is related to Lotto 6/49 and Lotto Max only in the sense that all three are national draw games distributed through the provincial and regional lottery corporations; each game has its own matrix, draw days, prize structure, and result, and a ticket for one game is never an entry into another game.

Canada Encore as a Related Game

Encore is a regional add-on game available in Ontario and, under related names such as Extra, in other regions. It is added to a lottery ticket for an extra 1 dollar. The player receives a seven-digit Encore number, and prizes are paid for matching digits from the right: matching all seven digits wins the 1 million Canadian dollar top prize, and matching the last six digits wins 100,000 Canadian dollars, with smaller prizes for fewer trailing digits matched.

Encore is independent of the main numbers on the same ticket. A ticket can win an Encore prize while winning nothing in the main draw, and a main-draw win does not affect the Encore result. The Encore number for a draw is published with that draw's results, and the example in the verification record for this pair shows a draw in which the Encore number was 8096921, no ticket matched all seven digits, and one ticket matching the last six digits won 100,000 Canadian dollars. Encore availability, price, and prize tables vary by region and should be confirmed through the regional lottery corporation before any amount is quoted.

Canada Draw Mechanics and Schedules

Canadian national draws are run by the Interprovincial Lottery Corporation and distributed through the five provincial and regional lottery organizations. Lotto 6/49 draws take place on Wednesday and Saturday, Lotto Max draws on Tuesday and Friday, and Daily Grand draws on Monday and Thursday. National draw results are typically published at about 10:30 pm Eastern Time on draw nights, and ticket sales close shortly before the draw.

Each draw is a separate random selection. A Lotto 6/49 draw produces six main numbers, a bonus number, and a Gold Ball Draw result. A Lotto Max draw produces seven main numbers, a bonus number, MaxMillions results when the jackpot threshold is met, and MaxPlus results. A Daily Grand draw produces five main numbers and a Grand Number. Results for one game never carry over into another game, and a ticket is valid only for the draws printed on it.

Canada Lotto Matrix History

Lotto 6/49 launched in June 1982 as Canada's first national game allowing players to select their own numbers. A Guaranteed Prize Draw side game, printing a 10-digit number on each ticket, was added in September 2013. On 14 September 2022 the game was split into two components on the same ticket: the Classic Draw, with a fixed 5 million dollar jackpot, and the Gold Ball Draw, which replaced the Guaranteed Prize Draw and carries the growing jackpot.

Lotto Max launched in September 2009 as a 7 from 49 game. In May 2019 it moved to 7 from 50, added a Tuesday draw alongside Friday, raised its cap from 60 million to 80 million dollars, and expanded its prize tiers. On 14 April 2026 it moved again to 7 from 52, four selections per 6 dollar play, a 90 million dollar cap, and the new MaxPlus layer. Tables and odds from any earlier format must be labeled with their format and date, never presented as current.

Canada Lotto Frequency Method and Simulated Set Demo

The frequency method counts how often each number has appeared in past draws and ranks numbers from most frequent to least frequent. It is a descriptive summary of history: it answers the question of what has been drawn before, and it says nothing about what will be drawn next, because each draw is an independent random selection from the same pool.

Canada Lotto Hot Numbers Method

The hot numbers method selects numbers that have appeared most often in a chosen window of recent draws. The draw mechanism has no memory: the balls are not weighted by past appearances, and a number that appeared often in the last fifty draws has exactly the same chance as any other number in the next draw.

A hot list is therefore a report about a chosen window, and different windows give different hot lists. Changing the window, adding or removing a few draws, or starting the window one draw later can move a number on or off the list. Canada Lotto Cold and Overdue Numbers Method

The cold and overdue method selects numbers that have appeared least often, or that have gone longest without appearing, on the theory that such numbers are due to appear. That theory is the gambler's fallacy applied to a lottery: a random draw does not compensate for past absences, and a number that has been absent for many draws is no more likely in the next draw than a number drawn in the previous draw.

Overdue counts are still useful as descriptive statistics. They show gaps in a chosen history, and long gaps are expected in random data: with 49 numbers and six drawn per draw, a typical number is absent from most draws, so gaps of many draws occur routinely without any mechanism behind them. Canada Lotto Rollover Patterns

Rollover means different things in the two main Canadian games, and the difference is the point of this record. In Lotto 6/49, the Classic jackpot does not roll: it is fixed at 5 million dollars every draw, win or no win. The growing jackpot is the Gold Ball jackpot, which starts at 10 million dollars, grows by 2 million dollars after each draw in which the gold ball is not drawn, and is capped at 68 million dollars. The Gold Ball Draw uses 30 balls, one of which is gold, and one ball is removed after each draw in which the gold ball is not drawn, so the chance of drawing gold rises through a cycle.

In Lotto Max, the main jackpot does roll: it grows from 10 million dollars until it is won or reaches the 90 million dollar cap, and once it reaches 50 million dollars, extra 1 million dollar MaxMillions prizes are added. Canada Lotto Verification Guide

A ticket should be verified against the official result for its own draw, not against a summary or a result from another draw night. The official sources are the provincial and regional lottery corporations: the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation, the British Columbia Lottery Corporation, the Western Canada Lottery Corporation, Loto-Quebec, the Atlantic Lottery Corporation, and the Interprovincial Lottery Corporation for the national games. Each corporation publishes results for the games it distributes, and the national result is the same across corporations for a given draw.

Four checks prevent the most common errors. First, confirm the game and the format date: Lotto Max results before 14 April 2026 belong to the 7 from 50 format, and results from that date belong to the 7 from 52 format; Lotto 6/49 results before 14 September 2022 belong to the single rolling jackpot, and later results split Classic and Gold Ball. Second, confirm the draw date and draw night. Third, check the main draw, the bonus tier, the Gold Ball or MaxMillions and MaxPlus layers, and any Encore or Extra add-on separately. Fourth, confirm the claim period, which is about one year from the draw date in each province, and keep the ticket until any prize claim is settled.

Canada Lotto 6/49 Versus Lotto Max Comparison and Responsible Play

Lotto 6/49 and Lotto Max differ in matrix, price, jackpot behavior, and odds. Lotto 6/49 costs 3 dollars, uses 6 numbers from 49, draws on Wednesday and Saturday, offers a fixed 5 million dollar Classic jackpot plus the Gold Ball jackpot starting at 10 million dollars and capped at 68 million dollars, and gives jackpot odds of 1 in 13,983,816 per Classic selection. Lotto Max costs 6 dollars, uses four selections of seven numbers from 52, draws on Tuesday and Friday, offers a jackpot starting at 10 million dollars and capped at 90 million dollars plus MaxMillions and MaxPlus layers, and gives jackpot odds of 1 in 33,446,140 per play.

The shorter jackpot odds for 6/49 do not make any particular 6/49 line likely, and the larger Lotto Max jackpot does not make any particular Lotto Max selection likely.
