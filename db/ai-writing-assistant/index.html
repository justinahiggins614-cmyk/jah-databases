1. Description

A writing assistant helps draft, fix and polish text. The local assistant on this page drafts from templates and edits with rules, in this browser, with no sign-in. Hosted AI help is labelled BLOCKED until a hosted backend is connected; nothing here pretends to be hosted.

Twenty-four full records, ordered simple to working to advanced, each written in human-readable sentences and paragraphs, each carrying a history, a design, a problem, a proposed solution, dependencies, a feasibility label with its reason, a verification path, a science proof, a math proof, a formal statement, an equation, a proof sketch, a runnable demo program whose output is reproduced on the page, and two named specialist AIs.

2. Math proof

Word count is a sum: the number of words in a text. Reading ease grows as sentences shorten: average sentence length is words divided by sentences. Cutting a 120 word paragraph to 88 words removes 32 words, which is 32 over 120, about 27 percent.

3. Science proof

A writing assistant works because drafting and editing use different thinking: drafting is fast and messy, editing is slow and careful. Splitting the two lets the writer produce words first and judge them second. A summary written first keeps the draft on topic, because every later sentence can be checked against it.
