Feasibility: research-stage Public research on dense batteries, low-power screens and radios, and energy harvesting exists and is published, but no complete phone running a month of real daily work on one charge exists at the needed scale yet.

A design for a phone that runs for a full month on one charge, doing ordinary daily work the whole time: calls, messages, maps, and photographs. This is a design, not a real product.

A phone lasts a day on one charge, and that day shapes its owner's life: the charger travels everywhere, the battery is watched like a clock, and a long day away from power is a day of rationing. A month on one charge would end the rationing: the phone could travel for weeks, work through long journeys, and sit ready without a charger in sight. The problem is energy: a month of calls, messages, maps, and photographs needs far more energy than any battery a phone can hold today, and the design must cut the phone's hunger, store more energy in less space, and prove the month with real daily work, not with the phone asleep. A month of idling is not a month of use, and the record must not confuse the two.

Worked example, Signature design: the same month-long phone sleeps between touches, harvests light and motion while it works, and measures its month by work done, with the work log published beside the charge log.

History

Running longer on one charge descends from the history of mobile phones, where each generation stretched the battery further by cutting hunger and raising density; the month-long phone is the same stretch, applied to a device that must last thirty times longer than today's.

Proposed solution

Let the phone sleep between touches and wake in less time than a blink; Harvest small amounts of energy from light and motion while it works; Measure the month by work done, not by hours awake, and publish the work log; Warn the owner days before the battery ends, with the work it can still finish; Charge from any ordinary socket, and report the refill time honestly.

Dependencies

A Phone With a Battery That Never Degrades Rank 2 · simple

Feasibility: breakthrough-dependent The design needs a specific breakthrough that has not happened: a battery chemistry or storage mechanism that shows no degradation after thousands of charges.

A design for a phone whose battery never degrades: it holds the same charge on its thousandth day as on its first, after years of daily charging. This is a design, not a real product.

A phone battery degrades a little with every charge, and after a year or two it holds noticeably less than it did when new. The degradation is slow, but it is certain, and it decides the phone's life: the owner charges more often, the phone dies earlier in the day, and eventually the battery, not the phone, decides when the phone is replaced. A battery that never degrades would end that clock: the phone would hold the same charge in its thousandth day as in its first, and the phone's life would be decided by its owner, not by its battery. The problem is the chemistry: every known battery chemistry wears with use, and the design must name the chemistry or the mechanism that does not.

Worked example, Signature design: the same unworn battery stores energy without chemical change, counts every charge, and publishes its capacity openly, so the never-degrades claim can be checked by its owner.

History

Batteries that do not degrade descend from the history of rechargeable cells, where each generation wore more slowly than the last, but none stopped wearing; the unworn battery is the missing chemistry, not the missing idea.

Proposed solution

Use a battery that stores energy without chemical change, so nothing wears; Count every charge and report the capacity, so the owner can see it is not falling; Charge slowly by default, and quickly only when the owner asks; Keep the battery cool while charging, because heat is what ages it fastest; Publish the capacity numbers openly, so the never-degrades claim can be checked.

A battery chemistry with no wear mechanism; a way to prove capacity after thousands of charges; charging that keeps the battery cool; a standard for reporting battery health honestly.

A Phone That Generates Its Own Network for Neighbors Rank 23 · complex

Feasibility: research-stage Public research on mesh networking, hotspots, and community networks exists and is published, but no complete phone that generates its own fair, protected network for neighbors exists at the needed scale yet.

A design for a phone that generates its own network for neighbors: it shares its connection with the phones around it, so a whole street can be online from one phone. This is a design, not a real product.

A neighborhood can lose its network while one phone still has a connection: a tower fails, a cable is cut, or a storm takes the service down, and every phone nearby goes dark except the one that still reaches. A phone that generates its own network for neighbors would share its connection with the phones around it, so a whole street can be online from one phone, until the service returns. The problem is the sharing: the sharing phone must divide its connection fairly, protect each neighbor's traffic from the others, and keep working itself while it serves them all. Without fairness and protection, the shared network is a gift that costs its giver their own connection.

Worked example, Signature design: the same neighbor-sharing phone lets its owner approve each neighbor, gives each a fair share, and keeps each neighbor's traffic separate.

History

Generating a network for neighbors descends from community networks and from hotspots, where one connection served many, and from the old habit of neighbors sharing what they had; the phone version needs that sharing to be fair and protected, not only generous.

Proposed solution

Let the neighbors ask to join, and let the owner approve each one; Give each neighbor a fair share, and show the shares plainly; Keep each neighbor's traffic separate, so no neighbor can read another's; Count the data shared, and report it, so the owner knows what was given; Shut the sharing down with one action, whenever the owner chooses.

Dependencies

Published research on mesh networking and hotspot sharing described in general terms; a radio that can serve many neighbors at once; a way to divide a connection fairly; a standard for protecting shared traffic.
