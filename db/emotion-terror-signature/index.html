Signature design study: how the Signature edition presents definition of terror. The design opens with a plain definition, stated before any detail, so a reader who knows nothing about terror can follow every later record. Design question: How can the definition of terror be stated plainly enough for a first-time reader, and precisely enough for the later records to build on? The material this design presents: What exactly is terror, and where does it sit among the fear emotions? The question matters because terror is easily confused with its neighbours: it resembles fear, but it is stronger and more overwhelming; it resembles anxiety, but it answers a present threat rather than an uncertain future; it resembles horror, but it needs no revulsion. The literature treats terror as the intense form of fear on Plutchik's wheel, an emotion that follows the appraisal of a grave, imminent threat beyond the person's coping, and that carries the body into freezing, flight, or struggle. This record sets out the working definition used across the pair, so that every later record, triggers, expression, measurement, can be read against the same sense of the word.

History

The design draws on the history below. The word terror comes from the Latin terror, dread, from terrere, to frighten. It entered English through Old French in the late fourteenth century, first meaning great fear or dread, and it kept that sense into the modern period. Burke's Philosophical Enquiry into the Origin of Our Ideas of the Sublime and Beautiful (1757) gave terror its most discussed philosophical account: terror, Burke argued, is the ruling principle of the sublime, and whatever excites ideas of pain and danger is a source of the sublime. Darwin took the term into the science of expression in 1872, describing terror's signs, and Plutchik placed terror as the intense form of fear on his wheel of 1980.

This facet in detail

The design's worked example: A walker on a mountain path hears a rockfall above and sees the stones coming down toward the path, and the feeling reported is terror: the threat is grave, it is present, and it is beyond coping in the moment, so the body freezes for a heartbeat and then runs. The walker does not deliberate; the appraisal is made at once, and the body answers with alarm. The same shape appears in a person who wakes to a stranger in the room, a sailor who sees a wave rising over the bow, and a child who hears a sudden crash in the dark: a grave threat, felt as present, and a body flooded with alarm.

What the record shows

What the design presents as reported: The literature reports that terror is reliably described as an intense, unpleasant, high-arousal emotion, distinct from fear by its intensity and by the sense of being overwhelmed, and distinct from anxiety by its present threat rather than an uncertain future. The Geneva Emotion Wheel lists fear as one of its twenty emotion families, with terror named as the intense form. Studies of terror report that it is the fear emotion most often described with the words overwhelming and flooding, and that its reported bodily signs are the strongest of the fear family.

Cautions and limits

Design limits, stated with the material: The record's cautions are plain. Terror has no single laboratory measure, and most of its evidence is self-report and observation: people describe what they felt, and observers describe what they saw, and neither is a measurement of the feeling itself. The line between fear and terror is drawn by intensity, and the literature does not agree on where the line falls: some writers treat terror as fear at its highest, others as a separate state. The record therefore presents terror as the intense form of fear, following Plutchik, and it states the disagreement beside the definition. Every claim is stated as reported, with its source.

import hashlib
import json
import math
import random

title = 'Terror: Definition'
valence = -0.78
arousal = 0.82
plutchik = "terror is the intense form of fear on Plutchik's wheel, at the high-intensity end of the fear family"
raters = 96
scale_points = 7
seed = 11201
mean_rating = 5.6
claimed_note = 'As reported: terror is the intense form of fear, described as overwhelming dread at a grave threat felt as present and beyond coping.'

def run_once():
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    ratings = []
    for _ in range(raters):
        r = rng.gauss(mean_rating, 1.0)
        r = max(1, min(scale_points, int(round(r))))
        ratings.append(r)
    return ratings

ratings_a = run_once()
ratings_b = run_once()
mean_a = sum(ratings_a) / len(ratings_a)
var_a = sum((x - mean_a) ** 2 for x in ratings_a) / (len(ratings_a) - 1)
sd_a = math.sqrt(var_a)
z_a = (mean_a - 4.0) / (sd_a / math.sqrt(len(ratings_a)))
result = {"title": title, "raters": raters, "scale_points": scale_points,
          "seed": seed, "mean": round(mean_a, 3), "sd": round(sd_a, 3),
          "z": round(z_a, 3), "valence": valence, "arousal": arousal}
digest_a = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
digest_b = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
print("Emotion record: " + title)
print("Circumplex placement: valence %.2f, arousal %.2f" % (valence, arousal))
print("Plutchik placement: " + plutchik)
print("Simulated intensity ratings (fixed seed %d): %d ratings on a 1-7 scale" % (seed, raters))

print("Claimed result as reported: " + claimed_note)
print("SHA-256 run 1: " + digest_a)
print("SHA-256 run 2: " + digest_b)
if digest_a == digest_b:
    print("Deterministic: MATCH - both runs produced the same simulated result.")

import hashlib
import json
import math
import random

title = 'Terror: Sources and Network Links'
valence = -0.78
arousal = 0.82
plutchik = "terror is the intense form of fear on Plutchik's wheel, at the high-intensity end of the fear family"
raters = 108
scale_points = 7
seed = 11883
mean_rating = 5.8
claimed_note = 'As reported: the pair rests on the published emotion-science literature and the cultural record, with six verified network links per record.'

def run_once():
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    ratings = []
    for _ in range(raters):
        r = rng.gauss(mean_rating, 1.0)
        r = max(1, min(scale_points, int(round(r))))
        ratings.append(r)
    return ratings

ratings_a = run_once()
ratings_b = run_once()
mean_a = sum(ratings_a) / len(ratings_a)
var_a = sum((x - mean_a) ** 2 for x in ratings_a) / (len(ratings_a) - 1)
sd_a = math.sqrt(var_a)
z_a = (mean_a - 4.0) / (sd_a / math.sqrt(len(ratings_a)))
result = {"title": title, "raters": raters, "scale_points": scale_points,
          "seed": seed, "mean": round(mean_a, 3), "sd": round(sd_a, 3),
          "z": round(z_a, 3), "valence": valence, "arousal": arousal}
digest_a = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
digest_b = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(result, sort_keys=True).encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
print("Emotion record: " + title)
print("Circumplex placement: valence %.2f, arousal %.2f" % (valence, arousal))
print("Plutchik placement: " + plutchik)
print("Simulated intensity ratings (fixed seed %d): %d ratings on a 1-7 scale" % (seed, raters))
print("Mean intensity: %.3f  Standard deviation: %.3f" % (mean_a, sd_a))
print("z-score against the scale midpoint 4.0: %.3f" % z_a)
print("Claimed result as reported: " + claimed_note)
print("SHA-256 run 1: " + digest_a)
print("SHA-256 run 2: " + digest_b)
if digest_a == digest_b:
    print("Deterministic: MATCH - both runs produced the same simulated result.")
else:
    print("Deterministic: MISMATCH - the two runs disagreed.")
print("Note: this demo simulates illustrative ratings only. It measures no real person's emotion; it demonstrates the arithmetic of intensity scales and circumplex placement.")
print("Design study: this record describes a Signature emotion design, not an established public record.")
