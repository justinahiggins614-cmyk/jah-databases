The themed world

Flicker's Cinema Meadow is an outdoor cinema meadow. Walk it in words:

  • The Screen Hill — the big screen on the hillside, blank until the evening's first video.
  • The Blanket Rows — blankets laid out in rows, one row for each channel: Sky Watch, Map Walk, Kitchen Table, Song Room, Science Bench, Book Shelf.
  • The Firefly Path — a path lit by Flicker and his friends, from the gate to the screen.
  • The Ticket Booth — where the ticket set is handed out, one ticket per video.

Tools

Two working tools, plain JavaScript, no backend, no key. They work on the mirror's own 12 entries only.

Search the sample catalog

Type a subject or a word — science, bread, maps, birds — and the search scores the catalog the mirror's own way: 3 points for a word in a title, 1 point for a word in a channel or subject.

Add up watch times

Enter video lengths in seconds, separated by commas, and the adder gives the total as minutes and seconds.

Quiz — which channel?

A quiz drawn from the sample catalog. It is a game, not a record.

Product lines

Three design lines. Every one is labeled Signature or Fun, and none is for sale: designs, printables, and demos only.

Cinema Meadow Ticket Set

Labeled Signature. A set of 12 printable tickets, one for each entry in the sample catalog, with the channel, subject and length on each ticket.

Making: Print the tickets on stiff card and cut along the lines. Hand one out for each video in the evening's lineup. A 3D-print version: print a ticket holder, 100 mm wide, with a 2 mm slot.

Flicker's Film Strip Cards

Labeled Signature. A strip of cards showing how a video is a row of still pictures, one after another, with sound beside them.

Making: Print the strip, cut it out, and fold it like a concertina. Each card is one still picture; flip through them fast to see the idea of motion.

Screen Night Poster

Labeled Fun. A poster of the cinema meadow at dusk: the screen, the blankets, and the animals. Labeled fun: it is a picture, not a record.

Making: Print the poster at A3 on heavy paper. Frame it, or pin it to a board.

Fun stuff
