History

The periodic table began as a puzzle: chemists in the 1800s knew dozens of elements but no order for them. John Newlands noticed in 1865 that every eighth element behaved alike, and in 1869 Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the elements by weight and by behaviour, leaving gaps where elements must exist, and the gaps were later filled by gallium, scandium, and germanium, exactly as he had predicted. In 1913 Henry Moseley showed that atomic number, the count of protons, is the true order, and in the 1940s Glenn Seaborg placed the actinides in their own row.
