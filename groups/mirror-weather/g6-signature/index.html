Section 2 — Math proof (standard math + metamath)

ID: mirror-weather-section-s2

Lineage: SIGNATURE LINE — written by Signature for this mirror; made by Signature, labeled Signature.

The mirror's demonstration model is a deterministic rule. For a place with sample temperature T and sample wind speed W, the feels-like temperature is: feels-like = T − W ÷ 2 The temperature band is a second rule: 25 degrees or more is warm; 15 to 24 degrees is mild; 5 to 14 degrees is cool; below 5 degrees is cold. Worked example: Silver Bay has a sample temperature of 18 degrees and a sample wind speed of 6. The feels-like temperature is 18 − 6 ÷ 2 = 18 − 3 = 15 degrees. At 18 degrees the band is mild. The same place always gives the same demonstration forecast, because the rule depends only on the fixed sample table.

Section 3 — Science proof

The science behind this mirror is weather measurement: temperature, wind, and precipitation. A real forecast starts from readings — thermometers, wind gauges, and rain gauges — feeds them into a model of the air, and reads out what the air is likely to do next. This mirror shows the shape of that work in miniature: a fixed table of readings and a rule that turns readings into a feels-like temperature and a band. That is where the honesty matters. A demonstration model over a sample table is not a real forecast: it has no live readings, no live model, and no claim about the sky anywhere. The mirror labels it as a demonstration model in the hint, in this section, and in Section 6, so no reader mistakes a sample condition for a real current condition.
